The Reader at the Cold Spring

A small fold of high ground above the southern spur where a thin spring comes up out of a basalt slot, a low half-roof of brushwood and goat-hide pegged out at the foot of the slot, a slow fire of olive-wood at the door of the half-roof, and an old man at the fire with a small leather-bound book on his knee and a face the body has known by description for thirty years without ever, by the body's own eyes, having read it.

The country had taken you up off the coast by the second goat-track or out of the cove by the third or off the cliff-path by no track at all, and the country had taken you up the long slow slope south of the spur to the small fold of high ground at the cold spring by the bell of the late afternoon. The fold was a fold the network had been keeping at the small careful distance from the imperial road that a fold a network kept needed. The basalt slot at the head was a small dark mouth in a low grey stone. The spring at its foot came up at the steady cold trickle a spring at that height came up at. The half-roof under the slot was a half-roof of brushwood and goat-hide pegged out at the foot of the rock by four small pegs of olive. There was a slow fire of olive-wood at the door of the half-roof. There was an old man at the fire.

He was perhaps seventy. He had a small white beard the country wind had been working on for many afternoons; the eyes under the dark brows had the steady quiet of a man who had spent his afternoons looking at a fire rather than at the road. The coat over the long grey shirt was a coat the country wove south of the second vineyard. The small leather-bound book on his knee was perhaps a hand wide; the leather was the dark red the Mardesran circle wrote into the cover of a book it had been carrying for several generations. There was no star at his temple. There did not, by his own person at the fire, need to be.

Soraya had come up the slope ahead of you by a quarter-bell and had laid the boat-sail at the back of the half-roof against the basalt and had gone down the slope again at the small clean unhurried pace of a station-keeper at her own station. The boy at the bailing-bucket and the cousin at the sail were at the small lean-to under the goat-hide at the back. There was the smell of boiled lentils. There was the smell of dry thyme. The wind off the upper slope was clean.

The man at the fire looked up. He set his small finger between the leaves of the book. He laid the book closed on the goat-hide at his knee. He gestured, in the small careful way the Mardesran circle had for gesturing a stranger to a fire, at the flat stone opposite.

"Khoda's road, brother," he said. The voice was very soft. "Sit. The fire holds. The lentils are at the pot. I am the Reader of Behesht. Soraya's word came up the slope at the small turning of the morning. The cousin's boat at the cove the cutter's morning had a third figure at the centre thwart who held an ash oar at the small particular hold the boy your mother's brother taught the boys of the kiln at six. The brother who held the oar at the small particular hold is the brother who has been minded for many afternoons to come up at this fire. I have been at this fire, brother, more afternoons than I have been at any other since the year of the second snow." He set his hand at the corner of the book. "The book on my knee was your mother's mother's. The book was your mother's. The book has been at this fire's keeping since your mother stopped getting out of the bed. Sit, brother. Let the country be quiet at the small unhurried hour. There is no morning's bell at this fire today."

He did not, in the speaking, lean. He waited for you to cross the four paces from the slope to the stone. He waited, by the small particular patience of a Reader, for the body to do whatever the body was going to do at the fire's edge. The slow olive smoke at the wind's small turning carried a moment over the spring's mouth and slipped off the rock.

The body had read the book at the corner of his hand before your mind had quite let it. The cover had a small worn place at the upper third where a thumb had been at it many afternoons. The thumb had been a woman's. The woman's thumb at the small worn place was the same small worn place a child's right thumb had put under hers at four when the child had asked, at the kiln-fire, what the small dark words at the head of the third page were and the woman had set her own thumb above the child's at the small worn place and read them aloud.
