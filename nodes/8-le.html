The Cooper's Mother at the Door

A single lane at the foot of a low ridge of olives, the fourth house on the right at the bell of the noon, a small inner yard at the back where a brick oven smokes, the cooper at his bench in the workshop at the lane's head, and a woman of perhaps sixty at the doorpost with a folded brown towel in her hand whose face the body has, at no level since the kiln-fire's afternoon, been minded to come back up the lane in dust-blue and silver olives toward.

The morning's ride had been the morning's ride. The brick-floor post had laid the green linen open at the breakfast-table; the case at the head — the matter of the half-fold at Behesht; brother of the order's question — had been the case the morning's hand had been minded to ride. You had taken the small dust-blue coat the post-priest had pressed at you in the back room and the small black leather case of the order's question at your saddle and the small purse of the bishop's standing rate at your inner pocket. The two men at the saddles behind you had been the post-priest's brother-in-law and the brother-in-law's nephew; they had the parish bounty at their saddles also, in case the morning's ride paid out at more than the morning's standing rate. The road east had taken you up the imperial road by the third milestone past Sadaiziche; you had cut south on the cart-track at the small wayside shrine and come into the country at the foot of a low ridge of olives by the bell of the noon. Behesht was at the lane's bend under an old chestnut. The fourth house on the right was the cooper's mother's.

You did not, in any of the morning's riding, lift the conversation off the morning's ride. The two men at the saddles behind you read the road. The dust-blue at the shoulder was a coat the country at the lane had been reading the cut of for as long as the country had been reading coats; the village did not, by any small particular motion at the foot of the lane, come out of the houses at the bell of the noon. The chestnut at the lane's head was very quiet. The cooper at his workshop had been told, by some small particular movement of the village's news at the morning's bell, to be at his bench and to not, by any movement of the hand at the bench, draw the morning's bell into the shop.

The woman at the fourth house's doorpost did not, by her face, have been told what the morning's bell was. The basket of clean laundry was at her doorstep. The folded brown towel was in her hand. She had stopped halfway through folding at the small particular sound of three horses at the lane's foot. She lifted her face at the bend's fourth step. The hair under the dark blue scarf was an iron grey; the scarf was the country pattern your mother's mother's loom had set the year of the second snow; the hands at the folding were the hands the body had read at every washing-line in your eighth year.

The body had read her face at one hundred and ten paces. The mind, by some small careful unwatching it had been at all morning, had not let it.

She did not, in the lifting of the face, change. She did not stand. She did not, by any small motion, set the folded brown towel aside. She looked at the dust-blue coat at the rider's shoulder. She looked at the small black leather case at the saddle. She looked, briefly, at the face under the coat's collar. She looked back at the basket. She bent. She folded the next small white square of linen the way she had folded a small white square of linen every Thursday since before your mother had stopped getting out of the bed.

"Khorshid," she said. Her voice was very dry. "The cooper is at the workshop. The cooper's wife is at the brick oven at the back. The cooper's mother is at the doorstep folding the cooper's wife's washing. The dust-blue coat at the rider's shoulder is the dust-blue coat a careful man at a brick-floor post puts at the shoulder of a man at his afternoon's breakfast for a morning's ride. The morning's ride is the morning's ride. I am sixty years, child. I have folded a small white square of linen every Thursday since my sister stopped getting out of the bed. I have one more in the basket. Speak first, child. Tell the cooper's mother, at the doorstep at the bell of the noon, what the morning's ride is for."

The two men at the saddles behind you did not, by any small motion, dismount. The cooper at the workshop did not, by any small motion, come out at the door. The chestnut at the lane's head did not, by any small breath of wind, move. The bell of the noon at the small chapel at the south end of the village did not, by any small particular accident of the bell-rope this morning, ring.
