The Bench at the Western Stage

A small wayside fountain three farsang past the ferry's western bank where the country track meets a dust-coloured cart-road south to the salt-line, an old stone trough under a single plane tree at the road's bend, a small low bench at the fountain's near side, and a country priest in a brown wool cassock at the bench with a clay cup half-full of the trough's cold water and the small careful sitting of an old man at a small careful pause in a long day's walking south.

The ferry-yard had been the ferry-yard. The bench under the cane had held the body the small careful half-bell the master had set at the bench, or had not — had held the body at the wall instead at the small particular angle the wall had held the body at the long unhurried hour the master had finished his piece of dry bread, and the boy at the ferry-rope had brought the small flat ferry round to the eastern bank at the bell of the noon, and the body had crossed the small brown current on the small flat ferry under the boy's two short steady pulls. The western bank had a small dust-coloured track at its foot. The track had gone west at the slow careful pace the body had been at all the morning of the noon and the afternoon of the third bell and the long single bell at the foot of the low ridge of poplars at the country's small narrowing.

The third farsang past the ferry was the small wayside fountain at the bend where the country track met an older cart-road that came down at a slow angle from the south's dust-coloured country and went on west to the salt-line. The fountain was a small stone trough under a single plane tree the country had been letting grow at the bend for many seasons. The water at the trough was the cold spring water the country's underground had carried up at a small careful interval since the year of the first chestnut. There was a small low bench at the trough's near side. The bench was a long flat stone laid across two smaller stones in the small careful country particular a country bench was laid at.

An old country priest was at the bench. He was perhaps seventy. He had a brown wool cassock of the small country pattern the priests of the country off the imperial road wore at no bishop's office — no embroidery at the breast, no white cord at the shoulder, no small cross at the haft of a belt-clasp. He had a small clay cup in his hand half-full of the trough's cold water. He had a small wooden staff laid against the bench at his elbow. He had a small woven satchel at his hip. He had a small dry crust of bread at the bench at his other side. He had the small careful sitting of a man who had been at the bench at the slow careful particular hour the bench was at and had not, by any small motion, much intention of being at any other.

He lifted his face at the small careful sound of your boots on the gravel at the bend's foot. He did not, by any small motion, set the cup down. He nodded once at the bench's far end. The plane tree above the bench shifted in a small breath of wind. A single dry leaf came down at the gravel between the bench and the trough.

"Friend." The voice was very dry. "The water at the trough is cold. The bench at the trough's near side holds two at a small unhurried hour. The cart-road south is empty since the bell of the noon and will be empty at the bell of the third also; the country track east goes back to the ferry-yard at the small brown river, which the friend has — by the small dust at the boots — come from this morning. The friend is going west by the small dust at the boots; the friend has not, by my own small honest reckoning at the bench, taken any small particular hurry at the going. Sit, friend. The bench holds."

He set the cup at the bench at his thigh. He lifted the small dry crust of bread. He broke a small piece at his thumb. He laid the small piece at the bench's far end — the end the body would, by the small careful particular of a country bench, sit at if the body sat. He did not look at the body. He looked at the plane tree's small breath of wind at the upper leaves.

"I am Father Marvan," he said, in the same dry voice. "I am at no parish, friend. I was at a parish, three valleys south of here, until the year of the first snow of the year before this one. The country at the third valley took its priest with the chestnut-fall and the parish has been writing itself, by the careful small particular hand of the country at the three valleys, into the country's own small drawer ever since. I walk, friend, between the four wayside fountains the country has at the small careful intervals of the slow road south. I sit at each at a small careful particular hour. I do not, friend, much carry a name to any small careful drawer of the empire's or the bishop's or the order's. I sit at the bench. The bench holds. The cold water at the trough is cold."

He lifted the cup again. He took a small careful sip. The plane tree at the bend let down a second small dry leaf at the gravel between the bench and the trough.

The bench held. The country at the bend was very quiet. There was a small careful interval at the south where the cart-road's empty afternoon waited at no particular bell, and the body had — by the small careful slow walking of these last bells since the western bank — been minded to be at the bench for as long as the bench's slow careful particular hour was at it.
