The Iron at the Brick-Floor

The brick-floor post at Bagh-e-Sang at the bell of the third hour of the morning after the doorstep at Behesht, the back room set out with the brazier at the inner wall and the long pine question-table at the centre and the small black leather case of the order's instruments laid open at the table's near end, the post-priest at the second door, and the bishop's deacon-of-the-south in a clean grey robe at the small triangular table at the window — a man of perhaps forty-five whose hand has been at the bishop's standing rate-book at the south stair for nine years and whose face the body has, at no level before this morning, been at any small careful interval of the road's eye.

The morning's ride back from Behesht had been the morning's ride back. The cooper at the workshop had been at the bench when the post-priest's brother-in-law and the brother-in-law's nephew had walked him out at the back gate at the bell of the second of the afternoon yesterday. The cooper's wife at the bread-oven had not, by any small motion, lifted her face from the second loaf at the small careful afternoon her loaf had been at. The cooper's mother at the doorstep had folded the next small white square of linen at the small careful three-fold a country woman folded a husband's clean towel at sixty years, and she had laid it on the basket, and she had picked up the next from the un-folded pile, and she had not looked up. The cooper had walked between the two saddles at the rear of the small dust-blue riding back along the cart-track at the small unhurried pace the morning's hand kept its riding at. He had not, by any small motion, set the conversation at the country between the two saddles. He had ridden through the bell of the dusk and through the bell of the half-dusk and through the bell of the first into the brick-floor post's inner yard at the bell of the second of the night with his hands folded at the small careful patience of a working man whose own honest accounting at his bench had been the work he had not yet finished at the morning's leaving.

The night at the brick-floor post had been the night the brick-floor post had at the small careful particular of a half-fold case-list's first morning. The post-priest had laid the green linen folder at the breakfast-table at the bell of the first of this morning. The case at the head had been the case the morning's hand had been minded to ride. The small black leather case of the order's instruments at the saddle yesterday had been laid open at the long pine question-table at the back room's centre at the bell of the second of this morning. The brazier at the inner wall had been lit at the bell of the half-second. The small iron with the half-fold mark at the haft had been at the second hook on the brazier's rim at the bell of the third.

The bishop's deacon-of-the-south had come up the imperial road at the bell of the third hour of the morning. The deacon-of-the-south was a man of perhaps forty-five. He had a clean grey robe at the small careful particular pressing of a senior clerk's robe at the bishop's south stair. He had a small embroidered cross at the breast at the small careful interval of the bishop's own particular stitch. He had a brown leather hand-folder under the elbow with the morning's rate-sheet at the lip and the bishop's small careful signature at the head. The face was a careful face — a small careful even pitch at the cheekbone, the eyes the steady careful eyes of a man whose afternoon at the south stair's rate-book had been at the careful interval the bishop had been minded to keep his accounting at for nine years.

He laid the hand-folder at the small triangular table at the window. He sat. He nodded once at the post-priest at the second door. He set the small careful gaze at you at the long pine question-table.

"Brother." The voice was very quiet. "Father Vali of the bishop's south stair. I have ridden up the imperial road this dawn at the bishop's word, at the bell of the third of the morning, because the morning's hand at the brick-floor post has — by the bishop's own pen at the green linen yesterday — been minded to read the matter of the half-fold at Behesht in the careful particular of a column whose first hand is a small particular brother whose face the bishop's drawer has not, in nine years, had at any of the south stair's afternoons." He set his hands at the table. "The brother at the question-table is — by the careful word of the morning — the brother whose careful hand has read the small inner case-list of the order's southern circuit these many years at the small careful particular of a man at no name. The bishop's drawer has now, by the morning's hand at Behesht, a small careful name. The morning's hand at the back room is, by the deacon's small careful afternoon's rate-sheet, the morning's first column at the bishop's southern stair." He turned the rate-sheet a half-turn at the small triangular table so the head of the column read at his side. "The cooper at the cell is at the cell. The iron at the brazier is at the second hook. The small black leather case is at the table's near end. The morning's first half-bell is, by the bishop's word, the brother at the table's. The morning's second half-bell is — by the deacon's small careful word at the bishop's drawer at the south stair — the deacon's."

He folded his hands. He waited.

The post-priest at the second door did not, by any small motion, lift his face. The brazier at the inner wall held. The small iron at the second hook held. The cooper at the cell at the back of the post did not — by any small breath of wind at the cell's window — speak.
