Bardas at the Inner Counter

A small inland fence's back room a half-day's ride west of Karkand, a low oil-lamp at the inner counter, a fat older man in a long brown coat on the high stool behind the counter with a small set of brass scales at his elbow, and the canvas-wrapped bundle from the salt-line's cart laid on a long pine table at the back wall under a piece of folded sacking.

The road past the salt-line had taken you west. The cart at the salt's edge had, by whatever small particular accounting at the eighty paces, become a cart you sat at the foot of with the brown ox's bell ahead of you for the half-bell after the noon. The two men on the cart's tail had been at the salt-line for as long as they had been at the salt-line. The man behind on the road had, by some small particular accountancy, not been at the road past the eighty paces. The canvas-wrapped bundle in the cart's back had been the canvas-wrapped bundle in the cart's back at the salt-line and was, by the bell of the third hour after, still the canvas-wrapped bundle in the cart's back. The cart had taken the empty western track at the foot of the dust-coloured hills for a half-day. At the small dust-coloured town where the track met an old caravan-road from the south, the cart had turned into a yard behind a low stone wall and the two men had got down and the ox had been put at a trough and the bundle had been carried in at the back door of a small house with a single oil-lamp at its inner counter.

The back room was the back room of a fence's house. The walls were low. The plaster had been new in the bishop's second year and was not new this morning. The inner counter was a low oak counter set under the lamp; behind it was a high stool; on the high stool sat a fat older man in a long brown coat with a wide leather belt at the waist and a small set of brass scales at his elbow. The brass scales were the small scales the fence's house weighed silver and small particular contraband on. The man on the stool was perhaps sixty. He had the small careful eyes of a man who had been weighing other men's small particular contraband at his counter for forty years. He had the small still hands of a man who did not, in any of the weighing, hurry.

The two men at the cart had been at this house before. The taller had laid the canvas-wrapped bundle onto the long pine table at the back wall under a piece of folded sacking the man on the stool had pointed at without lifting the hand. The shorter had taken from the inner pocket a small folded paper and had laid it at the counter's edge. The man on the stool had read it without lifting the paper. He had nodded once. He had pushed at them a small purse of cord that the taller had taken under his coat without opening it. They had gone out by the back door at the small unhurried pace of country men whose afternoon at this counter had been the afternoon they had been minded to be at it.

The man on the stool had not, in any of the doing, looked at you. You had stood at the inner door at the wall opposite the counter. The body had not, by any small motion, set itself to leaving.

He spoke without lifting the head.

"Khorshid Daiyani. Field-man, southern circuit. The order's column at Khaboran. The cooper's mother's nephew at Behesht." He lifted the small piece of dried apricot at the side of the counter, set it on his tongue, chewed slowly. "I am Bardas. I have, friend, kept the small careful eye on the order's southern circuit at the back of my counter since the autumn of the year of the open winter, which is the autumn the order put a careful hand at the southern stages of my cousin's husband's nephew's brother's small particular consignments along the coast. The order's careful hand cost me, that autumn, a year's takings. I have not, in the four years since, had at my counter the man whose careful hand it was. I have, at four hours' notice this morning by my man at Sadaiziche, had the news the man's careful hand has come up the western road in the saddle of a cart that I had a small particular interest in being at the salt-line. The cart is at the yard. The bundle is at the table. The man at the inner door is at the inner door."

He turned the small set of brass scales a half-turn. He looked, finally, up. The eyes under the brows were the steady careful eyes a fence kept for the moments his counter mattered. The small lamp behind him at the wall threw a single long shadow across the counter at the angle of his shoulder.

"Speak, friend. The counter is open. The bundle is at the table. The morning has, by my own small accounting, more than one possible afternoon."
