The First Consignment at the Inner Yard

The inner yard of Bardas's inland fence's house the morning after the counter, the back gate open onto a smaller dust-yard at the long shed's near wall, two country men at a small ox-cart with a low canvas-covered bed of small packets and a thin chained boy at the cart's tail, a tall narrow man in a country coat over a yellow shirt with a small bone-handled awl at a cord, and Bardas himself at the long shed's door with a small folded paper in the hand and the small careful afternoon eye of a fence whose morning has set the morning's first column at the brother at the gate's accounting.

The night at the small dust-coloured town had been the night the inner counter had set. You had slept four bells at the back room behind the inner counter on a small wool pallet the back wall kept for the small careful interval Bardas kept for a counter's twin at the dust of his first day. The pallet had been clean. The small lamp at the counter had been at the small constant interval it had been at the night through. There had been the small far sound of the small ox at the trough at the back gate at the bell of the dawn. There had been a small piece of country bread and white cheese at the inner counter's lip at the bell of the second of the morning the back wall's boy had set there at no asking. The four years' takings at Bardas's careful eye were, by the small piece of paper at the inner counter under the brass scales' edge, a small careful column the back wall's drawer had had open at the bell of the dawn — a small careful sum at the head, a small careful schedule of the small inland circuit's stages at the lip.

The morning's first inland delivery had come up to the back gate at the bell of the second of the morning. It had come up the south caravan-road from the small dust-coloured country south of here, by the small careful interval the country at the caravan-road had been moving small particular consignments at since before the bishop's clerk had been at his south stair. The ox-cart at the gate was a small country ox-cart with a low canvas-covered bed. The canvas at the bed had a small ridge at its middle. The ridge was the ridge a row of small packets lying side by side under canvas made when the cart's owners had laid them with the small particular care of country men whose morning's delivery was the morning's delivery.

The two country men at the cart's near side were perhaps thirty-five and forty. They had small country coats of dark brown wool and small dust-coloured caps and the small careful hands of country men who had been at the caravan-road's interval for many seasons. They did not, by any small motion, lift their faces at the body's coming through the back gate. They stood at the cart's tail.

At the cart's tail, by a small length of chain at the cart's iron ring, was a thin boy of perhaps twelve. He had a small country shirt of unbleached cloth. He had no shoes. He had the small careful steady eye of a country boy whose small careful interval at the cart's tail had been the small careful interval of a country boy at the cart's tail for some small careful days now.

The tall narrow man at the gate's inner side was a man you had read at the salt-line's cart yesterday at the canvas-wrapped bundle in the cart's back. He was the same narrow man. He had a small country coat over a yellow shirt and a small bone-handled awl at a cord at the inner pocket and a green-bronze ring at the third finger of the right hand. The middle finger of his right hand carried an old break at the second joint. He had been at the salt-line's cart canvas-wrapped at the bell of the noon yesterday; he was at the back gate this morning at the bell of the second; the bundle at the long pine table had been, by the small careful particular of Bardas's morning's reading, the man at the gate at no further canvas.

"Brother." The voice was the small dry voice of a man who had been at the counter's other side at no formal office these last winters. "Daryush. The cart at the gate is the cart at the gate. The country men are at the cart. The chain at the iron ring is at the boy. The morning's first consignment, by the counter's pen at the bell of the dawn, is at the brother at the gate."

He did not, by his face, lean. The small bone-handled awl at the cord stayed at the inner pocket.

Bardas was at the long shed's door at the inner yard. He had the small folded paper in his hand and the small careful afternoon eye of a fence whose morning's first column had set the brother at the gate's accounting. He spoke without lifting the voice from the small even pitch the inner counter had been at.

"Friend. The morning's column at the back wall is the morning's column. The cart at the gate carries four small packets at the canvas — the small careful work of three southern apothecaries of country who have been finding the bishop's clerk's particular interest in the small particular interval of their afternoons of late. The boy at the chain is a small particular small careful schedule the cart's owners have not, by the morning's clerk at the south, found a clean afternoon's drawer for at the salt-line's far end." He lifted the folded paper. "The counter's pen has written the morning's column at three rates. The friend at the gate is at the morning's first hand. The cart waits at the gate at the small careful interval the morning's first hand is at."

The two country men at the cart's tail did not, by any small motion, look up. The boy at the chain did not, by any small motion, look down. The thin grey morning at the small dust-coloured town came up at the wall of the long shed in the small careful slow particular it came up at every morning of the year.
