The Cliff Above the Boat

A switchback path down a low cliff at first light, a small fishing-boat drawn up on a strip of shingle in the cove below with three figures already at it, and a fourth figure at the cliff's lip ten paces behind you who has been on the path since the third bend.

You had come the night's stages with the network's last guide. There had been a fire above a spring, or a small high fold where a woman with a star-scar had nodded at the next ridge, or a coast-fold where a man with a long jaw had not asked you a name. The morning's last stage was a switchback path that came down a low limestone cliff to a thin strip of grey shingle at the back of a cove. The cove was the kind the country's coast made by accident — a piece of a headland's foot that the sea had thought to take and had not, yet, finished taking. A small fishing-boat was drawn up on the shingle on her larboard side. Three figures stood at her. The light was the small clean light of the half-hour before sunrise.

You had been told the boat. You had been told the three figures. You had been told the bell at which the cove was the cove the boat came in to and the bell at which it was not. You had been told, by the last guide, that you were the only passage the boat would take on this tide and that the boat would, by the swing of the wind, leave the cove before the sun cleared the lip of the cliff. The path was nine switchbacks. You were on the seventh.

The footfall came on the path behind you at the third switchback below the lip. You did not turn. The body, by long practice, had registered the foot's weight at the moment the foot was set on the first stone of the bend; the body had registered, by the rhythm of the next three steps, that the foot was a man's, alone, and that the man's pace had slowed by a half when he came round the lip and saw you on the path below. The man had then resumed at the careful pace of a man who had a reason to come down a switchback at the same speed as the man in front of him. You walked. You did not change your pace. You did not put your hand at the lining at the third button.

At the fifth switchback you paused as a man pauses to look at the cove — anyone coming down a switchback to a boat pauses once at the fifth switchback — and in the pause you read him at the corner of the eye. He was a tall man, perhaps thirty-five, in a dark coat that was the wrong cut for the coast. He had no sword at the hip but the hand at the left side was carried at the small high readiness of a hand that did not need a sword to do its work. He had — by the long quick reading the body gave him in the pause — no star-scar at the temple. He was not a Mardesran. The wind off the cove was at his coat and the coat blew open the small breath that showed, at the inner lining at the third button, the dark thin line of a folded knife in a fold the body knew.

You walked on. He walked on. The boat at the shingle below was a hundred paces by the path and twenty by the cliff if the cliff was a thing a man could come down by a rope. The three figures at the boat were busy at the boat. One of them — the smallest — had turned to look up the cliff a moment past and had, by the looking, registered both you and the man on the path behind you. The smallest figure did not, by any other sign, say what they had registered.

The path was the path. The cove was the cove. The boat would leave when the boat left. You had perhaps three switchbacks more before the path widened into the small grass shelf above the shingle, and on the shelf the path was a path two men walked side by side. The morning was the kind of morning a body had been at the back of for a long time. The morning, by the small slow turning of the small black-haired woman at the boat below — Soraya, or another woman wearing Soraya's face — was a morning the boat had been waiting at this cove a long time for, and had decided, by the wind, to wait one more half-bell for now.

You walked. Six more steps. The body had already counted, without your asking, the three things it could do at the shelf above the shingle. The body had not yet, by your own mind's part of the counting, decided which of the three the morning was being made for.
