Jawaharlal Nehru

First Prime Minister of India (1889–1964)

Jawaharlal Nehru, born in 1889 into a wealthy and prominent family, was the first Prime Minister of independent India and the principal architect of its modern state. Educated in England at Harrow and Cambridge and trained as a barrister, he returned home and was drawn into the independence movement, becoming the devoted disciple and chosen political heir of Mahatma Gandhi—though his outlook was more secular, socialist, and modern than his mentor's. Like Gandhi, he was jailed many times by the British.

When freedom came in 1947, partitioned and bloodied, Nehru took office as prime minister and held it until his death seventeen years later. He set out to build a secular, democratic republic with a parliamentary system and a planned, industrializing economy, founding institutions of science and learning and insisting that India's many faiths and peoples share a single citizenship.

On the world stage he was a founder, alongside Tito and Nasser, of the Non-Aligned Movement, steering newly independent nations on a course between the Cold War superpowers. At home he wrestled with the integration of the princely states, the bitter quarrel with Pakistan over Kashmir, and a chastening border war with China in 1962.

A gifted writer who composed The Discovery of India from a prison cell, Nehru died in office in 1964. His daughter Indira Gandhi and grandson Rajiv Gandhi would each in turn lead the nation he had shaped.
