Ja'far al-Sadiq

Sixth Imam of Shia Islam (c. 700–765)

Ja'far al-Sadiq, born around 700 in Medina, was the sixth Imam of Shia Islam and one of the most revered scholars of the early Islamic world, honoured in both the Shia and Sunni traditions. A descendant of the Prophet Muhammad through the line of Ali and Husayn, he inherited the spiritual authority of the Prophet's family at a turbulent moment, as the Umayyad caliphate fell and the Abbasids rose.

Ja'far chose the path of learning rather than political revolt. He gathered a celebrated circle of students in Medina and taught a vast range of subjects—law, theology, the interpretation of the Quran, and, by tradition, the natural sciences. His legal teachings became the foundation of the Ja'fari school of jurisprudence, the principal school of Twelver Shia Islam. So great was his reputation that scholars who would found major Sunni schools of law, including Abu Hanifa and Malik ibn Anas, are counted among those who learned from him, and the alchemist Jabir ibn Hayyan claimed him as his master.

Earning the title "al-Sadiq," meaning "the Truthful," he was admired for his piety, knowledge and probity. He steered the community of the faithful through dangerous times, declining to press the political claims of the Prophet's house while upholding its spiritual leadership—a stance that shaped the development of Shia thought, including the doctrine of the Imamate.

Ja'far al-Sadiq died in 765, by some accounts poisoned on the orders of a suspicious caliph. Through him passed the line of the Imams, and his immense scholarly legacy made him a foundational figure for a great branch of Islam.
