Joanna of Castile

Queen of Castile and Aragon, "the Mad" (1479–1555)

Joanna of Castile, remembered by history as Juana la Loca, Joanna the Mad, was a queen in name through nearly all her life yet a prisoner in fact, the daughter, wife, and mother of the mightiest rulers of her age.

She was the daughter of the Catholic Monarchs, Isabella I of Castile and Ferdinand II of Aragon, and in 1496 she was married to Philip the Handsome, son of the emperor Maximilian I, in an alliance that joined Spain to the house of Habsburg. The deaths of her elder brother and sister left her at last the heiress of all the Spanish crowns; her younger sister was Catherine of Aragon, who would become a queen of England.

Joanna loved her husband with a desperate jealousy, and his neglect and sudden death in 1506 were said to have unhinged her. Whether truly mad or merely declared so to be set aside, she found her husband, then her father, and finally her son ruling in her name while she was kept from power.

From 1509 she was confined in the palace of Tordesillas, where she lived shut away for forty-six years, nominally still queen alongside her son. There she died in 1555.

From her body came an empire: she was the mother of the emperors Charles V and Ferdinand I. Her grief, and her long captivity, all began with her marriage to Philip the Handsome.
