Charles Brandon, 1st Duke of Suffolk

Companion of Henry VIII, Duke of Suffolk (c. 1484–1545)

Charles Brandon was born about 1484, the son of William Brandon, who carried Henry Tudor's standard at Bosworth Field and was cut down in the battle that made the Tudor dynasty. Raised at court, the orphan grew into a powerful, athletic man and the closest friend of the young Henry VIII, his companion in the tiltyard and the hunt. In 1514 the king raised him to the dukedom of Suffolk, an astonishing height for a man of modest birth.

That same year Henry sent Brandon to France to escort home the king's widowed sister, Mary Tudor, who had been briefly married to the aged Louis XII. The two had an understanding, and rather than risk losing her to another match, they married secretly in France early in 1515. To wed a royal princess without the king's licence was close to treason; only the intercession of Cardinal Wolsey, and a crushing fine, won the couple Henry's pardon.

For three decades Suffolk remained at the centre of affairs, a soldier and courtier rather than a statesman. He led armies into France, sat in judgement at great trials, and in 1536 helped suppress the Lincolnshire rising during the upheavals of the Reformation. Loyal and unsubtle, he kept the king's favour where cleverer men rose and fell.

His marriage to Mary Tudor produced a daughter, Frances, whose own daughter was Lady Jane Grey; through that line Brandon's blood reached briefly to the English throne. After Mary's death he married the young Catherine Willoughby. Charles Brandon died in 1545, mourned by Henry as the one great noble who, the king said, had never knowingly betrayed a friend nor wronged an enemy.
