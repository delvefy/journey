Pedro I of Portugal

King of Portugal, "the Just" and "the Cruel" (1320–1367)

Pedro I of Portugal is remembered less for the deeds of his reign than for a love that became one of the great tragic legends of Europe—his devotion to Inês de Castro.

As crown prince he had been married to a Castilian princess, but he fell in love with her lady-in-waiting, the Galician noblewoman Inês de Castro. After his wife's death he lived openly with Inês and their children. His father, King Afonso IV, came to fear the sway of her ambitious Castilian kin over the future king, and in 1355 he had Inês murdered at Coimbra. Pedro rose in fury against his own father before an uneasy peace was patched between them.

When he came to the throne two years later, Pedro took a terrible vengeance. He hunted down the men who had killed Inês and put them to death with great cruelty, reportedly having their hearts torn out—an act that won him the name of the Cruel, though his stern, impartial justice also earned him the name of the Just. By tradition he declared that he had secretly wed Inês, and had her body raised from the grave, robed and crowned, so that his court should do homage to the dead queen.

His reign was otherwise marked by firm rule and even-handed law. He was the father of King Ferdinand I and, by another woman, of the future John I of Portugal, founder of the House of Aviz.

Above all, his grief for the murdered Inês de Castro passed into poetry and song, never to be forgotten.
