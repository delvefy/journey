Steven Spielberg

American filmmaker, master of the blockbuster (born 1946)

Steven Spielberg is the most commercially successful director in the history of the cinema and the central figure of the modern blockbuster age. With Jaws in 1975 he invented the summer blockbuster, sending audiences fleeing the water; and through Close Encounters of the Third Kind, the gentle fable E.T., the swashbuckling Indiana Jones adventures, and the dinosaur spectacle of Jurassic Park, he proved an unmatched showman with an instinct for wonder and fear alike.

Yet he refused to be only an entertainer. In Schindler's List he made one of the great films about the Holocaust, winning at last the Academy Awards that had long eluded him, and he followed it with the harrowing Saving Private Ryan and a run of grave historical dramas—Amistad, Munich, Lincoln, Bridge of Spies—that married popular storytelling to moral weight.

A founder of his own studio and a tireless producer, he shaped the careers and tastes of a whole generation of filmmakers, working again and again with the composer whose soaring themes became inseparable from his images.

The actor who played his most beloved hero, the whip-cracking archaeologist Indiana Jones, and who ranks among the biggest stars in the history of film, was Harrison Ford.
