Spike Lee

American filmmaker (b. 1957)

Spike Lee is one of the most distinctive and provocative voices in American cinema, a director who has made the experience of Black America his great and abiding subject.

Raised in Brooklyn, he announced himself with the independent comedy She's Gotta Have It and then with the incendiary Do the Right Thing, a portrait of a sweltering day of racial tension on a single city block that remains a landmark of American film. His “joints,” as he calls them, range across the textures of urban life—Mo' Better Blues, Jungle Fever, 25th Hour—and reached their epic height in Malcolm X, the sweeping biography he made with Denzel Washington in the title role.

Decades later he won an Academy Award for the screenplay of BlacKkKlansman. Restless and outspoken, a famous courtside fixture at his beloved Knicks, he has been both celebrated and argued with for forcing hard questions about race onto the screen.

His reach extended beyond film: in the late 1980s, playing his character Mars Blackmon, he made a series of unforgettable sneaker commercials with the basketball star Michael Jordan, asking whether it was the shoes that made the man.
