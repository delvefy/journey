Joachim Murat

Marshal of France and King of Naples (1767–1815)

Joachim Murat was the most dashing cavalry commander of the Napoleonic Wars and, for a time, a king. The son of a Gascon innkeeper, he rose through the armies of the Revolution and won Napoleon's favour by fetching the cannon that crushed a Paris insurrection in 1795. He became Napoleon's aide, then his finest leader of horse, and married the emperor's sister Caroline.

Made a Marshal of France, Murat led the great cavalry charges at Marengo, Austerlitz, Jena, and Eylau, conspicuous in flamboyant uniforms of his own design and reckless to the point of legend. Napoleon rewarded him with a grand duchy and then, in 1808, with the throne of Naples.

His ambition outran his loyalty. After commanding cavalry in the disastrous 1812 invasion of Russia, he quit the retreating army to safeguard his kingdom, and in 1814 he tried to keep his crown by deserting Napoleon for the Allies. When Napoleon returned for the Hundred Days, Murat rashly declared war on Austria and was beaten.

With his patron's final defeat, he made a desperate bid to recover Naples with a handful of followers, was captured in Calabria, and was shot by a firing squad in 1815. He had risen and fallen entirely in the orbit of Napoleon. Among the marshals who shared his battles and his tragic end was Michel Ney.
