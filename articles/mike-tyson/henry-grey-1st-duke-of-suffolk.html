Henry Grey, 1st Duke of Suffolk

English nobleman, father of Lady Jane Grey (1517–1554)

Henry Grey was born in 1517, heir to a leading noble house and from 1530 Marquess of Dorset. His fortunes were transformed by his marriage to Frances Brandon, daughter of Charles Brandon, Duke of Suffolk, and of Mary Tudor, the younger sister of Henry VIII. The match planted the Grey children squarely in the line of succession, for their blood ran back to Henry VII.

A convinced Protestant and a patron of reforming scholars, Grey was a courtier of more ambition than judgement. He sold the wardship of his brilliant eldest daughter, Jane, to the Lord Admiral Thomas Seymour, and after Seymour's fall drew close to the new master of the realm, John Dudley, Duke of Northumberland. When the male Brandon line died out, Grey was granted the dukedom of Suffolk in 1551.

The alliance with Dudley proved fatal. In 1553 the two dukes married Jane to Dudley's son Guildford and, as the boy-king Edward VI lay dying, backed the scheme to set Jane on the throne in place of the Catholic Mary. The coup collapsed within days. Suffolk, who had proclaimed his own daughter queen, abandoned her cause to save himself and was pardoned by Mary I.

He did not stay safe for long. Early in 1554, alarmed by the queen's planned marriage to Philip of Spain, Suffolk joined Wyatt's Rebellion, raising the Midlands without success. His treason removed the last reason to spare Jane: she was executed, and Suffolk followed her to the scaffold on Tower Hill in February 1554. His reckless gambling with his daughter's life made him one of the most tragic of the Tudor grandees.
