Artaxerxes III

Achaemenid King of Persia (reigned 358–338 BC)

Artaxerxes III, whose given name was Ochus, was the last strong ruler of the Persian empire, and he made his way to the throne through blood. To forestall any rival, he had his brothers and a host of other princes massacred, securing his power by terror before he had fairly begun to reign.

His great achievement was the recovery of Egypt. The wealthy Nile kingdom had thrown off Persian rule for some sixty years; Artaxerxes invaded, drove out its last native pharaoh, and brought it once more under the Great King—earning a reputation for harshness, for it was said he scorned the gods of the conquered. He crushed revolts in Phoenicia and among his satraps, and for a moment restored the empire to something like its old strength.

But his power leaned dangerously on others, above all the eunuch Bagoas, who managed his court and his armies. In 338 BC Bagoas turned on his master and poisoned him, then slaughtered most of his sons to clear the path for a puppet of his own choosing.

So the last vigorous reign of Persia ended in murder, just as a new power was rising in the west under Philip of Macedon. The distant kinsman whom the kingmaker Bagoas finally raised to the tottering throne—the Great King fated to meet Alexander and lose everything—was Darius III.
