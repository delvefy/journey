Donato Bramante

Italian architect of the High Renaissance (c. 1444–1514)

Donato Bramante, born near Urbino, was the architect who carried the Renaissance to its grandest, most classical phase. He began as a painter, schooled in the perspective and dignity of the Urbino court, before turning to building during the long years he spent in Milan in the service of Duke Ludovico Sforza. There he created the illusionistic choir of Santa Maria presso San Satiro, painting depth into a wall where no room existed, and remodeled Santa Maria delle Grazie, on whose refectory wall his friend Leonardo da Vinci painted the Last Supper.

When the French overran Milan in 1499, Bramante fled to Rome, and there his genius matured among the ancient ruins. In 1502 he built the Tempietto, a tiny circular shrine of perfect proportion that scholars have ever since regarded as the first true monument of the High Renaissance, a building that distills antiquity into a single harmonious form.

Pope Julius II, hungry to remake Rome, made Bramante his chief architect. For him Bramante laid out the vast Belvedere Court of the Vatican and, in 1506, began the most ambitious project of the age: the demolition of old St. Peter's and its replacement with an immense new basilica on a centralized plan crowned by a dome. He did not live to raise it; the work passed to Raphael and at last to Michelangelo.

Bramante died in 1514, the acknowledged master of the Roman school. The prince whose Milanese court had first given him the means and freedom to build, and whose fall scattered its artists across Italy, was Ludovico Sforza.
