Aemilius Paullus

Roman general and statesman (c. 229–160 BC)

Lucius Aemilius Paullus belonged to one of the proudest patrician houses of Rome, and carried the burden of a famous grief: his father had fallen in the great slaughter at Cannae, where Hannibal destroyed a Roman army. The son rose through a long and disciplined career to redeem that memory and crown the family name with the surname Macedonicus.

His supreme achievement came in 168 BC at the Battle of Pydna, where his legions broke the great phalanx of King Perseus and ended the ancient monarchy of Macedon, the kingdom of Philip and Alexander's heirs. Macedon was dismembered, and Rome's mastery of the Greek world was sealed. Paullus led a triumph of unmatched splendor through Rome, the captive king and the treasures of Macedon paraded before the people.

Yet he kept almost none of that wealth for himself, turning the spoils over to the state—a model of the old Roman virtue of incorruptible service. A cultivated man, he loved Greek learning, carried off the royal library of Macedon for his sons, and did much to bring Hellenic culture into Roman life, even as his triumph was darkened by the death of his two young sons.

His blood and his values passed to a son given in adoption to another great house, who would surpass even his father's fame as a soldier and a patron of Greek culture, and who would raze Carthage to the ground—Scipio Aemilianus.
