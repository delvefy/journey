Ulysses S. Grant

Union general and 18th President of the United States (1822–1885)

Ulysses S. Grant was the general who won the American Civil War for the Union and went on to the presidency. A West Point graduate who had drifted into failure and obscurity in civilian life, he found his calling when the war broke out. In the Western theatre he won the victories that first made his name—capturing forts on the rivers, surviving the carnage of Shiloh, and splitting the Confederacy with the masterly campaign against Vicksburg.

Recognising in him a commander who would fight, Abraham Lincoln gave Grant charge of all the Union armies. With grim determination he hammered Robert E. Lee through the bloody Overland Campaign and the long siege of Petersburg, until at Appomattox Court House in April 1865 he received Lee's surrender on generous terms, binding up the nation's wounds even in the moment of victory.

Elected president in 1868, Grant defended the freed slaves and the work of Reconstruction, pushing through enforcement of voting rights and breaking the first Ku Klux Klan, though his administration was stained by the corruption of those around him.

Ruined in old age by a swindling partner and dying of cancer, he raced against death to write his memoirs and provide for his family. The book—published by his friend Mark Twain—became a bestseller and a classic of the soldier's art, finished only days before he died in 1885. The commander he had served, and avenged, was Abraham Lincoln; the friend who gave his last work to the world was Mark Twain.
