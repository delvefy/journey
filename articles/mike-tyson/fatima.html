Fatima

Daughter of Muhammad (c. 605–632)

Fatima, born around 605 in Mecca, was the daughter of the Prophet Muhammad and his first wife Khadija, and one of the most revered women in all of Islam. The youngest of Muhammad's daughters and, by tradition, his most beloved, she was the only one of his children to outlive him and to leave descendants, so that the Prophet's bloodline ran on through her alone.

She grew up amid the trials of early Islam, sharing in the hardships of the Meccan persecution and the migration to Medina. There she married her father's cousin and devoted follower, Ali ibn Abi Talib, in a union celebrated as a model of pious simplicity. Their household—Muhammad, Ali, Fatima, and her two sons Hasan and Husayn—came to be venerated as the Ahl al-Bayt, the "People of the House," the holy family of the Prophet.

Fatima's life was short and ended in sorrow. Muhammad died in 632, and Fatima, grief-stricken, survived him by only a few months. Her final weeks were embittered by a dispute with the new caliph, Abu Bakr, over her inheritance and over the succession, a controversy that Shia tradition remembers with deep feeling.

She died, still young, in 632. To Muslims, and above all to the Shia, who call her al-Zahra, "the Radiant," Fatima is the supreme example of feminine virtue, counted among the perfect women of the faith. Through her sons—the gentle Hasan and the martyred Husayn—descended the line of the Imams, and the reverence for the Prophet's family that endures to this day flows above all from her.
