Fidel Castro

Cuban revolutionary and leader (1926–2016)

Fidel Castro was the revolutionary who seized power in Cuba and ruled the island for nearly half a century, turning a small Caribbean nation into a defiant fixture of the Cold War.

Born to a prosperous farming family and trained as a lawyer, he turned to insurrection against the dictator Fulgencio Batista. After a failed barracks attack and imprisonment—at his trial he proclaimed “history will absolve me”—he regrouped in exile and returned aboard the yacht Granma to wage guerrilla war from the Sierra Maestra. On the first day of 1959 his rebels triumphed and Batista fled.

Castro built a one-party communist state, nationalized industry, and aligned Cuba with the Soviet Union, drawing the relentless hostility of the United States. He crushed the US-backed exile invasion at the Bay of Pigs and, in 1962, brought the world to the brink of nuclear war when Soviet missiles on the island triggered a thirteen-day showdown with John F. Kennedy. He outlasted the embargo, countless assassination plots, and ten more American presidents, sending Cuban troops as far as Angola, where their stand helped hasten the fall of apartheid and won him the lasting gratitude of Nelson Mandela.

Invoking always the memory of the independence hero José Martí, he ruled until age and illness forced him to hand power to his brother, and he died in 2016. The charismatic Argentine doctor who had fought at his side from the Sierra Maestra and became the global icon of revolution was Che Guevara.
