Antonio Vivaldi

Italian Baroque composer (1678–1741)

Antonio Vivaldi was the brilliant Venetian master of the Baroque concerto, a composer whose bright, driving, inexhaustibly inventive music became some of the most popular ever written. Born in Venice and ordained a priest—nicknamed "the Red Priest" for his flaming hair—he set aside his clerical duties to devote himself to music, in which he had been trained as a virtuoso violinist.

For much of his career he taught at the Ospedale della Pietà, a Venetian home for orphaned and foundling girls, whose remarkable orchestra and choir he led to European fame. For them and for patrons across the continent he poured out music at astonishing speed—some five hundred concertos, along with operas, sonatas, and sacred works such as the radiant Gloria. Above all he composed The Four Seasons, a set of violin concertos that paint spring birdsong, summer storms, and winter ice in sound, and that remain beloved the world over.

Vivaldi gave the solo concerto its classic three-movement shape and a vividness all his own. His influence spread far beyond Venice: Johann Sebastian Bach studied and transcribed his concertos, absorbing his methods into the German tradition. Yet fashion turned against him; he died in poverty in Vienna in 1741 and was nearly forgotten until a modern revival restored his fame.

The elder Roman master who had first perfected the Baroque concerto and the art of the violin, and on whose foundations Vivaldi built, was Arcangelo Corelli.
