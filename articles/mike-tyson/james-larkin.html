James Larkin

Irish trade union leader (1874–1947)

James Larkin—“Big Jim”—was the thunderous orator and labour organizer who roused the Dublin working class and founded the union movement that still bears his stamp.

Born in the Liverpool slums to Irish parents, he worked the docks and rose through the British labour movement before coming to Ireland, where in 1909 he founded the Irish Transport and General Workers' Union. A fervent believer in the sympathetic strike and the power of “one big union,” he organized the city's unskilled poor and turned industrial disputes into moral crusades. With James Connolly he also helped found the Irish Labour Party.

His defining battle was the great Lockout of 1913, when some four hundred employers led by the businessman William Martin Murphy shut out twenty thousand workers in an effort to break his union. For six bitter months Dublin's families went hungry; relief was organized by allies such as Constance Markievicz, and a small Irish Citizen Army was raised to shield the strikers from the police. The workers were eventually starved back, but “Larkinism” had become a legend.

Larkin then spent nearly a decade in the United States agitating for socialism, where he was imprisoned during the Red Scare and later deported. Returning home, he founded a new union and remained a combative presence in Irish labour and left-wing politics until his death in 1947, mourned by a vast crowd. The young dramatist who served as the first secretary of his Citizen Army, and who would carry the world of Larkin's Dublin onto the stage, was Sean O'Casey.
