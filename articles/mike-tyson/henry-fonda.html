Henry Fonda

American actor (1905–1982)

Henry Fonda was one of the most distinguished actors of the American screen, celebrated for embodying upright, principled men of quiet conviction across more than four decades.

From the plains of Nebraska he came up through the stage to Hollywood, where he gave a series of indelible performances: the migrant Tom Joad in John Ford's The Grapes of Wrath, the young Abraham Lincoln, the frontier marshal of My Darling Clementine, and the lone holdout juror who turns a verdict in 12 Angry Men. His was a presence of understated decency and moral weight.

His closest friend in life was James Stewart, with whom he shared a struggling youth and a lifelong devotion despite their opposite politics. After a long career strangely unrewarded by the Academy, he won the Oscar at last for On Golden Pond, acting opposite Katharine Hepburn and his own daughter, just before his death.

He founded an acting dynasty: his children Jane and Peter both became stars, and the most famous of them, an actress and activist who matched his fame, was his daughter Jane Fonda.
