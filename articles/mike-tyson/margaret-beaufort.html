Margaret Beaufort

Countess of Richmond, mother of Henry VII (1443–1509)

Margaret Beaufort, born in 1443, was the great-granddaughter of John of Gaunt through his Beaufort children, and so carried in her veins a strand of royal Lancastrian blood that would one day prove priceless. Married as a child to Edmund Tudor, half-brother of Henry VI, she was widowed and, at just thirteen, gave birth to her only child, the future Henry VII, in 1457. The dangerous delivery left her unable to bear more children, and she devoted the rest of her long life to that one son.

Through the upheavals of the Wars of the Roses, Margaret guarded and advanced her son's slender claim with patience and cunning. When the Yorkist king Richard III alienated his supporters, she conspired—reaching across the divide to the queen-dowager Elizabeth Woodville—to unite the warring houses by marrying her son to Elizabeth of York. Her third husband, Thomas Stanley, held back his forces at the decisive battle and then turned them against Richard, helping to win Bosworth Field in 1485.

With her son crowned as Henry VII, Margaret became the most powerful woman in England, styling herself "Margaret R" and signing as the King's Mother. She managed great estates, dispensed patronage, and presided over the court with formidable authority. A woman of deep piety and learning, she became one of the great patrons of the age: she endowed Christ's College and St John's College at Cambridge, supported the new printing press of William Caxton, and founded professorships that endure today.

She lived just long enough to see her grandson, the young Henry VIII, succeed to a secure throne in 1509, before dying weeks later. From her single child sprang the entire Tudor dynasty.
