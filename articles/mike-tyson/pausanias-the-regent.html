Pausanias the Regent

Spartan general and regent (died c. 470 BC)

Pausanias was the Spartan commander who led the Greeks to their final victory over the Persian invasion, only to ruin himself by overreaching ambition. Of the royal Agiad house, he was nephew to King Leonidas I, and after that king fell at Thermopylae he governed Sparta as regent for Leonidas's young son.

In 479 BC he took supreme command of the combined Greek army at Plataea and there destroyed the Persian host that Xerxes had left behind under his general Mardonius, who was killed in the battle. It was the decisive land engagement of the war, and Pausanias was celebrated as the liberator of Greece. He went on to lead the allied forces in freeing Byzantium and the Aegean.

But success spoiled him. His arrogance toward the allies helped drive the leadership of the Greek cause from Sparta to Athens, and worse rumours followed: that he had begun secret dealings with Xerxes himself, dreaming of ruling Greece as a Persian vassal and even marrying into the royal house, and had taken to Persian dress and manners.

Recalled and suspected of stirring the helots to revolt, he was at last cornered. Taking sanctuary in the temple of Athena of the Brazen House, he was walled inside by his own countrymen and left to starve. His fall, set beside that of Themistocles, was recorded by Thucydides. The Persian general he had overcome at Plataea was Mardonius.
