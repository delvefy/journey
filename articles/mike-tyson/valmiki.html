Valmiki

The “first poet,” reputed author of the Ramayana (antiquity, by tradition)

Valmiki is revered in Indian tradition as the Adi-Kavi, the “first poet,” and the author of the Ramayana, the great Sanskrit epic that tells of the prince Rama, his exile, the abduction of his wife, and his victorious return. Whether a single historical poet of that name composed it is impossible to say, for the epic grew and was reshaped over centuries; but the tradition is unwavering that the work and the very art of poetry began with him.

Legend tells that Valmiki was once a brigand named Ratnakara who waylaid travellers in the forest, until holy men turned his heart and set him to meditate so long and so still that white-ant hills—valmika—rose over his motionless body, giving him his name. The most beloved legend explains how poetry itself was born: walking by a river, the sage saw a hunter shoot down one of a pair of mating birds, and his cry of grief broke spontaneously into measured verse. From that meter, born of sorrow, he is said to have composed the whole of the Ramayana.

By tradition he gave shelter in his forest hermitage to the banished Sita, where her twin sons were born and learned to sing their father's story from the poet's own lips.

Whatever the truth behind the legend, the Ramayana became one of the formative texts of Indian and indeed all South and Southeast Asian civilization, retold in countless languages and forms. Among those who retold it, the poet-saint who gave it to the people of northern India in their own speech was Tulsidas.
