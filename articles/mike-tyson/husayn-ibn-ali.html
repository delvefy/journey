Husayn ibn Ali

Grandson of Muhammad, martyr of Karbala (626–680)

Husayn ibn Ali, born in 626, was a grandson of the Prophet Muhammad—the younger son of Ali and Fatima—and is revered as the third Imam of Shia Islam. His martyrdom at Karbala became the most powerful and defining tragedy of the Shia faith, commemorated each year with grief and devotion.

Husayn grew up in the household of the Prophet, who is said to have cherished him dearly. After the deaths of his father Ali and his elder brother Hasan, Husayn became the head of the Prophet's family. For a time he lived quietly under the truce his brother had made with the Umayyad caliph Mu'awiya. But when Mu'awiya died in 680 and named his son Yazid as his successor, Husayn refused to recognise a ruler he regarded as impious and illegitimate.

Summoned by supporters in the Iraqi city of Kufa, Husayn set out with a small band of family and followers. The Umayyad governor intercepted them in the desert at Karbala. There, cut off from water and vastly outnumbered, Husayn and his companions were surrounded for days and then, on the tenth of Muharram—the day of Ashura—killed almost to a man. Husayn himself fell fighting, and his head was carried to Yazid.

The massacre at Karbala seared itself into the conscience of Islam. For Shia Muslims it became the supreme example of righteous sacrifice against tyranny, mourned every Ashura in processions and passion plays, and a rallying cry against injustice ever after. Through Husayn's surviving son the line of the Imams continued. No event did more to define the identity and the passion of Shia Islam than the death of the Prophet's grandson.
