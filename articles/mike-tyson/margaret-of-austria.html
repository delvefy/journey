Margaret of Austria

Governor of the Habsburg Netherlands (1480–1530)

Margaret of Austria was one of the most able stateswomen of her age, twice-widowed regent of the Netherlands and the guardian who raised the boy who would become the emperor Charles V.

Daughter of Maximilian I and Mary of Burgundy, she was sent as a small girl to France, betrothed to the heir of its throne and reared at the French court—only to be cast off when he married elsewhere. She wed next the heir of Spain, who died within months, and then the Duke of Savoy, who also died young; for him she raised the beautiful monastery of Brou. Twice a widow before she was twenty-five, she refused to marry a fourth time.

Her father then set her to govern the Habsburg Netherlands, which she ruled with patience and shrewdness from her court at Mechelen, a centre of music and art. To her, too, he entrusted his orphaned grandchildren, and she brought up the future Charles V, schooling him for the vast inheritance that awaited him.

As a diplomat she had no equal. In 1529 she met Louise of Savoy, the mother of King Francis I of France, and the two women negotiated the Treaty of Cambrai—the famous Ladies' Peace—which halted the long war between Charles V and Francis I.

She died in 1530, the Netherlands secure for her house. Her partner in that great peace had been the formidable Louise of Savoy.
