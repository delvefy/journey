Harsha

Emperor of Kannauj, last great ruler of northern India of his age (c. 590–647)

Harshavardhana, known simply as Harsha, was the last sovereign to bring most of northern India under a single hand before the long centuries of division that followed. He came to the throne of Thanesar as a youth, in tragic circumstances: his elder brother had been treacherously slain by a rival king and his widowed sister cast into danger. Harsha avenged his brother, rescued his sister, and set about building an empire.

Through years of campaigning he extended his power across the Indo-Gangetic plain, ruling from the great city of Kannauj. His ambition to push south, however, was checked at the Narmada river by the Chalukya king of the Deccan, and the two great powers divided India between them.

Harsha is remembered less for conquest than for the brilliance and generosity of his reign. A patron of every faith, inclining in later life toward Buddhism, he held magnificent assemblies at which he gave away his treasury to monks, scholars, and the poor. He was himself a man of letters, credited with the authorship of three Sanskrit plays. His court was a centre of learning that welcomed the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang, whose writings preserve a vivid picture of the king and his realm.

Having left no heir, Harsha saw his empire dissolve soon after his death in 647. The poet of his court, who immortalized his life in an ornate Sanskrit biography, the Harshacharita, was Banabhatta.
