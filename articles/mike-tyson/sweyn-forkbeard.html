Sweyn Forkbeard

Viking king of Denmark and England (c. 960–1014)

Sweyn Forkbeard, born around 960, was king of Denmark and, for a few weeks before his death, the conqueror and king of all England. The son of Harald Bluetooth, the king who first united Denmark and brought it to Christianity, Sweyn rose against his father and seized the Danish throne around 986.

A formidable Viking warlord, Sweyn turned his energies against England, then ruled by the hapless Æthelred the Unready. For two decades he led and supported devastating raids across the country, extracting enormous payments of Danegeld. The turning point came after Æthelred's massacre of Danes on St Brice's Day in 1002—an atrocity that, by tradition, claimed Sweyn's own sister. Vowing vengeance, Sweyn campaigned with mounting ferocity.

In 1013 he launched a full invasion, and this time he came to conquer. Town after town submitted; Æthelred fled across the sea to Normandy; and at the end of the year Sweyn was acclaimed king of England, the first Dane to rule the whole kingdom. His triumph was astonishingly brief. Barely five weeks later, in February 1014, Sweyn died suddenly at Gainsborough.

His death threw England back into turmoil and allowed Æthelred to return, but the Danish claim did not die with him. Sweyn's son, the young Cnut, would within two years complete his father's work and win England for good, going on to build the great North Sea Empire. Sweyn Forkbeard is thus remembered as the Viking king who broke Anglo-Saxon England and founded, however briefly, a Danish dynasty upon its throne.
