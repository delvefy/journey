George Lucas

American filmmaker, creator of Star Wars (born 1944)

George Lucas changed the business and the imagination of the movies more than almost anyone of his time. After the youthful nostalgia of American Graffiti he wrote and directed Star Wars in 1977, a space fairy tale—steeped in the films of Akira Kurosawa and the myth-patterns of Joseph Campbell—that became a cultural phenomenon, spawned an empire of sequels and merchandise, and remade Hollywood around the blockbuster franchise.

He created a second beloved saga in Indiana Jones, leaving its direction to his friend Steven Spielberg, and built an array of companies to serve his vision. His visual-effects house, Industrial Light & Magic, transformed what the screen could show; his THX standard reshaped how films sound; and a small computer-graphics division of his studio would grow into something he could not have foreseen.

For all his commercial genius he remained a somewhat reluctant, controlling artist, tinkering endlessly with his creations. In 2012 he sold his company to Disney for billions and turned to philanthropy.

That computer-graphics division he spun off in the 1980s was purchased and renamed Pixar by a young technology visionary who would go on to remake the digital world—Steve Jobs.
