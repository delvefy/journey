Warren Buffett

American investor, the “Oracle of Omaha” (born 1930)

Warren Buffett is the most celebrated investor of the modern age. From his unglamorous base in Omaha, Nebraska, he built the holding company Berkshire Hathaway—a failing textile mill when he took it over—into one of the largest and most admired enterprises in the world, compounding wealth at a rate that made him, for a time, the richest man alive and a financial legend in his own lifetime.

His method was patience itself: to buy great businesses at sensible prices and hold them, ignoring the fevers of the market. Investments in companies like Coca-Cola and American Express, held for decades, vindicated the approach again and again. Famous for his frugality, his plain living, his witty annual letters, and the carnival-like shareholder meetings he hosted with his partner Charlie Munger, he became a folk hero of capitalism.

Yet he resolved that his fortune should not pass to dynasty. Pledging to give away nearly all of it—much of it through the foundation of his friend Bill Gates—he helped launch a campaign urging the world's richest to do the same.

All of it rested on a discipline he had learned as a young man from a professor at Columbia, the thinker who founded the practice of value investing and wrote its bible, The Intelligent Investor—Benjamin Graham.
