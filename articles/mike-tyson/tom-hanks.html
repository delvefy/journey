Tom Hanks

American actor and filmmaker (b. 1956)

Tom Hanks is among the most beloved and respected actors of modern Hollywood, an everyman of such warmth and decency that he is often likened to the great James Stewart.

After early success in television and comedies such as Big, he reached the front rank with two consecutive Academy Awards for Best Actor: as a lawyer dying of AIDS in Philadelphia, opposite Denzel Washington, and as the guileless hero of Forrest Gump, alongside Robin Wright. Few actors have ever won the prize in back-to-back years.

A remarkable run of films followed—Apollo 13, Cast Away, Catch Me If You Can with the young Leonardo DiCaprio, and the voice of the cowboy Woody in the Toy Story films, which made him a hero to children as well. He became a favourite of the director Steven Spielberg, starring in Saving Private Ryan and several more of his pictures, and producing with him the war series Band of Brothers.

Admired for his craft and his character alike, he has also directed, produced, and written, a kind of national institution of American film.

His first Oscar-winning film paired him with another of the screen's finest, Denzel Washington.
