Averroes

Andalusian philosopher (1126–1198)

Ibn Rushd, known to the Latin world as Averroes, was born in 1126 in Córdoba in Muslim Spain and became the greatest philosopher of the western Islamic world. A man of many parts—jurist, physician, and astronomer—he served as a judge in Seville and Córdoba and as court physician to the Almohad caliphs who ruled Spain and North Africa.

His enduring achievement was a lifetime of meticulous commentary on the works of Aristotle, undertaken at his patron's request to make the Greek master intelligible. So thorough was this labor that medieval Europe knew him simply as "the Commentator." Lacking a copy of Aristotle's Politics, he commented instead on Plato's Republic, preserving political philosophy for his tradition.

Averroes was also a fierce defender of reason. When the theologian al-Ghazali attacked philosophy in his Incoherence of the Philosophers, Averroes answered with the Incoherence of the Incoherence, and in his Decisive Treatise he argued that philosophy and religious law, properly understood, could not contradict one another.

Though he fell from favor late in life and saw some of his books burned, his influence outlived him spectacularly. His Aristotle commentaries, translated into Latin and Hebrew, shaped Jewish thinkers such as Maimonides and ignited the movement of "Latin Averroism" in the Christian universities—a current so potent that Thomas Aquinas devoted himself to answering it. Averroes died in Marrakesh in 1198, his work destined to reshape the mind of medieval Europe.
