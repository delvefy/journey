Francis I

King of France (1494–1547)

Francis I, who ruled France from 1515 to 1547, was the very model of a Renaissance prince—chivalrous, cultured, ambitious, and extravagant. He opened his reign with a dazzling victory at Marignano that won him the duchy of Milan, and much of his long rule was consumed by the struggle with the Habsburg emperor Charles V for mastery of Italy and Europe.

That rivalry brought him both glory and humiliation. At the Battle of Pavia in 1525 his army was destroyed and Francis himself taken prisoner, carried off to captivity in Madrid and freed only after signing a punishing treaty he promptly disavowed. To counter Habsburg power he courted unlikely partners, meeting Henry VIII amid the fabled splendor of the Field of the Cloth of Gold and forging a startling alliance with the Ottoman sultan Suleiman.

Francis's most enduring legacy lay not in war but in culture. He poured the wealth of the crown into the arts, importing the Italian Renaissance wholesale into France. Above all, he persuaded the aging Leonardo da Vinci to settle in France, lodging him near the royal château of Amboise, where the great master spent his final years. Francis built the soaring château of Chambord, enriched Fontainebleau, and fostered learning and the French language.

He strengthened the authority of the crown and left France a cultural power. On his death in 1547 he was succeeded by his son Henry II.
