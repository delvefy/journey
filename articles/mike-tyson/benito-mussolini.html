Benito Mussolini

Italian dictator and founder of Fascism (1883–1945)

Benito Mussolini founded Fascism and ruled Italy for more than two decades as its dictator, “Il Duce.” A fiery socialist journalist in his youth, he broke with the left over the First World War and forged a new movement of nationalism, violence, and the cult of the strong leader. In 1922 his Blackshirts' March on Rome frightened the king into appointing him prime minister, and from there he dismantled Italian democracy and built a one-party totalitarian state that became the model for fascists everywhere.

Hungry for empire and Roman glory, he conquered Ethiopia, seized Albania, and joined Hitler in arming the nationalists in the Spanish Civil War. He bound Italy ever more tightly to Nazi Germany, and in 1940 dragged his unready country into the Second World War at Hitler's side, against the Britain of Churchill.

The war ruined him. His armies failed in Greece, Africa, and Russia; in 1943 his own Fascist council and the king deposed him. Rescued by German commandos, he was set up as the puppet ruler of a rump fascist republic in the north until, in the last days of the war in 1945, partisans captured and shot him, hanging his body in a Milan square.

The fellow dictator whom he and Hitler had helped to victory in the Spanish Civil War was Francisco Franco.
