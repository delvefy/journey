Louis XVI

King of France (1754–1793)

Louis XVI was the last king of old-regime France, a well-meaning, devout, but irresolute man who inherited the absolute throne built by Louis XIV and presided over its collapse. More at ease with hunting and his hobby of locksmithing than with the burdens of rule, he came to the crown in 1774 with his young queen, Marie Antoinette, at his side and a kingdom already deep in debt.

His reign's great gamble abroad was to back the American Revolution against Britain. The alliance, negotiated at his court by Benjamin Franklin, helped win American independence but pushed France toward bankruptcy. To meet the crisis Louis was forced at last to summon the Estates-General in 1789, for the first time in 175 years—and so, unwittingly, opened the floodgates of revolution.

Events swept past him. The Bastille fell, the old order was dismantled, and the king became first a constitutional monarch and then a virtual prisoner. His attempted flight to the frontier, halted at Varennes, destroyed what trust remained. The monarchy was abolished, and the king, now styled merely Citizen Capet, was tried for treason and, by a narrow vote, condemned.

On 21 January 1793 Louis XVI went calmly to the guillotine, his death stunning the courts of Europe and driving the Revolution toward its bloodiest phase. Among those who had argued most fiercely that the king must die was the incorruptible Jacobin, Maximilien Robespierre.
