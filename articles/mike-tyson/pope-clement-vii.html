Pope Clement VII

Medici pope, reigned 1523–1534 (1478–1534)

Pope Clement VII was the Medici pontiff whose hesitant, ill-fated rule saw Rome sacked, England lost to the Church, and the Reformation pass beyond recall.

Born Giulio de' Medici, the illegitimate and orphaned son of a murdered Medici, he was raised in Florence in the household of his uncle Lorenzo de' Medici, “the Magnificent,” alongside the cousin who became Pope Leo X. As a cardinal he was an able administrator, but the papacy exposed his fatal indecision. Caught between the two titans of the age, he swung from one to the other, and in joining a league against the Emperor he brought catastrophe upon Rome: in 1527 the mutinous troops of Charles V stormed and pillaged the city, besieging the pope in the Castel Sant'Angelo.

Humbled and dependent on the very emperor he had defied, he could not afford to offend him—and so, when Henry VIII of England demanded the annulment of his marriage to Catherine of Aragon, who was the emperor's aunt, Clement delayed and refused. Henry broke with Rome, and England was lost to the old faith forever.

For all his political failures he was a true Medici patron of art, employing Sebastiano del Piombo and commissioning from Michelangelo the Medici tombs, the Laurentian Library, and the great Last Judgment. He died in 1534. Among his last designs was the marriage of his young kinswoman Catherine de' Medici into the royal house of France—the house of the brilliant king who had been his ally and the emperor's lifelong rival, Francis I.
