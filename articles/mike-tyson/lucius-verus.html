Lucius Verus

Roman co-emperor (130–169)

Lucius Verus was Rome's first co-emperor, sharing the throne for eight years with the far more celebrated Marcus Aurelius. As a boy he had been adopted into the imperial house by Antoninus Pius, in accordance with the careful succession that Hadrian had laid down. When Antoninus died in 161, Marcus Aurelius—though the senior heir—insisted that the two rule jointly, an unprecedented division of supreme power that worked smoothly thanks to Marcus's deference and Lucius's lack of ambition.

Handsome, genial, and devoted to pleasure, games, and luxury, Lucius made a striking contrast to his grave, philosophical colleague. To him fell the command of the great war against the Parthian Empire in the East. Though the hard fighting was done by abler generals, the campaign of 161 to 166 recovered Roman prestige and ended in victory.

The triumph carried a hidden cost: the armies returning from the East brought with them the terrible Antonine Plague, which swept the empire for years and killed untold numbers. Lucius, who had married Marcus's daughter Lucilla, died suddenly in 169, probably of the same pestilence, leaving Marcus to rule alone.

Through that marriage and the bonds of the imperial family, Lucius was brother-in-law to the young prince who would one day inherit the throne and bring the age of the good emperors to a violent close—Commodus.
