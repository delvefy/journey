Grigori Rasputin

Russian mystic and confidant of the imperial family (1869–1916)

Grigori Rasputin was a Siberian peasant turned wandering holy man whose extraordinary influence over the last Russian imperial family helped to discredit the monarchy in its final years.

Born to a poor family in Siberia, he took up the life of a religious pilgrim and gained a reputation as a healer and man of God. Arriving in the capital, he was brought to the court, where he seemed able to soothe the suffering of the haemophiliac heir, the Tsarevich Alexei, when the physicians could do nothing. This won him the unshakeable faith of the Empress Alexandra, who saw in him a holy intercessor and a voice of the common Russian people.

Through her devotion he came to meddle in appointments and affairs of state, above all while the Tsar was away commanding the army in the Great War. His coarse manners, his drinking and womanising, and his sway over the throne caused immense scandal, and the rumours that swirled around him stained the dignity of the crown beyond repair.

In December 1916 a band of nobles lured him to a palace and killed him—a murder that grew into legend for the difficulty with which he is said to have died. But his death came too late, and within months the dynasty he had haunted was swept away.

His whole strange power had rested on the trust of one woman, the Empress Alexandra Feodorovna.
