Catherine of Aragon

Queen of England, first wife of Henry VIII (1485–1536)

Catherine of Aragon was the dignified and steadfast queen whose refusal to surrender her marriage helped split England from Rome. The youngest daughter of the Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand of Aragon and Isabella of Castile, she was sent to England as a girl to marry Arthur, Prince of Wales. When Arthur died within months, she lived for years in uncertain widowhood before marrying his younger brother, who became Henry VIII.

For two decades she was a devoted and popular queen. Learned and pious, she even governed as regent during Henry's war in France, when an English army crushed the Scots at Flodden. But of her many children only one, the future Mary I, survived infancy, and Henry grew desperate for a male heir.

When the king resolved to set Catherine aside and marry Anne Boleyn, she fought him with unbending courage. She insisted her union with Henry was lawful, appealed over his head to the pope, and looked for protection to her powerful nephew, the Emperor Charles V, whose influence helped stall any annulment in Rome. Henry's answer was to break with the Roman Church altogether, declare the marriage void, and wed Anne in 1533.

Stripped of her title and barred from seeing her daughter, Catherine never wavered, signing herself queen to the end. She died, neglected, in 1536. The crisis of her marriage—"the King's Great Matter"—destroyed the career of the minister who labored and failed to obtain the king's divorce, the great Cardinal Wolsey.
