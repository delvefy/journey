Afonso V of Portugal

King of Portugal, "the African" (1432–1481)

Afonso V of Portugal, surnamed the African, ruled for more than forty years in the generation before his country's great age of discovery, a king of crusading zeal whose ambitions reached both into Africa and toward the throne of Castile.

He won his epithet by his wars in Morocco, where he led armies across the strait and captured the strongholds of Arzila and Tangier in 1471. At home he gave his blessing to the ocean enterprise of his uncle Henry the Navigator, and after Henry's death he let out the further exploration of the African coast to a Lisbon merchant, so that the southward voyages went on.

His great gamble was for Castile. When the Castilian king died leaving a disputed daughter, Joanna, Afonso married his young niece and claimed the crown in her right. This set him against the formidable pair who would unite Spain, Isabella I of Castile and her husband Ferdinand II of Aragon. At the Battle of Toro in 1476 his cause was checked, and though he sought help even at the court of France, he won nothing; by treaty in 1479 he gave up the claim.

Disheartened, he spoke of abdicating and retiring to a monastery, and he died in 1481. The crown passed to his son John II, the Perfect Prince, who would rule far more sternly than his father.

Afonso was descended from the founder of his dynasty, John I of Portugal, whose victory long before had secured the kingdom's independence.
