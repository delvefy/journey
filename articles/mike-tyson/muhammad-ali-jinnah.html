Muhammad Ali Jinnah

Founder of Pakistan (1876–1948)

Muhammad Ali Jinnah, born in Karachi in 1876, was the founder of Pakistan, honored there as Quaid-e-Azam, the "Great Leader." Trained as a barrister in London, he entered Indian politics through the Indian National Congress, where his early career was devoted to bridging the divide between Hindus and Muslims—so much so that he was hailed as "the ambassador of Hindu-Muslim unity" and worked with Tilak on the Lucknow Pact of 1916.

But Jinnah grew estranged from the Congress as Gandhi remade it into a movement of mass civil disobedience, and increasingly fearful of the place of Muslims in a Hindu-majority independent India. Taking the leadership of the All-India Muslim League, he came to champion the idea that India's Muslims formed a distinct nation entitled to a state of their own—the demand crystallized in the Lahore Resolution of 1940.

Through relentless and skillful negotiation with the British and with Congress leaders such as Gandhi and Nehru, and against a backdrop of mounting communal bloodshed, Jinnah secured his goal. In August 1947 British India was partitioned and the new state of Pakistan was born, with Jinnah as its first Governor-General.

The triumph came at a terrible price: Partition unleashed massacres and one of the greatest migrations in history, uprooting millions. Already gravely ill with tuberculosis, Jinnah died barely a year later, in 1948, having willed a nation into being.
