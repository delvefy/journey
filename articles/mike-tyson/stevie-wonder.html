Stevie Wonder

American musician and songwriter (born 1950)

Stevie Wonder is the blind American musical genius whose extraordinary run of songs and albums made him one of the most influential artists in the history of popular music.

Blind from infancy, he was signed to Motown as a child prodigy—“Little Stevie Wonder”—and grew into a multi-instrumentalist and songwriter of staggering range. In the 1970s he produced a string of landmark albums, from Innervisions to Songs in the Key of Life, pioneering the use of synthesizers and pouring out hits like “Superstition” and “Isn't She Lovely.” He would win twenty-five Grammys and stand among the labelmates and rivals of the great Motown era.

His reach extended across the whole of music: he scored an early hit covering Bob Dylan's “Blowin' in the Wind,” later joined Paul McCartney for the anthem “Ebony and Ivory,” and was admired by the King of Pop, Michael Jackson. He used his fame for justice, leading the long campaign—crowned by his song “Happy Birthday”—to make the birthday of Martin Luther King Jr. a national holiday.

He carried forward the soul tradition of his forebear Ray Charles into a new age. The Motown peer and friend with whom he shared that golden era—a fellow genius of the label who turned soul toward social conscience—was Marvin Gaye.
