James Brown

American musician, the “Godfather of Soul” (1933–2006)

James Brown was the “Godfather of Soul” and the inventor of funk, a force of nature whose rhythmic revolution reshaped the whole course of popular music.

Raised in deep poverty in Augusta, Georgia, he clawed his way up through gospel and rhythm and blues to become the “Hardest-Working Man in Show Business.” His electrifying live act—captured on the legendary Live at the Apollo and at the 1964 T.A.M.I. Show, where the Rolling Stones had the unenviable task of following him—was unmatched. In the mid-1960s, with records like “Papa's Got a Brand New Bag” and “Cold Sweat,” he stripped the music down to a hypnotic, percussive groove and invented funk.

He was a force in his community too, releasing “Say It Loud—I'm Black and I'm Proud” and helping keep the peace after the assassination of Martin Luther King Jr. His influence on the dancing of Michael Jackson, the showmanship of Mick Jagger, and the funk of Prince was profound, and as the most sampled artist in history he became the bedrock of hip-hop. He died on Christmas Day, 2006.

A fellow son of Georgia soul, his contemporary whose aching ballads and explosive stage presence made him one of the most beloved voices of the 1960s, was Otis Redding.
