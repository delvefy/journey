Blanche of Castile

Queen and regent of France (1188–1252)

Blanche of Castile, born in 1188, was a granddaughter of the celebrated Eleanor of Aquitaine and a daughter of Alfonso VIII of Castile. As a girl she was chosen—by her grandmother Eleanor herself—to marry the future Louis VIII of France, sealing a peace between the Capetian and Plantagenet houses. The match made her queen of France, but her true greatness emerged after her husband's early death in 1226.

Left with a twelve-year-old son, the future Louis IX, Blanche became regent of France, and she ruled with iron resolve. The great barons, expecting to dominate a woman and a child, rose in revolt; Blanche outmanoeuvred and defeated them, holding the kingdom together and crushing every threat to her son's crown. She defended royal authority, negotiated shrewdly, and gave Louis the secure throne on which his own reputation would be built.

Her influence over Louis IX was profound and lifelong. Deeply pious, she shaped his devout character, and when he departed on crusade in 1248 she once again took up the regency, governing France in his absence. She is said to have declared that she would rather see her beloved son dead than guilty of a mortal sin.

Blanche died in 1252 while Louis was still overseas, never to see him return. Through her children she shaped the destiny of Europe: her son Louis IX became the sainted ideal of medieval kingship, and her youngest son, Charles of Anjou, carved out a Mediterranean empire. Few medieval women wielded power so effectively or so long as this Castilian princess who twice held France in her hands.
