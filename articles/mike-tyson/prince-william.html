Prince William

Prince of Wales, heir to the British throne (born 1982)

Prince William is the heir apparent to the British throne, the elder son of King Charles III and the future of the House of Windsor.

Born in 1982 as the first child of Charles and Princess Diana, he grew up as the most closely watched boy in Britain, and was only fifteen when his mother's death plunged the nation into mourning and put him at the emotional center of the royal story. Educated at Eton and at the University of St Andrews—where he met his future wife, Catherine—he trained and served as a search-and-rescue helicopter pilot before taking on royal duties full time.

His 2011 wedding was watched around the globe, and with three children he came to represent a steadier, more modern monarchy. He has built his public role around conservation and the environment, launching a major prize to spur solutions to climate change, and around mental health. When his father became king in 2022, William inherited the title Prince of Wales and the position of heir.

Through childhood grief and royal duty his constant companion was his younger brother, with whom he was once inseparable but later painfully estranged after the younger man left royal life—Prince Harry.
