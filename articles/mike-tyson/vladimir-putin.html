Vladimir Putin

President of Russia (born 1952)

Vladimir Putin has dominated Russia and loomed over world affairs for a quarter of a century. Born in Leningrad, he served as an officer of the Soviet KGB, including a posting in East Germany, before entering post-Soviet politics. The ailing President Boris Yeltsin plucked him from relative obscurity and made him acting president at the close of 1999, and Putin has held the levers of power ever since—as president, then prime minister, then president again—reshaping the constitution to extend his rule.

At home he reasserted the authority of the central state, harnessed the country's vast oil and gas wealth, and steadily suppressed independent media and political opposition. Abroad he set out to restore Russia's standing as a great power and to resist the expansion of Western influence along its borders.

That ambition led to confrontation: he seized and annexed Crimea from Ukraine in 2014, and in 2022 launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, igniting the largest war in Europe since 1945, drawing sweeping Western sanctions, and dividing the world. To some he is the man who made Russia strong again; to others, a dangerous authoritarian.

As relations with the West froze, he drew closer to the East, forging a strategic partnership with the leader of China—Xi Jinping.
