Origen

Christian theologian of Alexandria (c. 185–253)

Origen was the most learned and original scholar of the early Christian Church, the man who first forged a systematic Christian theology and the science of biblical study. Born into a devout family in Alexandria, he saw his father martyred in a persecution and threw himself, still young, into a life of austere discipline and ceaseless study, taking charge of the great catechetical school of the city.

His learning was prodigious. He compiled the Hexapla, an immense parallel edition of the Old Testament setting Hebrew and Greek versions side by side—the foundation of textual criticism of scripture. In On First Principles he attempted the first comprehensive account of Christian doctrine, and in Against Celsus he answered the most formidable pagan critic of the faith. He developed the allegorical reading of the Bible and, drawing deeply on the philosophy of Plato, fused Greek thought with Christian revelation.

His boldness carried risks. Some of his speculations—on the pre-existence of souls and the ultimate salvation of all—would be condemned by the Church centuries after his death. In his own day he suffered imprisonment and torture during the persecution of Decius, and died of his injuries.

Origen's vast legacy shaped all who came after, both those who revered him and those who fought his errors. Among the later Fathers who studied him closely, translated his works, and quarreled bitterly over his memory was the great scholar of the Latin Church, Jerome.
