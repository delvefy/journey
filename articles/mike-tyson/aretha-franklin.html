Aretha Franklin

American singer, the “Queen of Soul” (1942–2018)

Aretha Franklin was the “Queen of Soul,” the towering American singer whose voice fused gospel fervor with rhythm and blues to become the sound of a generation.

Raised in Detroit as the daughter of a famous Baptist preacher and civil rights figure, she learned to sing in his church and never lost the gospel power at the root of her art. After a slow start she found her voice at Atlantic Records in the late 1960s, pouring out a string of immortal recordings—above all “Respect,” which she transformed into an anthem of dignity for the civil rights and women's movements—and went on to win eighteen Grammys and become the first woman inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

Her life was woven into the great causes of her time. She was close to the movement of Martin Luther King Jr., whose family knew hers and at whose funeral she sang, and decades later she lent her voice to the inauguration of Barack Obama. Her influence on the singers who followed was incalculable, reaching down to global stars like Beyoncé.

She died in 2018, mourned as American royalty. The blind genius who had first fused gospel and the blues into soul itself—her great forerunner, with whom she also sang—was Ray Charles.
