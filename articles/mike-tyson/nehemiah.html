Nehemiah

Governor of Judah and rebuilder of Jerusalem (5th century BC)

Nehemiah served as cupbearer to the Persian king Artaxerxes I, a trusted post at the heart of the court at Susa. When word reached him that Jerusalem still lay open and defenseless, its walls broken and its gates burned, he was stricken with grief and begged the king for leave to go and repair it. Artaxerxes not only consented but made him governor of Judah.

His rebuilding of the city walls is one of the most vivid episodes in the Hebrew scriptures. Against the mockery, threats, and plots of neighboring officials—Sanballat of Samaria foremost among them—Nehemiah drove the work forward in a mere fifty-two days, the builders laboring with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other, ready to fight at the sound of a trumpet.

As governor he proved a stern social reformer. He canceled the debts that were crushing the poor and freed Jews enslaved to their own countrymen, enforced the Sabbath against the merchants, and, like Ezra, set himself against intermarriage with foreigners. His own blunt memoir preserves his deeds and his prayers in the first person.

The fortified city he restored sheltered a Temple that had been raised again two generations before his time, in the first wave of return from exile. The prince of the royal house of David who led that earlier homecoming and rebuilt the house of God was Zerubbabel.
