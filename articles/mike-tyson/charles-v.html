Charles V

Holy Roman Emperor (1500–1558)

Charles V was the most powerful European ruler of the sixteenth century, master of an empire so vast it was said the sun never set on it. Born in Ghent in 1500, he inherited through a remarkable convergence of dynasties the Habsburg lands of Austria and the Low Countries, the crowns of Spain with its new American possessions, and finally, in 1519, election as Holy Roman Emperor. No European had ruled such dominions since Charlemagne.

His reign was a ceaseless struggle to defend and hold together these scattered realms. In Italy and beyond he fought the long rivalry with Francis I of France. To the east and south he faced the Ottoman tide under Suleiman the Magnificent, whose armies reached Vienna. And across his German lands he confronted the religious earthquake of the Reformation: a devout Catholic, Charles presided over the Diet of Worms in 1521, where he placed Martin Luther under the ban of the empire, and spent decades in vain trying to suppress Protestantism.

Family bound him to the era's other crowns—his aunt Catherine of Aragon was the first wife of Henry VIII, drawing Charles into England's marital crisis—while in the Americas his captains Cortés and Pizarro toppled the Aztec and Inca empires.

Worn out by these endless cares, Charles did the almost unheard-of: he abdicated, dividing his inheritance between his son Philip II and his brother Ferdinand, and retired to a monastery, where he died in 1558.
