Meryl Streep

American actress (b. 1949)

Meryl Streep is widely regarded as the finest screen actress of her generation and the most honoured performer in the history of the Academy Awards, nominated more often than anyone who ever acted in film.

Famous for a chameleon's gift—mastering any accent, vanishing into any role—she emerged in The Deer Hunter alongside Robert De Niro and won her first Oscar opposite Dustin Hoffman in Kramer vs. Kramer. A second came for the shattering Sophie's Choice. Her range seemed without limit: the romance of Out of Africa, the autumnal love of The Bridges of Madison County with Clint Eastwood, the comedy of The Devil Wears Prada, the music of Mamma Mia!

Decades into her career she won a third Academy Award for one of her most uncanny transformations—her portrayal of the British prime minister in the biographical film The Iron Lady, in which she became Margaret Thatcher to the life.

That performance bound her name, for a season, to one of the most formidable political figures of the twentieth century, Margaret Thatcher.
