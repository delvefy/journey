Purandara Dasa

Poet-saint, father of Carnatic music (1484–1564)

Purandara Dasa is honoured as the father of Carnatic music, the classical tradition of southern India. A poet-saint of the Haridasa movement that grew from the teaching of Madhva, he gave the music of the south both a vast devotional repertoire and the very framework by which it is still taught.

By tradition he was born Srinivasa Nayaka, a rich merchant of gems and money, miserly until a humbling lesson—often told as a test involving his pious wife—turned him from wealth to God. He gave away his fortune, took up the begging bowl of a wandering devotee, and became a disciple of the great Madhva teacher Vyasatirtha at the court of Vijayanagara.

As a Haridasa he poured out devotional songs in Kannada, the devaranamas, addressed to Vishnu under the name Vittala and sealed with his signature, "Purandara Vittala." Tradition credits him with hundreds of thousands of compositions, of which a precious fraction survive, ranging from simple moral verses to hymns of great depth.

His most enduring legacy was pedagogical. Purandara Dasa is said to have arranged the graded exercises—the scales and patterns—through which beginners still enter Carnatic music, and to have fixed the raga in which that instruction begins. He thus made the tradition teachable and gave it lasting form.

For this he is called Karnataka Sangeeta Pitamaha, the grandsire of Carnatic music. The later masters of the art, above all Tyagaraja, looked back to him as their fountainhead, and his songs are sung in temples and concert halls across India to this day.
