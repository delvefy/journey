Robert Redford

American actor and director (b. 1936)

Robert Redford was one of the defining leading men of 1970s American cinema and, in his later career, a director and the founding patron of its independent film movement.

Golden-haired and effortlessly charismatic, he became a star in partnership with Paul Newman, with whom he made Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid and The Sting—a screen duo so beloved that the two became lifelong friends. He was the romantic lead of The Way We Were, the baseball hero of The Natural, and the investigative reporter Bob Woodward in All the President's Men.

He then turned to directing and won the Academy Award for Best Director with his very first film, Ordinary People. Beyond his own work, he changed the landscape of American film by founding the Sundance Institute and its festival, which became the great showcase and nursery of independent cinema. He was also a dedicated conservationist.

He had partnered Jane Fonda in film after film, but the friendship and screen pairing that defined him was with Paul Newman.
