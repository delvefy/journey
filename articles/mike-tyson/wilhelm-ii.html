Wilhelm II

Last German Emperor (1859–1941)

Wilhelm II was the last German Emperor and King of Prussia, whose reckless ambition helped drag Europe into the First World War. Born in 1859, a grandson of Queen Victoria and cousin to both King George V of Britain and Tsar Nicholas II of Russia, he was marked from birth by a withered arm and, many believed, by a corresponding restlessness and need to assert himself.

He came to the throne in 1888, and two years later dismissed the empire's architect, the formidable chancellor Otto von Bismarck—an act a famous cartoon depicted as "dropping the pilot." Taking the helm himself, Wilhelm pursued a blustering, expansionist foreign policy and an enormous naval buildup that frightened Britain and helped harden Europe into rival armed camps.

In the crisis of July 1914 he backed Austria-Hungary against Serbia, and the alliance system did the rest, plunging the continent into war. Yet as the conflict ground on, the bombastic Kaiser was steadily pushed aside; real power passed to his generals, Hindenburg and Ludendorff.

When Germany collapsed in defeat and revolution in November 1918, Wilhelm was forced to abdicate and fled into exile in the Netherlands. He never returned, living out more than two decades at his estate at Doorn and dying there in 1941. His reign is widely remembered as a study in how vanity and miscalculation at the top can help bring on catastrophe.
