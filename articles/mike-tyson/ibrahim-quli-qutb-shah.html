Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah

Sultan of Golconda (r. 1550–1580)

Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah was the fourth sultan of Golconda and the father of the founder of Hyderabad. Under him the kingdom grew strong and prosperous, and he is remembered for a rare openness between the Muslim and Hindu worlds of the Deccan.

His early life shaped that openness. As a young prince, driven from Golconda by the murderous quarrels of his dynasty, he found refuge for several years at the great Hindu empire of Vijayanagara, then governed by its powerful regent Rama Raya. There he learned the Telugu language and grew steeped in the culture of the south—so much so that Telugu poets later hailed him by an honoured name as a patron of their tongue.

Returning home, he secured the throne and ruled for thirty years. He strengthened Golconda's fortifications, dug the great reservoir of Hussain Sagar that still lies at the centre of modern Hyderabad, and laid out gardens and canals.

Yet gratitude did not outweigh the politics of survival. When the Muslim sultanates of the Deccan formed their confederacy against Vijayanagara, Ibrahim Quli joined them, and at the decisive Battle of Talikota in 1565 the allies shattered the empire that had once sheltered him. Rama Raya was taken and beheaded, and the magnificent capital was given over to ruin.

Ibrahim Quli died in 1580 and was succeeded by his son Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, who would found Hyderabad. His own career was bound up above all with the man who had been first his host and then his victim, the regent Rama Raya.
