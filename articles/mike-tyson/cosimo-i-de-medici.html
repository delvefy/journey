Cosimo I de' Medici

Duke of Florence, first Grand Duke of Tuscany (1519–1574)

Cosimo I de' Medici rebuilt the power of the Medici and forged the scattered lands of Tuscany into a strong, centralized state. Sprung from a junior branch of the family—his father was the famous soldier Giovanni delle Bande Nere—he was thrust onto the ducal throne of Florence as a youth of seventeen in 1537, after the murder of the dissolute Duke Alessandro. Many expected him to be a puppet; instead he proved one of the ablest and most ruthless princes of his age.

He crushed his enemies, mastered the old republican institutions, conquered the rival city of Siena, and was at last crowned the first Grand Duke of Tuscany. He reorganized the state's finances, navy, and administration with cold efficiency.

Cosimo was also a magnificent patron in the Medici tradition. He employed Giorgio Vasari, who raised the great government offices now famous as the Uffizi gallery and remodeled the ducal palaces; he commissioned Cellini's bronze Perseus and Bronzino's icy court portraits; and he founded an academy of the arts of design. So great was his reverence for Michelangelo that he sought to lure the aged master home and gave him a splendid funeral in Florence.

He died in 1574, his dynasty's rule over Tuscany secured for generations. The most illustrious of the Medici forebears whose golden age Cosimo sought to revive was Lorenzo de' Medici, called the Magnificent.
