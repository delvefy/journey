George Gershwin

American composer (1898–1937)

George Gershwin was the composer who fused the jazz and popular song of America with the forms of the European concert hall, creating a sound that was unmistakably his own and unmistakably American.

The son of Russian-Jewish immigrants in New York, he began as a song-plugger on Tin Pan Alley and, with his brother Ira as lyricist, poured out a stream of beloved songs and Broadway shows. But his ambition reached higher: with Rhapsody in Blue he wedded the blues to the symphony orchestra, and he went on to write the Concerto in F, the witty tone poem An American in Paris, and his great folk opera Porgy and Bess, with its immortal “Summertime.”

Hungry to deepen his craft, he sought lessons from the European masters Ravel and others, who assured him he had nothing to learn from them that would not spoil his gift. He also wrote for Hollywood, composing songs for the dancer Fred Astaire.

He died cruelly young, of a brain tumour, at only thirty-eight, his powers still growing. One of the great interpreters of his songs on screen was Fred Astaire.
