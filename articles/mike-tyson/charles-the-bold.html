Charles the Bold

Last Valois Duke of Burgundy (1433–1477)

Charles the Bold, born in 1433, was the last of the great Valois dukes of Burgundy, heir to his father Philip the Good's vast and glittering domain that stretched from the Low Countries to the borders of Switzerland. Proud, energetic and consumed by ambition, Charles dreamed of welding his scattered territories into a single kingdom—a revived realm lying between France and the Holy Roman Empire.

That dream set him at deadly odds with the French king Louis XI, his cousin and exact opposite in temperament. Where Louis schemed, Charles charged. He led the feudal princes' League of the Public Weal against the king and forced humiliating terms on him, and he cemented his standing by marrying Margaret of York, sister of the English king Edward IV. For a decade Burgundy seemed poised to become a major European power.

But Charles overreached. His attempt to expand eastward brought him into collision with the Swiss Confederation and the Duke of Lorraine. The disciplined Swiss pikemen shattered his magnificent army at Grandson and again at Morat in 1476. The following winter, besieging Nancy, Charles was defeated a final time and killed; his frozen body was found in a ditch days later.

His death without a male heir was a catastrophe for Burgundy and a triumph for his enemies. Louis XI seized the duchy itself for France, while the rest of the inheritance passed to Charles's only child, Mary of Burgundy. Her marriage to Maximilian of Habsburg carried the wealthy Low Countries into the house of Austria, shaping the rivalries of Europe for centuries. The most splendid court of the age died with its reckless duke.
