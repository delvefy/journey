Stanisław August Poniatowski

Last King of Poland (1732–1798)

Stanisław August Poniatowski was the last king of Poland and Lithuania, a cultivated reformer who presided helplessly over the destruction of his realm. As a young diplomat in St. Petersburg he became the lover of the future empress Catherine the Great, and it was she who, years later, engineered his election to the Polish throne in 1764, intending him as a pliant client of Russia.

He proved more than a puppet in matters of culture. An enlightened patron, he founded one of Europe's first state systems of education, gathered artists and writers, and championed the landmark Constitution of the Third of May 1791, a pioneering charter of reform. But politically his kingdom was doomed, squeezed among three predatory neighbours.

The reign was a long agony of dismemberment. Resistance such as the Bar Confederation only invited the First Partition of 1772, in which Catherine's Russia, the Prussia of Frederick the Great, and the Austria of Maria Theresa each tore away a piece of Poland. Further partitions followed, and the desperate national uprising led by Tadeusz Kościuszko in 1794 was crushed.

After the final partition erased Poland from the map in 1795, Stanisław August abdicated and died three years later in St. Petersburg, a king without a kingdom. The most celebrated of his family was his nephew, the soldier Józef Poniatowski.
