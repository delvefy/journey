Elizabeth I

Queen of England (1533–1603)

Elizabeth I, born in 1533, was Queen of England for forty-five years and the last monarch of the Tudor dynasty, presiding over an age of cultural splendor and national self-confidence. The daughter of King Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn, she was not yet three when her mother was executed and she herself declared illegitimate. She survived a perilous youth through the reigns of her half-brother Edward VI and her Catholic half-sister Mary I, who imprisoned her for a time in the Tower of London.

Coming to the throne in 1558, Elizabeth steered a middle course in religion, establishing a moderate Protestant Church of England that calmed—though never ended—the era's sectarian strife. She never married, using the prospect of her hand as a diplomatic tool and cultivating an image as the "Virgin Queen" wedded to her realm.

Her reign saw the flowering of English drama and poetry in the work of Shakespeare and Marlowe, and the daring voyages of seamen such as Francis Drake and Walter Raleigh. Catholic plots centered on her cousin Mary, Queen of Scots, led Elizabeth reluctantly to order Mary's execution in 1587. The following year she faced the great Spanish Armada sent by Philip II; its defeat became the defining triumph of her reign, sealed by her rallying speech to the troops at Tilbury.

Shrewd, learned, and politically masterful, Elizabeth gave her name to an entire era. She died in 1603 and, having no child, was succeeded by James VI of Scotland, uniting the two crowns.
