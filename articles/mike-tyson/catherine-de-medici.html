Catherine de' Medici

Queen and queen mother of France (1519–1589)

Catherine de' Medici was born in Florence in 1519, a daughter of the ruling Medici house and great-granddaughter of Lorenzo de' Medici. Orphaned within weeks of her birth, she was raised under the protection of her Medici kinsmen, and in 1533 a marriage was arranged for her, with the blessing of King Francis I, to his son Henry, the future Henry II of France.

For years she lived in the shadow of her husband's mistress and bore him ten children before Henry's death in a jousting accident in 1559. Three of her sons reigned in turn—Francis II, briefly married to Mary, Queen of Scots; Charles IX; and Henry III—and through them Catherine became the dominant figure in French politics for thirty years.

As queen mother and regent she tried to steer a middle course through the savage Wars of Religion, manoeuvring between Catholic and Huguenot factions. A great patron of art, architecture and court spectacle, she built the Tuileries and kept seers and scholars about her, among them the famous Nostradamus.

Her reputation, however, is forever bound to the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre of 1572, when the Paris wedding of her daughter to the Protestant Henry of Navarre—later Henry IV—turned into a slaughter of Huguenots across France. Her exact share of the blame is still debated, but it cast a long shadow over her name. She died in 1589, her dynasty collapsing around her.
