Jean-Michel Basquiat

American painter (1960–1988)

Jean-Michel Basquiat blazed through the New York art world of the 1980s, rising from the streets to international fame and dying at twenty-seven, leaving behind one of the most coveted bodies of work in modern art.

Born in Brooklyn to Haitian and Puerto Rican parents, he first drew notice spraying cryptic, poetic graffiti around downtown Manhattan under the tag SAMO. Turning to canvas, he erupted into the galleries as a leader of Neo-Expressionism, his raw and electric paintings crowded with words, skulls, crowns, and anatomy, charged with the history of Black America, the rhythms of jazz, and the energy of the street.

He drew on a wide inheritance, from the forms of Pablo Picasso to the improvisation of the great jazz players, and made of them something wholly his own. Fame came suddenly and overwhelmingly; he was taken up by dealers and collectors, and mentored by Andy Warhol, with whom he painted in collaboration.

But the pressures of celebrity and a deepening addiction overtook him, and in 1988 he died of a heroin overdose. In the decades since, his canvases have sold for sums beyond those of almost any American painter.

In his early days in downtown New York, before either was famous, he was for a time the boyfriend of a young singer named Madonna.
