Frederick Barbarossa

Holy Roman Emperor (c. 1122–1190)

Frederick I, known from his red beard as Barbarossa, was the emperor who tried to restore the Roman grandeur of his office and very nearly succeeded. A Hohenstaufen with Welf blood through his mother, he was chosen in 1152 partly to heal the feud between Germany's two great houses, and he set out to make the empire once more the supreme power of Christendom.

His ambition collided with the proud, rich cities of northern Italy. Six times he crossed the Alps to assert his rights, razing Milan in his fury, but the Lombard towns banded together in a league and, with the backing of Pope Alexander III, defied him. At Legnano in 1176 their citizen militia broke his knights in the field, a humiliation that forced him to make peace with the pope at Venice and, in time, to grant the cities their self-government.

In Germany he ruled more firmly, at last bringing down his mighty cousin Henry the Lion and stripping him of his duchies. He gave the empire a new dignity, reviving Roman law and the very idea of a sacred imperial majesty.

His end became legend. When Saladin captured Jerusalem, the aging emperor took the cross and led the greatest army of the Third Crusade overland toward the Holy Land—only to drown in 1190 while crossing a river in Anatolia, his host scattering in grief. Germans long told that he merely slept beneath a mountain, to wake in his country's hour of need. The crusading king of England who pressed on to fight Saladin where Barbarossa never arrived was Richard the Lionheart.
