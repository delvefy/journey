Karl Lagerfeld

German fashion designer (1933–2019)

Karl Lagerfeld was one of the most prolific and influential designers in the history of fashion, a creative force who reigned over the industry for half a century and made of himself a living emblem of it.

A German who made his name in Paris—winning a young designers' prize in his youth alongside the future Yves Saint Laurent—he worked for a string of houses and forged a decades-long partnership with Fendi. His greatest feat came in 1983, when he took the reins of the fading house of Chanel and, reinterpreting its founder's classic codes season after season, restored it to the summit of luxury.

He was as famous for his image as for his work: the powdered white ponytail, the dark glasses, the starched collars and fingerless gloves, and his pampered cat. Caustic, tireless, and endlessly inventive, he was also a skilled photographer who shot many of his own campaigns.

His life's central work was the stewardship of a legend not his own—the house and the legacy of Coco Chanel.
