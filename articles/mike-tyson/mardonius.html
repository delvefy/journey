Mardonius

Persian general (died 479 BC)

Mardonius was the foremost Persian commander of the great invasion of Greece and, in the pages of Herodotus, its most insistent champion. Son of one of the noblemen who had helped raise Darius the Great to the throne, he married a daughter of Darius and was thus son-in-law to one king and cousin to the next. As a young general he had reconquered Thrace and Macedon for Darius, though on that campaign his fleet was shattered in a storm rounding the stormy headland of Mount Athos.

At the court of Xerxes I it was Mardonius above all who urged the king to march against Greece, picturing easy conquest and rich rewards. When the venture met disaster at sea off Salamis and Xerxes resolved to return home, Mardonius begged to be left behind with a chosen army to finish the task.

Through the following winter he held Thessaly, tried in vain to detach Athens from the Greek alliance with generous terms, and then occupied and burned the abandoned city a second time. In the summer of 479 BC he gave battle on the plain of Plataea, where the combined Greeks under the Spartan Pausanias broke his host and cut him down. His death ended the Persian bid to conquer Greece.

His ambitions and his fall are among the great set-pieces of Herodotus. After Salamis it had been a shrewd ally of Xerxes, the warrior-queen Artemisia of Caria, who counselled the king to sail home and leave the war to Mardonius.
