Robert Dudley, 1st Earl of Leicester

English courtier, favourite of Elizabeth I (1532–1588)

Robert Dudley was born around 1532, a son of the powerful Duke of Northumberland. When his father's scheme to set Lady Jane Grey—married to Robert's brother Guildford—upon the throne collapsed in 1553, the Dudleys were ruined, and Robert was imprisoned in the Tower, where he shared captivity with the young Princess Elizabeth. The friendship formed there would shape his whole life.

On Elizabeth's accession in 1558 he became her Master of the Horse and, soon, the great love of her reign. Many expected the queen to marry him, and those hopes flared again and faded after the suspicious death of his first wife, Amy Robsart, in 1560. Created Earl of Leicester, he was so trusted that Elizabeth once offered him as a husband to Mary, Queen of Scots.

A staunch champion of the Protestant cause and a generous patron of players and scholars, Leicester led the English expedition sent in 1585 to help the Dutch against Spain, though the campaign brought him little glory.

His secret marriage to Lettice Knollys infuriated the queen and made him stepfather to her future favourite, the Earl of Essex. In 1588 he commanded the army mustered at Tilbury against the Spanish Armada, where Elizabeth made her famous speech—but within weeks he was dead. The queen mourned him as the dearest of her companions, keeping his last letter until she died.
