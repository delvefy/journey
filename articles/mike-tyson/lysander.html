Lysander

Spartan admiral and statesman (died 395 BC)

Lysander was the Spartan commander who finally won the long Peloponnesian War and brought imperial Athens to its knees. Said to be of humble birth, he rose by talent, daring, and a calculating ruthlessness. As admiral of the Spartan fleet he cultivated the friendship of the Persian prince Cyrus the Younger, whose gold let him build and pay a navy strong enough to challenge the Athenians at sea.

In 405 BC he caught the Athenian fleet off its guard at Aegospotami on the Hellespont and destroyed it almost without a fight, severing the lifeline of grain on which Athens depended. The starving city surrendered the next year, and Lysander watched as its great Long Walls were pulled down to the sound of flutes—the end of the Athenian empire. It was Lysander, too, who is held to have arranged the murder in exile of the city's brilliant renegade, Alcibiades.

At the peak of his power he set up narrow pro-Spartan juntas in the conquered cities, including the brutal Thirty Tyrants at Athens, and he was honoured as no living Greek had been before. His ambition, which some thought reached toward a crown, alarmed his own countrymen. He helped place Agesilaus II on the Spartan throne, only to find the new king determined to curb him.

Lysander fell in battle at Haliartus in 395 BC, in the war that turned a resentful Greece against triumphant Sparta. His life was later told by Plutarch.
