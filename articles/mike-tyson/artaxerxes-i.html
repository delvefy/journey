Artaxerxes I

Achaemenid King of Persia (reigned 465–424 BC)

Artaxerxes I, called the Long-Handed, came to the throne of the vast Persian empire through blood and intrigue. His father, Xerxes I, the king who had invaded Greece, was murdered in a palace plot, and the young Artaxerxes seized power only after destroying the courtier who had engineered the killing. From the splendor of Susa and Persepolis he then ruled the lands his grandfather Darius the Great had welded together.

His long reign was spent holding that inheritance steady. He crushed a dangerous revolt in Egypt that the Athenians had backed, and at last made peace with Athens itself, drawing a quiet end to the long wars between Greek and Persian. To his court came one of the most famous of his former enemies: Themistocles, the architect of the Greek victory at Salamis, now an exile, whom Artaxerxes received with honor and rewarded with the revenues of Asian cities.

To another people his memory is kinder still. In the Hebrew scriptures it is Artaxerxes who gives the exiled Jews leave to restore their religion and their city: he commissions the scribe Ezra to carry the Law to Jerusalem and, later, sends his cupbearer Nehemiah to rebuild its broken walls.

Thus the same king who closed the Greek wars also reopened the gates of Jerusalem. The scribe and priest whom Artaxerxes empowered to return to Judah, re-establish the Law of Moses, and refound the religious life of his people was Ezra.
