Trajan

Roman emperor (53–117)

Trajan was one of the greatest of the Roman emperors, remembered by the Senate's own acclamation as Optimus Princeps—"the best ruler." Born near Seville in Spain, he was the first emperor to come from the provinces, a sign of how Roman the wider empire had become. A career soldier of proven ability, he was adopted as heir by the aging emperor Nerva and succeeded him in the year 98.

Under Trajan the empire reached its greatest extent. In two hard campaigns he conquered Dacia, the gold-rich land north of the Danube, a triumph carved in the spiraling reliefs of the towering column that still bears his name in Rome. He annexed Arabia and, in his final years, drove east against Parthia, carrying Roman arms as far as the Persian Gulf.

At home he was an energetic builder and benefactor, raising a magnificent forum and market complex, extending roads and harbors, and funding a welfare scheme to support the children of Italy. His celebrated correspondence with Pliny the Younger, his governor in Bithynia, preserves the everyday business of ruling an empire—including a famous exchange on how to deal with the early Christians.

Affable, dutiful, and respectful of the Senate, Trajan set a standard against which later emperors were measured. He died in 117 on campaign in the East, and the throne passed to the kinsman and ward he is said to have adopted on his deathbed—Hadrian.
