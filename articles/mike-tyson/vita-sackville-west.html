Vita Sackville-West

English writer and garden designer (1892–1962)

Vita Sackville-West was an English poet, novelist, and garden-maker, as celebrated in her lifetime for her writing as she is remembered now for her loves and her gardens.

Born into the old aristocracy at the great house of Knole in Kent, she became a popular and prolific author. Her long pastoral poem The Land won a major prize, and her novels The Edwardians and All Passion Spent were widely read; she also wrote biographies, among them a life of the Restoration writer Aphra Behn.

Her private life was as unconventional as it was open. She married the diplomat and author Harold Nicolson, and though both took lovers of their own sex, their marriage endured in deep companionship. Her passionate friendship with Virginia Woolf was the inspiration for Woolf's Orlando, the fantastical tale of a hero who lives across centuries and changes from man to woman—called by Vita's son the longest love letter in literature.

With her husband she made, at Sissinghurst Castle, one of the most famous gardens in England, its celebrated White Garden a place of pilgrimage for gardeners ever since.

She died in 1962. The steadiest companion of her long life had been her husband, Harold Nicolson.
