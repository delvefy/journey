Maximilian I

Holy Roman Emperor (1459–1519)

Maximilian I was the Holy Roman Emperor who, more by marriage than by war, raised the House of Habsburg to mastery over Europe. The son of Emperor Frederick III, he won in 1477 the hand of Mary of Burgundy, heiress to the dazzling and wealthy Burgundian lands, and spent years defending that inheritance against the kings of France. Chivalrous, restless, and endlessly inventive, he was remembered as "the Last Knight."

As emperor he reformed the ramshackle institutions of the Empire, establishing new courts and administrative circles, though his ambitions always outran his treasury. A tireless patron and self-publicist, he enlisted the new art of printing and the genius of artists such as Albrecht Dürer to glorify his name with woodcut triumphs and a fantastical tomb.

His true masterpiece, however, was dynastic strategy. Through a web of carefully arranged marriages he gathered crowns for his descendants, inspiring the famous boast: "Let others wage war; you, happy Austria, marry." He wed his son Philip the Handsome to Joanna, heiress of Spain, a match whose consequences were staggering.

From that union came his grandson Charles V, who would inherit Austria, the Netherlands, Spain, and the Spanish realms of the New World—an empire on which the sun never set. Maximilian died in 1519, on the eve of his grandson's accession, having laid the foundations of Habsburg supremacy. The Spanish queen whose daughter he had secured for his own son, and whose throne fed this vast inheritance, was Isabella I of Castile.
