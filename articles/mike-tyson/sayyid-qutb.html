Sayyid Qutb

Egyptian Islamist thinker (1906–1966)

Sayyid Qutb was the Egyptian writer whose radical reinterpretation of Islam became the intellectual foundation of modern jihadist movements.

Born in a village of Upper Egypt and educated in Cairo, he began as a literary critic and education official with secular leanings. A long stay in the United States around 1950 repelled him—he recoiled from what he saw as its materialism, racism and moral looseness—and on his return he committed himself fully to the Muslim Brotherhood, becoming its foremost thinker. He called Muslims back to the pure faith and society first established by the Prophet Muhammad, condemning the modern world, even nominally Muslim states, as a new age of pagan ignorance.

When the Brotherhood broke with Egypt's new rulers, Qutb was thrown into prison and brutally treated. There he wrote his most incendiary works, the vast Quran commentary In the Shade of the Qur'an and the slim, explosive manifesto Milestones, which summoned a committed vanguard to wage struggle against the godless order and restore the sovereignty of God on earth.

Released and then re-arrested, he was tried and hanged in 1966, becoming a martyr in the eyes of his followers. His writings, carried abroad by his brother, shaped a generation of militants and lay behind the ideology of Osama bin Laden. The Egyptian president whose government imprisoned and finally executed him—the towering champion of Arab nationalism—was Gamal Abdel Nasser.
