Maria Theresa

Habsburg ruler of Austria (1717–1780)

Maria Theresa was the only woman to rule the Habsburg dominions in her own right and one of the most capable sovereigns of her age. The daughter of Emperor Charles VI, she inherited Austria, Hungary, and Bohemia in 1740, her succession guaranteed by a special decree—which her rivals promptly ignored. At twenty-three she found herself fighting for her very throne as Frederick the Great seized her rich province of Silesia and a coalition moved to dismember her lands.

She proved equal to the crisis. Rallying her peoples—most famously the Hungarian nobles, before whom she appeared with her infant son—she preserved nearly all her inheritance through the long War of the Austrian Succession, though Silesia was lost for good. Though her husband bore the title of Holy Roman Emperor, it was Maria Theresa who ruled.

Pious, strong-willed, and tirelessly industrious, she reformed the army, finances, administration, and schools, dragging the sprawling monarchy toward the modern state. She was also the mother of sixteen children, whom she deployed across the courts of Europe in a vast diplomacy of marriage—wedding her youngest daughter, Marie Antoinette, to the heir of France. As a child even the prodigy Mozart performed before her at her palace of Schönbrunn.

She died in 1780, mourned as the matriarch of her dynasty. The son who had ruled beside her as co-regent and now succeeded her, burning to remake the empire by Enlightenment reason, was Joseph II.
