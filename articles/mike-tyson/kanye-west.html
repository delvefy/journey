Kanye West

American rapper, producer, and designer (b. 1977)

Kanye West is one of the most influential and most controversial figures in twenty-first-century music, an artist whose restless invention reshaped hip-hop even as his conduct made him a lightning rod.

He first made his name as a producer at Roc-A-Fella Records, crafting beats for Jay-Z and others, before stepping forward as a rapper with The College Dropout. Across a string of bold and varied albums he kept remaking his sound—lush and orchestral, then raw and electronic, then maximal and operatic—winning a great cache of awards and an army of imitators. He built his Yeezy clothing and sneaker line into a fashion empire.

His fame was shadowed by spectacle and scandal: the snatched microphone at an awards show, public struggles with his mental health, a startling embrace of Donald Trump, a presidential campaign of his own, and finally a series of antisemitic outbursts that cost him his partnerships and much of his standing.

For years his marriage to Kim Kardashian made the couple inescapable in popular culture. But his career had been launched, and his craft first shaped, in the studio of his mentor, Jay-Z.
