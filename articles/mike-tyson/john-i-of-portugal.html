John I of Portugal

King of Portugal, founder of the House of Aviz (1357–1433)

John I of Portugal, called of Good Memory, founded the royal House of Aviz and secured his country's independence at a moment when it seemed about to be swallowed by Castile.

An illegitimate son of King Pedro I, he was Grand Master of the military Order of Aviz when, on the death of his half-brother, the kingdom fell into crisis. The heiress was bound to the Castilian crown, and many feared absorption into Castile. John put himself at the head of the resistance and in 1385 was acclaimed king by the Cortes. That same year, with his brilliant captain Nuno Álvares Pereira, he met the invading Castilian army at Aljubarrota and won a crushing victory that fixed Portugal's separate destiny for centuries.

He sealed his triumph with a lasting English alliance. By the Treaty of Windsor of 1386—the oldest alliance still in force in the world—he bound Portugal to England, and he married Philippa of Lancaster, the daughter of John of Gaunt. Their many gifted children became known as the Illustrious Generation, and among them was the prince who launched the age of discovery, Henry the Navigator.

In 1415 John crowned his reign by seizing Ceuta on the African shore, the first Portuguese foothold overseas, and he raised the great monastery of Batalha in thanks for Aljubarrota.

He was the son of Pedro I, whose tragic devotion to Inês de Castro had already passed into the legend of Portugal.
