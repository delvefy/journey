George Washington

First President of the United States (1732–1799)

George Washington, born in Virginia in 1732, was the commander who won American independence and the first President of the United States, revered as the "Father of His Country." A planter and surveyor who first saw war on the frontier in the French and Indian War, he was chosen in 1775 to lead the ragged Continental Army against the might of Britain.

His genius lay less in brilliant battlefield maneuvers than in endurance, judgment, and the ability to hold an army—and a cause—together through years of hardship, from the desperate crossing of the Delaware to the freezing winter at Valley Forge. With crucial French aid, his victory at Yorktown in 1781 secured the independence the colonies had declared from George III.

At war's end Washington astonished the world by resigning his commission and returning to his farm, surrendering power he might have kept. Called back to chair the Constitutional Convention of 1787, he was then unanimously elected the nation's first president. In office he set enduring precedents, and by voluntarily stepping down after two terms he established the peaceful transfer of power as a republican principle. His Farewell Address warned against party faction and foreign entanglement.

A slaveholder who freed his slaves in his will, Washington embodied both the ideals and the contradictions of the republic he founded. He died in 1799, mourned as the indispensable man of the American Revolution.
