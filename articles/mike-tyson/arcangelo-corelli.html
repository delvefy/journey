Arcangelo Corelli

Italian Baroque composer and violinist (1653–1713)

Arcangelo Corelli was the master who, more than anyone, established the violin as the king of instruments and laid the foundations of the Baroque concerto. Born near Bologna and settled for most of his life in Rome, he composed comparatively little—a handful of collections of trio sonatas, solo violin sonatas, and his famous Concerti Grossi—yet every one became a model studied and imitated across Europe.

His clear, balanced, deeply satisfying style defined good taste in instrumental music for a generation, and his disciplined approach to violin playing shaped the technique of all who followed. As a performer and teacher in Rome he was idolized, leading magnificent concerts under the patronage of the city's grandest figures.

Chief among his patrons were Cardinal Ottoboni and the celebrated Queen Christina of Sweden, who held court in Rome after her abdication; to her Corelli dedicated his first published works. His influence radiated outward to the whole next generation—to Vivaldi in Venice, to the young Handel, who met him in Rome, and to Bach in Germany.

Honored and prosperous, Corelli died in Rome in 1713 and was buried in the Pantheon among the great. The remarkable royal patron who had drawn him into her brilliant Roman circle—a queen who had stunned Europe by giving up her throne—was Christina of Sweden.
