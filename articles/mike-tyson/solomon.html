Solomon

King of Israel (traditionally c. 970–931 BC)

Solomon, son of David and Bathsheba, was the third king of the united kingdom of Israel and, in tradition, the wisest of all rulers. The Hebrew Bible relates that when God offered him any gift, the young king asked not for riches or long life but for wisdom to govern his people justly—and was granted wisdom, wealth, and honor besides. The famous tale of his judgment, in which he proposed to divide a disputed infant in two and so exposed the true mother, became the emblem of his discernment.

His reign was remembered as a golden age of peace and prosperity. With cedar and craftsmen supplied by Hiram of Tyre, Solomon built the great Temple in Jerusalem to house the Ark of the Covenant, along with a magnificent royal palace, and he enriched his kingdom through far-flung trade. The Queen of Sheba, hearing of his fame, journeyed to test him with hard questions. Tradition credited him with the books of Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of Songs.

Yet splendor bred trouble. His many foreign wives drew him toward their gods, and his heavy taxes and forced labor bred resentment. After his death the kingdom split in two under his son Rehoboam.

Honored across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam—where he is the prophet Sulayman—Solomon became the very measure of wise and splendid kingship, a model that later sovereigns, including the Ottoman Suleiman the Magnificent, deliberately claimed for themselves.
