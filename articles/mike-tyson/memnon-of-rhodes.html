Memnon of Rhodes

Greek mercenary commander in Persian service (c. 380–333 BC)

Memnon of Rhodes was a Greek soldier of fortune who became the most dangerous defender of the Persian empire in its final hour. With his brother Mentor he served the Great King, and he bound himself to the Persian nobility by marrying Barsine, daughter of the powerful satrap Artabazus. When Alexander crossed into Asia in 334 BC, it was Memnon, more than any Persian, who grasped how to fight him.

He counseled a hard but sound strategy: lay waste the country before the invader, deny him supplies, and carry the war by fleet into Greece and Macedon, where revolt might trap Alexander far from home. But the proud Persian satraps scorned to burn their own lands and overruled him, choosing instead to meet Alexander at the Granicus—where they were beaten.

Even so, Memnon fought on with skill, conducting a stubborn defense of the great city of Halicarnassus and then taking command of the Persian fleet. Sweeping the Aegean, he seized islands and seemed about to set the Greek world ablaze behind Alexander's back—the one move that might have changed the war.

Then, in 333 BC, illness struck him down at the siege of Mytilene, and with him died Persia's best hope. After his death his widow, a noblewoman of great beauty and high Persian blood, passed into the keeping of the conqueror and bore him a son. That widow of Memnon was Barsine.
