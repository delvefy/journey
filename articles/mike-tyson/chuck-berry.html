Chuck Berry

American rock-and-roll pioneer (1926–2017)

Chuck Berry was, more than any other single figure, the architect of rock and roll—the man who fused rhythm and blues with country and a teenager's wit into a brand-new music.

Born in St. Louis, he was steered to the famous Chess Records by the bluesman Muddy Waters, and his 1955 hit “Maybellene” launched a string of songs that defined the form: “Roll Over Beethoven,” “Sweet Little Sixteen,” and the anthem “Johnny B. Goode.” He pioneered the guitar style, the showmanship—his famous “duckwalk”—and above all the storytelling lyrics of cars, school and young love that gave rock its subject matter.

His influence on the generation that followed was total. The Beatles' John Lennon, who once quipped that another name for rock and roll might simply be “Chuck Berry,” performed alongside him, and the Rolling Stones' Keith Richards spent a lifetime devoted to his music. A mercurial, difficult man whose career was interrupted by spells in prison, he saw his “Johnny B. Goode” launched into space on the Voyager Golden Record, and he played on almost to his death in 2017.

The other towering founder of rock and roll—the white Southern singer who brought the new music to a mass audience and became its King—was Elvis Presley.
