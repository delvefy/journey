Horatio Nelson

British admiral (1758–1805)

Horatio Nelson was the greatest naval hero in British history, the admiral whose victories secured his country's command of the seas against Revolutionary and Napoleonic France. Born in Norfolk in 1758, the son of a clergyman, he went to sea as a boy and rose swiftly through the Royal Navy, losing the sight of one eye and then his right arm in the service—wounds that only burnished his legend.

A daring and inspired tactician who scorned timid convention, Nelson annihilated the French fleet at the Battle of the Nile in 1798, marooning Napoleon's army in Egypt. At Copenhagen in 1801 he famously raised his telescope to his blind eye to ignore an order to break off, and pressed on to victory. His scandalous love affair with Lady Emma Hamilton made him as notorious in the drawing room as he was glorious at sea.

His supreme moment came off Cape Trafalgar on 21 October 1805. Signaling that "England expects that every man will do his duty," he led his fleet against the combined French and Spanish navies and shattered them, ending forever the threat of invasion and ensuring a century of British naval supremacy.

In the hour of triumph a French marksman's bullet struck him down. He died as the battle was won, murmuring that he had done his duty. Britain gave him a state funeral and laid him in St Paul's, and raised his statue high above Trafalgar Square.
