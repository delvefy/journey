Jimi Hendrix

American rock guitarist (1942–1970)

Jimi Hendrix was the most influential electric guitarist in the history of rock, who in a career of barely four years redefined what the instrument could do.

Born in Seattle and self-taught—playing a right-handed guitar upside down—he paid his dues in the backing bands of touring R&B acts before being brought to London in 1966. There he detonated the British rock scene, and the album Are You Experienced announced a new sonic world of feedback, distortion and sheer invention. “Purple Haze,” “Voodoo Child” and his transformative cover of Bob Dylan's “All Along the Watchtower” became anthems; even the Beatles' Paul McCartney was among the admirers who helped launch him at the Monterey festival, where he set his guitar on fire.

His scorching, feedback-laced rendition of “The Star-Spangled Banner” at Woodstock became the defining musical image of the 1960s. Then, in 1970, he died in London at the age of twenty-seven, joining the tragic club of rock stars lost at that age.

His influence runs through every guitarist who followed, not least the Minneapolis virtuoso Prince. The British contemporary who shared his throne as a guitar god—a friend devastated by his death—was Eric Clapton.
