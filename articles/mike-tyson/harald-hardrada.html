Harald Hardrada

King of Norway, the last great Viking (1015–1066)

Harald Hardrada, born in 1015, was king of Norway and one of the most storied warriors of the Viking Age—indeed often called its last great figure. His epithet, "Hardrada," means "hard ruler," and he earned it. As a boy of fifteen he fought at the Battle of Stiklestad in 1030 in support of his half-brother, King Olaf, and was wounded in the defeat that cost Olaf his life and won him sainthood.

Forced into exile, Harald embarked on an extraordinary adventure. He made his way to the Byzantine Empire, where he served for years in the elite Varangian Guard of the emperors at Constantinople, fighting from Sicily to the Holy Land and amassing a fortune. He spent time at the court of Yaroslav the Wise of Kiev, married Yaroslav's daughter, and at last returned north laden with wealth and renown.

In 1046 he won the throne of Norway, which he ruled with an iron hand for two decades, waging a long and inconclusive war against Denmark and crushing all domestic opposition. But his ambitions reached further. In 1066, asserting a claim to the English crown and allied with Harold Godwinson's rebel brother Tostig, he launched a great invasion of England.

It was the last gamble of his life. He won an early victory, but on 25 September 1066 the English king Harold Godwinson fell upon his army at Stamford Bridge in Yorkshire and destroyed it; Harald Hardrada was killed by an arrow to the throat. His death is often taken to mark the end of the Viking Age. Three weeks later the victorious Harold would himself fall at Hastings, so that the year 1066 swept away two kings and an entire era.
