Frank Sinatra

American singer and actor (1915–1998)

Frank Sinatra was the most important popular singer of the twentieth century, an artist whose phrasing and feeling transformed the American song, and a star whose glamour and turbulence made him a legend off the stage as well.

The son of Italian immigrants in Hoboken, he became the idol of swooning bobby-soxers in the 1940s, then reinvented himself as the mature master of the Great American Songbook, recording a series of albums that set the standard for popular singing. His easy, conversational delivery, built on perfect control of breath and rhythm, influenced nearly every singer who followed.

He was a serious actor too, winning an Academy Award for From Here to Eternity and giving fine performances in The Man with the Golden Arm and The Manchurian Candidate. With Dean Martin and Sammy Davis Jr. he led the Rat Pack, the very image of Las Vegas cool.

His life ran close to power and to danger. He threw himself into politics, campaigning for John F. Kennedy and joining his glittering circle, before the president, wary of Sinatra's rumoured underworld ties, drew away.

Charismatic, generous, and quick-tempered, he was a fixture of celebrity society and a friend of the most famous star of the age, Marilyn Monroe. He died in 1998.
