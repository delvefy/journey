Jyeshtadeva

Mathematician of the Kerala school (c. 1500–1575)

Jyeshtadeva was a mathematician and astronomer of the Kerala school whose great work, the Yuktibhasha, is among the most remarkable scientific texts of pre-modern India. Written in the Malayalam language rather than Sanskrit, it was composed around 1530 for the instruction of students.

The Yuktibhasha—its name means roughly "explanation of rationale"—set out not merely the results of Kerala mathematics but their proofs. It gave systematic derivations of the infinite series for the sine, cosine, and arctangent that Madhava had discovered, together with the series for pi, and demonstrated the geometry and algebra behind them. For this reason some historians have called it the first text of the calculus.

Jyeshtadeva drew directly on the Tantrasamgraha of his older contemporary Nilakantha and on the legacy of Madhava, weaving their findings into a connected and reasoned whole. He studied within the unbroken teaching line of the school and was himself the teacher of Achyuta Pisharati.

Beyond pure mathematics the work treated astronomy—planetary motion, eclipses, and the corrections needed for accurate prediction—always with the same insistence on showing why each rule held true.

For three centuries the Yuktibhasha remained little known outside Kerala, but it is now recognized as a singular monument: a complete, proof-based account of an Indian mathematics that had reached the very threshold of the calculus. Through Jyeshtadeva the reasoning of the Kerala masters was saved from being lost.
