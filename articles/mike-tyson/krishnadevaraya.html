Krishnadevaraya

Emperor of Vijayanagara (r. 1509–1529)

Krishnadevaraya was the greatest ruler of the Vijayanagara Empire and one of the most celebrated kings in the history of southern India, a conqueror and a patron of letters whose reign is remembered as a golden age.

Coming to the throne in 1509, he proved an almost unbeaten commander. He broke the power of the Deccan sultanates that pressed on his northern frontier, winning the great Battle of Raichur against Bijapur, and he humbled the Gajapati kings of Odisha to the east, extending his dominion across the whole of the Tamil and Telugu south. Travellers reported his capital to be among the largest and richest cities in the world.

He was as devoted to culture as to war. His court was adorned by eight great poets known as the Ashtadiggajas, foremost among them Allasani Peddana, and he was himself an author of distinction, composing the Telugu poem Amuktamalyada as well as works in Sanskrit. He endowed temples across his realm, raising towering gateways at Hampi and lavishing gifts on the shrine of Tirupati.

His reign also opened a new chapter in India's contact with Europe. The Portuguese, newly established at Goa on his frontier, became valued partners in trade, above all for the war-horses his cavalry needed; their governor Afonso de Albuquerque courted his friendship against their common enemy, Bijapur.

Krishnadevaraya died in 1529 at the height of his power, and the empire he had raised so high passed in time to the control of his son-in-law, Rama Raya, under whom it would meet its ruin.
