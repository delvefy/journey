Yi Sun-sin

Korean admiral of the Joseon dynasty (1545–1598)

Yi Sun-sin, born in 1545, was a naval commander of Korea's Joseon dynasty and is revered as one of the greatest admirals in history. Though trained as an army officer and a man of stern, incorruptible character, he was appointed to command a provincial navy shortly before catastrophe struck: in 1592 the Japanese ruler Toyotomi Hideyoshi launched a massive invasion of Korea, the opening of the Imjin War.

While Korea's armies crumbled on land, Yi's fleet won victory after victory at sea, severing the Japanese supply lines that ran across the strait. He is famous for deploying the "turtle ships"—heavily armoured, cannon-bristling vessels often called among the first ironclads—and for his flawless grasp of tides, currents and the treacherous coastal waters. At the Battle of Hansan Island he annihilated a Japanese fleet with a crescent-shaped trap.

Court intrigue and the jealousy of rivals saw him stripped of command and imprisoned, and his successor promptly lost almost the entire navy. Recalled in desperation in 1597 with only a dozen ships left, Yi achieved his most astonishing feat at the Battle of Myeongnyang, where, exploiting a roaring tidal current in a narrow strait, he routed a Japanese armada many times his size.

The war ended with Hideyoshi's death and the Japanese withdrawal. At the final Battle of Noryang in 1598, pursuing the retreating enemy alongside a Ming Chinese fleet, Yi Sun-sin was struck by a bullet and died, reportedly ordering that his death be concealed so as not to halt the battle. Undefeated in his naval career, he became the supreme national hero of Korea, honoured to this day.
