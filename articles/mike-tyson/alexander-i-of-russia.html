Alexander I of Russia

Emperor of Russia (1777–1825)

Alexander I ruled Russia through the most dramatic years of its struggle with Napoleon and emerged as one of the arbiters of Europe. The grandson of Catherine the Great, who supervised his enlightened education, he came to the throne in 1801 after the murder of his father, a palace coup whose shadow troubled his conscience for life. His early reign promised liberal reform.

Then came Napoleon. Beaten at Austerlitz, Alexander made an uneasy peace with the French emperor on a raft at Tilsit in 1807, only to see the alliance collapse. When Napoleon invaded in 1812, the tsar refused to treat even as Moscow burned, and the Grande Armée was destroyed in its terrible winter retreat. Alexander's armies pursued the French to the gates of Paris in 1814.

At the Congress of Vienna he stood among the makers of the new order, and he founded the mystical Holy Alliance of conservative monarchs. His later years grew increasingly devout and reactionary.

He died suddenly at Taganrog in 1825, his death so unexpected that legend later claimed he had faked it to live out his days as a wandering holy man. His reign and the war of 1812 were immortalised in Leo Tolstoy's War and Peace, where he appears alongside his adversary Napoleon. The general who masterminded the ruin of the French invasion was Mikhail Kutuzov.
