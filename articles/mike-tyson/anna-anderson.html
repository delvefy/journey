Anna Anderson

Claimant to the identity of Grand Duchess Anastasia (c. 1896–1984)

Anna Anderson was the most famous and most stubborn of the many women who claimed to be Grand Duchess Anastasia, the supposed survivor of the murder of the Russian imperial family.

In 1920 she was pulled from a Berlin canal after trying to drown herself, and would at first give no name. In time she let it be understood that she was the youngest daughter of Tsar Nicholas II, escaped by some miracle from the cellar at Yekaterinburg. For the rest of her life she held to the story.

Her claim divided the surviving Romanovs and the Russian exiles—a few were convinced, most were certain she was an impostor—and it dragged through the German courts in what became the longest-running lawsuit in that country's history, ending without a verdict either way. She eventually settled in the United States, married an eccentric Virginia professor, and lived out her days in obscurity tinged with fame.

Only after her death did science close the question: DNA tests matched her not to the Romanovs but to the family of a missing Polish factory worker, and her claim collapsed.

Yet her tale had already conquered the popular imagination, above all through the 1956 film Anastasia, in which Ingrid Bergman gave an Academy Award–winning performance.
