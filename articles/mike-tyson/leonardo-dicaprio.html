Leonardo DiCaprio

American actor and environmentalist (born 1974)

Leonardo DiCaprio rose from a Los Angeles childhood to become one of the most famous film stars in the world. A precociously gifted young actor—nominated for an Academy Award while still a teenager for What's Eating Gilbert Grape—he was launched into global superstardom by Titanic in 1997, the seafaring romance that became one of the highest-grossing films ever made and turned him into an idol of millions.

Rather than coast on his fame, he sought out demanding work with the finest directors of his time. With Martin Scorsese he made a long run of films—The Aviator, The Departed, The Wolf of Wall Street—and he worked with Christopher Nolan on the dream-heist Inception and with Quentin Tarantino on Django Unchained and Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. Long passed over by the Academy, he finally won the Best Actor Oscar for his harrowing performance in the frontier survival epic The Revenant.

Beyond the screen he became one of the most prominent celebrity advocates for action on climate change, founding an environmental organization, serving as a United Nations messenger, and producing documentaries on the warming planet.

The other great leading man of his Hollywood generation, with whom he was often compared and with whom he finally shared the screen in Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, was Brad Pitt.
