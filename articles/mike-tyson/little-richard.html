Little Richard

American rock-and-roll pioneer (1932–2020)

Little Richard was the wild, ecstatic founding genius of rock and roll, the self-styled “Architect” whose pounding piano and screaming voice set the music's pulse racing.

Born in Macon, Georgia, and steeped in gospel, he erupted onto records in 1955 with “Tutti Frutti” and followed it with “Long Tall Sally,” “Good Golly Miss Molly” and “Lucille,” a torrent of joyous, frenzied energy. Flamboyant, pompadoured and gloriously theatrical, a queer Black performer who shattered conventions, he made his very presence a revolution.

His influence ran everywhere. Elvis Presley and others covered his songs; the young Paul McCartney learned to scream by imitating him, and the Beatles made “Long Tall Sally” their own; a guitarist named Jimi Hendrix served an early apprenticeship in his band; and his outrageous showmanship pointed the way to flamboyant heirs like Prince. Periodically he renounced rock and roll for the pulpit, torn all his life between the sacred and the profane, before dying in 2020.

Out of the same Georgia soil and the same explosive style came the young performer who idolized him and would build from it the empire of funk—the Godfather of Soul, James Brown.
