Louise of Savoy

Regent of France, mother of Francis I (1476–1531)

Louise of Savoy was one of the great political women of the French Renaissance, the mother of a king whom she raised, advised, and twice ruled in his place.

Widowed young, she gave herself wholly to her two remarkable children, and when her son inherited the throne unexpectedly as Francis I, her long ambition was crowned. She remained the firmest support of his reign.

Her greatest hour came in disaster. When Francis was utterly defeated and taken prisoner by the armies of Charles V at the Battle of Pavia in 1525, France seemed leaderless and exposed. Louise, named regent, held the kingdom together: she found the means to govern, kept the realm from collapse, opened negotiations for her son's release, and even sought alliances abroad to balance the emperor's power.

Four years later she brought the long struggle to a pause by her own diplomacy. Meeting Margaret of Austria, the regent of the Netherlands, she helped negotiate the Treaty of Cambrai of 1529, the Ladies' Peace, which freed Francis's captive sons and won France a breathing space in the Italian Wars.

She died in 1531, having seen her dynasty secured. Besides the king, she was mother to a daughter of rare gifts, the writer and queen Marguerite de Navarre.
