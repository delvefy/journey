Leonardo da Vinci

Italian Renaissance polymath (1452–1519)

Leonardo da Vinci, born near the Tuscan town of Vinci in 1452, is the supreme embodiment of the Renaissance ideal of the universal genius. Painter, engineer, anatomist, and inventor, he pursued knowledge across every field with insatiable curiosity. Apprenticed in Florence to the artist Verrocchio, he later served patrons in Milan, Rome, and finally France.

As a painter he completed comparatively few works, yet among them are the most famous pictures ever made: the enigmatic Mona Lisa and the monumental Last Supper. His mastery of light, shadow, and the soft blending called sfumato transformed the art of his age. But painting was only one of his pursuits.

He filled thousands of notebook pages, written in a left-handed mirror script, with investigations into anatomy, geometry, optics, water, geology, and the flight of birds. He dissected human bodies to draw their structure with unprecedented accuracy, designed flying machines and war engines, and sketched ideas centuries ahead of their realization. In him the artist and the scientist were one.

His rivalry with the younger Michelangelo was the talk of Florence, and his restless, questioning mind left many projects unfinished. In 1516 the French king Francis I lured him across the Alps, settling him in comfort near the royal court, and there, at Amboise, Leonardo died in 1519. His paintings made him immortal, and his notebooks, scattered and long neglected, revealed a mind that ranged across the whole of nature.
