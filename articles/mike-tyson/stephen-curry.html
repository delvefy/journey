Stephen Curry

American basketball player (b. 1988)

Stephen Curry is the greatest shooter in the history of basketball, a player whose uncanny range and accuracy from far beyond the arc changed how the game is played at every level.

The son of a professional player, he was slight of build and little recruited, and proved himself first at a small college before joining the Golden State Warriors. There he became the engine of a dynasty, leading the team to four championships and a record-breaking season of seventy-three wins. He was twice named the league's most valuable player, once by the unanimous vote of every elector—the first time that had ever happened—and he holds the all-time record for three-point baskets.

What set him apart was not size or leaping but a revolution in style: he made the long, quick three-point shot the heart of the game, and a generation of children grew up imitating him. His joy on the court and his dazzling handling of the ball made him one of the most beloved athletes of his time.

His grand rivalry was with LeBron James, whom he met again and again in the championship round. For two of his titles he was joined by another of the era's giants, Kevin Durant.
