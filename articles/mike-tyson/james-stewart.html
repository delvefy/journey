James Stewart

American actor (1908–1997)

James Stewart was one of the most beloved and admired actors of Hollywood's golden age, the very image of the decent, idealistic American, with a halting drawl and an air of complete sincerity.

He rose to fame in the warm-hearted films of Frank Capra, championing the common man in Mr. Smith Goes to Washington and finding redemption in the perennial favourite It's a Wonderful Life. He won the Academy Award for The Philadelphia Story, sharing the screen with Cary Grant and Katharine Hepburn.

A genuine war hero, he flew bombing missions over Germany and rose to high rank in the reserves. After the war his work grew darker and richer—tormented heroes in a great run of Westerns, and four unforgettable films for Alfred Hitchcock, among them Rear Window and the haunting Vertigo.

For all his fame he remained modest and steady, his integrity a byword in a fickle town. His closest friend through it all, despite their opposite politics, was a fellow actor he had known since their struggling youth, Henry Fonda.
