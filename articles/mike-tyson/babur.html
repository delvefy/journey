Babur

Founder of the Mughal Empire (1483–1530)

Babur, born in 1483 in the Fergana Valley of Central Asia, was the founder of the Mughal Empire in India. Through his veins ran the blood of two great conquerors: he descended from Timur on his father's side and from Genghis Khan on his mother's. He inherited a petty kingdom at the age of twelve and spent his restless youth in a losing struggle to win and hold the cities of Central Asia, even seizing and again losing the coveted Samarkand.

Pushed southward, he carved out a base at Kabul and then turned his gaze toward the wealth of India. In 1526, at the First Battle of Panipat, his smaller army—armed with field artillery and matchlock guns still novel in the subcontinent—destroyed the much larger force of the Sultan of Delhi, Ibrahim Lodi, who fell on the field. With that victory the Mughal Empire was born, and Babur secured it with further triumphs over the Rajputs.

He was far more than a warrior. In the Baburnama, the frank and vivid memoir he wrote in his native Turkic, he recorded his battles, his longings, and his keen eye for nature, fruit, and gardens, leaving one of the great autobiographies of any age.

Babur ruled his new Indian empire only briefly, dying in 1530 and passing the throne to his son Humayun. It would be his grandson Akbar who raised the dynasty he founded to imperial greatness.
