Maximilian II

Holy Roman Emperor (1527–1576)

Maximilian II, Holy Roman Emperor, was a ruler remembered for his moderation in an age of fanaticism, a Catholic prince who leaned in sympathy toward the Protestants and sought, in vain, to reconcile a Christendom tearing itself apart.

The son of the emperor Ferdinand I, he was a nephew of Charles V and was schooled for a time at the Spanish court, where he came to resent its stiff devotion and his rivalry with his cousin Philip II of Spain. He married another of Charles's children, his cousin Maria of Austria, a far sterner Catholic than himself.

As emperor from 1564 he ruled the Austrian, Bohemian, and Hungarian lands with a gentle and tolerant hand, granting concessions to his Lutheran subjects and dreaming of a reunited Church. But he would not break with Rome, and so satisfied neither side, while his irresolution left the great questions unanswered.

On his eastern frontier he carried on the grinding war against the Ottoman power of Suleiman the Magnificent, who died campaigning against Maximilian's Hungary; the struggle ended only in an uneasy truce bought with tribute.

Cultivated, humane, and tolerant, but rarely decisive, he left the empire's deepest divisions to his sons. He died in 1576 and was succeeded by Rudolf II. His own crown had come to him from his father, Ferdinand I, who had built the Austrian house of Habsburg.
