Christoph Willibald Gluck

German composer, reformer of opera (1714–1787)

Christoph Willibald Gluck was the composer who reformed opera and rescued it from artifice, restoring drama to its heart. Born near the Bohemian border and trained in Italy, he wandered the musical capitals of Europe—Milan, London, Vienna, Paris—before settling on the mission that made him famous. The Italian opera of his day had become a vehicle for vain singers and empty display; Gluck set out to make music serve the story once more.

With the librettist Calzabigi he created the great "reform operas," beginning with Orfeo ed Euridice in 1762 and continuing with Alceste, whose preface became a manifesto for "a beautiful simplicity." Moving to Paris under the protection of his former singing pupil, now Queen Marie Antoinette, he produced Iphigénie en Aulide and Iphigénie en Tauride, igniting a famous public war between his admirers and the partisans of his Italian rival.

Court composer to the Emperor Joseph II, Gluck stripped opera of its excesses and gave it dignity and feeling, clearing the path along which Mozart and the masters of the next century would advance. He died in Vienna in 1787, honored as the man who had remade his art.

In his early travels Gluck had gone to London, where he encountered—and came to revere—the colossus of the Baroque, the master of oratorio and opera whose grandeur overwhelmed him: George Frideric Handel.
