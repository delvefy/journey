Shah Jahan

Mughal emperor (1592–1666)

Shah Jahan, grandson of Akbar and fifth of the Mughal emperors, presided over the artistic and architectural zenith of his dynasty. Coming to the throne in 1628, he ruled an empire of immense wealth and refinement, and his reign is remembered as a golden age of Mughal building, painting, and courtly splendor.

His name is forever bound to the Taj Mahal. When his cherished wife Mumtaz Mahal died in 1631, Shah Jahan poured the resources of his empire into raising for her, on the bank of the Yamuna at Agra, a mausoleum of white marble inlaid with precious stones—a monument so perfect in its symmetry and grace that it became one of the most beloved buildings on earth and an enduring emblem of love and grief.

He built much else besides: the great Red Fort and the Jama Masjid of Delhi, to which he moved his capital, and the fabulous jeweled Peacock Throne. But his wars and grandeur drained the treasury, and his final years were tragic. When he fell ill in 1657, his sons turned on one another in a war of succession.

The victor was his ruthless son Aurangzeb, who killed his brothers, seized the throne, and imprisoned the aging emperor in the fort at Agra. There Shah Jahan spent his last eight years, it is said, gazing across the river at the tomb he had built. He died in 1666 and was laid to rest beside Mumtaz in the Taj.
