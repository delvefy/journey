Inês de Castro

Noblewoman of Portugal, "queen crowned after death" (c. 1325–1355)

Inês de Castro was a Galician noblewoman whose love for the heir to the Portuguese throne, and her murder for it, made her one of the most enduring tragic figures of European history and literature.

Of the noble Castro family, she came to Portugal among the attendants of the princess who married the crown prince, the future Pedro I. Pedro fell in love with her, and after his wife's death the two lived together openly and had children. But Inês's powerful Castilian kinsmen, and the danger they posed to the recognised heir, alarmed the old king, Afonso IV.

Fearing for the succession and the independence of the realm, Afonso IV resolved on a dreadful remedy. In 1355, while Pedro was away, royal agents came to Inês at her residence in Coimbra and put her to death. The place is remembered still as the Quinta das Lágrimas, the Estate of Tears.

Pedro's grief turned to fury and, when he became king, to a terrible vengeance upon her killers; by tradition he proclaimed that he had secretly married her and had her corpse enthroned and crowned. Today the lovers lie in facing tombs in the monastery of Alcobaça, carved so that on the Day of Judgment they will rise and at once behold one another.

Her death became a favourite theme of poets and playwrights, and above all it was set among the glories of Portugal by Luís de Camões in his great epic.
