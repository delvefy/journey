Zerubbabel

Davidic prince and governor of Judah (6th century BC)

Zerubbabel was the prince who led the first homecoming of the Jews from their Babylonian captivity. A grandson of Judah's last reigning king and thus an heir of the house of David, he was the natural leader of the exiles when the Persian conqueror Cyrus the Great decreed that they might return to Jerusalem and rebuild the house of their God. Appointed governor of the small restored province, he brought the first wave of returnees back to a ruined and half-empty land.

His great task was the rebuilding of the Temple. With the high priest at his side he restored the altar and laid the foundations, but the work soon stalled under the hostility of neighbors and the discouragement of the people. It was the fiery preaching of the prophets Haggai and Zechariah that roused him to finish it, and in the reign of Darius the Great the Second Temple at last rose on the holy mount, some seventy years after the first had been destroyed.

Around Zerubbabel gathered the hopes of a people longing for the restoration of David's line; the prophets spoke of him as a chosen signet ring of the Lord. Yet he never took a crown, and after the Temple's completion he vanishes from the record, his fate unknown.

His authority had flowed from his ancestry, from the royal house carried into exile. The grandfather whose captivity in Babylon Zerubbabel's return reversed, the last anointed king to sit in Jerusalem before the fall, was Jehoiachin.
