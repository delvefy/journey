Cary Grant

British-American film actor (1904–1986)

Cary Grant was the screen's supreme embodiment of elegance, wit, and effortless charm, a leading man whose poise defined Hollywood sophistication for a generation.

Born Archibald Leach in Bristol, he ran away as a boy with a troupe of acrobats, crossed to America, and remade himself into the impossibly debonair figure the world came to know. “Everyone wants to be Cary Grant,” he once said; “even I want to be Cary Grant.”

He was the master of screwball comedy, sparkling in Bringing Up Baby, His Girl Friday, The Awful Truth, and The Philadelphia Story, where he played opposite Katharine Hepburn. But he showed darker shades too, and became the favourite leading man of the director Alfred Hitchcock, for whom he made Suspicion, Notorious, To Catch a Thief, and the dazzling chase North by Northwest.

He never won a competitive Academy Award, though he was given an honorary one, and his lightness of touch was so complete that audiences forgot it was acting at all. He retired gracefully in the 1960s while still admired, and died in 1986.

No collaboration shaped his later image more than his work with the master of suspense, Alfred Hitchcock.
