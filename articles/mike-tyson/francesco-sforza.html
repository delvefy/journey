Francesco Sforza

Condottiero and Duke of Milan (1401–1466)

Francesco Sforza was the soldier of fortune who carved out a dukedom and founded a dynasty by sheer ability. The son of the condottiero Muzio Attendolo Sforza, he learned war in the saddle and became the most sought-after mercenary captain in Italy, selling his sword in turn to Milan, Venice, Florence, Naples, and the Pope, and feared by all of them.

His path to power ran through marriage. In 1441 he wed Bianca Maria Visconti, the illegitimate but beloved daughter of Filippo Maria Visconti, Duke of Milan, binding the upstart soldier to the ancient ruling house. When the duke died in 1447 leaving no legitimate son, Milan threw off princely rule and proclaimed itself the Ambrosian Republic. Francesco bided his time, then turned on the republic he had been hired to defend, blockaded the city into starvation, and in 1450 entered Milan as its new duke.

As ruler he proved as shrewd as he had been bold. He made a lasting friend of Cosimo de' Medici and helped negotiate the Peace of Lodi in 1454, whose balance of power gave Italy a rare generation of quiet. He strengthened the great Castello Sforzesco and ruled with a firm, capable hand.

Machiavelli would later cite him as the perfect example of a man who became a prince by his own arms and talent rather than by inheritance or luck. The dynasty he founded would pass to his son Ludovico, who lost it to France. The duke whose daughter he married, and whose death opened Milan to him, was Filippo Maria Visconti.
