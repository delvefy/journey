Theodosius I

Roman emperor, "the Great" (347–395)

Theodosius the Great was the last emperor to rule over a united Roman Empire and the ruler who made Christianity its official faith. Born in Spain, the son of a distinguished general, he was raised to the imperial purple in the East in 379, in the wake of the catastrophe at Adrianople, where the Goths had annihilated a Roman army and slain the emperor Valens. Theodosius stabilized the shattered frontier by settling the Goths within the empire as allied peoples.

A fervent believer in Nicene orthodoxy, he transformed the religious landscape of the Roman world. His Edict of Thessalonica in 380 proclaimed Nicene Christianity the religion of the state; he summoned a great council at Constantinople to confirm the creed, and he moved steadily against the old paganism—closing temples, ending the ancient Olympic Games, and stripping away the last public rites of the gods.

Even so mighty an emperor bowed to the moral authority of the Church: after his soldiers massacred the people of Thessalonica, Bishop Ambrose compelled him to do public penance. In war he overthrew a succession of Western usurpers, and at the Battle of the Frigidus in 394 he reunited the whole empire under his sole rule—the last man ever to hold it whole.

At his death in 395 he divided the empire between his sons, a partition into East and West that would never be undone. In making the faith supreme, Theodosius completed the revolution begun three generations earlier by the first Christian emperor—Constantine the Great.
