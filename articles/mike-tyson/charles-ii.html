Charles II

King of England, Scotland and Ireland (1630–1685)

Charles II was born in 1630, the eldest surviving son of Charles I and Henrietta Maria. His youth was swallowed by the Civil War, and after his father's execution in 1649 he tried to recover the throne by force, only to be routed by Oliver Cromwell at Worcester in 1651. His dramatic escape—famously hiding in an oak tree—passed into legend, and he spent nine years as a poor and wandering exile on the Continent.

The collapse of the Commonwealth after Cromwell's death allowed his triumphant Restoration in 1660. Witty, easygoing and pleasure-loving, the "Merry Monarch" presided over a glittering and licentious court. He married Catherine of Braganza but left no legitimate heir, scattering instead a brood of children by mistresses such as Nell Gwyn.

His reign saw the Great Plague of 1665 and the Great Fire of 1666, after which he charged Christopher Wren with rebuilding London's churches and St Paul's Cathedral. A patron of science, he granted the charter that created the Royal Society. In foreign affairs he leaned secretly on his cousin Louis XIV, accepting French subsidies through the clandestine Treaty of Dover.

His later years were troubled by bitter struggles with Parliament over the succession of his Catholic brother James, struggles that gave birth to the Whig and Tory parties. Charles dissolved Parliament and ruled alone at the last, and on his deathbed in 1685 was received into the Catholic Church. His brother succeeded him as James II.
