King David

King of Israel (traditionally c. 1010–970 BC)

David was the second king of Israel and, in the Hebrew Bible, its greatest, a figure of such stature that an ancient inscription referring to the "House of David" attests to the dynasty he founded. Tradition makes him a shepherd boy of Bethlehem, the youngest son of Jesse, secretly anointed by the prophet Samuel while Saul still reigned.

He won early fame, the Bible relates, by felling the Philistine giant Goliath with a single stone from his sling, and he rose at Saul's court as a warrior and a harpist whose music soothed the troubled king. But Saul's jealousy turned murderous, and David spent years as a fugitive and outlaw, twice sparing the life of the king who hunted him.

After Saul's death David was made king, united the tribes of Israel, captured Jerusalem and made it his capital, and brought the Ark of the Covenant into the city. A gifted poet to whom tradition credits many of the Psalms, he expanded and secured his kingdom. Yet his reign was shadowed by his own sins—his adultery with Bathsheba and the death he arranged for her husband—and by the bloody revolt of his son Absalom.

David was succeeded by his son Solomon. Honored as king and prophet across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, he became the measure of the ideal ruler, and from his line, scripture promised, the Messiah would come—a hope Christians see fulfilled in Jesus, the "Son of David."
