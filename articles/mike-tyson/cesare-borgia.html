Cesare Borgia

Italian condottiero and prince (c. 1475–1507)

Cesare Borgia was the most notorious soldier-prince of Renaissance Italy, the son of Cardinal Rodrigo Borgia—the future Pope Alexander VI—and brother of the famed Lucrezia. Made a cardinal at eighteen through his father's influence, he scandalized the Church by resigning the red hat, the first ever to do so, in order to pursue a career of arms and ambition.

With papal money and a French alliance behind him, Cesare set out to conquer a princedom of his own in the Romagna of central Italy. He proved a commander of cold brilliance, mixing swift force with calculated treachery—most infamously luring his rebellious captains to the town of Senigallia and having them strangled. He employed Leonardo da Vinci as a military engineer, and his audacious statecraft was studied at first hand by the Florentine envoy Niccolò Machiavelli, who would immortalize him as the model of the bold "new prince" in The Prince.

But Cesare's power rested entirely on his father. When Alexander VI died in 1503 and the Borgias' bitter enemy became Pope Julius II, the edifice crumbled with astonishing speed. Cesare was arrested, escaped to Spain, and fought on as a mercenary captain until he was killed in an obscure skirmish in Navarre in 1507.

His motto had been "Aut Caesar aut nihil"—Caesar or nothing. He had gambled everything on fortune and his father's life, and lost both.
