Freddie Mercury

Lead singer of Queen (1946–1991)

Freddie Mercury was the flamboyant frontman of the rock band Queen, possessor of one of the most powerful and theatrical voices in popular music.

Born Farrokh Bulsara on the island of Zanzibar and raised partly in India before his family settled in England, he fronted Queen from 1970 alongside the guitarist Brian May. With his soaring four-octave range and irrepressible showmanship he led the band through anthems that became part of the cultural furniture—“Bohemian Rhapsody,” “We Are the Champions,” “Somebody to Love,” “Don't Stop Me Now.” He even ventured into opera, recording an album with the soprano Montserrat Caballé.

His performance with Queen at the Live Aid concert of 1985 is often called the greatest live rock performance ever given. He moved among the era's superstars—his friend Elton John, and Michael Jackson, with whom he recorded duets—and in 1991 he died of AIDS-related illness, having announced his diagnosis only the day before, a loss mourned around the world.

One of his most memorable collaborations was the chart-topping duet “Under Pressure,” recorded by Queen with the restless, ever-changing genius of glam and art rock—David Bowie.
