George IV

King of the United Kingdom (1762–1830)

George Augustus Frederick was born in 1762, the eldest son of George III and Queen Charlotte. Witty, cultivated and hopelessly extravagant, he grew into a notorious figure of fashionable society, friend of the dandy Beau Brummell and ally of the Whig opposition against his father's ministers. In 1785 he secretly and illegally married the Catholic widow Maria Fitzherbert; a decade later, to clear his vast debts, he made an official and disastrous marriage to Caroline of Brunswick.

When his father's recurring madness rendered the king incapable, George was made Prince Regent in 1811. The "Regency" that followed lent its name to an era of elegance, even as Britain fought and finally overcame Napoleon Bonaparte, whose defeat at Waterloo by the Duke of Wellington was celebrated across the realm.

A discerning, spendthrift patron of art and architecture, George commissioned the fantastical Royal Pavilion at Brighton, laid out Regent Street and Regent's Park, and began the transformation of Buckingham Palace. He succeeded as king in 1820 and at once sought to divorce his wife; the public proceedings against Caroline made him deeply unpopular.

Increasingly obese and reclusive, he made a celebrated state visit to Scotland in 1822, the first by a reigning monarch in generations. He died in 1830 and, having no surviving legitimate child, was succeeded by his brother as William IV, with the crown passing soon afterward to his niece, Queen Victoria.
