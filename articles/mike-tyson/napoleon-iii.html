Napoleon III

Emperor of the French (1808–1873)

Napoleon III, born Charles-Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte in 1808, was the nephew of Napoleon Bonaparte and the last monarch to rule France. He spent his youth in exile, nursing the family's imperial ambitions and the spell of his uncle's legend. When the Revolution of 1848 swept away the monarchy, he rode that legendary name to victory as the first elected president of the new French Republic.

Forbidden by the constitution to seek a second term, he seized power in a coup in 1851 and the next year proclaimed himself Emperor, inaugurating the Second Empire. At home his reign brought sweeping modernization: he commissioned Baron Haussmann's dramatic rebuilding of Paris into a city of grand boulevards, encouraged railways, banking, and industry, and signed free-trade agreements.

Abroad his record was mixed and ultimately fatal. He allied with Britain in the Crimean War, aided the cause of Italian unification, and pursued a calamitous adventure in Mexico, installing a puppet emperor who was overthrown and shot. His undoing came in 1870, when Bismarck maneuvered him into the Franco-Prussian War; the French armies were shattered, and the Emperor himself was captured at the Battle of Sedan.

The Second Empire collapsed at once, and France proclaimed a republic. Napoleon III went into exile in England, where he died in 1873—a ruler who modernized his country yet lost it an empire, forever overshadowed by the uncle whose name had made him.
