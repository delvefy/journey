Bimbisara

King of Magadha, patron of the Buddha (c. 558–491 BC)

Bimbisara was the king who first raised the realm of Magadha, in the eastern Ganges valley, into the foremost power of northern India—the kingdom that would in time give rise to the empires of the Nandas and the Mauryas. A ruler of the Haryanka line, he came to the throne young and reigned for some five decades, governing from his capital at Rajagriha among the hills.

He enlarged Magadha by both war and diplomacy. He annexed the neighbouring kingdom of Anga, with its rich river trade, and he secured his frontiers through marriage alliances with the royal houses of Kosala, of the Licchavis of Vaishali, and of Madra, weaving a web of kinship across the region. Tradition credits him with an orderly administration and a network of village and district officials.

Bimbisara is best remembered for his place in the religious ferment of his age. He was a contemporary and protector of both great teachers then wandering his country: he honoured Mahavira, and he became a friend and lay disciple of the Buddha, to whom he is said to have given the Bamboo Grove near Rajagriha as a retreat for the monastic community.

His end was grim. According to the traditions, his ambitious son imprisoned the old king and let him die in confinement, seizing the throne for himself. That son, who would carry Magadha's power to new heights and war against his neighbours, was Ajatashatru.
