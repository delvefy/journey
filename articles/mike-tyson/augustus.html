Augustus

First Roman emperor (63 BC–14 AD)

Augustus, born Gaius Octavius in 63 BC, was the first emperor of Rome and the founder of the imperial system that endured for centuries. The great-nephew and adopted heir of Julius Caesar, he was only eighteen when Caesar's murder thrust him into the deadly politics of the late Republic. Allying with Mark Antony and Lepidus in the Second Triumvirate, he helped avenge Caesar by defeating the assassins Brutus and Cassius at Philippi.

The alliance soon dissolved into rivalry with Antony, who had joined his fortunes to Cleopatra of Egypt. The contest was settled at the naval Battle of Actium in 31 BC, after which Antony and Cleopatra took their own lives, leaving Octavian master of the Roman world. He had ended a century of civil war.

Careful never to seem a king, he cloaked his power in the forms of the old Republic. In 27 BC the Senate granted him the name Augustus, and as princeps, or first citizen, he governed an empire while appearing to restore tradition. His long reign inaugurated the Pax Romana, a stretch of peace and prosperity; he reorganized the army and provinces, and boasted that he had found Rome a city of brick and left it one of marble.

His age was also a golden age of letters, which he fostered through patronage of poets such as Virgil. Ruling for more than forty years, Augustus was succeeded by his stepson Tiberius and was declared a god after his death in 14 AD.
