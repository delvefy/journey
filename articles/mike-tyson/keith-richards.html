Keith Richards

Guitarist of the Rolling Stones (born 1943)

Keith Richards is the guitarist of the Rolling Stones and the riff-making heart of the band, an artist often called the very embodiment of rock and roll.

A boyhood acquaintance of Mick Jagger in Dartford, he reconnected with him over a shared love of American blues and rhythm and blues, and together they built the Rolling Stones and one of music's greatest songwriting partnerships. From the open-tuned crunch of “(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction” to “Jumpin' Jack Flash,” “Honky Tonk Women” and “Start Me Up,” his riffs became the scaffolding of rock. He once joined John Lennon in a one-off supergroup at the Stones' “Rock and Roll Circus,” a meeting of the era's titans.

Famous for hard living and improbable survival, he became a figure of legend—his raffish, piratical persona even inspiring a film character—while remaining a fiercely serious student of the music. He has played on, defiant and grinning, for more than sixty years.

Above every other influence he revered the man who, more than anyone, invented the language of rock-and-roll guitar, whose songs he learned note for note and whose genius he spent a lifetime honoring—Chuck Berry.
