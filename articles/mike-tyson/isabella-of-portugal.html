Isabella of Portugal

Holy Roman Empress, Queen of Spain (1503–1539)

Isabella of Portugal was Holy Roman Empress and Queen of Spain, the beloved wife of Charles V and the mother of Philip II, and one of the most capable royal women of the sixteenth century.

A daughter of King Manuel I of Portugal and sister of John III, she was sought in marriage for her beauty and her great dowry, and in 1526 she wed her cousin, the emperor Charles V. What began as a match of policy became a true devotion between them.

Charles's vast inheritance kept him forever travelling between his many realms, and during his long absences from Spain he trusted Isabella with the government as regent. She proved a shrewd and dignified ruler, presiding over the council, managing the treasury, and holding the loyalty of the Castilian grandees in her husband's name.

She bore him several children, among them the heir who would become Philip II of Spain. But in 1539, still only thirty-five, she died after a difficult childbirth. The emperor was inconsolable; he withdrew into mourning, never married again, and long afterward had the painter Titian portray her from memory, keeping the likeness beside him to the end of his life.

Through her children the blood of Portugal flowed into the great houses of Europe. Her daughter Maria of Austria married into the imperial line and became, like her mother, a Holy Roman Empress.
