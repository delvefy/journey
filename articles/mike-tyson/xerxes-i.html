Xerxes I

King of the Persian Empire (c. 518–465 BC)

Xerxes I, son of Darius the Great and of Atossa, daughter of Cyrus the Great, ruled the Achaemenid Empire from 486 BC and is remembered chiefly for his colossal invasion of Greece. After putting down revolts in Egypt and Babylon, he resolved to complete his father's unfinished business and avenge the Persian defeat at Marathon.

In 480 BC he led one of the largest armies the ancient world had ever assembled, bridging the Hellespont with a roadway of boats; Herodotus tells that when a storm wrecked the first bridge, Xerxes had the waters themselves whipped in punishment. His advance was checked at the narrow pass of Thermopylae, where the Spartan king Leonidas and his small band fought to the death, buying time and immortal glory. Xerxes pressed on, captured Athens, and burned its temples.

But the campaign turned to disaster at sea. Lured into the cramped straits of Salamis, his great fleet was shattered by the Greeks under Themistocles. Xerxes withdrew to Asia, leaving his general Mardonius behind; the following year the Persian land army was crushed at Plataea, ending the invasion for good.

Back in his heartland, Xerxes turned to monumental building at Persepolis, raising the Gate of All Nations and a vast columned hall. Some traditions identify him with King Ahasuerus of the biblical Book of Esther. In 465 BC he was assassinated in a palace intrigue and succeeded by his son Artaxerxes I.
