Raphael

Italian Renaissance painter (1483–1520)

Raphael, born in Urbino in 1483, completed the great trinity of the High Renaissance alongside Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo. The son of a court painter, he trained under Perugino and quickly surpassed him, absorbing Leonardo's subtle modeling and Michelangelo's command of the human body to forge a style celebrated above all for its grace, clarity, and serene harmony. His tender Madonnas became among the most beloved images in Western art.

Called to Rome by Pope Julius II, Raphael was set to decorating the papal apartments in the Vatican. There he painted his supreme masterpiece, The School of Athens, in which the philosophers of antiquity gather beneath soaring arches—Plato and Aristotle at the very center, one pointing to the heavens, the other to the earth. Into the crowd Raphael slipped knowing portraits, giving Plato the features of Leonardo and modeling the brooding Heraclitus on his rival Michelangelo.

Enormously productive and much in demand, he ran a busy workshop, painted portraits and altarpieces, and after Bramante's death took charge of the rebuilding of St. Peter's. His charm and diplomacy made him as beloved in person as his art was admired.

Raphael died suddenly in 1520, at only thirty-seven, and was buried with honor in the Pantheon. In his brief life he had defined an ideal of beauty and balance that academies would hold up as the standard for centuries.
