Harold Nicolson

British diplomat, author, and diarist (1886–1968)

Harold Nicolson was a British diplomat, politician, and man of letters whose diaries became one of the great chronicles of twentieth-century public life.

He entered the Foreign Office as a young man and served as a junior diplomat at the Paris Peace Conference of 1919, the gathering that redrew the map of Europe after the Great War. He later set down a sharp and disillusioned account of it in Peacemaking 1919, a classic of its kind that judged the statesmen and their bargains with a cool eye.

Leaving diplomacy, he turned to writing and to politics, sitting for a time in Parliament and producing a steady stream of biography and essay. He was chosen to write the official life of King George V, a work much praised for its tact and craft, and his own Diaries and Letters, published after his death, preserved the texture of his age.

His marriage to the writer Vita Sackville-West was famously unorthodox yet enduring, and together they made the celebrated garden at Sissinghurst. He moved easily among the writers of Bloomsbury and counted Virginia Woolf among his friends.

His commission to record a reign joined his name to that of the monarch whose life he chronicled, King George V.
