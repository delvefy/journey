Filippo Maria Visconti

Last Visconti Duke of Milan (1392–1447)

Filippo Maria Visconti was the last of his line to rule Milan, a strange and secretive prince who rebuilt a shattered inheritance through patience, gold, and the swords of hired captains. He was the younger son of Gian Galeazzo Visconti, the great duke whose empire had collapsed after his death; when Filippo Maria's brutal elder brother was assassinated in 1412, the duchy, much reduced, fell to him.

Against the odds he restored it. By careful diplomacy and relentless warfare he won back most of Lombardy, holding off the rival powers of Venice and Florence in decades of shifting conflict. Unable to lead armies himself, he relied on the finest mercenary generals money could buy—among them the formidable Francesco Sforza, whom he both employed and feared.

His character was as guarded as his policy. Reputedly ungainly and ashamed of his appearance, he shut himself in his castle, surrounded by astrologers and guards, trusting almost no one and moving by secret design. To bind Sforza to his interest he gave the captain his cherished illegitimate daughter, Bianca Maria, in marriage—a decision that would change the fate of Milan.

When he died in 1447 he left no legitimate son, and with him the Visconti dukedom came to an end. Milan briefly became a republic before Sforza seized the crown his marriage had made possible. For all his own achievements, Filippo Maria lived in the shadow of his father, the founder of the Milanese state and the most powerful prince of his age, Gian Galeazzo Visconti.
