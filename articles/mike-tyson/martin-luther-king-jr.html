Martin Luther King Jr.

Leader of the American civil rights movement (1929–1968)

Martin Luther King Jr. was the moral leader of the American struggle for racial justice and one of the great apostles of nonviolence in the twentieth century. A Baptist minister steeped in the Black church, he grounded his cause in the teachings of Jesus and modelled its method on the nonviolent resistance of Mahatma Gandhi, whose India he visited to study at first hand.

He rose to national prominence leading the Montgomery bus boycott of 1955, and through the Southern Christian Leadership Conference he carried the campaign across the South—Birmingham, where he wrote his famous letter from jail, and Selma, where the marches helped force the issue of voting rights. At the 1963 March on Washington he delivered, before a quarter of a million people, the “I Have a Dream” speech that became the anthem of the movement. His pressure helped win the Civil Rights Act and the Voting Rights Act, and in 1964 he received the Nobel Peace Prize.

In his last years he broadened his message to the poverty of all races and to opposition to the Vietnam War, making new enemies even as he widened his vision. On April 4, 1968, he was assassinated on a Memphis balcony, at only thirty-nine.

The nation later honoured him with a holiday, and many saw his dream partly fulfilled in the presidency of Barack Obama. His great rival within the movement, who preached a more militant road to Black liberation, was Malcolm X.
