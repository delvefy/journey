Adolf Hitler

Dictator of Nazi Germany (1889–1945)

Adolf Hitler, born in 1889 in Braunau am Inn, Austria, was the dictator of Nazi Germany whose aggression and genocidal racism made him the central figure of the deadliest conflict in human history. A failed art student in Vienna, he absorbed the antisemitic and pan-German currents of the city before serving as a corporal in the German army during the First World War, a defeat he never accepted.

After the war he joined a tiny nationalist party in Munich and remade it as the Nazi Party, channeling resentment of the Treaty of Versailles and economic ruin into a movement built on propaganda and violence. His 1923 Beer Hall Putsch failed, and during imprisonment he dictated Mein Kampf, setting out his ideology of racial hierarchy and expansion. Exploiting the Great Depression, the Nazis grew into Germany's largest party, and in January 1933 Hitler was appointed chancellor.

He swiftly destroyed democracy, banning opposition and proclaiming himself Führer of a totalitarian state. Rearming Germany, he annexed Austria and Czechoslovakia, then invaded Poland in September 1939, igniting the Second World War in Europe. His regime carried out the Holocaust, the systematic murder of some six million Jews along with millions of Roma, disabled people, political prisoners, and others.

Early conquests gave way to catastrophe after the failed invasion of the Soviet Union and the defeat at Stalingrad. As Allied armies converged on Berlin in April 1945, Hitler killed himself in his bunker. He left behind a devastated continent and remains the modern world's enduring symbol of tyranny and mass murder.
