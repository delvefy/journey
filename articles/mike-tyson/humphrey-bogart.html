Humphrey Bogart

American film actor (1899–1957)

Humphrey Bogart was the defining male star of classic Hollywood, later named by the American Film Institute the greatest of them all, an actor who made world-weary toughness and wounded honour into an art.

After long years as a stage actor and a player of movie gangsters, he broke through in 1941 with The Maltese Falcon, playing the detective Sam Spade for the director John Huston. The next year, as the cynical, broken-hearted café owner Rick in Casablanca, opposite Ingrid Bergman, he became an immortal figure of the screen, his clipped voice and trench coat the very image of the romantic loner.

He went on to embody film noir and the honourable cynic in The Big Sleep, Key Largo, and In a Lonely Place, and he won the Academy Award for The African Queen, in which he was paired with Katharine Hepburn under Huston's direction.

On a film set he met the young Lauren Bacall, who became his wife and frequent partner; “Bogie and Bacall” passed into legend. A relentless smoker, he died of cancer in 1957, still at the height of his fame.

Among his great leading ladies none was more formidable than his African Queen co-star, Katharine Hepburn.
