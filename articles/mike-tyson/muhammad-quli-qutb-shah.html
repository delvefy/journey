Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah

Sultan of Golconda, founder of Hyderabad (1565–1612)

Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, fifth sultan of the Qutb Shahi dynasty of Golconda, is remembered as a king who was also a poet and a city-builder, and whose reign marked the cultural height of his line.

His most lasting act was the founding of a new capital. The old fortress-city of Golconda had grown crowded and short of water, and in 1591 he laid out a fresh city on the banks of the river Musi, which became Hyderabad. At its very heart he raised the Charminar, the four-arched, four-minareted monument that remains the emblem of the city to this day.

He was, besides, the first ruler to leave a great body of verse in the Deccani tongue, an early form of Urdu. His collected poems, the Kulliyat, sing of love, of the gardens and festivals of his new city, and of the seasons of the Deccan, and they give him a place among the founders of Urdu literature.

His reign was rich and peaceful within, though the politics of India were darkening. To the north the Mughal emperor Akbar was extending his power into the Deccan and demanding the submission of its sultanates; Golconda, like its neighbours, was obliged to acknowledge a wary tribute while preserving its independence.

Muhammad Quli died in 1612 and was succeeded by his nephew. The strength and wealth he inherited had been built up before him by his father, Ibrahim Quli Qutb Shah, under whom Golconda had risen to be a great power of the south.
