Bob Marley

Jamaican reggae musician and global icon (1945–1981)

Bob Marley carried the music of Jamaica to the entire world and became one of the most beloved and recognizable figures in the history of popular music. Rising from the Trench Town slums of Kingston with his band the Wailers, he transformed reggae from a local sound into a global language of liberation, and his image—dreadlocked, exultant—became a universal emblem of freedom and joy.

A devout Rastafarian, he poured his faith into his songs, which fused irresistible rhythm with messages of justice, unity, and resistance to oppression. “Redemption Song,” with its plea to emancipate the mind from mental slavery, drew directly on the words of Marcus Garvey; “One Love,” “Get Up, Stand Up,” and “Exodus” became anthems sung on every continent. Caught in the violent politics of Jamaica, he survived an assassination attempt and later brought rival leaders together on stage at a famous peace concert.

He died of cancer in 1981, only thirty-six years old, and was mourned as a prophet of his people; his greatest-hits collection has sold tens of millions of copies in the decades since.

At the heart of his Rastafari faith stood a living emperor whom the believers revered as the returned messiah, the ruler of Ethiopia—Haile Selassie.
