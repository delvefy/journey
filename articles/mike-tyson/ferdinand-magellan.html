Ferdinand Magellan

Portuguese explorer (c. 1480–1521)

Ferdinand Magellan led the first expedition to sail around the world, the supreme feat of the age of discovery. Born in Portugal of the minor nobility, he served the Portuguese crown in its wars and voyages in India and the East Indies before a quarrel with his king drove him to offer his services to Spain. There he won the backing of the young Emperor Charles V for a bold scheme: to reach the rich Spice Islands of the East by sailing west, around or through the newly found American continent.

In 1519 he set out with five ships and some two hundred and seventy men. He crossed the Atlantic, probed the coast of South America through bitter cold, crushed a mutiny, and at last discovered the stormy passage near the continent's tip that still bears his name—the Strait of Magellan. Beyond it lay an ocean so broad and calm that he called it the Pacific.

The crossing was a nightmare of starvation and scurvy, but the fleet reached the islands of the Philippines in 1521. There Magellan was drawn into a local war and killed in a skirmish on the shore of Mactan, never completing the voyage he had conceived.

One battered ship, the Victoria, struggled home to Spain in 1522 under Juan Sebastián Elcano, having circled the globe and proved the earth round and vast beyond imagining. Magellan's daring belonged to a tradition of Portuguese navigation opened by the captain who had first found the sea road to the East—Vasco da Gama.
