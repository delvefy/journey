Artaxerxes II

Achaemenid King of Persia (reigned 404–358 BC)

Artaxerxes II ruled the Persian empire for nearly half a century, the longest reign of any of its kings, through wars, rebellions, and the endless intrigues of his own family. His mother, the formidable queen Parysatis, schemed at his court, and his younger brother coveted his throne.

That brother, Cyrus the Younger, raised an army in Asia Minor—including ten thousand Greek mercenaries—and marched into the heart of the empire to seize the crown. At the Battle of Cunaxa in 401 BC the rebellion was crushed and Cyrus killed, but the stranded Greeks fought their way home across hostile lands in a march immortalized by Xenophon in the Anabasis.

Artaxerxes lost Egypt to rebellion, yet in the Greek world he proved a subtle master. He played the quarreling cities against one another, frustrated the Spartan king Agesilaus, and finally dictated the King's Peace, by which the Greeks themselves acknowledged the Great King as the arbiter of their affairs and surrendered the cities of Asia to him—a triumph of gold and diplomacy.

His long reign closed amid the great Revolt of the Satraps and the usual palace bloodshed, and Plutarch, drawing on court gossip, made him the subject of one of his Lives. The ruthless son who succeeded him, reconquered rebellious Egypt, and butchered his rivals to secure the throne was Artaxerxes III.
