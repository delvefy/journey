Louis XVIII

King of France (1755–1824)

Louis XVIII was born at Versailles in 1755, a grandson of Louis XV and the younger brother of the reigning Louis XVI. Known before the Revolution as the Count of Provence, he was an ambitious, bookish prince who chafed in the shadow of his brother and the queen, Marie Antoinette, and intrigued for influence at a court he could not command.

When revolution came he fled France in 1791 and became the chief figure among the royalist émigrés. After Louis XVI was guillotined in 1793 and the imprisoned boy-king Louis XVII died in 1795, the Count of Provence proclaimed himself king as Louis XVIII, though he held no territory and wandered for two decades through the courts of Europe—Italy, Russia, and finally England.

His chance came only with the fall of Napoleon Bonaparte. Restored to the throne in 1814, he granted the Charter of 1814, a constitutional compromise that preserved many gains of the Revolution under a Bourbon crown. When Napoleon returned for the Hundred Days in 1815, Louis fled again, and was set back on his throne only after the victory at Waterloo of the Duke of Wellington.

His second reign was a cautious balancing act between liberals and vengeful ultraroyalists. He looked back to the grandeur of his ancestor Louis XIV but accepted that the absolute monarchy could not simply be revived. Childless and gout-ridden, he died in 1824, the only French king of his era to die on the throne, and was succeeded by his brother Charles X.
