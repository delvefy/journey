Vivien Leigh

British actress, twice an Academy Award winner (1913–1967)

Vivien Leigh was one of the most beautiful and gifted actresses of the classic cinema, remembered above all for two of the most demanding roles ever written for a woman. Born in British India and raised in England, she fought her way through a famous and exhausting talent search to win the part of Scarlett O'Hara in Gone with the Wind, the lavish 1939 epic of the American South, and her fiery, willful Scarlett brought her instant immortality and the first of her two Academy Awards.

Twelve years later she won the second for an utterly different role, the fragile, doomed Blanche DuBois in the film of Tennessee Williams's A Streetcar Named Desire, opposite a young actor whose raw new style was about to change the art of screen acting forever.

She was also a distinguished stage actress, often appearing alongside her husband, the great Laurence Olivier, with whom she formed the most glamorous theatrical partnership of the age. But behind the radiance lay great suffering: she battled bipolar disorder and recurrent tuberculosis, illnesses that strained her marriage, interrupted her career, and at last took her life at only fifty-three.

Her co-star in A Streetcar Named Desire, the actor who overturned every convention of screen performance, was Marlon Brando.
