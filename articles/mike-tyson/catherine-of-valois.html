Catherine of Valois

Queen of England, matriarch of the Tudors (1401–1437)

Catherine of Valois, born in 1401, was the daughter of the mad king Charles VI of France and Isabeau of Bavaria, and grew up amid the chaos of her father's reign and the English invasions. Her hand became one of the great prizes of the Hundred Years' War. After his victory at Agincourt, Henry V of England demanded her in marriage, and by the Treaty of Troyes in 1420 the match was sealed: Catherine wed Henry, and her husband was recognised as heir to her father's throne.

The marriage was brief. In 1421 Catherine bore a son, the future Henry VI, who would be proclaimed king of both England and France. But within months Henry V died on campaign, leaving Catherine a widow at twenty-one, mother of an infant king and a stranger in a foreign court suspicious of her influence.

It was then that she made the most consequential choice of her life. Quietly, and without the council's leave, she formed a union with a Welsh squire of her household, Owen Tudor. The couple had several children before the relationship came to light, and Catherine spent her last years in retirement, dying in 1437.

In her own time the marriage was a curiosity that produced little stir. Yet from it sprang a dynasty: her grandson by Owen Tudor, Henry Tudor, would seize the English crown in 1485 as Henry VII. Thus Catherine of Valois—daughter, wife, and mother of kings—became also, through her humble second marriage, the matriarch of the royal house of Tudor.
