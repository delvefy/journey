Zhou Enlai

First Premier of the People's Republic of China (1898–1976)

Zhou Enlai was the indispensable statesman of Communist China, its first premier from the founding of the People's Republic in 1949 until his death, and for much of that time its foreign minister as well. A founding leader of the party and a master administrator, he served as the loyal and stabilizing number two to Mao Zedong through every upheaval, surviving purges that destroyed others by his discretion, diligence, and skill.

Abroad he was the urbane face of the revolution, admired even by adversaries for his charm and intelligence. He led China at the Geneva and Bandung conferences, championing peaceful coexistence and solidarity among the newly independent nations, and in 1972 he engineered the astonishing opening to the United States, hosting an American president in Beijing.

At home, during the madness of the Cultural Revolution, he worked quietly to limit the damage—protecting officials, scholars, and historic treasures from the Red Guards, and shielding his old comrade Deng Xiaoping so that he might one day return. So beloved was he that the public mourning at his death in 1976 swelled into a spontaneous protest against the radicals around Mao.

The American president whose historic visit to China in 1972 he hosted and helped to broker was Richard Nixon.
