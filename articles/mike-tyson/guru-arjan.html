Guru Arjan

Fifth Sikh Guru, compiler of the Adi Granth, first Sikh martyr (1563–1606)

Guru Arjan, the fifth Sikh Guru, gave the young faith two of its most enduring foundations: its central shrine and its scripture. He completed the building of the Harmandir Sahib at Amritsar—the Golden Temple—setting it low, with doors opening to all four directions, so that people of every caste and creed might enter freely.

His greatest work was the compilation, in 1604, of the Adi Granth, the first authorized book of Sikh scripture. Into it he gathered the hymns of Guru Nanak and the Gurus who followed, adding to them the verses of revered saints of other backgrounds, among them the mystic Kabir—a scripture deliberately woven from many strands of devotion. He enshrined the completed volume in the heart of the Golden Temple.

Under his leadership the Sikh community grew greatly in numbers and organization, becoming a power that drew the attention, and the suspicion, of the Mughal state. When Guru Arjan was thought to have given shelter and blessing to a rebellious prince, the emperor ordered his arrest.

He was subjected to brutal torture and died in 1606, the first of the Sikh Gurus to be martyred. His death transformed the faith: his son took up arms and began to arm the community for its own defence. The Mughal emperor under whose order he was seized and killed, the son of Akbar, was Jahangir.
