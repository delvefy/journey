George III

King of Great Britain (1738–1820)

George III, who reigned from 1760 to 1820, was the British king whose long rule spanned the loss of one empire and the life-and-death struggle against another. The first of his Hanoverian line to be born in Britain and to speak English as his native tongue, he came to the throne determined to play an active part in government.

His reign is forever marked by the American Revolution. The taxes his ministers imposed on the thirteen colonies provoked resistance, then rebellion, and in 1776 the colonists declared their independence, branding the king a tyrant in the Declaration itself. Led by George Washington and aided by France, they won their freedom, and Britain lost the heart of its first empire.

Yet George III also reigned through the French Revolution and the titanic wars against Revolutionary and Napoleonic France—the era of Nelson's Trafalgar and Wellington's Waterloo—during which Britain stood as Napoleon's most implacable foe. A frugal, pious family man fond of farming, he was nicknamed "Farmer George."

His later years were darkened by a recurring mental illness that became permanent in 1810, forcing the appointment of his son as Prince Regent. Blind, deaf, and deranged, the old king died in 1820, his afflictions later dramatized in the play and film The Madness of King George. He left a Britain transformed by revolution abroad and industry at home.
