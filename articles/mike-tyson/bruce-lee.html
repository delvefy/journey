Bruce Lee

Martial artist and actor (1940–1973)

Bruce Lee was the martial artist and actor who introduced kung fu to the world and became, in a handful of films and a tragically short life, a global icon.

Born in San Francisco and raised in Hong Kong, he trained in Wing Chun under the master Ip Man before forging his own fighting philosophy, Jeet Kune Do, built on adaptability and economy of motion—“be water,” he urged. In the United States he taught martial arts to celebrity students, among them the actor Steve McQueen and the basketball giant Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, whom he later cast as his towering opponent in Game of Death.

Frustrated by the limited roles offered an Asian actor in Hollywood, he returned to Hong Kong and made a string of electrifying films—The Big Boss, Fist of Fury, and The Way of the Dragon, with its famous Colosseum duel against Chuck Norris—before the Hollywood co-production Enter the Dragon. He died suddenly in 1973, only thirty-two, weeks before that film made him a worldwide sensation, his legend sealed by his early death.

He left a void in martial-arts cinema that one performer above all would fill, a former bit-part stuntman in Lee's own films who reinvented the genre with acrobatic comedy—Jackie Chan.
