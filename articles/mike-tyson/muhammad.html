Muhammad

Prophet and founder of Islam (c. 570–632 AD)

Muhammad, born around 570 AD in Mecca into the Quraysh tribe, is revered by Muslims as the final prophet of God and the founder of Islam. Orphaned in childhood and raised by kinsmen, he grew up to be a respected merchant, so trusted that he was nicknamed al-Amin, "the trustworthy." He married Khadija, a wealthy widow who became his first and steadfast supporter.

According to Islamic tradition, around the year 610, while meditating in a cave near Mecca, Muhammad received the first of many revelations from God through the angel Gabriel. Gathered over the following two decades, these recitations became the Quran. He began to preach an uncompromising monotheism—submission, islam, to the one God—in a city devoted to many idols, and he and his followers met fierce hostility.

In 622 the community migrated to the city of Medina, an event called the Hijra that marks the start of the Islamic calendar. There Muhammad became not only a religious teacher but the leader of a growing polity, guiding his followers through years of conflict with Mecca. In 630 he entered Mecca in triumph and cleared the sacred shrine of the Kaaba of its idols.

By his death in 632 he had united much of Arabia under the new faith. Muslims regard him as the last in a line of prophets that includes Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, and his teachings launched a religion and civilization that swiftly reshaped much of the world.
