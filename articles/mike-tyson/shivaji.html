Shivaji

Founder of the Maratha Empire (1630–1680)

Shivaji Bhonsale, born in 1630 at a hill fort in the western Deccan, founded the Maratha Empire and became one of the most celebrated figures in Indian history. The son of a Deccan general, he began in his teens to seize forts and carve out an independent Hindu kingdom in defiance of the mighty Sultanate of Bijapur and the Mughal Empire of Aurangzeb.

He was a military innovator of genius. Mastering the rugged terrain of the Western Ghats, he pioneered fast-moving guerrilla warfare, built and held a network of hill forts, and even created one of the first Indian naval forces to guard his coast. His exploits became legend: the deadly face-to-face encounter in which he killed the Bijapur general Afzal Khan with concealed claw-weapons, his audacious escape from Aurangzeb's court at Agra hidden in a basket, and his bold raid on the rich Mughal port of Surat.

In 1674 he had himself crowned Chhatrapati, a sovereign king, founding a Maratha state built on able administration, religious tolerance, and the promotion of the Marathi language. His tolerance extended to protecting mosques and the dignity of captured women, earning admiration even from foes.

Shivaji died in 1680, but the empire he founded grew into the dominant power of eighteenth-century India. Centuries later his memory was revived as a rallying symbol of Indian self-rule, taken up by nationalist leaders such as Bal Gangadhar Tilak.
