Marcus Garvey

Jamaican Pan-Africanist and Black nationalist leader (1887–1940)

Marcus Garvey was the most powerful prophet of Black pride and Pan-African unity in the early twentieth century. Born in Jamaica, he founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association, which grew into the largest mass movement of people of African descent the world had ever seen, with branches across the Americas, the Caribbean, and Africa. He told millions that they were the heirs of a great civilization and should take pride in their race, build their own institutions, and look toward a redeemed Africa.

To bind the scattered diaspora together he launched the Black Star Line, a Black-owned shipping company; its collapse led to a conviction for mail fraud and his deportation from the United States, where he had built his movement. His rivalry with the scholar W. E. B. Du Bois split Black America between competing visions of advancement.

His influence outran his lifetime. His call to “look to Africa” for a coming king helped inspire the Rastafari faith, which reveres him as a prophet; his nationalism shaped the parents of Malcolm X and the leaders of African independence.

And his cry to emancipate the mind from mental slavery was carried, a generation after his death, to every corner of the earth in the songs of his fellow Jamaican—Bob Marley.
