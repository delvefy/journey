Pedro Calderón de la Barca

Spanish dramatist of the Golden Age (1600–1681)

Pedro Calderón de la Barca was the last great master of the Spanish Golden Age theatre, the figure who gave the popular drama of his predecessors its most polished and philosophical form. Born in Madrid and schooled by the Jesuits, he studied at Salamanca, served as a soldier in the king's campaigns, and rose to become the leading court dramatist of Philip IV, for whom he devised spectacular entertainments at the Buen Retiro palace. He was knighted into the Order of Santiago.

His masterpiece is La vida es sueño—Life Is a Dream—in which the imprisoned prince Segismundo, freed and then deceived into believing his liberty a dream, wrestles with fate, free will, and the illusory nature of power and existence. He also wrote the sturdy honour-drama The Mayor of Zalamea and the tense The Surgeon of His Honour.

Ordained a priest in 1651, Calderón turned increasingly to the auto sacramental, the allegorical one-act play performed at Corpus Christi, of which The Great Theatre of the World is the most celebrated. Where Lope de Vega had been spontaneous and abundant, Calderón was architectural, symbolic, and severe.

Long after his death in 1681 his reputation soared anew among the German Romantics, above all Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, who admired and staged his work at Weimar and ranked him among the supreme poets of the theatre. Calderón wrote at the court of Philip IV, the same court served by Spain's greatest painter, Diego Velázquez.
