Cnut the Great

King of England, Denmark and Norway (c. 995–1035)

Cnut the Great, born around 995, was a Danish prince who became the most powerful ruler in northern Europe—king of England, Denmark and Norway, the master of a great "North Sea Empire." He was the son of the Viking king Sweyn Forkbeard, who had conquered England in 1013. When Sweyn died and the English restored their king Æthelred the Unready, the young Cnut returned, and after Æthelred's death he fought the late king's son Edmund Ironside for the crown. On Edmund's death in 1016, Cnut took all England.

To secure his new kingdom he married Emma of Normandy, Æthelred's widow, binding the old dynasty to the new. Though he had seized power by the sword, Cnut ruled England not as a foreign tyrant but as a wise and Christian king. He kept the peace, upheld English law and the Church, and won the loyalty of his subjects, dividing the land into great earldoms.

The most famous story told of him captures his character. To rebuke flattering courtiers who claimed he could command anything, Cnut had his throne set on the seashore and ordered the rising tide to halt—and when it soaked him, he declared that the power of kings was empty beside the power of God. Whether true or not, the tale made him a byword for humble wisdom.

Cnut died in 1035, and his empire did not long outlive him; his sons reigned briefly, and in time the old English line returned in the person of his stepson, Edward the Confessor. But for a generation Cnut had ruled an empire spanning the northern seas, the greatest of the Viking kings to sit an English throne.
