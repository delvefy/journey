Chand Bibi

Warrior-queen and regent of the Deccan (c. 1550–1599)

Chand Bibi was a princess of the Ahmadnagar Sultanate who became one of the most celebrated women warriors of Indian history, remembered above all for her defence of the Deccan against the Mughal empire of Akbar.

She was a daughter of Hussain Nizam Shah I of Ahmadnagar, and was married to Ali Adil Shah I of the neighbouring Sultanate of Bijapur, becoming its queen. When her husband died without an heir, she acted as regent for her young nephew Ibrahim Adil Shah II, steering Bijapur through the intrigues of its nobles and the rivalries of the Deccan courts.

Returning to her native Ahmadnagar in its hour of crisis, she took up the regency for her grand-nephew when the armies of Akbar, led by his son Prince Murad, laid siege to the capital in 1595. Her conduct during that siege made her legend: when the besiegers breached the walls, she is said to have appeared in armour, sword in hand, rallying the defenders and directing the breach to be repaired through the night. The Mughals were forced to negotiate, and she agreed to cede the province of Berar to buy peace.

The respite was brief. When the Mughals returned, the defence faltered amid suspicion and disunity within the fortress. In 1599 Chand Bibi was killed by her own soldiers, who turned on her in the belief that she meant to surrender, and Ahmadnagar soon fell.

Cultured as well as courageous—she was said to know several languages and to paint and play music—Chand Bibi became a lasting symbol of resistance, and the cause she upheld was carried on after her by the regent Malik Ambar.
