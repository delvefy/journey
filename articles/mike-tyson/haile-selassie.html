Haile Selassie

Emperor of Ethiopia (1892–1975)

Haile Selassie was the last emperor of Ethiopia and the final ruler of the ancient Solomonic dynasty, which traced its line—and the legitimacy of its crown—back to King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. Born Tafari Makonnen and known before his coronation as Ras Tafari, he gave his name to a faith that would arise far away in the Caribbean. Styled the “Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah,” he set about dragging his ancient realm toward the modern world with a constitution, schools, and the abolition of slavery.

His reign was defined by a great trial. When Fascist Italy invaded Ethiopia in 1935, he led the doomed resistance and then carried his people's cause to the League of Nations in Geneva, warning the assembled powers that if they abandoned Ethiopia, the same fate awaited them all—a prophecy soon fulfilled. Driven into exile, he was restored to his throne in 1941 as the Allied tide turned.

In his later years he became an elder statesman of a liberating Africa, helping to found the Organization of African Unity in his capital, while half a world away the Rastafari revered him as a living god. But famine, stagnation, and discontent undid him: a Marxist military junta deposed him in 1974, and he died, a prisoner, the next year.

The fascist dictator whose invasion drove him into exile and tested the conscience of the world was Benito Mussolini.
