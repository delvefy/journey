Mary I of England

Queen of England, "Bloody Mary" (1516–1558)

Mary I was the first woman to rule England in her own right and the queen who tried, with fire and conviction, to drag her country back to the Catholic faith. The daughter of Henry VIII and his first wife, Catherine of Aragon, she spent a happy childhood as a beloved princess—until her father set her mother aside to marry Anne Boleyn. Mary was declared illegitimate, separated from her mother, and pressured to renounce her religion, an ordeal that left her devout and unbending.

After the brief reign of her Protestant half-brother Edward VI and the nine-day usurpation of Lady Jane Grey, Mary swept to the throne in 1553 on a wave of popular support. She at once moved to restore the authority of the pope and to undo the English Reformation. Her marriage to Philip II of Spain, the great Catholic champion, was deeply unpopular and produced no child, despite agonizing false pregnancies.

Determined to root out heresy, she had nearly three hundred Protestants burned at the stake, among them the reforming Archbishop Thomas Cranmer. These executions earned her the lasting name "Bloody Mary." Her reign brought further humiliation when England lost Calais, its last foothold in France.

Childless and broken in health, Mary died in 1558, her Catholic restoration destined to be swept away by her Protestant half-sister and successor, Elizabeth I. The woman whose marriage to her father had cast Mary into disgrace, and altered the course of her life, was Anne Boleyn.
