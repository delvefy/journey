Firdawsi

Persian poet, author of the Shahnameh (c. 940–1020)

Abu'l-Qasim Firdawsi, born near Tus in Khorasan around 940, is the national poet of Iran and the author of the Shahnameh, the "Book of Kings," the longest epic ever composed by a single hand. A landowner of the old Persian gentry, he gave some thirty years of his life to the poem, working in growing poverty as the language and traditions of pre-Islamic Persia receded around him.

The Shahnameh runs to nearly fifty thousand rhyming couplets and tells the whole sweep of Iranian kingship: from the first mythical sovereigns, through the legendary Kayanian dynasty and the towering hero Rostam, down to the historical Sasanian kings and their fall before the armies of Islam. Firdawsi built upon an earlier prose compilation and took up the unfinished verse of the poet Daqiqi, weaving a thousand of his lines into the work.

The poem was dedicated to Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni, the great conqueror at whose court the poet hoped for reward. Tradition holds that Mahmud promised a gold piece for every couplet but sent silver instead; the proud Firdawsi gave the money away and, it is said, composed a bitter satire against the king. He died around 1020, by legend unrewarded, and was buried at Tus.

His achievement was to fix the Persian language and the memory of its kings in a single monument. He sang the deeds of figures from the just Sasanian Khosrow I to the world-conqueror Alexander, whom Persian tradition had adopted as one of its own. Written almost wholly in pure Persian, the epic is credited with carrying the language through centuries of Arabic ascendancy.

The Shahnameh became the foundation of all later Persian literature, recited and lavishly illustrated for a thousand years, and Firdawsi endures as the supreme national poet of the Persian-speaking world.
