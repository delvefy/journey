John Adams

Second President of the United States (1735–1826)

John Adams was a leading champion of American independence and the second President of the United States. A Massachusetts lawyer of fierce integrity, he first won notice by defending the British soldiers tried after the Boston Massacre, insisting that even the unpopular deserved a fair trial. In the Continental Congress he became the foremost advocate of separation from Britain, nominating George Washington to command the army and pressing the cause that produced the Declaration of Independence.

He spent the war years as a diplomat in Europe, winning loans and recognition in the Netherlands and joining Benjamin Franklin in negotiating the Treaty of Paris that secured American independence. Returning home, he served two terms as Washington's vice president before his own election as president in 1796.

His single term was stormy: an undeclared naval war with France, the divisive Alien and Sedition Acts, and a damaging rift with fellow Federalist Alexander Hamilton. Adams counted it his proudest achievement that he kept the country out of a full war. Defeated for reelection by Thomas Jefferson in 1800, he retired to his farm.

There he and Jefferson, estranged rivals, rekindled a magnificent late friendship in letters, and by a stroke of history both died on the same day, the fiftieth anniversary of independence in 1826. His son, John Quincy Adams, would follow him into the presidency.
