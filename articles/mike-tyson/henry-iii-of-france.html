Henry III of France

Last Valois king of France (1551–1589)

Henry III was born in 1551, the favourite son of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici. As a young prince he won military renown commanding royal armies against the Huguenots, and in 1573 his reputation helped secure his election as King of Poland. He had scarcely arrived in Kraków when the death of his brother Charles IX called him home, and he slipped away to claim the throne of France in 1574.

His reign was consumed by the Wars of Religion. Cultivated, devout and devoted to a circle of court favourites, Henry found himself trapped between the Protestant party of Henry of Navarre—the future Henry IV—and the powerful Catholic League led by Henry, Duke of Guise. This three-cornered struggle became known as the War of the Three Henrys.

When the League and the Guises grew strong enough to drive him from Paris in 1588, the king struck back ruthlessly, having the Duke of Guise and his brother the cardinal murdered at the Château de Blois. The deed appalled Catholic France and isolated him, so that he allied at last with Henry of Navarre to recover his capital.

That alliance never bore fruit for him. In August 1589, as his army besieged Paris, a fanatical friar named Jacques Clément stabbed him to death. Dying childless, Henry III was the last of the Valois; the crown passed to Henry of Navarre and the new Bourbon dynasty.
