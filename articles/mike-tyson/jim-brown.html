Jim Brown

American football player, actor and activist (1936–2023)

Jim Brown was the greatest running back in the history of American football, a figure of such force on the field and off that his fame far outlived his short career.

Playing for the Cleveland Browns from 1957 to 1965, he led the league in rushing in nearly every season and dominated the sport as few athletes ever have—then walked away at the height of his powers, only twenty-nine, to become a movie star in films like The Dirty Dozen. He was part of the famous Black cultural circle that gathered around Sam Cooke and the young Muhammad Ali, and he absorbed the militancy of Malcolm X.

His greatest act of leadership came off the field. In 1967 he convened the “Cleveland Summit,” gathering the era's leading Black athletes to stand publicly with Muhammad Ali after Ali refused induction into the army. A founder of Black economic-empowerment efforts, he remained a forceful, sometimes controversial public figure until his death in 2023.

Among the young athletes Jim Brown summoned to that historic summit—then a college basketball phenomenon, soon to become the most prolific scorer in the history of his sport—was Kareem Abdul-Jabbar.
