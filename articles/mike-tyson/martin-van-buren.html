Martin Van Buren

Eighth President of the United States (1782–1862)

Martin Van Buren was the eighth president and the consummate political organiser of his age, the man who did more than any other to build the modern American party system. Born to a Dutch-speaking family in Kinderhook, New York, he rose through the rough politics of his state to create a disciplined machine, and then a national party, the Democrats, as the heir to the principles of Thomas Jefferson.

His genius for maneuver earned him the nicknames the Little Magician and the Red Fox. He hitched his fortunes to Andrew Jackson, serving as his Secretary of State and then vice president and emerging as his chosen successor—outmaneuvering his rival John C. Calhoun for the prize.

Elected president in 1836, he had the misfortune to take office just as the Panic of 1837 plunged the country into a long depression that defined and doomed his term. He created an independent treasury but could not revive prosperity, and in 1840 he was swept from office by William Henry Harrison's rollicking log-cabin campaign.

He sought the Democratic nomination again in 1844 but lost it over his refusal to back the annexation of Texas, and in 1848 he ran on the antislavery Free Soil ticket. He died at Kinderhook in 1862. The man who had wrested the 1844 nomination from him was James K. Polk.
