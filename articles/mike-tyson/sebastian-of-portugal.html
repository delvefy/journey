Sebastian of Portugal

King of Portugal, "the Desired" (1554–1578)

Sebastian of Portugal, called the Desired, was the young king whose reckless crusade in Morocco destroyed himself, his army, and at length the independence of his country.

He came to the throne as a child of three on the death of his grandfather John III, and grew up under the regency of his grandmother and his great-uncle, schooled by the Jesuits in a burning religious zeal. Frail in body but fierce in spirit, he dreamed above all of a holy war against the Muslim powers of North Africa, and he could not be turned from it.

In 1578 he led a great expedition across the strait to restore a deposed claimant to the Moroccan throne. At Alcácer Quibir his host met the army of the reigning sultan in a battle so deadly that it is remembered as the Battle of the Three Kings, for all three royal leaders perished on the field. The Portuguese army was annihilated, and Sebastian himself was killed, his body never surely identified afterwards.

He left no heir, and his death opened a fatal crisis. His aged great-uncle Cardinal Henry reigned briefly, and when he too died in 1580, the powerful Philip II of Spain—Sebastian's uncle, grandson like him of the emperor Charles V—seized the vacant crown, beginning sixty years of Spanish rule over Portugal.

So strong was the longing for him that a legend grew, called Sebastianism, that the lost king would one day return. The throne he had inherited had been his grandfather's, John III.
