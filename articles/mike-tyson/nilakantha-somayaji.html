Nilakantha Somayaji

Astronomer of the Kerala school (1444–1544)

Nilakantha Somayaji was the most influential astronomer of the Kerala school after its founder, Madhava, and by tradition lived to the age of a hundred. Born into a Brahmin family of Kerala, he studied under Damodara, in a line of teachers reaching back to Madhava himself, and became the school's great systematizer.

His chief work, the Tantrasamgraha, completed in 1501, gathered and refined the mathematical astronomy of his predecessors, setting out Madhava's infinite series in polished form. He also wrote a long and searching commentary on the Aryabhatiya, the Aryabhatiya-bhasya, which preserved much of the school's reasoning and defended the methods of Aryabhata.

Nilakantha's boldest contribution was to planetary theory. He proposed a revised model in which the five planets—Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn—circle the Sun, while the Sun in turn moves around the Earth. This geo-heliocentric scheme, reached a century before Tycho Brahe arrived at a similar arrangement in Europe, gave a far better account of the planets' motions than the older systems.

He was at once a patient observer and a rigorous mathematician, insisting that astronomical constants be corrected against observation and that results be demonstrated rather than merely asserted. His teaching shaped the later course of the Kerala school.

The proofs and series gathered by Nilakantha passed into the celebrated Yuktibhasha of his successor Jyeshtadeva, through which the achievements of Kerala mathematics were most fully recorded. Nilakantha stands among the foremost astronomers of pre-modern India.
