Kareem Abdul-Jabbar

American basketball legend (born 1947)

Kareem Abdul-Jabbar was one of the greatest basketball players in history, the unstoppable center whose “skyhook” made him professional basketball's all-time leading scorer for nearly forty years.

Born Lew Alcindor in New York, he dominated college basketball before converting to Islam and taking his new name. In the NBA he won championships with the Milwaukee Bucks and then, in the dazzling “Showtime” era alongside Magic Johnson, with the Los Angeles Lakers—six titles and six Most Valuable Player awards in all. He was also a committed activist, drawn as a young man into the orbit of Jim Brown's Cleveland Summit, and in retirement a prolific author and public intellectual.

He took his place in the pantheon of the game's titans, his scoring record standing until the era of Michael Jordan and LeBron James—and it was James who at last surpassed his mark in 2023, with the old champion looking on. Kareem stood astride the sport for two decades.

Beyond basketball, he was a devoted student of the martial arts, training under and befriending the master whose dazzling philosophy and films made kung fu a global phenomenon—and with whom he filmed an unforgettable screen duel. That master was Bruce Lee.
