Elizabeth II

Queen of the United Kingdom (1926–2022)

Elizabeth II was Queen of the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth realms for over seventy years, the longest reign in British history and one of the most familiar lives of the modern age. Born in 1926, she was not expected to rule until the abdication of her uncle Edward VIII in 1936 made her father King George VI and her his heir. As a young princess during the Second World War she trained as a driver and mechanic, and in 1947 she married Prince Philip.

She acceded to the throne in 1952, on her father's death, and her first Prime Minister was the aging Winston Churchill. Over the decades she would work with fifteen prime ministers in all, reigning through the dismantling of the British Empire, the rise of the modern Commonwealth, the Cold War, and sweeping social change.

Scrupulously neutral in politics and devoted to her sense of duty, Elizabeth became a global symbol of stability and continuity. Her reign was not without storms—the marital troubles of her children, the death of Princess Diana in 1997, and recurring family scandals tested the monarchy—yet her personal standing endured, marked by Golden, Diamond, and Platinum Jubilees.

She died in 2022 at Balmoral, having reigned across the lifetimes of most of her subjects, and was succeeded by her son, Charles III. Her image, on stamps and coins across the world, made her perhaps the most widely depicted person in history.
