Ruhollah Khomeini

Supreme Leader of Iran (1902–1989)

Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini was the Shia cleric who led the Iranian Revolution of 1979 and founded the Islamic Republic, becoming the first Supreme Leader of a new theocratic state.

A respected scholar in the holy city of Qom, he emerged in the 1960s as the most uncompromising religious critic of the Shah's pro-Western, secularizing rule. Arrested and driven into a long exile, he developed and preached the doctrine that an Islamic jurist should hold supreme authority over the state. From abroad his recorded sermons stoked a mass movement, and when the Shah fled in early 1979 Khomeini returned to a delirious welcome and swiftly made himself the unchallenged ruler of Iran.

His rule was uncompromising at home and abroad. He blessed the students who seized the American embassy and held its staff hostage, wrecking the presidency of Jimmy Carter, while covert American arms dealings during the Reagan years would later erupt into the Iran-Contra scandal. He crushed his domestic rivals without mercy, and in 1989 issued a notorious religious edict calling for the death of the novelist Salman Rushdie.

The defining ordeal of his reign was a long and ruinous war that consumed Iran for eight years, costing hundreds of thousands of lives before he reluctantly accepted a ceasefire he likened to drinking poison. He died in 1989, mourned by vast and frenzied crowds. The Iraqi dictator who had launched that war against him was Saddam Hussein.
