Michael Jackson

American singer and dancer, the “King of Pop” (1958–2009)

Michael Jackson was one of the most famous entertainers who ever lived. He began as a child prodigy, the electrifying lead voice of the Jackson 5, his family group on the Motown label, mentored by Diana Ross. As a solo artist he reached heights no performer had touched: his 1982 album Thriller, made with the producer Quincy Jones, became the best-selling record in history, and its successors Bad and Dangerous sold in the tens of millions.

He transformed popular culture as much by sight as by sound. His short films—“Thriller,” “Billie Jean,” “Beat It”—turned the music video into an art form, and his dancing, crowned by the gliding “moonwalk,” made him the most imitated performer on earth. He used his fame for charity, co-writing the famine-relief anthem “We Are the World.”

Yet his later life grew strange and troubled: an ever-changing appearance, the fantasy world of his Neverland ranch, and grave allegations of child abuse, of which he was acquitted at trial but which shadowed his name. In 2009, rehearsing for a comeback, he died of an overdose of a powerful anaesthetic given by his physician, and the world reacted with an outpouring of grief.

Through the storms of his celebrity he kept a few steadfast friends. The closest among them, who stood by him publicly through his trials, was the screen legend Elizabeth Taylor.
