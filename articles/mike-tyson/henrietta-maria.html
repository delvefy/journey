Henrietta Maria

Queen of England, Scotland and Ireland (1609–1669)

Henrietta Maria was born in Paris in 1609, the youngest daughter of King Henry IV of France and his queen, Marie de' Medici. In 1625, at the age of fifteen, she was married to the new English king, Charles I. Her Roman Catholic faith made the union deeply unpopular in Protestant England, and the early years of the marriage were cold; but after the death of Charles's favourite, the Duke of Buckingham, the royal couple grew genuinely devoted to one another.

As queen she presided over a brilliant and cultivated court, commissioning masques, patronising painters such as Anthony van Dyck, and exercising growing influence over her husband. To his Puritan opponents, her Catholicism and her hold over the king became symbols of all they feared, and helped poison the politics that slid toward civil war.

When war came she threw herself into the royalist cause, pawning the crown jewels abroad to buy arms and crossing the Channel with men and money, until Parliament impeached her and she fled to France in 1644. There she learned of her husband's execution by the regime of Oliver Cromwell in 1649.

She lived out long years of exile at the French court, mother of two future kings, Charles II and James II. She returned briefly to England after the Restoration of 1660 before retiring once more to France, where she died in 1669. The American colony of Maryland preserves her memory.
