Cassander

King of Macedonia (c. 355–297 BC)

Cassander was a son of Antipater, the trusted regent whom Alexander had left to govern Macedonia, and he became one of the most ruthless of the Diadochi who tore apart the conqueror's empire. Alexander is said to have disliked him intensely, and after the king's death Cassander pursued power with a cold determination that spared no one of the royal house.

Passed over when his father named another general as regent, Cassander fought his way to mastery of Macedonia and Greece, taking Athens and dominating the Greek cities. To secure his throne he set about destroying what remained of Alexander's family: he had the king's mother put to death, then imprisoned and killed Alexander's widow Roxana and their young son Alexander IV, and finally arranged the murder of the last son, Heracles—extinguishing the Argead line root and branch.

Yet he was a builder as well as a killer. He married Alexander's half-sister and founded in her honor the great city of Thessalonica, and he rebuilt Thebes, which Alexander had razed. With Lysimachus, Seleucus, and Ptolemy he joined the coalition that broke the most dangerous of their rivals at the battle of Ipsus, and he took for himself the title of king.

The most celebrated of all the royal victims he destroyed was the formidable queen who had borne and shaped Alexander himself, captured and condemned by Cassander—Olympias.
