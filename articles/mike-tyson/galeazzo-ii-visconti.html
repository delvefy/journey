Galeazzo II Visconti

Lord of Pavia and co-ruler of Milan (c. 1320–1378)

Galeazzo II Visconti shared the great Visconti inheritance with his brothers, taking the western lands and making his capital at Pavia while the fierce Bernabò ruled Milan and the east. If he lacked his brother's terrible reputation, he matched him in ambition and surpassed him in taste, building one of the most splendid courts of fourteenth-century Italy.

He was, above all, a patron of learning. He drew the great poet Petrarch into his service, giving him a house in Milan and sending him on embassies as the ornament and voice of the Visconti name. In 1361 he founded the University of Pavia, an institution that would outlast his dynasty by centuries, and he raised at Pavia a vast and luxurious castle admired across Europe.

Galeazzo understood that marriages were worth more than armies. He bound his house to the thrones of the continent, most dazzlingly in 1368, when he wed his daughter Violante to Lionel of Antwerp, a son of King Edward III of England, in a Milanese feast of legendary extravagance. His son Gian Galeazzo he married to a princess of France.

Plagued in his last years by gout, he died in 1378 and left his portion to that son, who would one day reunite all the Visconti lands and make himself Duke of Milan. The English prince who came to Pavia to marry Galeazzo's daughter, and who died in Italy only months after the wedding, was Lionel of Antwerp.
