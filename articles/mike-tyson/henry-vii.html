Henry VII

First Tudor king of England (1457–1509)

Henry VII, born in 1457, was the son of Edmund Tudor and the formidable Lady Margaret Beaufort, through whom he carried a slender Lancastrian claim to the English throne. The Wars of the Roses scattered his family; Henry spent fourteen years in exile in Brittany and France while the house of York ruled. The disappearance of the princes in the Tower and the growing unpopularity of Richard III at last gave the obscure exile his chance.

In 1485 Henry landed in Wales, gathered support, and met Richard at Bosworth Field. Richard was killed in the fighting, his crown reputedly plucked from a thornbush and set on Henry's head. The new king dated his reign from the day before the battle, so that Richard's supporters could be branded traitors, and secured his position by marrying Elizabeth of York, daughter of Edward IV, uniting the warring roses.

His reign was a long exercise in consolidation. He crushed the pretenders Lambert Simnel and Perkin Warbeck, who claimed to be Yorkist heirs, and bound the great nobles with bonds and fines rather than trusting their loyalty. Through careful ministers he filled the treasury until the crown was solvent and feared, building the machinery of strong monarchy that his successors would inherit.

Henry wove his dynasty into Europe by marriage: his elder son Arthur to Catherine of Aragon, his daughter Margaret to James IV of Scotland—a match that would one day bring the Stuarts to the English throne. When Arthur died young, the succession passed to his second son. Henry VII died in 1509, leaving a secure crown and a full treasury to that son, the young Henry VIII.
