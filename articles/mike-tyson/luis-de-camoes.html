Luís de Camões

National poet of Portugal (c. 1524–1580)

Luís de Camões is the greatest poet of the Portuguese language and the author of its national epic, Os Lusíadas, a work that crowned the age of discovery in verse.

Born to a family of minor nobility, he led a stormy youth at the court of Lisbon, from which he was banished after a street brawl. He served as a soldier in North Africa, where he lost his right eye, and then sailed east to Portuguese India. For years he wandered and fought across the East, from Goa to Macau on the coast of China, and by tradition he survived a shipwreck near the Mekong by swimming ashore with the manuscript of his poem held above the water.

Returning at last to Lisbon, he published Os Lusíadas in 1572. Modelled on the epics of Homer and on the Aeneid of Virgil, it takes for its hero the navigator Vasco da Gama and his voyage to India, and around that voyage it weaves the whole history of Portugal—among its most famous passages the lament for the murdered Inês de Castro. He dedicated the work to the young king Sebastian, in whom he placed the hopes of his nation.

For all his genius, Camões won little reward and died in poverty in 1580, in the very year before Portugal lost its independence to Spain. He has since become the symbol of his country and its language; the day of his death is kept as Portugal's national day.
