Queen Victoria

Queen of the United Kingdom (1819–1901)

Queen Victoria reigned over the United Kingdom for nearly sixty-four years, from 1837 to 1901, lending her name to an entire age. She came to the throne at eighteen, and in 1840 married her cousin Prince Albert, in a devoted partnership that produced nine children and profoundly shaped her reign. Albert, a champion of industry, science, and the arts who masterminded the Great Exhibition of 1851, was her closest counsellor until his early death in 1861, after which the grieving queen withdrew into seclusion for years as the "Widow of Windsor."

Her long reign coincided with the zenith of British power. The Victorian era saw an unprecedented surge of industry, science, and empire—British dominion eventually spanning a quarter of the globe—and in 1876 Victoria was proclaimed Empress of India. At home the great rivalry of her prime ministers Disraeli and Gladstone played out around her largely ceremonial, constitutional throne, whose prestige she helped restore.

Through the marriages of her many children and grandchildren into the royal houses of the Continent, Victoria became known as "the grandmother of Europe"—among her grandsons was Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany, who would help plunge the continent into war.

Having survived several assassination attempts and become the very emblem of her era's values, Victoria died in 1901, closing the century she had defined. She was succeeded by her son, Edward VII.
