Æthelred the Unready

King of England (c. 966–1016)

Æthelred the Unready, born around 966, was king of England for nearly four decades, in a long and disastrous reign overwhelmed by Viking invasions. His famous nickname, "Unready," is a pun on his name—Æthelred means "noble counsel," and Unræd means "no counsel" or "ill-advised"—and it stuck because his reign seemed a catalogue of bad decisions and misfortune.

Coming young to the throne in 978 after the murder of his half-brother, Æthelred faced renewed Danish raids that grew ever more destructive. Lacking the strength or the unity to defeat them, he repeatedly bought the Vikings off with vast payments of silver known as the Danegeld—a policy that only invited further attacks. In 1002, in a fateful blunder, he ordered the massacre of Danes living in England on St Brice's Day, which gave the Danish king Sweyn Forkbeard a pretext for all-out war.

The kingdom unravelled. To strengthen his position Æthelred married Emma of Normandy, but the Viking onslaught only intensified. In 1013 Sweyn Forkbeard conquered England outright, and Æthelred fled into exile in Normandy. He returned the next year after Sweyn's sudden death, but the respite was brief; he died in 1016 as his son Edmund Ironside carried on the hopeless struggle against Sweyn's son Cnut.

Within months England had passed entirely to the Danes under Cnut. Æthelred is remembered as one of England's least successful kings—yet through his marriage to Emma he was the father of Edward the Confessor, and so a link in the chain that led to the great crisis of 1066.
