Catherine Parr

Sixth and last queen of Henry VIII (1512–1548)

Catherine Parr, born in 1512 into a well-connected gentry family, was already twice widowed when Henry VIII chose her as his sixth wife in 1543. Cultivated and devout, she brought calm to the ageing, ailing king's household and an unusual measure of intellectual seriousness to his court.

Her great political service was to the royal family she married into. Catherine drew Henry's estranged daughters, Mary and Elizabeth, back to court and helped secure their place in the line of succession restored by statute in 1543. She oversaw the humanist education of the young Elizabeth and of Edward VI, gathering reform-minded scholars around her.

A committed evangelical, Catherine became the first queen of England to publish under her own name: her devotional Prayers or Meditations appeared in 1545, and the bolder Lamentation of a Sinner after Henry's death. Her Protestant leanings nearly destroyed her; conservative councillors obtained a warrant for her arrest, and only a tearful, clever submission—professing that she debated theology merely to distract the king from his pain—saved her. In 1544 Henry trusted her enough to name her regent while he campaigned in France.

When Henry died in 1547 Catherine swiftly married her old suitor Thomas Seymour, brother of the Lord Protector, and took the teenage Elizabeth into her household, where Seymour's improper attentions to the girl caused a scandal. Pregnant for the first time at thirty-five, Catherine bore a daughter in 1548 but died days later of childbed fever. Buried at Sudeley Castle, she was the only one of Henry's wives to outlive him and the most learned—a quiet architect of the Tudor succession.
