Mary Tudor, Queen of France

Queen consort of France, sister of Henry VIII (1496–1533)

Mary Tudor, born in 1496, was the youngest surviving child of Henry VII and Elizabeth of York, and the favourite sister of the future Henry VIII. Celebrated across Europe for her beauty, she was a valuable piece on the diplomatic board. As a child she was betrothed to the young Archduke Charles of Habsburg, the future Emperor Charles V, but in 1514 the alliance shifted and her brother married her instead to the ageing, gout-ridden King Louis XII of France.

The marriage was brief. Louis, delighted with his teenaged bride, died less than three months after the wedding, on the first day of 1515, leaving Mary a widow at eighteen. The new French king, Francis I, eager to be rid of her and to forestall a dangerous remarriage, hovered over her future. But Mary had extracted a promise from Henry before she left England that, if she outlived Louis, she might choose her second husband herself.

She chose for love. Charles Brandon, Duke of Suffolk—Henry's closest friend, sent to bring her home—married her secretly in Paris early in 1515. It was a perilous act: to wed a princess of the blood without royal consent verged on treason. Only a heavy fine and Cardinal Wolsey's mediation reconciled the couple to the furious king.

Mary lived out her years largely away from court, where she could not abide the rise of Anne Boleyn and the casting-off of her friend Catherine of Aragon. She died in 1533. Through her daughter Frances she became grandmother to Lady Jane Grey, so that the blood of the "French Queen" reached, for nine tragic days, to the throne of England itself.
