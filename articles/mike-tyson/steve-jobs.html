Steve Jobs

Co-founder of Apple and technology visionary (1955–2011)

Steve Jobs was the visionary—and the showman—of the personal-technology age. In 1976 he co-founded Apple with the engineer Steve Wozniak in a California garage, and with the Apple II and then the Macintosh he helped bring the computer out of the laboratory and into the home, championing the graphical screen and mouse that made machines friendly to ordinary people.

Forced out of the company he had founded, he spent his exile building the firm NeXT and nurturing the animation studio Pixar, which he had bought from Lucasfilm and which transformed cinema with Toy Story. Then, in 1997, he returned to a faltering Apple and engineered one of the greatest comebacks in business history. In quick succession came the iMac, the iPod, the iTunes store, and—in 2007—the iPhone, devices that reshaped how the world listens, communicates, and lives.

Famous for his obsessive devotion to design and simplicity, his spellbinding product launches, and a demanding, mercurial temperament, he made Apple the most valuable company on earth. Cancer cut his life short in 2011, and he was mourned as the emblematic inventor-entrepreneur of his time.

His great rival across the whole span of the personal-computer revolution, the co-founder of Microsoft whose software ran the machines Apple's did not, was Bill Gates.
