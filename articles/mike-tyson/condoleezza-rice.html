Condoleezza Rice

American stateswoman and scholar (born 1954)

Condoleezza Rice was the scholar of the Soviet Union who became America's national security adviser and the first Black woman to serve as Secretary of State.

Born in segregated Birmingham, Alabama, during the civil rights struggle, she was a gifted child pianist who turned to political science, becoming an expert on the Soviet Union and a professor and provost at Stanford. She served on the National Security Council at the very end of the Cold War, helping manage German reunification and the unwinding of the empire of Mikhail Gorbachev.

As national security adviser and then Secretary of State to George W. Bush, she was a central architect of his foreign policy through the wars that followed September 11. As a Russia specialist she dealt warily with Vladimir Putin, including the 2008 war over Georgia, and she succeeded her cabinet colleague Colin Powell at the State Department. Afterward she returned to Stanford and its Hoover Institution.

Throughout her public life she never abandoned the piano, performing in concert with celebrated musicians. On one memorable occasion she sat down at the keyboard to accompany the Queen of Soul herself—Aretha Franklin.
