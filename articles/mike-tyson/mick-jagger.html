Mick Jagger

Lead singer of the Rolling Stones (born 1943)

Mick Jagger is the lead singer and strutting showman of the Rolling Stones, the frontman who for six decades has embodied the swagger and danger of rock and roll.

He co-founded the band in 1962 and made it, with its raw blues-rooted sound, the great rebellious counterpart to the Beatles—whose John Lennon and Paul McCartney handed the young Stones an early hit and remained friendly rivals. As the Stones' singer and lyricist he gave the world “(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction,” “Sympathy for the Devil” and “Gimme Shelter,” while his electric, sexually charged stage presence redefined what a frontman could be.

A fixture of celebrity culture, he moved through the orbit of Andy Warhol, who designed the Stones' notorious Sticky Fingers cover, and duetted with his friend David Bowie on “Dancing in the Street.” Knighted and seemingly tireless, he has toured into his ninth decade.

His indispensable partner in all of it—the other half of one of the most famous songwriting teams in music, the riff-making soul of the Rolling Stones—is Keith Richards.
