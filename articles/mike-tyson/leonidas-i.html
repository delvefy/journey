Leonidas I

King of Sparta (c. 540–480 BC)

Leonidas I, king of Sparta, won undying fame for his stand at the pass of Thermopylae, one of history's most celebrated acts of military sacrifice. A son of King Anaxandridas of the Agiad royal line, he came to the throne around 489 BC and married Gorgo, a daughter of King Cleomenes I and herself remembered for her sharp counsel.

When Xerxes led his enormous Persian army into Greece in 480 BC, the allied Greek states sent a small force north to hold the narrow coastal pass at Thermopylae while their fleet engaged the enemy at sea. Leonidas took command, bringing with him his personal guard of three hundred Spartans alongside several thousand other Greeks.

For three days they held the pass against overwhelming numbers, the cramped ground neutralizing the Persians' advantage. The position was lost only when a local man named Ephialtes betrayed a mountain path that allowed the Persians to come around behind them. Learning he was being encircled, Leonidas dismissed most of the army but remained with his three hundred Spartans and a few hundred others to cover the retreat. They fought to the last man, and Leonidas fell, his body bitterly contested.

Though a defeat, the stand became the supreme emblem of courage against impossible odds, immortalized in Herodotus and in the epitaph that bids the passer-by tell the Spartans that here, obedient to their laws, they lie. Its memory steeled the Greeks toward their eventual triumph.
