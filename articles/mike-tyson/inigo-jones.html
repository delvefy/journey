Inigo Jones

English architect (1573–1652)

Inigo Jones was born in London in 1573, the son of a clothworker, and rose to become the first great classical architect England produced. Travels in Italy steeped him in the buildings of antiquity and, above all, in the work of Andrea Palladio, whose treatise he studied and annotated and whose serene, rule-bound manner he resolved to bring home.

He began at the Stuart court not as a builder but as a designer of masques, the lavish allegorical entertainments of James I's reign. For these he created scenery, costumes and stage machinery of unprecedented ingenuity, introducing movable scenes and the proscenium arch to the English stage. His long partnership with the poet Ben Jonson produced dozens of masques before collapsing into a famous quarrel over whether spectacle or verse was the soul of the work.

As Surveyor of the King's Works he at last built in stone. The Queen's House at Greenwich and the Banqueting House at Whitehall, with its ceiling by Rubens, introduced a pure Palladian classicism decades ahead of English taste; it was from a window of that very Banqueting House that his later patron, Charles I, stepped to his execution.

A royalist, Jones suffered in the Civil War, his fortunes broken and his fines heavy, and he died in 1652. Yet his austere classicism, neglected in his own day, would be triumphantly revived in the Palladian movement of the following century.
