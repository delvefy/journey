Winston Churchill

British prime minister and wartime leader (1874–1965)

Sir Winston Leonard Spencer Churchill, born in 1874 at Blenheim Palace into the aristocratic Marlborough family, led Britain through the Second World War and ranks among the most consequential statesmen of the twentieth century. The son of a prominent Tory politician and an American heiress, he first won fame as a young cavalry officer and war correspondent, escaping captivity during the Boer War.

Entering Parliament in 1900, he held a succession of high offices, including First Lord of the Admiralty in the First World War, where the failed Gallipoli campaign nearly ended his career. Through the 1930s, out of power, he warned insistently against the rising threat of Nazi Germany and the folly of appeasement, earning a reputation as a lonely prophet.

When Germany overran Western Europe in May 1940, Churchill became prime minister. His defiant oratory—"we shall fight on the beaches," "blood, toil, tears and sweat," "their finest hour"—steeled British resolve through the Battle of Britain and the Blitz. As one of the "Big Three" alongside Roosevelt and Stalin, he helped shape the Allied path to victory.

Voters rejected him in the 1945 election even as the war ended, but in 1946 his "Iron Curtain" speech named the emerging Cold War. He returned as prime minister from 1951 to 1955 and won the 1953 Nobel Prize in Literature for his prolific histories and memoirs. A painter, soldier, and writer as well as a politician, Churchill died in 1965 and was honored with a state funeral.
