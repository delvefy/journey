Li Bai

Tang dynasty poet, the “Banished Immortal” (701–762)

Li Bai is, with his friend Du Fu, one of the two supreme poets of China. Born at the height of the Tang dynasty, of uncertain and possibly Central Asian origins, he spent his life as a wanderer, swordsman, and seeker of Daoist transcendence, and earned the title “Banished Immortal”—a spirit exiled from heaven into the world of men. Some thousand of his poems survive, prized for their spontaneity, soaring imagination, and music.

His verse celebrates wine and moonlight, friendship and parting, the great rivers and mountains, and a yearning to slip free of the dusty world—a sensibility steeped in the carefree wandering of Zhuangzi. He could dash off a masterpiece, it was said, in a single inspired and intoxicated sitting.

Around 742 his fame brought him to the brilliant court of Emperor Xuanzong at Chang'an, where he was appointed to the Hanlin Academy. Legend tells that the emperor himself honoured him and that the haughty poet made the most powerful eunuch of the palace pull off his boots. But his unruliness and his drinking soon cost him his place, and he resumed his roving life.

The rebellion of An Lushan caught him up in its ruin: having joined the cause of a prince later branded a traitor, he was arrested and condemned to exile, then pardoned on the road. Legend says he drowned at last reaching from a boat to embrace the moon's reflection in the water. His dearest companion among poets, paired with him as the twin summit of Chinese verse, was Du Fu.
