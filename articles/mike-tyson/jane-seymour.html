Jane Seymour

Queen of England, third wife of Henry VIII (c. 1508–1537)

Jane Seymour was the third wife of Henry VIII and the queen who at last gave him the son he had torn his kingdom apart to obtain. A daughter of the Wiltshire gentry, she served as a lady-in-waiting to both of Henry's earlier queens, Catherine of Aragon and Anne Boleyn. Quiet, gentle, and demure where Anne had been brilliant and combative, she drew the king's eye as his second marriage collapsed.

Henry married Jane in the spring of 1536, only days after Anne Boleyn was beheaded. As queen she cultivated an image of meek wifeliness and used what influence she had for the cause of peace within the royal family, helping to reconcile Henry with his eldest daughter, the future Mary I, who had long been cast out.

In October 1537, after three decades of the king's desperate longing for a male heir, Jane gave birth to a healthy son. The kingdom rejoiced. But within days she fell ill from the ordeal of the birth and died, probably of an infection, having been queen for scarcely more than a year.

Henry mourned her as his "true and loving wife," kept her memory dear among all his queens, and chose to be buried beside her. Of his six wives, she alone received the funeral of a queen. The child whose birth cost Jane her life, and who would inherit his father's throne as a boy, was Edward VI.
