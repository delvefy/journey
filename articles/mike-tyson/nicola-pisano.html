Nicola Pisano

Italian sculptor, father of Giovanni Pisano (c. 1220–1284)

Nicola Pisano, born around 1220 probably in southern Italy, was the sculptor who first revived the grandeur of ancient art and so is often called the founder of modern Italian sculpture. He may have been trained in the classicising court culture of the Emperor Frederick II before settling in Tuscany, and throughout his life he studied the carved Roman sarcophagi that survived in Pisa, borrowing from them the dignity, weight and noble bearing of his figures.

His first great work, the marble pulpit of the Pisa Baptistery, completed in 1260, announced a revolution. Its panels, crowded with solemn, sculptural figures plainly modelled on antique reliefs, broke decisively with the flat, linear manner of medieval carving. He followed it with an even richer pulpit for Siena Cathedral and, with his son Giovanni and his pupils, the Fontana Maggiore, the great public fountain of Perugia.

Nicola gathered a workshop that trained the next generation of Italian sculptors. Among his pupils were his own brilliant son Giovanni Pisano, who would carry his art toward Gothic drama, and Arnolfo di Cambio, who became the leading sculptor and architect of Florence. Through them his influence spread across Tuscany and beyond.

He died around 1284. In reaching back past the medieval centuries to the example of Rome, Nicola Pisano anticipated the central impulse of the Renaissance, and Vasari rightly placed him among the first masters to lead the arts out of their long decline.
