Muthuswami Dikshitar

Composer of the Carnatic Trinity (1775–1835)

Muthuswami Dikshitar was one of the Trinity of Carnatic music, ranking beside Tyagaraja and Syama Sastri, and the most learned and architectural of the three. Born at Tiruvarur in the Tamil country, he composed chiefly in Sanskrit, in a stately, slow-moving style that set him apart from his peers.

His some five hundred compositions are marked by grandeur and precision. He drew on the full system of seventy-two parent scales, and is celebrated for great cycles of songs—above all the Navagraha kritis on the nine planets and the Kamalamba Navavarana on the goddess of his native town. Many of his works are hymns composed on the deities of particular temples he visited on long pilgrimages.

As a young man Dikshitar travelled to the holy city of Kashi with his teacher, a wandering ascetic, and there absorbed the music of the north, so that the colour of Hindustani ragas runs through his work. He sealed his compositions with the name Guruguha, a form of the god Subrahmanya.

Uniquely among the masters, he experimented with Western music: at Madras he heard the tunes of the British military band and set Sanskrit verses to their melodies, the so-called nottuswara songs. His brother Baluswami was among the first to bring the violin into Carnatic performance.

Dikshitar's union of devotion, vast learning, and structural majesty made him a composer's composer, and his great cycles remain pinnacles of the tradition. With Tyagaraja and the elder Syama Sastri he completed the Trinity that defines Carnatic music to this day.
