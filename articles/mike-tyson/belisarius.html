Belisarius

Byzantine general (c. 505–565)

Belisarius was the greatest general of the age of Justinian and one of the finest commanders in the history of the Roman world. Rising young to high command, he first made his name in the East, winning a brilliant victory over the Persians at the Battle of Dara against heavy odds. When the Nika riots erupted in Constantinople and nearly swept Justinian from his throne, it was Belisarius and his troops who crushed the mob and saved the emperor.

He then became the instrument of Justinian's dream of reconquering the West. With a small army he destroyed the Vandal kingdom of North Africa in a single swift campaign, capturing its king. Crossing next to Italy, he took Sicily, Naples, and Rome, which he held against the Goths through a famous year-long siege, and at last captured Ravenna, the Ostrogothic capital itself.

Yet his reward was suspicion. Recalled by a jealous emperor, given too few resources for a second, grinding Italian war, he nonetheless soldiered on with unshaken loyalty and even rose once more to save Constantinople from a barbarian raid in his old age. A later legend, almost certainly false, told that the ungrateful emperor had him blinded and left to beg in the streets—an image that haunted artists for centuries.

The wars and wonders of his time were recorded by the man who served as his secretary and accompanied his campaigns, the finest historian of the age—Procopius.
