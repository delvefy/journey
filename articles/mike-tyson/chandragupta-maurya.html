Chandragupta Maurya

Founder of the Maurya Empire of India (c. 350–295 BC)

Chandragupta Maurya founded the first great empire to unify most of the Indian subcontinent. According to tradition he rose from obscure origins under the guidance of his shrewd Brahmin counsellor Chanakya, the author of the Arthashastra, a celebrated treatise on statecraft. Together they overthrew the wealthy and unpopular Nanda dynasty of Magadha and seized its capital, Pataliputra, on the Ganges.

His rise came in the turbulent years after Alexander the Great had invaded the Punjab and withdrawn. Chandragupta extended his power across northern India and into the lands the Macedonians had touched, until he confronted Alexander's successor Seleucus on the Indus frontier. The two concluded a famous treaty: Seleucus ceded the eastern provinces in exchange for five hundred war elephants and a marriage alliance, and sent the ambassador Megasthenes to Pataliputra, whose account gave the Greek world its fullest picture of India.

Chandragupta's empire was governed through an elaborate bureaucracy, a network of spies, a great standing army, and a far-reaching system of roads, much as Megasthenes and the Arthashastra describe. He built a state that his son Bindusara and grandson Ashoka would carry to its height.

By Jain tradition, in his later years Chandragupta renounced his throne, became an ascetic follower of the teacher Bhadrabahu, and ended his life by the rite of fasting unto death at Shravanabelagola in the south. Whatever the truth of his end, he had laid the foundations of imperial India. His grandson, who would rule almost the whole subcontinent and devote his reign to the teachings of the Buddha, was Ashoka the Great.
