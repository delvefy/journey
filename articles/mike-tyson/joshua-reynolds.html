Joshua Reynolds

English portrait painter (1723–1792)

Joshua Reynolds was born in Devon in 1723, the son of a clergyman and schoolmaster, and trained in London under the portraitist Thomas Hudson. A formative stay in Italy from 1750 immersed him in the works of the Renaissance masters—above all Michelangelo, Raphael and Titian—whose grandeur he sought to graft onto the English portrait. The lessons of Rembrandt and the Venetians shaped his handling of light and colour.

Returning to London, he rose swiftly to become the most sought-after portrait painter of the age, recording the great figures of his time in a dignified "Grand Manner." His studio was the hub of a brilliant social world, and with his closest friend Samuel Johnson he founded the celebrated Literary Club, whose members included Edmund Burke and many of the leading minds of the day.

In 1768 he became the first president of the new Royal Academy of Arts, was knighted the following year, and over two decades delivered his fifteen Discourses on Art to its students. These lectures, urging the study of the antique and the Italian masters, became the most influential body of English art theory; his final address closed reverently on the name of Michelangelo.

Prolific and honoured, Reynolds painted some two thousand works and was appointed principal painter to George III. His eyesight failed near the end, and he died in 1792, being buried with great ceremony in St Paul's Cathedral. His chief rival for the public's favour was the gifted Thomas Gainsborough.
