Ronald Reagan

40th President of the United States (1911–2004)

Ronald Reagan was the fortieth President of the United States and one of the most consequential American leaders of the late twentieth century, a former film actor who reshaped his country's politics and helped bring the Cold War to its end.

He made his first career in Hollywood, where he led the Screen Actors Guild, before turning to conservative politics and winning the governorship of California. Elected president in 1980, he cut taxes, loosened regulation, and built up the military in a programme that came to be called Reaganomics, all the while projecting a sunny optimism that made him “the Great Communicator.” He survived an assassin's bullet early in his first year.

Abroad his great drama was the Cold War. After years of confrontation he found an unexpected partner in the Soviet reformer Mikhail Gorbachev, and through a series of summits the two men wound down the nuclear standoff; at the Berlin Wall he issued his famous challenge to tear it down. His staunchest ally throughout was Margaret Thatcher.

A fellow Californian in a party once led by Richard Nixon, he left a legacy and a slogan—to make America great again—later taken up by Donald Trump. He died in 2004 after a long illness. The leader who shared his greatest achievement was Mikhail Gorbachev.
