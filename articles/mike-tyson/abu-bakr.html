Abu Bakr

First Rashidun caliph (c. 573–634)

Abu Bakr, born around 573 in Mecca, was the closest friend and companion of the Prophet Muhammad and, on Muhammad's death, the first of the Rashidun caliphs—the first successor to lead the Muslim community. A respected and prosperous merchant, he was, by Sunni tradition, the first adult man to embrace Islam, and he remained the Prophet's most steadfast supporter through the persecutions in Mecca.

His loyalty was legendary. He accompanied Muhammad on the perilous Hijra, the migration to Medina, hiding with him in a cave from their pursuers—an episode honoured in the Quran. He gave his daughter Aisha in marriage to the Prophet, binding the two families, and he was so trusted that Muhammad asked him to lead the prayers in his final illness.

When Muhammad died suddenly in 632, the community faced its first great crisis. At the gathering of Saqifa, Abu Bakr was chosen as caliph, though some who favoured Ali accepted his leadership only reluctantly—the first seed of the Sunni-Shia division. As caliph he acted decisively: he crushed the revolt of the Arabian tribes in the Ridda Wars, holding the young Muslim state together by force, and launched the first campaigns against the Persian and Byzantine empires.

He is also credited with the first steps to gather the scattered revelations of the Quran into a single collection. Abu Bakr ruled only about two years, dying in 634, but before his death he named the strong Umar as his successor, ensuring a smooth transition. Revered as "al-Siddiq," the Truthful, he is honoured by Sunni Muslims as the greatest of the Prophet's companions.
