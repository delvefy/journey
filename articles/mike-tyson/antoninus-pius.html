Antoninus Pius

Roman emperor (86–161)

Antoninus Pius gave the Roman Empire one of the longest and most tranquil reigns in its history, the serene high noon of the Pax Romana. A respected senator of a Gaulish-Roman family, he was chosen by the aging Hadrian as heir, on the condition that he in turn adopt the young Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus to secure the succession beyond him. He took the throne in 138.

His byname, "Pius"—the dutiful—reflected both his insistence that the Senate honor the memory of his adoptive father and his reputation for conscientious virtue. For twenty-three years he governed with such mildness and competence that there was little of the drama historians love to record: few wars, no purges, no extravagance. He scarcely left Italy, yet the empire ran smoothly, its treasury full, its provinces at peace.

What campaigning there was took place on the edges of the world. In Britain his armies pushed the frontier north and raised a new turf rampart, the Antonine Wall, beyond Hadrian's older barrier. Frugal, patient, and just, he embodied the ideal of the unspectacular good ruler.

His successor and adoptive son, the philosopher-emperor Marcus Aurelius, opened his Meditations with a tender catalogue of the virtues he had learned from Antoninus. On Antoninus's death in 161 the throne passed, as Hadrian had planned, to two heirs ruling together—Marcus Aurelius and his colleague Lucius Verus.
