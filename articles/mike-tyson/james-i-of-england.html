James I of England

King of Scotland and England (1566–1625)

James was born in 1566, the son of Mary, Queen of Scots. He became King James VI of Scotland as a baby, when his mother was driven from her throne, and was raised by stern Protestant tutors while she lived out her captivity and, in 1587, her execution in England. On the death of the childless Elizabeth I in 1603, James inherited the English crown as well, uniting the two kingdoms under a single sovereign as the first of the Stuart line in England.

His reign nearly ended before it had matured. In 1605 a band of Catholic conspirators planted gunpowder beneath Parliament, intending to destroy king and lords together; the plot was betrayed and Guy Fawkes seized among the barrels. James also presided over a brilliant culture: Shakespeare's company became the King's Men under his patronage, Inigo Jones designed his court masques, and Francis Bacon rose to be his Lord Chancellor.

Learned and opinionated, James wrote on kingship, witchcraft and tobacco, and championed the divine right of kings, which set him quarrelling with his Parliaments over money and prerogative. He kept an uneasy peace with Spain and, in 1618, sent the aged Walter Raleigh to the scaffold.

His most lasting monument was the magnificent English Bible he commissioned, the King James Version of 1611. He died in 1625 and was succeeded by his son Charles I, who would inherit his clashes with Parliament and bring them to catastrophe.
