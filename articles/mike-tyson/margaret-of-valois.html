Margaret of Valois

Queen of France and Navarre (1553–1615)

Margaret of Valois was born in 1553, a daughter of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici and sister to three kings of France. Beautiful, witty and unusually learned, she was a dazzling ornament of a court torn by religious war. In August 1572 her mother and brother Charles IX married her to the Protestant Henry of Navarre, the future Henry IV, hoping the union would reconcile the faiths.

The hope was drowned in blood. Within days of the wedding, the Catholic celebrations gave way to the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre, and Margaret's marriage began amid slaughter. It proved loveless and childless; both spouses took lovers, and Margaret was repeatedly caught up in the intrigues between her husband, her brother Henry III, and the crown.

For years she was kept in near-captivity at the remote château of Usson in the Auvergne. When Henry of Navarre at last won the throne and needed a legitimate heir, their marriage was annulled in 1599, freeing him to wed Marie de' Medici.

In her final years Margaret reconciled with the king, returned to Paris, and presided over a salon famous for its art, music and learning, while composing memoirs that were among the earliest by a Frenchwoman. She died in 1615. More than two centuries later Alexandre Dumas immortalised her, half in fact and half in fiction, as the heroine of his novel La Reine Margot.
