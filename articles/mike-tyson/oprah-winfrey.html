Oprah Winfrey

American media mogul and talk-show host (born 1954)

Oprah Winfrey is the American broadcaster who built the most influential talk show in television history and became one of the most powerful figures in popular culture.

Born into rural poverty in Mississippi and scarred by a traumatic childhood, she rose through local television to take over a struggling Chicago talk show, which she swiftly transformed into the nationally syndicated Oprah Winfrey Show. For a quarter-century it dominated daytime television, and her confessional, empathetic style made her a confidante to millions. Her on-air book club could turn an unknown novel into a bestseller overnight, and her endorsement carried extraordinary weight—not least in 2008, when her embrace of Barack Obama gave a powerful boost to his campaign.

She was a cultural force far beyond the studio. Her acting debut came in the film The Color Purple, directed by Steven Spielberg, earning her an Oscar nomination, and her interviews became television events—none more watched than her 1993 sit-down with Michael Jackson. She built a media empire, became the first Black female billionaire, and gave away vast sums to education and charity.

It was Oprah, decades later, who drew out the explosive confidences of Prince Harry and his wife in a globally watched interview. Through her whole life her cherished guide and friend, whom she called her mentor and second mother, was the poet Maya Angelou.
