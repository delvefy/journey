Dion of Syracuse

Syracusan statesman and disciple of Plato (c. 408–354 BC)

Dion was a prince of the Syracusan court, brother-in-law to the great tyrant Dionysius I and one of the most powerful men in the city. When Plato came to Sicily, the young Dion fell wholly under his spell, embracing the philosophical life with a convert's intensity and dreaming that wisdom might one day govern a state.

His chance seemed to come when Dionysius I died and his pleasure-loving son inherited the throne. Dion hoped to make a philosopher-king of the youth and persuaded Plato to return to Syracuse to teach him. But the experiment foundered on the new tyrant's vanity and suspicion; Dion was exiled, his fortune seized, and even his wife given to another.

From exile he resolved on action. Landing in Sicily with a small band in 357 BC, he was welcomed by a people weary of tyranny and swept Dionysius II from power, becoming for a moment the master of Syracuse and the apparent vindication of Plato's hopes.

The triumph soured. Dion's stern, aristocratic manner disappointed the citizens who had looked for liberty, and in 354 BC he was murdered by a trusted companion from Plato's own Academy. Plutarch told his story and paired it with that of the Roman Brutus, two high-minded men undone by the tyranny they fought. The fickle young ruler whom Dion first tried to teach and then overthrew was Dionysius II of Syracuse.
