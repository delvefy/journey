Robert Catesby

Leader of the Gunpowder Plot (1572–1605)

Robert Catesby was born about 1572 into a prominent Catholic family of Warwickshire, and grew up under the harsh penal laws that fined and imprisoned those who clung to the old faith. Tall, charismatic and devout, he chafed at the persecution of his fellow Catholics. In 1601 he joined the rebellion of the Earl of Essex against Elizabeth I, was wounded and captured, and was ruinously fined for his part.

The accession of James I in 1603 raised Catholic hopes of toleration, and their swift disappointment turned Catesby to a far more violent design. He became the mastermind of the Gunpowder Plot, conceiving a scheme to destroy the king and the Protestant lords at the opening of Parliament and to raise a Catholic rising in the chaos that followed.

Gathering a small band of trusted men—among them Thomas Wintour and the explosives expert Guy Fawkes—he set the plan in motion for November 1605. When it was betrayed and Fawkes seized in the cellar beneath the Lords, Catesby fled into the Midlands and tried, in vain, to rouse the country.

Cornered with a handful of followers at Holbeche House in Staffordshire, he was shot down on 8 November 1605, reportedly dying clutching an image of the Virgin. His body was afterwards dug up and beheaded, and his head set on show at the very Parliament he had meant to destroy.
