Milo of Croton

Greek Olympic wrestler (6th century BC)

Milo of Croton was the most celebrated athlete of the ancient world, a wrestler whose strength passed swiftly out of record and into legend. Six times he won the wrestling crown at Olympia, and many times more at the other great games, dominating his sport across decades in a way no one had done before.

The tales told of him were prodigious. He was said to have carried a full-grown bull on his shoulders the length of the stadium and then devoured it in a day; to have trained from boyhood by lifting a calf each morning until it grew into that bull; to have stood holding a pomegranate so firmly that no one could pry his fingers open, yet so gently that the fruit was unmarked. How much was true no one could say, but the stories made him a second Heracles.

He came from Croton, the city where Pythagoras had founded his brotherhood, and Milo belonged to that circle; by one account he saved the philosopher's companions when a hall began to collapse, propping up the failing roof with his own body. When Croton went to war with luxurious Sybaris, Milo led its soldiers to victory, fighting, men said, in a lion's skin and his Olympic garlands.

Even his death became a fable: trying in old age to tear apart a tree, his hands were trapped in the closing wood, and he was set upon by wolves. The later traveler whose great description of Greece preserved his feats and his end for posterity was Pausanias the Geographer.
