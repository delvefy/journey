Domenico Ghirlandaio

Florentine Renaissance painter (1448–1494)

Domenico Ghirlandaio, born in Florence in 1448, was one of the most accomplished and prolific painters of the early Renaissance and the master of its largest and busiest workshop. The son of a goldsmith, he became renowned for grand fresco cycles that combined sacred subjects with vivid, lifelike portraits of contemporary Florentines set against the city's own streets and palaces.

His finest achievements are the fresco cycles he painted for two great mercantile families allied to the Medici: the Sassetti Chapel in Santa Trinita and, above all, the Tornabuoni Chapel in Santa Maria Novella, completed in 1490. Crowded with elegant figures and rich detail, these works are a matchless window onto the faces and fashions of Florence at its height. He was also summoned to Rome in 1481 to work alongside Botticelli and Perugino on the walls of the Sistine Chapel.

Ghirlandaio's workshop trained a generation of painters, but its greatest pupil was a boy who entered it around 1487: the young Michelangelo Buonarroti learned the rudiments of fresco there before being drawn away to the sculpture garden of Lorenzo de' Medici. That single apprenticeship would have secured Ghirlandaio's fame even had he painted nothing.

He absorbed influences widely, including the realism of the Flemish Portinari Altarpiece that had arrived in Florence. Industrious and good-natured, he was said to wish for commissions grand enough to cover the whole circuit of the city walls. He died of fever in 1494, only forty-five. His life and works were later lovingly recorded by Giorgio Vasari.
