Caracalla

Roman emperor (188–217)

Caracalla was the violent and capable son of Septimius Severus whose brief reign left one of the most far-reaching laws in Roman history. Born in 188 and bearing the official name Marcus Aurelius Antoninus, he was nicknamed for the hooded Gallic cloak he liked to wear. He inherited the empire jointly with his younger brother Geta when their father died in 211—an arrangement poisoned by mutual hatred.

Within months Caracalla had Geta murdered, reportedly in their mother Julia Domna's arms, and slaughtered thousands of his brother's supporters, erasing Geta's name from monuments across the empire. Ruling alone, he proved a brutal master, infamous for ordering a massacre of the populace of Alexandria.

Yet in 212 he issued the Antonine Constitution, granting Roman citizenship to nearly every free inhabitant of the empire—a momentous act that, whatever its fiscal motive, transformed what it meant to be Roman. In the capital he raised the enormous and luxurious Baths of Caracalla, whose ruins still astonish.

A restless soldier, Caracalla modeled himself obsessively on Alexander the Great, aping his dress and dreaming of eastern conquest, until he was cut down by an assassin in 217 while campaigning against Parthia. The throne passed briefly to his murderer's faction, but the Severan house soon reclaimed it in the person of a teenage priest-emperor from Syria—Elagabalus.
