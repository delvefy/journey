John III of Portugal

King of Portugal, "the Pious" (1502–1557)

John III of Portugal ruled the empire of the discoveries at its widest extent, a sovereign under whom Portuguese power circled the globe even as the seeds of religious severity were sown at home.

Succeeding his father Manuel I in 1521, he turned his attention to the lands across the Atlantic, organising the settlement of Brazil through hereditary captaincies and a royal governor, and so laying the foundations of the great colony. Across Africa and Asia his fortresses and factories carried on the trade in spices, gold, and slaves that made Lisbon rich.

His dynasty was knit closely to the house of Habsburg. He married Catherine of Austria, the sister of the emperor Charles V, while his own sister wed the emperor himself—a double alliance that bound the crowns of Portugal and Spain.

A devout king, he brought the Inquisition into Portugal in 1536 to enforce religious conformity. Yet his piety also opened a more far-reaching chapter: he welcomed the newly founded Society of Jesus and, asking its leader Ignatius of Loyola for missionaries, sent Francis Xavier to carry Christianity to India, the East Indies, and Japan.

Sorrow shadowed his later years, for most of his children died before him. The crown therefore passed at his death in 1557 to his small grandson Sebastian. He was the son of Manuel I and the brother of Isabella of Portugal, who as empress would be the mother of Philip II of Spain.
