Maya Angelou

American poet and memoirist (1928–2014)

Maya Angelou was the American poet and memoirist whose account of a wounded Southern childhood became one of the most beloved and influential autobiographies of the twentieth century.

Born Marguerite Johnson and raised in segregated Arkansas, she survived a childhood of trauma that left her mute for years, then poured the gift of language she recovered into an astonishingly varied life as dancer, singer, actress, journalist and activist, living for a time in Egypt and Ghana. The civil rights movement claimed her energies: she served as a northern organizer for Martin Luther King Jr., and worked alongside Malcolm X to build a new organization shortly before his murder—losses that scarred her deeply.

Her masterpiece, I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings, made her famous and gave countless readers a voice for their own pain and resilience. As a poet of dignity and endurance—“And Still I Rise”—she became a national treasure, chosen to recite a poem at the 1993 inauguration of Bill Clinton, and a friend to figures from Nelson Mandela to Oprah Winfrey, who cherished her as a mentor.

Honoured with the Presidential Medal of Freedom, she died in 2014, mourned as one of America's great moral voices. The writer and friend who had famously goaded and encouraged her into setting down the story of her life was James Baldwin.
