Ralph Waldo Emerson

American essayist and philosopher (1803–1882)

Ralph Waldo Emerson was born in Boston in 1803 and trained for the Unitarian ministry, but resigned his pulpit over points of doctrine and turned to the life of a writer and lecturer. A journey to Europe brought him friendships with the leading minds of Britain, among them Thomas Carlyle, with whom he corresponded for decades.

Settling in Concord, Massachusetts, he became the prophet of Transcendentalism. His little book Nature in 1836 announced its themes, and his address “The American Scholar” called on his countrymen to throw off intellectual dependence on Europe, a summons hailed as a declaration of cultural independence. In essays such as “Self-Reliance” and “The Over-Soul” he preached trust in the individual conscience and the presence of the divine in nature and the self.

A magnetic public lecturer, he ranged across history and character, and in Representative Men took the measure of great minds from Plato to Goethe. His Concord home became the hearth of a remarkable circle.

Emerson mentored the young Henry David Thoreau, championed the unknown Walt Whitman, and inspired writers far beyond America—Friedrich Nietzsche kept his essays as a companion. He died in Concord in 1882, the founding voice of American philosophy and letters.
