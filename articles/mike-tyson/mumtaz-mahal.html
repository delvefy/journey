Mumtaz Mahal

Mughal empress, for whom the Taj Mahal was built (1593–1631)

Mumtaz Mahal—“the Exalted One of the Palace”—was the cherished chief empress of Shah Jahan, and the woman whose memory inspired the most famous building in the world. Born Arjumand Banu Begum into the same eminent Persian family as her aunt, the empress Nur Jahan, she was betrothed to Prince Khurram and married him in 1612.

Theirs became a legendary devotion. She was his constant companion, the keeper of his imperial seal and his closest confidante, and she travelled with him even on his military campaigns through the years of struggle before he won the throne. She bore him fourteen children over nineteen years; among her sons were two who would later tear the empire apart in war—the gentle scholar Dara Shikoh and the austere Aurangzeb.

In 1631, while accompanying the emperor on campaign in the Deccan, she died at Burhanpur giving birth to her fourteenth child. Shah Jahan's grief was overwhelming, and from it arose an act of devotion in stone.

Over more than a decade he raised above her grave at Agra the Taj Mahal, a mausoleum of white marble inlaid with precious stones, perfect in its proportions and reflected in its long pool—a monument that has come to stand for love itself. There she lies, and there the emperor was at last laid beside her. Her eldest and best-loved son, the mystic prince who longed to reconcile Islam and Hinduism, was Dara Shikoh.
