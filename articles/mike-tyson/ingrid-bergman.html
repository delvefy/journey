Ingrid Bergman

Swedish film actress (1915–1982)

Ingrid Bergman was one of the most luminous and beloved stars of classic cinema, a Swedish actress who won three Academy Awards and whose naturalness on screen set her apart in the age of the studios.

Brought from Sweden to Hollywood, she became a great star with her role opposite Humphrey Bogart in Casablanca, one of the most cherished films ever made. She won her first Oscar for Gaslight and gave some of her finest performances for the director Alfred Hitchcock, in Spellbound and Notorious.

At the height of her fame she scandalised America by leaving her husband for the Italian director Roberto Rossellini, with whom she had a child; condemned even on the floor of the United States Senate, she was driven from Hollywood for years, making austere films in Italy instead.

Her return was a triumph. She won a second Academy Award for Anastasia, playing a woman who might be the lost Russian grand duchess, and a third, years later, for Murder on the Orient Express. Her last great role came in Ingmar Bergman's Autumn Sonata.

She died on her sixty-seventh birthday in 1982, remembered for grace, courage, and that unforgettable face. Of all her films none is more loved than the one she made with Humphrey Bogart.
