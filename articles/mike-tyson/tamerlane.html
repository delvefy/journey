Tamerlane

Turco-Mongol conqueror (1336–1405)

Timur, known in the West as Tamerlane, was a Turco-Mongol conqueror who built a vast empire from his capital at Samarkand and consciously fashioned himself as the heir of Genghis Khan. Born around 1336 in Transoxiana and lamed early in life by an arrow—hence "Timur the Lame"—he rose through warfare to dominate Central Asia. Lacking direct descent from Genghis, he ruled in the name of puppet khans and married into the Chinggisid line to claim legitimacy.

For three decades his armies ranged with terrible energy across Persia, the Caucasus, Mesopotamia, northern India, and Anatolia. He stormed Delhi in 1398, leaving mountains of dead, and in 1402 he crushed and captured the Ottoman sultan Bayezid at the Battle of Ankara, nearly extinguishing the young Ottoman state. His campaigns were among the bloodiest in history, remembered for towers built of skulls.

Yet the same ruler poured the plunder of empires into making Samarkand a jewel of Islamic art and learning, adorned with magnificent mosques and tombs; his grandson Ulugh Beg became a renowned astronomer. Timur patronized Persian and Turkic culture even as he devastated cities.

He died in 1405 on the march to invade Ming China. His sprawling empire soon fractured, but his line endured: a great-great-great-grandson, Babur, would sweep into India and found the Mughal Empire. Tamerlane's fearsome legend later inspired works such as Marlowe's Tamburlaine.
