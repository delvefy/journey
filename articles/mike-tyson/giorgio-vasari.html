Giorgio Vasari

Painter, architect, and first art historian (1511–1574)

Giorgio Vasari, born in Arezzo in 1511, was a painter and architect of real talent, but his lasting fame rests on a book. A protégé of the Medici, he trained in Florence among the great masters and as a young man came to know Michelangelo, whom he revered above all other artists. He built a busy career executing frescoes and decorations for popes and princes, and as an architect he designed the Uffizi in Florence—now one of the world's great museums—and the elevated corridor that still bears his name.

Yet it is as the first art historian that Vasari is remembered. In 1550, and in a greatly expanded edition of 1568, he published the Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects, a series of biographies tracing the progress of Italian art from Cimabue and Giotto down to his own day. Part history, part criticism, part affectionate anecdote, it remains the single most important source for the lives of the Renaissance masters.

Vasari shaped how we still think about that age. He framed the story as a "rebirth"—a rinascita, the root of our word Renaissance—rising from the medieval "Gothic" to a peak in his hero Michelangelo. His judgments were not always fair to non-Florentines, and his anecdotes are sometimes more vivid than accurate, but his enthusiasm and detail gave art history its founding text.

As Cosimo I de' Medici's favoured artist, Vasari helped fashion the cultural image of ducal Florence. He died in 1574, leaving behind both the Uffizi and a book without which the names of Leonardo, Raphael, and Giotto would be far dimmer to us today.
