Charles IX of France

King of France (1550–1574)

Charles IX was born in 1550, the son of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici. He came to the throne in 1560, a boy of ten, on the early death of his brother Francis II, and throughout his reign real power lay with his mother, who governed as regent and never wholly relinquished her grip on her fragile son.

His years on the throne were swallowed by the Wars of Religion. Catherine at first sought compromise between Catholics and Huguenots, but as Charles grew up the Protestant admiral Gaspard de Coligny won a strong personal influence over him, pressing the king toward war with Spain. Alarmed, Catherine and the Catholic party turned against the admiral.

The crisis broke in August 1572. The Paris wedding of his sister Margaret of Valois to the Protestant Henry of Navarre, the future Henry IV, had gathered the Huguenot leaders in the capital. After a failed attempt on Coligny's life, the king was persuaded to sanction a pre-emptive slaughter, and the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre engulfed Paris, killing Coligny and thousands of Protestants.

Charles, never strong in body or mind, was said to be haunted by the bloodshed. A patron of poets and the protector of the aged seer Nostradamus, he sank into ill health and died of consumption in 1574, only twenty-three. He was succeeded by his brother Henry III.
