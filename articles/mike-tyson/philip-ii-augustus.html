Philip II Augustus

King of France (1165–1223)

Philip II Augustus, born in 1165, was one of the most successful kings in French history, the monarch who transformed the Capetian crown from a weak lordship into the dominant power of France. Coming to the throne in 1180, he made it his life's work to break the vast Angevin Empire of the English kings that surrounded and overshadowed his realm.

He pursued this aim with patience and guile across three English reigns. He intrigued against Henry II by supporting the rebellions of Henry's sons; he set out with Richard the Lionheart on the Third Crusade, only to quarrel bitterly with him and return home early to plot against him; and against the hapless King John he struck the decisive blow. Between 1202 and 1204 Philip conquered Normandy, Anjou, Maine and Touraine, tearing the heart out of the Angevin lands and quadrupling the territory under direct royal control.

His triumph was sealed on the battlefield. In 1214 a great coalition assembled against him—the Emperor Otto IV, the Count of Flanders, and King John—and Philip crushed it at the Battle of Bouvines, one of the most decisive battles of the Middle Ages, confirming French dominance and helping his ally Frederick II to the imperial throne. He had earlier campaigned in the Holy Land against Saladin before abandoning the crusade.

At home Philip strengthened royal government, granted the rising towns charters, walled Paris, built the fortress of the Louvre, and filled the treasury. Through his son Louis VIII and his grandson Louis IX—whose mother was Blanche of Castile—he founded a line that would dominate Europe. When Philip Augustus died in 1223, France was the foremost kingdom of Christendom.
