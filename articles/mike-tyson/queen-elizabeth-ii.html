Queen Elizabeth II

Queen of the United Kingdom, 1952–2022 (1926–2022)

Queen Elizabeth II was the longest-reigning monarch in British history, a figure of extraordinary continuity and duty who embodied the Crown for seventy years.

She became heir to the throne only when her uncle abdicated and her father became king, and she succeeded him in 1952 as a young woman of twenty-five. Her reign would span an age of vast change—the end of empire, the rise and transformation of the Commonwealth she cherished, and the social upheavals of seven decades. Through it all she remained scrupulously dutiful and above politics, and in 2015 she surpassed even the reign of her great-great-grandmother Queen Victoria.

Fifteen prime ministers served her, the first being the aged Winston Churchill, who doted on the new young queen, and the last appointed only days before her death. She weathered the storms of the modern monarchy: an “annus horribilis” of family scandal, the painful collapse of her son's marriage, and the convulsion of national grief that tested the Crown to its limits. Her steadfastness carried the institution through, and her Platinum Jubilee in 2022 was met with deep affection.

She died at Balmoral in September 2022, mourned across the world, and was succeeded by her son. The most famous and most tragic figure of her family's modern story—the daughter-in-law whose glamour, unhappiness and sudden death shook the monarchy—was Princess Diana.
