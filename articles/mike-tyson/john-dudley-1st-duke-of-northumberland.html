John Dudley, 1st Duke of Northumberland

English statesman and de facto ruler of England (c. 1504–1553)

John Dudley was born about 1504, the son of Edmund Dudley, a financial minister to Henry VII who was executed for treason in 1510 when the young Henry VIII needed scapegoats for his father's grasping taxation. Restored in blood, John rose through soldiering and royal service to become Viscount Lisle and Lord High Admiral, a trusted commander in Henry VIII's wars against France and Scotland.

When the boy-king Edward VI succeeded in 1547, real power lay with the Lord Protector, Edward Seymour, Duke of Somerset. Dudley, now Earl of Warwick, crushed Kett's Rebellion in Norfolk in 1549, then exploited the discontent that followed to topple Somerset. By 1551 he was Lord President of the Council and Duke of Northumberland, the effective ruler of England, governing in alliance with the reformers and pressing forward the Protestant settlement that Thomas Cranmer was shaping.

His ambition overreached as Edward sickened. To keep both power and the new religion, Dudley married his son Guildford to Lady Jane Grey and persuaded the dying king to alter the succession by his "Device," naming Jane his heir and excluding the Catholic Mary and her sister Elizabeth. When Edward died in July 1553, Dudley proclaimed Jane queen.

The gamble failed within days. Mary rallied the country to her legitimate claim, the council deserted Dudley, and his march against her collapsed. Captured, he abjectly recanted his Protestantism in a vain bid for mercy and was beheaded on Tower Hill in August 1553. He left a ruined name but a formidable brood: his son Robert would become Elizabeth I's lifelong favourite, the Earl of Leicester.
