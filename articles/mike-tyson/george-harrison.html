George Harrison

English musician, the “quiet Beatle” (1943–2001)

George Harrison was the lead guitarist of the Beatles, the “quiet” one whose songwriting and spiritual searching came to flower in the band's later years and after its end.

The youngest of the four, he long stood in the shadow of John Lennon and Paul McCartney before emerging as a composer of the first rank, giving the Beatles “Something” and “Here Comes the Sun”—and, with his friend Eric Clapton on lead guitar, “While My Guitar Gently Weeps.” He opened Western pop to the music and spirituality of India, studying the sitar and embracing Hindu devotion.

When the band dissolved he flourished with the sprawling solo masterpiece All Things Must Pass and organized the Concert for Bangladesh, the first great charity rock event. Later he found joyful company in the Traveling Wilburys, the supergroup he formed with his friend Bob Dylan and others. He survived a knife attack in his home before dying of cancer in 2001, mourned as the most soulful of the Beatles.

The bandmate who had kept the beat behind him through all the Beatles' years, his good-humored friend and the drummer of the most famous group in history, was Ringo Starr.
