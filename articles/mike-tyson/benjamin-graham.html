Benjamin Graham

Economist and investor, father of value investing (1894–1976)

Benjamin Graham was the founder of value investing and the teacher of its greatest practitioners. Born in London and raised in New York after his family's fortunes collapsed, he knew financial hardship early, and the lesson of the 1929 crash—which battered his own investments—hardened into a lifelong philosophy of caution and analysis.

For decades he taught at Columbia Business School while running his own investment partnership, and there he worked out the principles that would govern modern investing. He insisted on a sharp distinction between investment and speculation, on estimating a security's true “intrinsic value” apart from the mood of the market, and on always demanding a “margin of safety” between price and worth. He personified the market as the moody “Mr. Market,” whose daily quotations a sensible investor should exploit rather than obey.

His ideas reached the world through two books that became scripture for investors: the rigorous textbook he wrote in the depths of the Depression, and the more accessible Intelligent Investor, which his most famous pupil, Warren Buffett, would call the best book on investing ever written.

That foundational textbook, Security Analysis, he did not write alone. His collaborator, a fellow Columbia professor whose name is forever joined with his as the authors of the value-investing canon, was David Dodd.
