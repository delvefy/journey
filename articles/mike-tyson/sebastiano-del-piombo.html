Sebastiano del Piombo

Venetian-Roman Renaissance painter (c. 1485–1547)

Sebastiano del Piombo was the painter who married the glowing colour of Venice to the heroic design of Rome, and who became, for a time, Raphael's only serious challenger.

Born Sebastiano Luciani in Venice, he trained under Giovanni Bellini and absorbed the soft, tonal manner of Giorgione, whose unfinished works he is thought to have helped complete. Around 1511 he was lured to Rome to decorate the pleasure-villa of the banker Agostino Chigi, and there his career took its decisive turn.

In Rome he fell into the orbit of Michelangelo, who, locked in rivalry with Raphael, took up the Venetian as his champion. Michelangelo supplied Sebastiano with powerful figure drawings, and Sebastiano clothed them in rich Venetian colour, most famously in The Raising of Lazarus, deliberately set against Raphael's Transfiguration. He became a portraitist of grave magnificence, fusing the two great traditions of Italian art. Raphael's early death left him the leading painter in Rome.

After living through the horrors of the Sack of Rome, he was rewarded with a comfortable and lucrative church office—keeper of the leaden papal seal, the piombo that gave him his name—and thereafter he painted little, enjoying his wealth. Late in life he even quarrelled with Michelangelo over technique. He died in Rome in 1547. The pope who heaped that office and his favour upon him, the Medici pontiff of the Sack and of Henry VIII's great divorce, was Clement VII.
