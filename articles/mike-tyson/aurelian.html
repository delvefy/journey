Aurelian

Roman emperor, "Restorer of the World" (214–275)

Aurelian was the soldier-emperor who, in just five years, pulled the Roman Empire back together at the lowest point of its third-century collapse. Born of humble Balkan stock and risen through the ranks by sheer ability, he took the throne in 270 to find the Roman world broken into three: a Gallic Empire in the west, the realm of Palmyra in the east, and a shrunken Rome in the middle.

With ferocious energy he set about reconquest. In the East he marched against the wealthy desert empire of Palmyra and its formidable queen, defeating her armies, taking her capital, and carrying her back to Rome as a captive. In the West he reabsorbed the rebel Gallic state. By 274 the empire was whole again, and a grateful world hailed him as Restitutor Orbis, the Restorer of the World.

He matched conquest with consolidation: he ringed the city of Rome itself with massive new walls that still stand, reformed the debased coinage, gave up the exposed province of Dacia to shorten the frontier, and promoted the cult of the Unconquered Sun as a unifying faith for the realm.

A stern disciplinarian, Aurelian was murdered in 275 by officers deceived into fearing his wrath, his great work cut short. The most famous of his enemies—the brilliant and ambitious queen whose eastern empire he overthrew—was Zenobia.
