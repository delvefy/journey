Pope Francis

First Jesuit pope (born 1936)

Pope Francis, born Jorge Mario Bergoglio in Buenos Aires in 1936, became in 2013 the head of the Roman Catholic Church—the first pope ever from the Americas, the first from the Southern Hemisphere, and the first member of the Jesuit order to hold the office. The son of Italian immigrants to Argentina, he had risen through the Argentine Church to become Archbishop of Buenos Aires and a cardinal, known for his austere personal life and his closeness to the poor of his city.

On his election, following the historic resignation of his predecessor Benedict XVI, he chose a name no pope had ever taken—Francis—in homage to Saint Francis of Assisi, the medieval friar who had embraced poverty and the care of creation. The choice announced the tone of his papacy.

Francis became known worldwide for his emphasis on humility, mercy and social justice. He spoke constantly on behalf of the poor, of migrants, and of the marginalised; he urged action on poverty and on the environment, devoting a major encyclical, Laudato si', to the care of the planet. He sought to reform the Vatican's administration and finances and to make the Church a more pastoral institution, while holding to its core doctrines.

His warmth and simplicity—living in a guesthouse rather than the papal palace, washing the feet of prisoners—made him one of the most recognised religious leaders in the world, even as his reforms drew criticism from traditionalists. In taking the name of the gentle saint of Assisi, the Jesuit pope linked the modern Church to one of Christianity's most beloved figures.
