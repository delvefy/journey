Nostradamus

French astrologer and seer (1503–1566)

Michel de Nostredame was born in 1503 at Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, in the south of France, into a family of recent converts from Judaism. He studied at Avignon and later at Montpellier, and made his early living as an apothecary and physician, winning a local reputation for his treatment of plague victims even as the disease carried off his own first wife and children.

Settling at Salon-de-Provence, he turned increasingly to astrology and prophecy. From 1550 he issued popular yearly almanacs, and in 1555 he began to publish Les Prophéties, a vast collection of cryptic quatrains written in an allusive mixture of French, Latin and other tongues. Their deliberate obscurity lent them an air of timeless mystery.

The verses brought him sudden celebrity. Queen Catherine de' Medici took him under her protection, summoned him to the French court, and her son Charles IX later named him Counsellor and Physician-in-Ordinary. His fame was sealed when a quatrain was read, after the event, as having foretold the death of King Henry II in a tournament in 1559.

By tradition his prophecies have since been stretched to anticipate countless later events, though scholars regard the quatrains as vague and freely reinterpreted after the fact. He died at Salon in 1566. Whatever the truth of his predictions, Nostradamus remains the most famous prophet of the modern age, his name a byword for foretelling the future.
