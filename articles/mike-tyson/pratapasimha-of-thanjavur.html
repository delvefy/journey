Pratapasimha of Thanjavur

Maratha king of Thanjavur (r. 1739–1763)

Pratapasimha was the Maratha king of Thanjavur from 1739 to 1763, one of the Bhonsle rulers who governed the rich Kaveri delta of the Tamil south. He reigned in turbulent times, as the contest between the British and the French for southern India—the Carnatic Wars—swept across the region and the neighbouring Nawab of Arcot pressed upon his borders.

His kingdom had been founded two generations before by his ancestor Ekoji, who sprang from the same house as the great Shivaji, builder of Maratha power in the Deccan. By Pratapasimha's day the Thanjavur Marathas, though shorn of much of their independence and made to pay tribute, still held their ancient capital and its temples.

Amid the wars Pratapasimha manoeuvred between the rival European companies and the Indian powers, now allied with one party, now another, struggling to preserve his throne. Thanjavur survived as a kingdom, if a diminished one, where it might easily have been swallowed whole.

His more lasting fame is as a patron. Under Pratapasimha the court of Thanjavur remained a great centre of music and letters, sustaining composers and scholars—among them the masters whose art would culminate in the Carnatic Trinity. The disciplined classicism of court musicians such as Pachimiriyam Adiyappa was nurtured under his protection.

Pratapasimha stands in the line of the Bhonsle kings who carried the Maratha name to the far south of India, heirs in their distant way to the founder of their southern fortunes, Ekoji of Thanjavur.
