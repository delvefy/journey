Duccio

Sienese painter, founder of the Sienese school (c. 1255–1319)

Duccio di Buoninsegna, born in Siena around 1255, was the founder of the Sienese school of painting and, with his Florentine contemporary Giotto, one of the two artists who led Italian painting out of the Byzantine age. Where Giotto pursued weight and drama, Duccio sought grace: he kept the shimmering gold grounds and elegant line of the Byzantine tradition but filled them with a new tenderness, delicacy of colour, and gentle human feeling.

His masterpiece is the Maestà, a vast double-sided altarpiece completed in 1311 for the high altar of Siena Cathedral. Its great central panel shows the Virgin enthroned in majesty among ranks of saints and angels; the reverse told the story of Christ's Passion in dozens of jewel-like scenes. When it was finished, the people of Siena are said to have carried it to the cathedral in joyful procession, with trumpets and ringing bells. The earlier Rucellai Madonna, painted for a church in Florence, is another monument of his art.

Duccio's lyrical style shaped a brilliant generation of Sienese painters who followed him, above all Simone Martini and the Lorenzetti brothers, and made Siena for a time the rival of Florence in the arts. Though documents reveal a man often in debt and repeatedly fined for petty offences, his paintings are serene and devout.

He died around 1319. Largely overshadowed for centuries while Florentine art held the spotlight, Duccio has been restored by modern scholarship to his rightful place as one of the founders of European painting.
