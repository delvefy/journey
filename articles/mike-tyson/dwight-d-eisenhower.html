Dwight D. Eisenhower

Allied commander and 34th President of the United States (1890–1969)

Dwight D. Eisenhower was the supreme commander who led the Western Allies to victory over Nazi Germany and then served two terms as President of the United States.

Born in Texas and raised in Abilene, Kansas, he graduated from West Point and spent decades as a little-noticed staff officer until the patronage of General George Marshall lifted him, in the crisis of the Second World War, to high command. Appointed Supreme Allied Commander by Franklin D. Roosevelt, he directed the invasions of North Africa, Sicily and Italy, and then planned and launched the Normandy landings of June 1944, the vast amphibious assault that opened the Western Front against Adolf Hitler. Working in close, sometimes stormy partnership with Winston Churchill and coordinating with the advancing armies of Joseph Stalin in the East, he accepted the unconditional surrender of Germany in 1945.

His immense prestige carried him to the White House in 1952 under the slogan “I like Ike.” As president he ended the Korean War, built the interstate highway system, and in his farewell address warned the nation against the “military-industrial complex.” In the Cold War he faced Nikita Khrushchev, with whom a hoped-for thaw collapsed after a U-2 spy plane was shot down over Soviet territory, and he handed the unresolved crises over Cuba and Berlin to his young successor, John F. Kennedy. Through it all his hardest wartime partner had been the proud leader of the Free French, Charles de Gaulle.
