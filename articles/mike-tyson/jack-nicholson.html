Jack Nicholson

American actor of the New Hollywood and after (born 1937)

Jack Nicholson, with his devilish grin and dark glasses, was one of the most charismatic and acclaimed actors of his generation, winner of a record-tying three Academy Awards. He rode the wave of the New Hollywood to stardom with Easy Rider and Five Easy Pieces, and gave a string of indelible performances that defined the American screen for decades.

He was the doomed detective of Chinatown, the rebellious inmate McMurphy of One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, and the axe-wielding madman of Stanley Kubrick's The Shining, bellowing “Here's Johnny!” through a shattered door. He could be tender in Terms of Endearment and As Good as It Gets, both of which won him Oscars, and gleefully villainous as the Joker in Batman. A famous fixture courtside at Los Angeles Lakers games, he became as recognizable off screen as on.

Among his friends and neighbours was Marlon Brando, with whom he shared a hilltop above Hollywood and the screen in The Missouri Breaks—a link between two generations of the Method's greatest practitioners.

His adversary in the electric courtroom climax of A Few Good Men, the young officer who demanded a truth he was warned he could not handle, was Tom Cruise.
