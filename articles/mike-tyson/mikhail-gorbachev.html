Mikhail Gorbachev

Last leader of the Soviet Union (1931–2022)

Mikhail Gorbachev was the eighth and final leader of the Soviet Union, the reformer whose policies hastened the end of the Cold War and the dissolution of the state he had set out to save.

Born to a peasant family in the Stavropol region, he rose steadily through the Communist Party apparatus and in 1985 became General Secretary, the youngest member of the ruling Politburo. Confronting a stagnant economy and an ossified bureaucracy, he launched two famous programmes: perestroika, a restructuring of the economy and administration, and glasnost, an opening of public debate that lifted censorship and exposed long-buried truths.

Under glasnost the crimes of the Stalin era were aired openly and many of Joseph Stalin's victims were rehabilitated, for Gorbachev cast his reforms as a return to a purer founding ideal, away from the dictatorship that had distorted it. Abroad he withdrew Soviet forces from Afghanistan and negotiated deep cuts in nuclear arms with the American president Ronald Reagan. When the nations of Eastern Europe broke from Moscow in 1989, he declined to send in the tanks, and the Berlin Wall fell.

For this he won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1990 and became the first and only President of the Soviet Union, his arms-control diplomacy backed by Margaret Thatcher as well as Reagan. But the forces he unleashed could not be contained: a hardline coup against him failed in 1991, real power passed to Boris Yeltsin, and that December the Union dissolved and he resigned. Celebrated in the West and blamed at home by a public that would later turn to Vladimir Putin, he had invoked the founding ideals of Vladimir Lenin while completing the de-Stalinization first dared by his reforming predecessor, Nikita Khrushchev.
