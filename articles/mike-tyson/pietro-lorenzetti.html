Pietro Lorenzetti

Sienese painter (c. 1280–1348)

Pietro Lorenzetti, born in Siena around 1280, was the elder of the two painter brothers who, with Simone Martini, carried Sienese art to its fourteenth-century summit. Trained in the tradition of Duccio, he developed a robust, emotionally powerful style, drawing on the monumental naturalism of Giotto and the dramatic, expressive sculpture of Giovanni Pisano to give his figures unusual solidity and feeling.

His major achievement is the cycle of frescoes of the Passion of Christ in the Lower Church of San Francesco at Assisi, painted in the 1320s. Crowded, intense and spatially daring, the scenes—especially a grief-stricken Deposition and a Last Supper lit by a kitchen fire beneath a starry sky—reveal a bold experimental mind. In Siena his great Birth of the Virgin of 1342 set its holy scene within a convincingly rendered architectural interior, a striking advance in the depiction of space.

Pietro shared his brother Ambrogio's fascination with perspective and the observed world, and the two often worked in a related spirit, though Pietro's art was the more rugged and dramatic. Together the Lorenzetti made Siena, alongside Florence, a fountainhead of the new painting.

Their story ended in the same disaster. Both brothers are thought to have died in 1348, when the Black Death swept away perhaps half the population of Siena and silenced a generation of its artists at a stroke. Pietro Lorenzetti left behind some of the most moving religious paintings of the age, and a reputation, recorded by Vasari, as one of the masters of the Sienese golden age.
