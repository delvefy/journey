Prince

American musician (1958–2016)

Prince was the protean musical genius of Minneapolis, a songwriter, performer and virtuoso who fused funk, rock, pop and soul into a sound entirely his own.

A self-taught prodigy who played nearly every instrument on his early records, he built a flamboyant, boundary-defying persona around explosive talent. His 1984 album and film Purple Rain made him a global superstar, and through the decade he poured out hit after hit—“When Doves Cry,” “Kiss,” “1999”—while forming, with Michael Jackson and Madonna, the triumvirate that ruled 1980s pop. He drew on the soul tradition of forebears like Marvin Gaye even as he reinvented it.

Fiercely independent, he waged a long war with his record label over the ownership of his music, even abandoning his name for an unpronounceable symbol in protest. Astonishingly prolific, he left vaults of unreleased recordings, and onstage he was a showman of dazzling intensity. He died in 2016 of an accidental overdose, mourned worldwide.

Above all he was a guitar virtuoso of staggering power, endlessly likened to the man who had redefined what the electric guitar could do—the incandescent genius of the 1960s, Jimi Hendrix.
