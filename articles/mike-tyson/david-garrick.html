David Garrick

English actor and theatre manager (1717–1779)

David Garrick was born in Hereford in 1717 and raised in Lichfield, where he became a pupil of Samuel Johnson. In 1737 the two set out together to seek their fortunes in London, Johnson to write and Garrick, after a brief spell in the wine trade, to the stage. In 1741 his electrifying performance as Richard III at Goodman's Fields made him an overnight sensation.

Garrick swept away the stiff, declamatory manner of the day in favour of a more natural and emotionally expressive style of acting, and dominated the English theatre for a generation. From 1747 he was manager and part-owner of the Drury Lane Theatre, which he ran for nearly thirty years, reforming its stagecraft, improving its lighting, and banishing spectators from the stage itself.

Above all he made himself the great champion of William Shakespeare, reviving and adapting the plays, excelling in their leading roles, and organising the famous Shakespeare Jubilee at Stratford-upon-Avon in 1769, which did much to establish the playwright's modern cult. Wealthy, sociable and admired, Garrick moved among the circle of Johnson, Edmund Burke and Joshua Reynolds.

He retired in 1776, selling his share of Drury Lane to Richard Brinsley Sheridan, and died three years later. He was buried in Poets' Corner in Westminster Abbey, fittingly at the foot of Shakespeare's monument, the most celebrated actor his country had yet produced.
