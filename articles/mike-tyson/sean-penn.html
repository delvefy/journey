Sean Penn

American actor and director (b. 1960)

Sean Penn is one of the most intense and admired American actors of his generation, a two-time Academy Award winner whose off-screen life has been as turbulent and public as his performances are searching.

The son of a film director, he emerged in the 1980s and soon won a name for ferocious commitment to his roles. He starred opposite Al Pacino in Carlito's Way, gave a harrowing performance as a condemned killer in Dead Man Walking, and won his first Oscar as a grieving father in Clint Eastwood's Mystic River, and a second as the murdered gay-rights leader Harvey Milk.

He turned director as well, guiding Jack Nicholson through The Crossing Guard and The Pledge, and earning wide praise for Into the Wild. Restless and combative, he became known for political activism and journalism—relief work after disasters, controversial meetings with foreign leaders, and headline-making interviews.

His private life drew constant attention, above all his brief, stormy marriage to Madonna in the 1980s.

He married afterward the actress Robin Wright, with whom he spent much of the next two decades.
