Thomas Seymour

Lord High Admiral of England (c. 1508–1549)

Thomas Seymour was born about 1508, younger brother of Edward Seymour and of Jane Seymour, whose marriage to Henry VIII made the Seymours one of the foremost families at the Tudor court. Charming, handsome and reckless, Thomas built a career in diplomacy and at sea, and on Henry's death in 1547 was created Baron Seymour of Sudeley and Lord High Admiral.

He had courted the widowed queen Catherine Parr even before Henry's reign ended; within months of the king's death he married her, securing both a royal wife and a place near the throne. But Thomas burned with jealousy of his elder brother Edward, who as Lord Protector ruled England in the name of their nephew, the boy-king Edward VI. Where Edward held the regency of the realm, Thomas was granted mere guardianship of the king's person, and he never accepted the lesser role.

His schemes grew ever bolder. He courted the young king with secret gifts of pocket money, took the wardship of Lady Jane Grey into his household with a plan to marry her to Edward, and behaved so improperly toward his stepdaughter, the teenage Princess Elizabeth, that she was removed from Catherine Parr's house in scandal. After Catherine died in childbirth in 1548, Thomas plotted more openly to seize control of the king.

In January 1549 he was arrested, allegedly after breaking into the royal apartments and shooting a dog that barked at him. Charged with thirty-three articles of treason, he was attainted by Parliament; his own brother signed the death warrant. He was beheaded on Tower Hill in March 1549, his ambition and his life undone together.
