Gerald Ford

38th President of the United States (1913–2006)

Gerald Ford was the 38th President of the United States, the only person ever to hold the office without being elected to it or to the vice-presidency.

A football star and Navy veteran from Michigan, he spent a quarter-century in Congress, rising to lead the Republican minority. When Vice-President Agnew resigned in disgrace, Ford was appointed to replace him; and when the Watergate scandal forced Richard Nixon from office in 1974, Ford suddenly became president. His most fateful act came at once—a full pardon of Nixon, meant to spare the nation further trauma but widely condemned, and a wound to his own popularity from which he never fully recovered.

He governed amid stagflation and the final collapse of South Vietnam, kept Henry Kissinger on as his diplomatic chief, and sealed the Helsinki Accords with the Soviet leader Leonid Brezhnev. He survived two assassination attempts in a single month. In 1976 he fought off a fierce primary challenge from Ronald Reagan only to lose the presidency narrowly to Jimmy Carter, and he left office having steadied the country after its constitutional crisis.

Among the young aides who ran his White House—serving as his chief of staff before going on to a long and powerful career of his own—was Dick Cheney.
