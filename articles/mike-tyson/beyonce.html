Beyoncé

American singer and performer (b. 1981)

Beyoncé is one of the most celebrated musicians of her age and the most-awarded artist in the history of the Grammys, an entertainer whose voice, spectacle, and ambition have set the standard for modern pop.

She first rose as the lead singer of the group Destiny's Child before launching a solo career of extraordinary scope. Across a sequence of albums she remade herself again and again—from the exuberant hits of her early records to the searching, deeply personal Lemonade and the euphoric Renaissance—while her live performances became byzantine spectacles of choreography and sound.

Beyond the music she became a cultural force, weaving themes of race, womanhood, and Black history through her art, as in her landmark festival performance celebrating Black college tradition. She sang at the inauguration of Barack Obama, whom she and her husband strongly supported, and she has often named Michael Jackson as the idol who shaped her sense of what a performer could be.

Her marriage to the rapper Jay-Z made the two a reigning couple of music. Of her own generation, the only female artist of comparable reach and fame is Taylor Swift.
