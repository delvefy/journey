Harold Godwinson

Last Anglo-Saxon king of England (c. 1022–1066)

Harold Godwinson, born around 1022, was the last crowned Anglo-Saxon king of England, whose brief reign ended in the catastrophe of 1066. As Earl of Wessex he was the greatest nobleman in the land, the chief power behind the throne of the childless king Edward the Confessor, and a capable soldier and statesman.

When Edward died in January 1066, the leading men of England chose Harold as king. But his crown was claimed by two formidable rivals: William, Duke of Normandy, who insisted Edward had promised him the throne and that Harold himself had sworn an oath to support that claim; and the great Norwegian warrior-king Harald Hardrada, supported by Harold's own estranged brother Tostig.

The blows fell almost together. In September 1066 Hardrada and Tostig invaded the north; Harold marched his army at astonishing speed and crushed them at the Battle of Stamford Bridge, where both the Norwegian king and his own brother were killed. It was a complete victory—but only days later word came that William had landed on the south coast.

Harold raced south and met the Normans near Hastings on 14 October. His exhausted army fought all day behind its shield wall, but in the end it broke, and Harold was slain—according to tradition struck in the eye by an arrow, as depicted in the Bayeux Tapestry. His death ended both his reign and Anglo-Saxon England itself. He remains a tragic, heroic figure: the king who won one great battle only to lose his kingdom and his life in another, three weeks apart.
