Diane de Poitiers

French noblewoman and royal favourite (1500–1566)

Diane de Poitiers was born in 1500 into an aristocratic French family. Married at fifteen to the much older Louis de Brézé, Grand Sénéchal of Normandy, she was widowed in 1531 and thereafter wore only the black and white of mourning, a striking austerity that became part of her legend. Celebrated for a beauty and poise she retained into old age, she made her way at the court of Francis I.

From the late 1530s she became the mistress of the future Henry II, a prince twenty years her junior, and held his devotion for the rest of his life. When he came to the throne in 1547 she became the most powerful woman in France, often overshadowing the queen, Catherine de' Medici, in influence over the king, his policy and his court—where she also helped oversee the upbringing of the royal children, among them Mary, Queen of Scots.

A discerning patron, she commissioned the elegant Château d'Anet and received from Henry the lovely Château de Chenonceau, and her image was woven into the huntress-goddess imagery beloved of the Renaissance court.

Her power ended abruptly with Henry's death in the tournament of 1559. Catherine de' Medici, freed at last from her rival, drove her from court and made her surrender Chenonceau. Diane withdrew to her château at Anet, where she lived out her remaining years and died in 1566.
