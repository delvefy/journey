Xuanzang

Tang Buddhist monk, pilgrim to India, and translator (602–664)

Xuanzang was a Chinese monk of the early Tang whose sixteen-year pilgrimage to India became one of the most celebrated journeys in history. Distressed by the contradictions and gaps in the Buddhist scriptures as they had reached China, he resolved to travel to the homeland of the faith to seek the texts in their original form. Around 629, defying an imperial ban on leaving the country, he set out alone across the deserts and mountains of Central Asia.

Following the Silk Road through its oasis kingdoms, enduring sandstorms, bandits, and the high passes, he reached India and spent many years there. He studied at the great monastic university of Nalanda under its aged abbot, mastering the subtle Yogachara, or “Consciousness-Only,” philosophy, and travelled the length and breadth of the subcontinent. He won the friendship of the powerful king Harsha, who held a vast religious assembly in his honour.

In 645 he returned to the Tang capital bearing hundreds of manuscripts, relics, and images, and was welcomed as a hero; the emperor himself received and supported him. Xuanzang devoted the rest of his life to translating the texts he had brought back—an enormous labour—and founded a school of Chinese Buddhism upon them. His record of the journey, the Great Tang Records on the Western Regions, remains a priceless source for the history of seventh-century India and the lands between.

Centuries later his pilgrimage was spun into the beloved comic novel Journey to the West, with its monkey-king companion. The Indian monarch who became his great patron and host was Harsha.
