Toshiro Mifune

Japanese film actor (1920–1997)

Toshiro Mifune, born in 1920 in Qingdao, China, to Japanese parents, became the most internationally famous Japanese actor of the twentieth century and the magnetic face of his country's golden age of cinema. He drifted into acting almost by accident after the war, but his raw energy and commanding screen presence soon caught the eye of the director Akira Kurosawa, beginning one of the great partnerships in film history.

Over sixteen films together, Mifune and Kurosawa created some of cinema's most unforgettable characters. He was the bandit in Rashomon, the exuberant peasant-warrior Kikuchiyo in Seven Samurai, the doomed lord of Throne of Blood, and—above all—the laconic, masterless swordsman of Yojimbo and Sanjuro, an archetype that would echo through countless Westerns and action films. His range ran from explosive ferocity to quiet dignity.

Beyond Kurosawa, Mifune embodied the samurai ideal again and again, most famously portraying the legendary swordsman Miyamoto Musashi in Hiroshi Inagaki's Samurai Trilogy, the first part of which won an Academy Award. His fame opened doors abroad: he acted in international productions, including a role under Steven Spielberg, and late in his career brought gravity to the warlord Toranaga in the television epic Shōgun.

After a creative falling-out, his long collaboration with Kurosawa ended, and his later years were quieter. Toshiro Mifune died in 1997. Charismatic, intense and utterly distinctive, he remains the definitive image of the samurai on screen and one of the few actors of his nation to achieve true worldwide stardom.
