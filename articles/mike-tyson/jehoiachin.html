Jehoiachin

King of Judah (reigned 598–597 BC)

Jehoiachin, also called Jeconiah, was a king of Judah whose reign lasted barely three months and whose true history was a lifetime of captivity. He came to the throne as a youth, at the worst possible moment, when the armies of Babylon were already closing on Jerusalem in punishment for his father's rebellion. Rather than see the city stormed, the young king surrendered.

He was carried off to Babylon in 597 BC, together with the queen mother, the nobles, the soldiers, the skilled craftsmen, and the treasures of the Temple—the first great deportation of the Jews into exile. In his place the conqueror set his uncle Zedekiah on a puppet throne, whose later revolt would bring down Jerusalem and its Temple in fire.

For some thirty-seven years Jehoiachin languished as a prisoner. The prophet Jeremiah pronounced a bitter doom upon him, and the exiled prophet Ezekiel reckoned the years of his own visions by the years of the captive king's exile. At last a new Babylonian ruler released him from prison and gave him an honored seat at the royal table; clay ration-tablets unearthed in Babylon, recording provisions for the king of Judah, confirm that he lived. Through his grandson Zerubbabel the royal line of David would one day return.

His captivity, and the destruction of his kingdom, were the work of the most powerful monarch of the age, the Babylonian king who broke Jerusalem and led its people away—Nebuchadnezzar II.
