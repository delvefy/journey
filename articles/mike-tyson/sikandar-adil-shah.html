Sikandar Adil Shah

Last Sultan of Bijapur (1668–1700)

Sikandar Adil Shah was the ninth and last sultan of the Adil Shahi dynasty of Bijapur, in whose short reign the once-brilliant Deccan kingdom was extinguished by the Mughal empire.

He came to the throne as a child of four on the death of his father Ali Adil Shah II, and real power lay with rival ministers and regents whose factions tore the court apart. The sultanate, already weakened by decades of war with the Marathas and by the loss of its southern provinces, had little strength left to meet the storm gathering in the north.

That storm was the emperor Aurangzeb, who had resolved to bring the whole Deccan under direct Mughal rule. In 1685 his armies closed upon Bijapur and laid siege to its great walls. The defenders held out through hunger and bombardment for more than a year, but in 1686 the city fell, and the boy-sultan was led before the emperor.

Aurangzeb spared his life but ended his line. Sikandar was stripped of his throne, granted a small pension, and held in confinement at the fortress of Daulatabad, where he died in 1700, still a prisoner, not yet thirty years old.

With his fall the Adil Shahi dynasty, which had ruled Bijapur for nearly two centuries, came to its end. The very next year Aurangzeb turned upon Bijapur's old neighbour and sometime ally, the Sultanate of Golconda, ruled by Abul Hasan Qutb Shah, to complete his conquest of the south.
