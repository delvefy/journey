Eric Clapton

British rock and blues guitarist (born 1945)

Eric Clapton is one of the most celebrated guitarists in the history of rock, a British bluesman whose devotion to the music of the American South reshaped popular music.

He emerged in the 1960s with a virtuosity so admired that fans scrawled “Clapton is God” on London walls. Through the Yardbirds, John Mayall's Bluesbreakers, the power trio Cream, and Derek and the Dominos he produced classics like “Sunshine of Your Love” and the anguished “Layla.” A reverent disciple of the Delta blues, he idolized the legendary Robert Johnson and later recorded with the King of the Blues, B.B. King. His long solo career brought “Wonderful Tonight” and the heartbreaking “Tears in Heaven,” written after the death of his young son.

He shared his era's guitar throne with Jimi Hendrix, whose arrival in London astonished him and whose death he mourned deeply.

His closest friendship, though, was with a Beatle—the “quiet” one—on whose song “While My Guitar Gently Weeps” he played the soaring lead, and whose wife, Pattie Boyd, inspired “Layla” and eventually married Clapton without ending the two men's bond. That friend was George Harrison.
