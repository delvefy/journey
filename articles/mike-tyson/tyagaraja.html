Tyagaraja

Composer-saint of Carnatic music (1767–1847)

Tyagaraja was the greatest composer of Carnatic music and, with Muthuswami Dikshitar and Syama Sastri, one of the "Trinity" who brought the southern Indian classical tradition to its summit. Born at Tiruvarur and living most of his life in the village of Thiruvaiyaru on the Kaveri, he was a Telugu Brahmin and a lifelong devotee of the god Rama.

He composed many hundreds of kritis—songs uniting melody, rhythm, and devotional poetry—almost all addressed to Rama and almost all in the Telugu language. Among them are the celebrated Pancharatna, the "five gems," still sung in chorus at his memorial each year, and two musical dramas. His works fused the deepest devotion with subtle musical craft, and they remain the core of the Carnatic concert repertoire.

Tyagaraja inherited and perfected the tradition that Purandara Dasa had founded two centuries before. He explored the system of ragas with extraordinary inventiveness, giving many their definitive character, and through his many disciples he shaped the lineages that carried Carnatic music forward.

He lived in simple devotion and is famous for having refused the riches pressed on him by royal patrons, asking in song whether wealth or nearness to God brought true happiness. He chose the path of the singing saint rather than the court musician.

After his death his memory was honoured in the annual Tyagaraja Aradhana festival at Thiruvaiyaru, where musicians gather to sing his works. Revered almost as a saint, Tyagaraja stands at the heart of Carnatic music, his nearest peer the great Muthuswami Dikshitar.
