Khosrow II

Last great Sasanian king of Persia (c. 570–628)

Khosrow II, called Parviz, "the Victorious," was the last great king of Sasanian Persia, reigning from 590 to 628 over an empire that he carried to its widest extent and then to ruin. Driven from his throne early in his reign by the usurper Bahram Chobin, he fled to the Byzantine Empire and was restored with the help of the emperor Maurice, to whom he became almost as a son.

When Maurice was murdered in 602 by the usurper Phocas, Khosrow seized the pretext for war against Byzantium, and the result was the greatest and last of the Roman-Persian wars. His armies swept across the East: they took Antioch and Damascus, captured Jerusalem in 614—carrying off the relic of the True Cross—overran Egypt, and reached the very shores opposite Constantinople. For a moment the ancient glory of Persia seemed reborn, and Khosrow ruled from a court of legendary splendour with his beloved queen Shirin.

It did not last. The Byzantine emperor Heraclius struck back with brilliant campaigns deep into the Persian heartland, and by 627 had shattered Khosrow's armies. Discredited in defeat, Khosrow was overthrown and put to death by his own son in 628. By tradition he had earlier received a letter from the Prophet Muhammad summoning him to Islam, and contemptuously tore it to pieces.

The long war had exhausted Persia utterly. Within little more than a decade of Khosrow's death, the armies of Islam would sweep away the Sasanian Empire entirely. He is remembered as a magnificent but ultimately disastrous king—the last splendour of ancient Persia before its fall, and the lover of Shirin in immortal Persian romance.
