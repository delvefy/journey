Jesus Christ

Jewish preacher, central figure of Christianity (c. 4 BC–c. 30 AD)

Jesus of Nazareth was a Jewish teacher and preacher in Roman-ruled Judea whose life and message gave rise to Christianity, the world's largest religion. Born around 4 BC and raised in Galilee, he is, according to the Gospels, baptized by John the Baptist before beginning a public ministry around the age of thirty.

He proclaimed the coming of the Kingdom of God and called his hearers to repentance, love of God and neighbor, mercy, and forgiveness. Teaching in vivid parables and in sermons such as the Sermon on the Mount, he gathered a band of disciples, among them the twelve apostles, and the Gospels record him healing the sick and working wonders. His following grew, and with it the wariness of religious authorities and the Roman administration.

Entering Jerusalem at the Passover, he was, the Gospels relate, betrayed by his disciple Judas Iscariot, arrested, and tried. He was crucified under the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate around 30 AD. Christians believe that he rose from the dead on the third day and ascended into heaven, and worship him as the Son of God and the promised Messiah, or Christ.

Carried across the Roman world by apostles such as Paul and recorded in the Gospels, his teaching reshaped Western civilization and much of the world beyond it. He is honored, too, in Islam, which reveres him as a great prophet. Few lives, however briefly documented, have left so vast a mark on human history.
