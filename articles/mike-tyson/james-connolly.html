James Connolly

Irish socialist and republican martyr (1868–1916)

James Connolly was the Marxist organizer and theorist who fused socialism with Irish nationalism and died before a firing squad for his part in the 1916 Rising.

Born into desperate poverty among Irish immigrants in Edinburgh, he educated himself and became a tireless socialist agitator in Scotland, Ireland and the United States, where he worked within the Industrial Workers of the World. Steeped in the writings of Karl Marx, he founded the Irish Socialist Republican Party and argued in works such as Labour in Irish History that national freedom and the emancipation of the worker were one and the same cause.

Back in Dublin he helped build the Irish Transport and General Workers' Union and stood at the centre of the great Lockout of 1913, when employers tried to starve the city's workers into submission. To defend the strikers from police violence he helped raise the Irish Citizen Army, a small workers' militia. When the chance for insurrection came, he threw this force in alongside the nationalist volunteers, joining the secret military council and serving as commandant of the Dublin garrison.

In the General Post Office during Easter Week he fought beside Patrick Pearse until a shattered ankle left him unable to stand. After the surrender he was carried out to be shot, tied to a chair because of his wounds—the last of the leaders executed, and the one whose killing stirred the deepest anger. W. B. Yeats wove his name into the roll of the dead in “Easter, 1916.” The fiery union leader with whom he had built the Irish labour movement was James Larkin.
