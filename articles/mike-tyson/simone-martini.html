Simone Martini

Sienese painter of the International Gothic (c. 1284–1344)

Simone Martini, born in Siena around 1284, was the most refined and influential of Duccio's heirs and a founder of the elegant style known as International Gothic. Building on Duccio's grace, he developed a courtly art of flowing line, sumptuous colour and gilded splendour that delighted princes and prelates across Europe.

His public masterpiece is the great Maestà fresco of 1315 in the council hall of Siena's Palazzo Pubblico, an enthroned Virgin presiding over the city's government. To the same hall is attributed the famous image of the warlord Guidoriccio da Fogliano riding across a barren landscape. Most beloved of all is the radiant Annunciation he painted in 1333 for Siena Cathedral, the angel Gabriel's greeting streaming in gold toward a shrinking, startled Virgin—a work made with his brother-in-law Lippo Memmi.

In his later years Simone was drawn to Avignon, where the popes then held their court, and there he entered the most celebrated friendship of his career. The poet Petrarch, also resident at Avignon, became his admirer; Simone painted a portrait, now lost, of Petrarch's beloved Laura and decorated the poet's treasured manuscript of Virgil. Petrarch repaid him with two sonnets, praising Simone as an artist who must have ascended to heaven to behold such beauty.

Simone died at Avignon in 1344. His luminous, decorative manner spread far beyond Siena, shaping courtly painting from Italy to France and helping to define the visual language of the late Gothic age. With Duccio and the Lorenzetti, he made the school of Siena one of the glories of fourteenth-century art.
