Dustin Hoffman

American actor (b. 1937)

Dustin Hoffman is one of the defining actors of the New Hollywood of the 1960s and 1970s, a performer of obsessive craft who disappeared so completely into his parts that he helped redraw the idea of the leading man.

He burst out as the aimless young hero of The Graduate, then proved his range as the doomed hustler of Midnight Cowboy. He endured the tortures of Marathon Man opposite Laurence Olivier—whose gentle rebuke, to just try acting rather than suffer for it, became a Hollywood legend—and played the reporter Carl Bernstein beside Robert Redford in All the President's Men.

He won two Academy Awards for Best Actor: as the struggling father of the divorce drama Kramer vs. Kramer, acting against Meryl Streep, and as the autistic savant whose brother is played by Tom Cruise in Rain Man. He also charmed audiences in the cross-dressing comedy Tootsie and sparred with Robert De Niro in the satire Wag the Dog.

His co-star in the wrenching custody drama that won him his first Oscar was Meryl Streep.
