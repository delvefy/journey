Elizabeth Woodville

Queen of England, consort of Edward IV (c. 1437–1492)

Elizabeth Woodville, born about 1437, was the daughter of a knight and of Jacquetta of Luxembourg, and the widow of a Lancastrian soldier when she caught the eye of the young Yorkist king Edward IV. In 1464 Edward married her in secret—an astonishing match, for she was a commoner and a widow with two sons, and kings were expected to wed foreign princesses for reasons of state.

The marriage transformed her into queen and her numerous relatives into a powerful court faction. The rapid enrichment of the Woodvilles bred deep resentment, above all in the king's mighty cousin, the Earl of Warwick—"the Kingmaker"—whose plans for a French royal bride the secret wedding had wrecked. Warwick's fury helped drive him into rebellion, and Elizabeth twice fled into sanctuary as the throne lurched between York and Lancaster.

Her gravest trial came after Edward IV's death in 1483. Her brother-in-law Richard of Gloucester seized power, declared her marriage invalid and her children bastards, and took the throne as Richard III. Her two young sons, the Princes in the Tower, vanished into the fortress and were never seen again.

Stripped of power but not of resolve, the widowed queen entered into a remarkable conspiracy with the Lancastrian Margaret Beaufort to overthrow Richard by marrying her eldest daughter, Elizabeth of York, to Henry Tudor. The plan succeeded at Bosworth in 1485. Elizabeth Woodville thus became mother-in-law to Henry VII and grandmother to the future Henry VIII—the matriarch through whom Yorkist blood passed into the Tudor line. She spent her final years in quiet retirement and died in 1492.
