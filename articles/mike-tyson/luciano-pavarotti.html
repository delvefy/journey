Luciano Pavarotti

Italian operatic tenor (1935–2007)

Luciano Pavarotti was the most popular operatic tenor of the late twentieth century, an artist who carried the bel canto tradition out of the opera house and to a mass public of millions. Born in Modena, the son of a baker who was himself a keen amateur tenor, he trained as a schoolteacher before committing to singing and making his debut as Rodolfo in Puccini's La Bohème in 1961.

His gleaming, sunlit voice and effortless top notes soon made him famous; a feat of nine ringing high Cs in a Donizetti aria earned him the title “King of the High Cs.” An early tour partnering the soprano Joan Sutherland sharpened his technique and launched his international career, which centred, like Caruso's before him, on the Metropolitan Opera.

What set Pavarotti apart was his reach beyond the connoisseur. His recording of Puccini's “Nessun dorma” became an anthem at the 1990 football World Cup, and that same year he joined two colleagues in the wildly successful Three Tenors concerts, which brought arias to stadium crowds. His charity galas paired him with pop and rock stars.

Rooted in the Italian repertory of Giuseppe Verdi and Giacomo Puccini, and conscious heir to the legend of Enrico Caruso, he died in Modena in 2007. The most famous of the colleagues with whom he shared the Three Tenors stage was the Spaniard Plácido Domingo.
