Agesilaus II

King of Sparta (c. 444–360 BC)

Agesilaus II, lame from birth and never expected to rule, became king of Sparta around 400 BC with the backing of the admiral Lysander, whose overweening power he soon moved to check. For nearly forty years he embodied the Spartan state at the height of its dominance and through the long decline that followed.

Early in his reign he carried the war into Asia Minor against the Persian empire, winning victories and dreaming of freeing the Greek cities there, until the outbreak of war at home—stirred up by Persian gold among Sparta's Greek rivals—forced his recall. He fought his way back through hostile Greece and won a hard battle at Coronea, and for a generation his energy and courage upheld Spartan supremacy.

But a new power was rising in Thebes. At Leuctra in 371 BC the Theban general Epaminondas shattered the Spartan army with a bold new tactic, ending forever the myth of Spartan invincibility. Epaminondas then carried fire and sword into the Peloponnese, freeing the long-enslaved Messenians and standing before the unwalled city of Sparta itself, which the aged Agesilaus defended with desperate resolve.

To fill Sparta's empty treasury, the old king hired himself out in his eighties as a mercenary commander in Egypt, and died on the journey home around 360 BC. His friend Xenophon wrote a glowing memorial of him, and his life was recorded among the parallel biographies of Plutarch. His great antagonist remained the Theban Epaminondas.
