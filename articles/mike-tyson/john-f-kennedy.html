John F. Kennedy

35th President of the United States (1917–1963)

John F. Kennedy was the youngest man ever elected president of the United States and the first Catholic to hold the office, and his brief, glamorous presidency left an outsized mark on the American imagination. A decorated navy hero of the Second World War and scion of a wealthy and ambitious political family, he won the 1960 election over Richard Nixon and summoned the nation with his ringing call to ask not what their country could do for them.

His thousand days in office were crowded with Cold War crisis. The botched Bay of Pigs invasion humiliated him early; but in the Cuban Missile Crisis of 1962 he faced down the Soviet Union in the most dangerous confrontation of the nuclear age and emerged with the world stepped back from the brink. He launched the race to land a man on the Moon, founded the Peace Corps, and, haltingly at first, threw the weight of his office behind the cause of civil rights.

His youthful style and beautiful family lent his White House the glow later called “Camelot.” Then, on November 22, 1963, he was shot dead in Dallas, a trauma that scarred the nation and spawned endless mystery.

The leader of the movement whose demands for justice his administration came at last to champion, and who would be martyred not long after him, was Martin Luther King Jr.
