Heracles of Macedon

Son of Alexander the Great (c. 327–309 BC)

Heracles was a son of Alexander the Great by the Persian noblewoman Barsine, born during the great conqueror's eastern campaigns and named for the legendary hero from whom the Macedonian royal house claimed its descent. Had he been born in wedlock he might have inherited the world; as it was, his illegitimacy and his Persian blood left him on the margins of the empire his father had won.

When Alexander died suddenly in 323 BC, the boy was passed over. The generals raised up instead Alexander's posthumous legitimate son and his feeble half-brother as puppet kings, and Heracles was left to grow up quietly with his mother, far from the center of power, while the marshals tore the empire apart.

For years obscurity was his protection. But one by one Alexander's kin were destroyed in the succession wars, until Heracles, now a youth, remained the last of the conqueror's sons. In 309 BC an ambitious general produced him as a candidate for the throne of Macedonia—and that proved his death warrant.

For the ruler who held Macedonia would not suffer a living son of Alexander to threaten him; he bought off the boy's sponsor, and Heracles and Barsine were quietly murdered, ending Alexander's bloodline forever. The master of Macedonia who ordered that last killing was Cassander.
