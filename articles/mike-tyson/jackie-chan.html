Jackie Chan

Hong Kong actor and martial artist (born 1954)

Jackie Chan is one of the most beloved and recognizable film stars in the world, the Hong Kong actor who reinvented the martial-arts movie as breathtaking, death-defying comedy.

Trained from childhood in the brutal regimen of a Peking Opera school alongside lifelong collaborators like Sammo Hung, he broke into film as a stuntman—working, among other roles, on the movies of Bruce Lee. Cast at first as a would-be successor to Lee, he failed in that mold and found success only when he turned the formula on its head, blending dazzling kung fu with slapstick humor and astonishing stunts that he performed himself, breaking countless bones over the years.

Films like Drunken Master and the Police Story series made him a giant of Asian cinema, and the Rush Hour movies carried him to Hollywood stardom. Recognized everywhere on earth and honored at last with an Academy Award, he became a global ambassador for his art.

His genius for physical comedy and ingenious, dangerous stunts came not only from kung fu but from the silent-film clowns he studied and adored—above all the immortal creator of the Little Tramp, Charlie Chaplin.
