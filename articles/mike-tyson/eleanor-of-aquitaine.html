Eleanor of Aquitaine

Duchess of Aquitaine, queen of France and England (c. 1122–1204)

Eleanor of Aquitaine, born around 1122, was the greatest heiress in Europe and one of the most remarkable women of the Middle Ages—duchess of Aquitaine in her own right, and successively queen of France and queen of England. Beautiful, strong-willed and highly cultivated, she presided over courts famed for poetry and the new ideals of courtly love.

At fifteen she inherited the vast duchy of Aquitaine and at once married the heir to France, becoming queen as the wife of Louis VII. She even accompanied him on the disastrous Second Crusade. But the marriage was unhappy and produced no son, and in 1152 it was annulled. Within weeks Eleanor made a far bolder match, marrying Henry of Anjou, who the next year became Henry II of England. Their union joined her southern French lands to his to form the sprawling Angevin Empire.

The marriage was passionate and stormy. Eleanor bore Henry many children, among them the future kings Richard the Lionheart and John. But the couple fell out bitterly, and in 1173 Eleanor backed her sons in a great revolt against their father. Henry crushed the rising and kept his formidable queen under guard for some sixteen years.

Released on Henry's death in 1189, Eleanor entered her most powerful phase. She championed her favourite son, Richard, ruling England as regent while he was away on the Third Crusade and then a prisoner in Germany, and she personally raised his colossal ransom. Even in her seventies she crossed the Pyrenees to fetch a bride for the next generation. She finally retired to the abbey of Fontevraud, where she died in 1204, aged about eighty-two—wife of two kings and mother of two more.
