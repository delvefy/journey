Darius III

Last Achaemenid King of Persia (reigned 336–330 BC)

Darius III, a distant prince of the royal blood, was lifted onto the throne of Persia by the kingmaking eunuch Bagoas, who had cleared his path with poison—though Darius soon turned the tables and forced Bagoas to drink his own draught. He inherited the vast empire of Cyrus and Xerxes at the very moment a young king of Macedon was preparing to fall upon it.

Against Alexander the Great he proved tragically overmatched. At the river Granicus his frontier army was broken; at Issus, in 333 BC, Darius himself faced Alexander and lost his nerve, fleeing the field and leaving his mother, wife, and children to be captured—though Alexander treated them with famous courtesy. Two years later, at Gaugamela, Darius gathered the full might of the empire and was again routed, fleeing as Babylon, Susa, and Persepolis fell into Macedonian hands.

A fugitive in his own realm, he was seized by one of his satraps and murdered in flight, his killer proclaiming himself king in his place. Alexander, finding the body, gave the last Achaemenid a royal burial and hunted down the regicide.

So ended the empire that had ruled Asia for more than two centuries. The most capable of all the commanders who fought for Darius was not a Persian but a Greek soldier of fortune, the one general whose caution and skill Alexander is said to have respected—Memnon of Rhodes.
