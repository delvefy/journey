Abraham

Patriarch of the Abrahamic faiths (traditionally 2nd millennium BC)

Abraham is honored as the founding patriarch of the Hebrew people and the father of monotheism, revered in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam alike. The book of Genesis places his origins in Ur of the Chaldees in Mesopotamia, though his historical existence lies beyond the reach of evidence and belongs to sacred tradition.

According to that tradition, God called Abram to leave his homeland and travel to the land of Canaan, promising that he would become the father of a great nation and that through his offspring all the families of the earth would be blessed. This covenant—sealed with a new name, Abraham, and the sign of circumcision—became the foundation of Israel's relationship with God.

Though he and his wife Sarah were old and childless, Abraham fathered Ishmael by the servant Hagar and, against all expectation, Isaac by Sarah. The most searching episode of his life was the command to sacrifice Isaac: Abraham bound his son upon the altar and raised the knife, only to be stopped at the last instant, a ram provided in the boy's place. The story stands across the faiths as the supreme model of trust in God.

Through Isaac, tradition traces the people of Israel and the line that leads to David and to Jesus; through Ishmael, the Arab peoples and the lineage of Muhammad. So it is that three of the world's great religions look back to Abraham as their common father in faith.
