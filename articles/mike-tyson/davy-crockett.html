Davy Crockett

American frontiersman and congressman (1786–1836)

David Crockett, celebrated in legend as the King of the Wild Frontier, was a Tennessee backwoodsman whose homespun wit and frontier exploits made him a folk hero in his own lifetime. Born in the hills of East Tennessee, he won early notice as a hunter and storyteller and served as a scout in the Creek War under Andrew Jackson.

He parlayed his backwoods fame into politics, winning election to the Tennessee legislature and then to Congress. There he turned against his old commander: almost alone, he opposed Jackson's Indian Removal Act on moral grounds, and his break with the all-powerful president, along with his quarrels over frontier land policy, eventually cost him his seat. Embittered, he is said to have told his constituents that they could go to a warmer place while he went to Texas.

Go to Texas he did, arriving in time to join the revolt against Mexico. He took his place among the defenders of the Alamo mission at San Antonio, and when Santa Anna's army stormed it in March 1836, Crockett died with the rest of the garrison.

His death sealed his transformation into myth, retold in almanacs, stage plays, and a later wave of popular song and film. He fell in the same revolution that Sam Houston would win, and at his side in the doomed defence of the Alamo stood the knife-fighter Jim Bowie.
