Gian Galeazzo Visconti

First Duke of Milan (1351–1402)

Gian Galeazzo Visconti raised Milan to the height of its power and very nearly made himself master of all northern Italy. Son of Galeazzo II, he at first shared the Visconti dominions with his uncle Bernabò, a violent and feared tyrant. In 1385 the quiet, calculating nephew struck: he lured Bernabò into a friendly meeting outside the walls, seized him, and threw him into prison, where he soon died. At a stroke Gian Galeazzo held the whole inheritance alone.

What followed was a campaign of conquest pursued more by gold, marriage, and diplomacy than by the sword. One by one the cities of the north and center fell into his hands—Verona, Vicenza, Padua, then Pisa, Siena, Perugia, and Bologna—until Florence stood almost encircled, the last great obstacle to a Visconti kingdom of Italy.

In 1395 he crowned his ascent by purchasing from the Holy Roman Emperor the title of Duke of Milan, transforming his lordship into a recognized principality. His ambition expressed itself in stone as well: he began the vast Gothic cathedral of Milan and the Certosa of Pavia, monuments to a dynasty that meant to endure.

Then, in 1402, with Florence seemingly at his mercy, he died suddenly of plague. His assembled empire, held together only by his will, broke apart almost at once, and his sons inherited a diminished state. The uncle whom he had overthrown to win his sole dominion, and whose fall is still remembered as the great act of his rise, was Bernabò Visconti.
