Elizabeth of York

Queen of England, first Tudor queen consort (1466–1503)

Elizabeth of York, born in 1466, was the eldest daughter of the Yorkist king Edward IV and his queen, Elizabeth Woodville. Her childhood as a royal princess was shattered when her father died in 1483 and her uncle, Richard of Gloucester, seized the throne as Richard III, declared her and her siblings illegitimate, and confined her two young brothers—the Princes in the Tower—who were never seen again.

In this perilous time Elizabeth became the focus of a daring plan. Margaret Beaufort and Elizabeth Woodville conspired to betroth her to the exiled Lancastrian claimant, Henry Tudor, so uniting the rival houses of York and Lancaster. When Henry won the crown at Bosworth in 1485, he honoured the pledge, and in 1486 they married. The Tudor rose, white petals within red, became the emblem of their union and of the peace it brought after thirty years of civil war.

As queen, Elizabeth was gentle, pious and popular, content to leave politics to her husband and her formidable mother-in-law. She bore Henry several children who lived: Arthur, the heir who died young; the future Henry VIII; Margaret, who married into Scotland and founded the line of the Stuarts; and Mary, briefly queen of France. Through these children the blood of York flowed on into every later English monarch.

Elizabeth died in 1503, on her thirty-seventh birthday, of complications after the birth of a short-lived daughter. Henry VII, who had come to love her, was said to be heartbroken. Tradition holds that her serene image, drawn from life, became the model for the queen on the English deck of playing cards.
