Ferdinand I, Holy Roman Emperor

Holy Roman Emperor, founder of the Austrian Habsburg line (1503–1564)

Ferdinand I was the younger brother of Charles V and the founder of the Central European monarchy of the Habsburgs, the line that would rule Austria, Bohemia, and Hungary for centuries.

Born and raised in Spain, he stood at first in the shadow of his elder brother, who had inherited the whole vast patrimony of their house. But Charles, unable to govern everything himself, handed to Ferdinand the Austrian hereditary lands and had him chosen King of the Romans, heir to the imperial dignity.

Ferdinand's marriage to Anna of Bohemia and Hungary brought him a far greater destiny. When her brother, the king of Hungary, fell at the disastrous Battle of Mohács against the Turks in 1526, Ferdinand claimed his crowns and so joined Bohemia and Hungary to the house of Austria. With them came the full weight of the Ottoman war: Suleiman the Magnificent besieged Vienna in 1529, and Ferdinand spent his life defending the eastern marches of Christendom.

Within the Empire he was the architect of compromise. As his brother's deputy he negotiated the Peace of Augsburg in 1555, by which each German prince might fix the religion of his own land, bringing a hard-won truce to the wars of faith. When Charles abdicated, the imperial crown passed at last to Ferdinand.

He died in 1564 and was succeeded by his son Maximilian II. He and his brother were the sons of the tragic queen Joanna of Castile.
