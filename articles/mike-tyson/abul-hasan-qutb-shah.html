Abul Hasan Qutb Shah

Last Sultan of Golconda, called Tana Shah (r. 1672–1687)

Abul Hasan Qutb Shah, known by the affectionate name Tana Shah, was the eighth and last sultan of the Qutb Shahi dynasty of Golconda, whose fabulous wealth in diamonds made it a byword for riches across the world.

By tradition he had lived in obscurity as a disciple at a Sufi shrine before being raised, almost by chance, to the throne. Once king, he left much of the work of government to two gifted Hindu Brahmin brothers, the ministers Madanna and Akkanna, under whom the kingdom was efficiently run and its revenues swelled by the trade in gems from the mines of the Deccan, the only diamond fields then known.

In the great contest of the age he leaned toward the enemies of the Mughals, entering into alliance with the Marathas; Shivaji himself was received with honour at his court in 1677. This friendship with Hindu powers, and his reliance on Hindu ministers, gave the emperor Aurangzeb the pretext he sought.

In 1687 Aurangzeb marched on Golconda and besieged its mighty fortress. The walls held for months and defied every assault, until at last a bribed officer opened a gate by night and the imperial troops poured in. Abul Hasan was taken and, like the last sultan of Bijapur before him, imprisoned at Daulatabad, where he died about 1699.

So ended the dynasty that had founded the city of Hyderabad and raised its great gateway, the Charminar, under the most celebrated of its kings, Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah.
