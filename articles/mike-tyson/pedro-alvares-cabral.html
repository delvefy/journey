Pedro Álvares Cabral

Portuguese navigator, discoverer of Brazil (c. 1467–c. 1520)

Pedro Álvares Cabral was the Portuguese nobleman who led the second great armada to India and, on the way, became the first European to reach the shores of Brazil.

In 1500 King Manuel I gave him command of a powerful fleet to follow up Vasco da Gama's newly opened sea-route to the Indies. Standing well out into the Atlantic to catch the favourable winds, Cabral's ships ran further west than any before them and made landfall on an unknown coast, which he claimed for the Portuguese crown. That land was Brazil, and his chance arrival opened the way to its long Portuguese settlement.

Turning east again, the fleet was caught in a violent storm off the Cape of Good Hope, and several ships went down—among them that of the veteran Bartolomeu Dias, the very man who had first rounded the Cape years before. Cabral pressed on to India and reached Calicut, where he founded a trading post; but after his factory was attacked and his men killed, he bombarded the city and then secured a friendlier treaty at Cochin, sailing home with a rich cargo of spices.

For all the importance of his voyage, Cabral fell from favour at court and was passed over for the next expedition, which was given to da Gama. He retired from the sea and died in obscurity around 1520.

His fate was forever joined to that of the great pilot who perished in his fleet, Bartolomeu Dias.
