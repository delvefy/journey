Samuel Taylor Coleridge

English Romantic poet and critic (1772–1834)

Samuel Taylor Coleridge was a poet and thinker of dazzling, fragmentary genius, co-founder with Wordsworth of the English Romantic movement. The son of a country vicar, he was a brilliant talker and voracious reader whose mind ranged across poetry, philosophy, theology, and politics. With Wordsworth he produced the epoch-making Lyrical Ballads of 1798, to which he contributed his haunting masterpiece, The Rime of the Ancient Mariner.

His poetic gift, though never large in output, was unforgettable. The opium-dream vision of Kubla Khan—"In Xanadu did Kubla Khan a stately pleasure-dome decree"—and the eerie, unfinished Christabel remain among the marvels of English verse. But ill health and a ruinous addiction to opium scattered his energies and strained even his friendship with Wordsworth.

Coleridge was also one of the great critics and thinkers of his age. He traveled to Germany, immersed himself in the new idealist philosophy of Kant, and did much to carry that thought into the English-speaking world. His Biographia Literaria and his lectures on Shakespeare reshaped literary criticism.

Steeped in German letters, Coleridge translated Schiller and wrestled with the great drama of the age, drawn always to the towering presence who more than any other embodied the genius of German literature—Johann Wolfgang von Goethe.
