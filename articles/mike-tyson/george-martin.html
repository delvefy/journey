George Martin

British record producer, the “fifth Beatle” (1926–2016)

George Martin was the British record producer whose musical sophistication shaped the sound of the Beatles so profoundly that he was often called the “fifth Beatle.”

Classically trained and head of EMI's Parlophone label—where he first made his name producing comedy records—he took a chance on the Beatles in 1962 after other companies had turned them down. Over the years that followed he became the indispensable collaborator of John Lennon and Paul McCartney, translating their ideas into sound: the string quartet of “Yesterday,” the dizzying orchestral climax of “A Day in the Life,” the tape loops and studio trickery that made each album a leap forward. He worked the same magic for the songs of Ringo Starr and George Harrison.

Beyond the Beatles he founded his own studios and produced a wide range of artists and film scores across a long career, and he was knighted for his services to music. In 1997 he produced what became the best-selling single in history.

That record was a rewritten elegy for Princess Diana, performed at her funeral by the superstar whose decades of hits Martin helped crown—Elton John.
