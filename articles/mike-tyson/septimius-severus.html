Septimius Severus

Roman emperor (145–211)

Septimius Severus was the hard-driving general who restored order after the chaos that followed Commodus's murder and founded a new imperial dynasty. Born at Leptis Magna in Roman North Africa, of mixed Punic and Italian stock, he was the first emperor to come from that part of the world. He climbed the senatorial and military ladder and, as commander of the powerful Danube legions, was well placed when the throne fell vacant.

When the Praetorian Guard murdered the emperor Pertinax and shamelessly auctioned the empire, Severus marched on Rome, seized power in 193, and over the next four years crushed his rival claimants in hard-fought civil wars. To shore up his legitimacy he had himself declared, by a bold fiction, the son of Marcus Aurelius, grafting his family onto the revered Antonine line.

His rule rested frankly on the sword. He raised the soldiers' pay, enlarged the army, and pushed the Senate aside, advising his heirs to enrich the troops and care for little else. Abroad he warred successfully against Parthia and sacked its capital, and he strengthened the frontiers of Africa. With his formidable Syrian wife, Julia Domna, he presided over a brilliant court.

In his last years he campaigned in Britain to subdue its northern tribes, dying at York in 211. He left the empire jointly to his two bitterly rival sons, the elder of whom would soon murder the younger and rule alone—Caracalla.
