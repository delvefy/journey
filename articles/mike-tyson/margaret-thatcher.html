Margaret Thatcher

Prime Minister of the United Kingdom (1925–2013)

Margaret Thatcher was the first woman to serve as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom and the longest-serving of the twentieth century, a leader so forceful and divisive that her name became an ideology.

A grocer's daughter from Grantham who trained as a chemist and then a barrister, she rose to lead the Conservative Party and won power in 1979. Her programme—privatising state industries, curbing the trade unions, taming inflation, and shrinking the role of the state—was so distinctive that it is still called Thatcherism. The Soviet press, recoiling at her fierce anti-communism, gave her the name the Iron Lady, which she wore with pride.

She led Britain to victory in the Falklands War, broke a year-long miners' strike, and survived an IRA bomb that nearly killed her. Abroad she was the closest ally of the American president Ronald Reagan in the last battles of the Cold War, and an early voice urging the West to deal with the reformer Mikhail Gorbachev.

Revered by some and bitterly opposed by others, she was at last forced from office by her own party in 1990, after eleven years serving under Queen Elizabeth II. She died in 2013.
