Girolamo Savonarola

Italian Dominican friar and reformer (1452–1498)

Girolamo Savonarola was the fiery friar who, for a few astonishing years, made puritan Florence tremble at the word of God. Born in Ferrara and ordained a Dominican, he came to Florence and there unleashed sermons of such apocalyptic power—denouncing vice, luxury, tyranny, and above all the corruption of the Church under the Borgia pope Alexander VI—that vast crowds packed the cathedral to hear him prophesy doom and renewal.

When the French invasion of 1494 drove the Medici from Florence, Savonarola rose to become the spiritual master of a reborn republic. He turned the city toward austerity and piety, his followers gathering up luxuries, cosmetics, books, and paintings to be consumed in the famous Bonfire of the Vanities. Even great artists fell under his spell, Botticelli among them.

But he had made deadly enemies. Pope Alexander VI excommunicated him, and Savonarola's defiance, together with the Florentines' weariness of his harsh rule, sealed his fate. In 1498 he was seized, tortured, condemned, and hanged and burned in the great square of the city he had ruled.

To Machiavelli he became the example of the "unarmed prophet" who must fall; to others, a martyr and a forerunner of the Reformation. The brilliant humanist philosopher who had helped bring Savonarola to Florence and who, won over at the last, died wishing to be buried in the Dominican habit, was Pico della Mirandola.
