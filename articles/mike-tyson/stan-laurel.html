Stan Laurel

British-American comedian (1890–1965)

Stan Laurel was the comic genius behind half of Laurel and Hardy, the most beloved comedy duo in the history of film.

Born in the north of England, he served his apprenticeship in the music halls, where as a young man he was the understudy to Charlie Chaplin in Fred Karno's celebrated troupe—the two future legends crossing the Atlantic to America on the same boat. Laurel knocked about silent comedy for years, working at the Hal Roach studio among the same generation of clowns as Buster Keaton and Harold Lloyd, before fate paired him with a portly, courtly performer and changed screen history.

As the thin, bewildered, weeping innocent to Oliver Hardy's exasperated bumbler, Laurel created comedy of perfect timing and gentle absurdity that has never dated—the bowler hats, the necktie twiddle, the helpless “another fine mess.” Off-screen he was the team's true author, devising and shaping their gags with meticulous care, and the partnership glided effortlessly from silent films into sound in classics like Sons of the Desert and Way Out West.

So bound was he to his partner that when death dissolved the team he never performed again, and he was honored at the last with a special Academy Award. The other half of that immortal duo—the genial, slow-burning “Ollie” whose dignity Laurel forever punctured—was Oliver Hardy.
