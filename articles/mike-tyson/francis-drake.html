Francis Drake

English sea captain and privateer (c. 1540–1596)

Francis Drake was the most famous English seaman of the Elizabethan age, a privateer and explorer whom his countrymen hailed as a hero and the Spanish cursed as a pirate—"El Draque," the Dragon. Born of humble Devon stock, he went early to sea and made his fortune raiding Spanish ports and treasure fleets in the Caribbean, harrying the wealth that flowed from the New World to the coffers of Philip II of Spain.

His greatest exploit began in 1577, when he set out with a small squadron and returned nearly three years later having sailed around the world—the second expedition ever to circumnavigate the globe, and the first whose commander survived the whole voyage. Aboard his ship the Golden Hind he had plundered Spanish vessels along the Pacific coast of the Americas and claimed a stretch of California for his queen. When he came home laden with treasure, Elizabeth I knighted him on the deck of his own ship.

In 1587 he "singed the King of Spain's beard" with a daring raid on Cádiz, and in 1588 he served as a vice admiral in the fleet that scattered the great Spanish Armada, sealing his legend. Restless to the last, he died of dysentery in 1596 during a final, unsuccessful raid in the Caribbean and was buried at sea off the coast of Panama.

Drake's voyage around the world followed a path opened a generation earlier by the Portuguese navigator whose expedition first encircled the globe—Ferdinand Magellan.
