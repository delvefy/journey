Katharine Hepburn

American film actress (1907–2003)

Katharine Hepburn was one of the supreme actresses of the American screen, the holder of a record four Academy Awards for Best Actress and, by the reckoning of the American Film Institute, the greatest female star of classic Hollywood.

Fiercely independent, patrician, and sharp-tongued, she defied the conventions of the studio era—wearing trousers, guarding her privacy, and playing strong, clever women. After an early Oscar for Morning Glory she shone in the screwball comedies Bringing Up Baby and The Philadelphia Story, the latter a triumphant comeback she engineered herself.

Her enduring partnership, on screen and off, was with Spencer Tracy, with whom she made nine films across a quarter-century, from Adam's Rib to Guess Who's Coming to Dinner. She matched Humphrey Bogart in The African Queen, and won further Oscars for The Lion in Winter and On Golden Pond, acting into her eighties.

Late in her career she joined the great stage actor Laurence Olivier in the television film Love Among the Ruins, a pairing of two titans that won them both awards.

She died in 2003 at the age of ninety-six, an emblem of dignity and self-possession. Among her most delightful screen partners was the incomparable Cary Grant.
