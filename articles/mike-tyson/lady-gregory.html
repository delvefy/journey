Lady Gregory

Irish dramatist and co-founder of the Abbey Theatre (1852–1932)

Lady Gregory was the dramatist, folklorist and tireless organizer who, more than anyone, made the Abbey Theatre and the Irish Literary Revival a reality.

Born Augusta Persse into the Galway gentry and widowed early after marrying the former colonial governor Sir William Gregory, she found her vocation in middle age in the Irish language and the folklore of her native west. Her estate at Coole Park became the gathering place of a literary movement, and her retellings of the old Gaelic sagas helped give that movement its mythic material.

In 1899, with W. B. Yeats and others, she founded the Irish Literary Theatre, which by 1904 had become the Abbey, the national theatre of Ireland. For nearly three decades she was its most devoted director and manager, and a popular playwright in her own right with brisk one-act comedies. She was also a fierce defender of artistic freedom: she staged George Bernard Shaw's play The Shewing-Up of Blanco Posnet in open defiance of the British censor, and decades later it was she who welcomed the young Sean O'Casey to the Abbey stage.

Her loyalty was tested most severely during the riots that greeted one of her playwrights' starkly realistic visions of the Irish peasantry, when she stood firmly behind the work against an outraged public. She kept the theatre alive through every quarrel and financial crisis until her death at Coole in 1932. The brilliant, short-lived dramatist whose Playboy of the Western World had caused that uproar, and whom she championed to the last, was J. M. Synge.
