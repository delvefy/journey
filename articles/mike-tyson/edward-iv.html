Edward IV

Yorkist king of England (1442–1483)

Edward IV, born in 1442, was the eldest surviving son of Richard, Duke of York, the great Yorkist claimant whose feud with the house of Lancaster plunged England into the Wars of the Roses. When his father was killed in 1460, the eighteen-year-old Edward took up the cause, and with the military backing of his cousin Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick—"the Kingmaker"—he seized the crown from the saintly but feeble Henry VI. His crushing victory in the snow at Towton in 1461 confirmed him as king.

Tall, handsome and able, Edward proved a far stronger ruler than his Lancastrian rival. But his secret marriage in 1464 to Elizabeth Woodville, a commoner's widow, outraged Warwick, who had been negotiating a French match. The Kingmaker turned against him, briefly driving Edward into exile and restoring Henry VI in 1470. Edward returned the next year, destroyed Warwick at the Battle of Barnet, and annihilated the Lancastrian army at Tewkesbury. Henry VI died in the Tower soon after, almost certainly murdered.

The second half of Edward's reign was peaceful and prosperous. He restored royal finances, encouraged trade, and patronised the new craft of printing under William Caxton, while indulging freely in the pleasures of the table and the bedchamber. His health broke early, and he died suddenly in 1483, only forty.

His death proved a catastrophe for his line. He left two young sons under the protection of his brother Richard of Gloucester, who within months had seized the throne as Richard III and consigned the boys to oblivion in the Tower. Yet through his daughter Elizabeth of York, who married Henry VII, Edward's blood flowed on into the Tudor dynasty.
