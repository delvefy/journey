Toni Morrison

American novelist and Nobel laureate (1931–2019)

Toni Morrison was the towering American novelist whose lyrical, unflinching books about Black life and history won her the Nobel Prize in Literature.

Born Chloe Wofford in Ohio, she studied at Howard and Cornell—writing her thesis on the theme of alienation in modernist fiction—before spending nearly two decades as an editor at Random House, where she quietly reshaped American letters by championing Black writers. In that role she shepherded into print the autobiographies of figures such as the boxer Muhammad Ali, even as she began producing her own fiction at night.

Her novels—The Bluest Eye, Song of Solomon, and above all Beloved, the harrowing tale of a fugitive from slavery—fused the cadences of Black oral tradition with the dreamlike intensity she admired in Gabriel García Márquez. Beloved won the Pulitzer Prize, and in 1993 Morrison became the first Black woman to win the Nobel. Her reach widened further when Oprah Winfrey made her a fixture of her book club and brought Beloved to the screen; she also delivered the eulogy at the funeral of her friend James Baldwin.

She died in 2019, mourned as a giant of world literature. The Southern novelist of broken families and haunted history whose work she had studied as a young woman, and who left a deep mark on her own, was William Faulkner.
