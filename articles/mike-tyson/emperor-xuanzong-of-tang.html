Emperor Xuanzong of Tang

Tang emperor of the golden age, brought down by rebellion (685–762; r. 712–756)

Li Longji, the emperor known to history as Xuanzong and to poets as the “Brilliant Emperor,” presided over the most dazzling era of the Tang dynasty. He seized power amid the palace coups that followed the turbulent reigns of his grandmother and aunt, and his long Kaiyuan reign became a byword for prosperity, order, and cultural splendour. With able ministers he reformed the administration and secured the frontiers, and his capital, Chang'an, grew into the greatest city on earth.

He was himself a connoisseur of music and theatre, founding the Pear Garden academy that earned him lasting fame as the patron of Chinese opera, and his court drew the finest talents of the age—among them the poets Li Bai and Du Fu. For a generation the empire flourished as never before.

But in his later years the emperor grew complacent and besotted with his beautiful consort Yang Guifei, allowing power to slip to her relatives and favourites. In 755 the trusted frontier general An Lushan rose in a great rebellion that shattered the dynasty's peace. Xuanzong fled toward the safety of Sichuan, and at the Mawei post station his own mutinous guards forced him to consent to the death of Yang Guifei.

Broken by the loss, he abdicated to his son and lived out his days as a grieving retired emperor, dying in 762. He had first climbed to power in the shadow of his formidable grandmother, the only woman ever to rule China as emperor in her own name—Wu Zetian.
