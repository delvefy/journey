William Shakespeare

English playwright and poet (1564–1616)

William Shakespeare, born in 1564 in the market town of Stratford-upon-Avon, is widely regarded as the greatest writer in the English language and the world's pre-eminent dramatist. The son of a glover, he married Anne Hathaway as a young man and, by the early 1590s, had made his way to London as an actor and playwright in the city's booming theaters.

He became a leading member and part-owner of the playing company known as the Lord Chamberlain's Men, which under King James I was renamed the King's Men, and he held a share in the famous Globe Theatre on the south bank of the Thames. Over some two decades he wrote around thirty-eight plays and a sequence of 154 sonnets.

His range was extraordinary. He wrote sparkling comedies such as A Midsummer Night's Dream and Twelfth Night, English histories like Henry V and Richard III, and the towering tragedies—Hamlet, Macbeth, King Lear, and Othello—that probe ambition, madness, jealousy, and grief. Drawing on the biographer Plutarch, he dramatized the Roman world in Julius Caesar and Antony and Cleopatra. His mastery of character and his shaping of the English tongue, coining countless words and phrases still in use, have never been surpassed.

Shakespeare prospered, retired to a fine house in Stratford, and died in 1616. Seven years later two of his colleagues gathered his plays into the First Folio, the volume that preserved his work for the world. His plays remain the most performed and studied in history.
