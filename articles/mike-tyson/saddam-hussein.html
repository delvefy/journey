Saddam Hussein

President of Iraq, 1979–2003 (1937–2006)

Saddam Hussein was the brutal dictator of Iraq for nearly a quarter-century, a ruler whose ambition embroiled his country in three catastrophic wars and ended on the gallows.

Born near Tikrit, he rose through the Ba'ath Party by ruthlessness and cunning, seizing full power in 1979. He built a totalitarian police state and a vast cult of personality modelled consciously on Joseph Stalin, crushing the Kurds with poison gas and drowning all dissent in blood. Casting himself as heir to Mesopotamia's ancient glory, he rebuilt the ruins of Babylon in the name of Nebuchadnezzar and styled himself a new Saladin, the Tikriti warrior who had once driven the crusaders from Jerusalem.

His wars defined and finally destroyed him. He launched an eight-year struggle against the Iran of Ruhollah Khomeini that bled both nations white. Then in 1990 he invaded and annexed Kuwait, provoking the world. A decade of crippling sanctions followed, and in 2003 a fresh invasion led by George W. Bush, justified by weapons that were never found, toppled his regime. Pulled from a hole in the ground, he was tried by his countrymen and hanged in 2006.

But it was his seizure of Kuwait that had first brought the full weight of the world's armies against him, when a vast coalition assembled to drive his forces out in the Gulf War of 1991. The American president who built that coalition and commanded that war was George H. W. Bush.
