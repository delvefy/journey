Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Indian nationalist leader (1856–1920)

Bal Gangadhar Tilak, honored as "Lokmanya"—accepted by the people—was among the first and fiercest leaders of the Indian struggle for independence, a full generation before Mahatma Gandhi. Born in Maharashtra in 1856, he became a teacher, scholar, and crusading journalist, founding newspapers through which he attacked British rule and roused national feeling.

Within the Indian National Congress he led the radical "extremist" wing against the cautious moderates, insisting that freedom would not be granted but must be demanded. His ringing slogan—"Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it"—became a watchword of the movement. To turn elite politics into a mass cause, he transformed religious and historical celebration into nationalist theater, founding public festivals and reviving the memory of the Maratha hero Shivaji as a symbol of resistance to foreign domination.

The British jailed him repeatedly, including a long and harsh imprisonment in Mandalay for sedition, which only deepened his stature. After his release he helped launch the Home Rule League with Annie Besant and worked, in the Lucknow Pact of 1916, to bring Hindus and Muslims—cooperating with the young Muhammad Ali Jinnah—into a common front.

Tilak died in 1920, at the very moment Gandhi was rising to lead the national movement into its decisive phase. He is remembered as a father of militant Indian nationalism, who carried the cause from the drawing room to the street.
