Robert Devereux, 2nd Earl of Essex

English courtier and soldier (1565–1601)

Robert Devereux became the 2nd Earl of Essex as a boy on his father's death, and was brought to court under the wing of his stepfather, Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester. Tall, handsome and headstrong, he rose swiftly to become the last great favourite of the ageing Elizabeth I, who indulged his charm even as his arrogance made him dangerous enemies, chief among them Walter Raleigh and the Cecils.

He won popular glory with the daring raid on Cádiz in 1596, and gathered about him a brilliant following that included the clever lawyer Francis Bacon as his friend and adviser. Yet his judgement never matched his ambition.

In 1599 he was sent to crush rebellion in Ireland, but bungled the campaign, made an unauthorised truce with the rebel earl, and deserted his post to burst unannounced into the queen's chamber. Disgraced and stripped of his lucrative privileges, he grew desperate.

In February 1601 he gambled everything on a wild attempt to raise the City of London and seize the court. The rising collapsed within hours. Tried for treason—and prosecuted, to general shock, by his old friend Bacon—he was convicted and beheaded at the Tower. Among the lesser men swept up in his doomed revolt was the young Robert Catesby, who would later plot a far deadlier treason of his own.
