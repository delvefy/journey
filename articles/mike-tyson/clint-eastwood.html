Clint Eastwood

American actor and film director (born 1930)

Clint Eastwood is one of the most durable and influential figures in the history of the cinema, famous first as an actor and then, for an even longer span, as a director. After early television work he became an international star as the squinting, poncho-clad “Man with No Name” in a trilogy of Italian-made Westerns, and he sealed his tough-guy image as the hard-bitten detective Dirty Harry, growling his challenge to a cornered gunman.

From the 1970s he built a parallel career behind the camera that ultimately eclipsed even his stardom. As a director he favoured a spare, swift, unsentimental style, and he won the Academy Award for Best Director twice—for the elegiac anti-Western Unforgiven and for the boxing tragedy Million Dollar Baby, both of which also won Best Picture.

His later films ranged widely: the murder mystery Mystic River, the Japanese-perspective war drama Letters from Iwo Jima, the brooding Gran Torino, and the controversial American Sniper. He continued to direct, with remarkable vigour, well into his nineties, and even served a term as mayor of his California town.

The actor who starred for him in both Unforgiven and Million Dollar Baby, winning an Academy Award under his direction, was Morgan Freeman.
