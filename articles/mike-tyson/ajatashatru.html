Ajatashatru

King of Magadha of the Haryanka dynasty (reigned c. 492–460 BC)

Ajatashatru carried the kingdom of Magadha to the height of its early power. He was the son of Bimbisara, and by the traditions of both the Buddhists and the Jains he came to the throne by violence—imprisoning his aged father and seizing the crown, a deed the Buddhist accounts blame on the scheming of the Buddha's renegade cousin Devadatta.

Once king, he proved a formidable and ruthless ruler. He warred against his neighbour and kinsman Prasenajit of Kosala, and he waged a long campaign against the powerful Licchavi confederacy centred on Vaishali, which he finally broke; tradition remembers him deploying new engines of war, a great catapult and a covered chariot armed with a mace. To guard his frontier on the Ganges he fortified the village of Pataligrama, the seed of the imperial city of Pataliputra that later empires would make their capital.

He lived amid the religious awakening of his age. The Buddha died during his reign, and the Buddhist texts portray the guilt-haunted king visiting the sage to ask what good the holy life might bring; the Jains likewise count him, under the name Kunika, among their devotees. After the Buddha's death he is said to have received a portion of the relics and to have given his protection to the first great council of monks, gathered to fix the master's teachings in memory.

At that council the foremost reciter, who had been the Buddha's constant attendant and could repeat his every discourse, was Ananda.
