Princess Diana

Princess of Wales (1961–1997)

Diana, Princess of Wales, was the most famous and most photographed woman of her age, whose glamour, compassion and tragic death transfixed the world.

Born Lady Diana Spencer into the English aristocracy, she married the heir to the British throne in 1981 in a wedding watched by some 750 million people, and the shy nursery assistant was transformed overnight into a global icon. She bore two sons who would carry the royal line forward, and she brought to the stiff institution a warmth that captivated the public—shaking the hands of AIDS patients when others recoiled, and walking through live minefields to draw the world's attention to their victims.

But the fairytale was hollow. Her marriage was loveless and unhappy, shadowed by her husband's attachment to another woman, and it ended in a painful public separation and divorce. Freed from the palace yet hounded ceaselessly by the press, she remained the object of obsessive global fascination.

In August 1997 she was killed in a car crash in Paris while being pursued by photographers, and the wave of public grief that followed shook the monarchy to its foundations—her funeral, with Elton John's reworked elegy, watched by billions, just days before the death of her friend Mother Teresa. The husband from whom she had parted, the man who would in time become king, was Charles—King Charles III.
