Giovanni Pisano

Italian Gothic sculptor (c. 1250–1315)

Giovanni Pisano, born around 1250, was the foremost Italian sculptor of his age and a key figure in the passage from medieval to Renaissance art. He was the son and pupil of Nicola Pisano, who had revived the dignity of classical sculpture, and Giovanni carried that inheritance forward, fusing his father's gravity with the emotional intensity and dynamic movement of French Gothic art, which he probably knew at first hand.

His genius is displayed above all in a series of great carved pulpits. With his father he worked on the pulpit of Siena Cathedral and the Fontana Maggiore in Perugia; alone he created the magnificent pulpits of the church of Sant'Andrea in Pistoia and of Pisa Cathedral, crowded with figures of astonishing energy and pathos. As architect and chief sculptor of the facade of Siena Cathedral, he peopled it with prophets and sibyls who lean and turn as if alive.

Around 1305 Giovanni carved a serene marble Madonna and Child for the Scrovegni Chapel in Padua—the very chapel whose walls Giotto was then covering with his revolutionary frescoes, so that the two greatest artists of the age met in a single building.

Giovanni Pisano died around 1315. His expressive, deeply human figures influenced not only later sculptors but painters such as the Lorenzetti, who drew on his drama and tenderness. With his father Nicola, he stands at the headwaters of the great Italian tradition in sculpture, and Vasari honoured both Pisani among the first revivers of the arts.
