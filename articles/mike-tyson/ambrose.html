Ambrose

Bishop of Milan, Doctor of the Church (c. 339–397)

Ambrose was the great bishop of Milan who asserted the authority of the Church over the most powerful rulers of the Roman world. The son of a high imperial official, he was trained in law and serving as the Roman governor of northern Italy when, in 374, the bishopric of Milan fell vacant amid bitter strife between Catholics and Arians. The people spontaneously acclaimed the popular and even-handed governor as their bishop—though he had not yet even been baptized.

Giving away his wealth and plunging into the study of scripture and theology, Ambrose became one of the four great Latin Doctors of the Church. He was a fearless champion of Nicene orthodoxy against the Arians and an even bolder defender of the Church against the state. He refused an imperial demand to surrender a basilica to the Arian court, and most famously he compelled the emperor himself to do public penance after a massacre of civilians at Thessalonica—a dramatic assertion that even emperors stood under the moral law of God.

A magnetic preacher, Ambrose drew to the faith the brilliant young rhetorician Augustine of Hippo, whom he baptized in 387, shaping the greatest theologian of the Latin Church. He also enriched Christian worship by introducing congregational hymn-singing.

Ambrose died in 397, having raised the prestige of the episcopate to new heights. The emperor whom he famously brought to his knees in penance, and who made Nicene Christianity the official religion of Rome, was Theodosius I.
