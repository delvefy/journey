Barack Obama

44th President of the United States (born 1961)

Barack Obama became, in 2009, the first African American president of the United States, the culmination of a movement for which Martin Luther King and others had struggled and died. Born in Hawaii to a Kenyan father and an American mother, he worked as a community organizer in Chicago, excelled at Harvard Law, and rose with astonishing speed from the Illinois legislature to the United States Senate to the White House, carried by a message of hope and change and by his own gifts as an orator.

He consciously took Abraham Lincoln as his model, launching his campaign in Lincoln's Springfield and taking the oath on Lincoln's Bible, and he revered Nelson Mandela as the hero of his political awakening. In office he steered the country out of the financial crisis of 2008, enacted the sweeping health-care reform that bears his name, and ordered the raid that killed Osama bin Laden. Abroad he reached a nuclear accord with Iran, joined the Paris climate agreement, and opened relations with Cuba; he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in his first year.

His presidency drew fierce opposition as well as devotion, and the cool, professorial style that admirers prized, critics found aloof. He defeated two Republican nominees to win and keep the office before handing it on after two terms.

The man who succeeded him in the White House—who had risen in part by attacking him and who became his great political antagonist—was Donald Trump.
