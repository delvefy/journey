Aisha

Wife of Muhammad, "Mother of the Believers" (c. 614–678)

Aisha bint Abi Bakr, born around 614, was the daughter of Abu Bakr and a wife of the Prophet Muhammad, and one of the most important and influential women in the history of Islam. Married to Muhammad as a young girl, she became, by most accounts, his favourite wife. Their household in Medina placed her at the very centre of the early Muslim community, and she is honoured by Sunni Muslims with the title "Mother of the Believers."

When Muhammad died in 632—in Aisha's chamber, by tradition, where he was also buried—she was still young, but her prestige as the Prophet's widow and the daughter of the first caliph made her a formidable figure. She became one of the foremost authorities on Muhammad's words and deeds, transmitting over two thousand hadith, and her testimony shaped Islamic law, ritual and the memory of the Prophet's life. Scholars came to her for rulings.

She also stepped, fatefully, into politics. After the murder of the third caliph Uthman, Aisha opposed the new caliph Ali, demanding vengeance for Uthman's blood. In 656 she rode at the head of an army against him at the Battle of the Camel near Basra—so called because the fiercest fighting raged around the camel on which she sat in her litter. Ali's forces won; Aisha was captured and respectfully escorted back to Medina.

She withdrew from politics thereafter, devoting her remaining years to teaching and the transmission of religious knowledge until her death around 678. Learned, strong-willed and revered, Aisha left a deep mark on Islamic scholarship and remains one of its most celebrated women.
