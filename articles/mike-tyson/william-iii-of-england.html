William III of England

Prince of Orange and King of England (1650–1702)

William was born in The Hague in 1650, the posthumous son of the Prince of Orange and of Mary, daughter of King Charles I of England, and great-grandson of William the Silent. In 1672, when the armies of Louis XIV overran the Dutch Republic in its "year of disaster," the young prince was raised to stadtholder and rallied his countrymen, opening the dykes to halt the French and beginning a lifelong struggle against French power.

He made himself the tireless organiser of the European coalitions against Louis XIV, and in 1677 strengthened his English ties by marrying his cousin Mary, the Protestant daughter of the future James II. When James, now king, alarmed England with his Catholicism and the birth of a Catholic heir, leading Protestants invited William to intervene.

In 1688 he landed with a Dutch army; James's support melted away and he fled to France. This "Glorious Revolution" placed William and Mary on the throne as joint sovereigns in 1689, but only after they accepted the Bill of Rights, which bound the crown to Parliament and shaped Britain's constitutional monarchy. William crushed James's cause in Ireland at the Battle of the Boyne in 1690.

The rest of his reign was given to war and diplomacy against France. Mary died in 1694, and William, childless, ruled alone until a fall from his horse killed him in 1702. He was succeeded by his sister-in-law Anne.
