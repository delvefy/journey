Hugo van der Goes

Flemish painter of Ghent (c. 1440–1482)

Hugo van der Goes, born around 1440, was, after the deaths of van Eyck and van der Weyden, the foremost painter of the southern Netherlands. He became a master in the painters' guild of Ghent around 1467 and won a high reputation for ambitious, emotionally powerful religious works that built on the achievements of his great Flemish predecessors.

His towering achievement is the Portinari Altarpiece, painted around 1475 for Tommaso Portinari, the agent of the Medici bank in Bruges. This enormous triptych of the Adoration of the Shepherds, with its monumental figures, its vividly individual peasants and its symbolic still life of flowers, was shipped to Florence and installed in a church there. Italian painters were struck by its northern realism and emotional force, and its influence rippled through Florentine art, leaving its mark on Domenico Ghirlandaio and others.

At the height of his fame, van der Goes withdrew from the world. Around 1477 he entered a monastery near Brussels as a lay brother, though he continued to paint and to receive distinguished visitors. There, some years later, he was seized by a profound melancholy and a conviction that he was damned, attempting to harm himself; a near-contemporary account by a fellow monk describes his breakdown in vivid detail. He died in 1482.

Van der Goes left few certain works, but his grandeur of conception and psychological depth mark him as one of the most original of the early Netherlandish masters—and, through the Portinari Altarpiece, a rare northern bridge into the art of the Italian Renaissance.
