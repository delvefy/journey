Rosa Luxemburg

Polish-German Marxist revolutionary (1871–1919)

Rosa Luxemburg was one of the most brilliant theorists and fiercest militants of the international socialist movement. Born in Russian Poland to a Jewish family in 1871, she took a doctorate in Switzerland and made her way to Germany, where she became a leading voice of the Social Democrats and of the Second International, writing and speaking in several languages with rare force and clarity.

Holding fast to revolutionary Marxism, she attacked the "revisionists" who would soften it into mere reform, analysed the power of the mass strike, and in her major economic work probed the dynamics of capitalist expansion and imperialism. She was also a democrat to the core: in her debates with Lenin she condemned the rule of a narrow party elite and warned that "freedom is always the freedom of the one who thinks differently."

Above all she opposed the slaughter of the First World War, breaking with her own party for supporting it and going to prison for her defiance. With Karl Liebknecht she founded the Spartacus League—named for the rebel gladiator—which grew into the German Communist Party.

When the Spartacist rising in Berlin was crushed in January 1919, Luxemburg and Liebknecht were seized and murdered by counter-revolutionary soldiers, and her body was flung into a canal. She became a lasting martyr of the socialist left. The whole structure of her thought rested on the foundations laid by Karl Marx and his lifelong collaborator, Friedrich Engels.
