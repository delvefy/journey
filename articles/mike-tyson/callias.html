Callias

Athenian aristocrat and diplomat (5th century BC)

Callias, nicknamed “the Rich,” was by repute the wealthiest man in Athens, his fortune drawn from the silver mines of Laurium. He belonged to the priestly Kerykes family and held the hereditary honor of torchbearer at the sacred Mysteries of Eleusis, a dignity that placed him among the first men of the city. He married Elpinice, sister of Cimon, binding his wealth to one of Athens's proudest houses.

For all his riches, his lasting fame rests on a single act of statesmanship. After decades of war between the Greeks and the Persian empire—a struggle in which Cimon's victories had broken Persian power in the Aegean—Callias was sent eastward to make terms. The agreement he is said to have brought home, the Peace of Callias, recognized the freedom of the Greek cities of Asia and barred the Persian fleet from the Aegean, drawing a line under two generations of conflict.

Some later writers doubted whether so formal a treaty had ever truly been signed, and the question is argued still; but the tradition of Callias's embassy endured as the diplomatic seal upon the Greek triumph. He moved in the great world of Periclean Athens, his house a meeting-place of the powerful and the learned.

The peace bore his name, but it was struck with a mightier party across the sea: the Great King of Persia, son of the Xerxes who had once invaded Greece, with whom Callias treated on behalf of victorious Athens—Artaxerxes I.
