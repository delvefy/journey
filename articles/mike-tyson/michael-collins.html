Michael Collins

Irish revolutionary leader (1890–1922)

Michael Collins was the guerrilla mastermind of the Irish War of Independence and the negotiator whose treaty with Britain both created the Irish state and cost him his life.

Born on a farm in West Cork, he spent his early career as a clerk in London, where he joined the secret Irish Republican Brotherhood. He fought in the General Post Office during the Easter Rising of 1916 and emerged from internment as the most formidable organizer of the independence movement. As Director of Intelligence he built a ruthless network of informers and assassins that blinded and demoralized the British administration in Dublin, while as Minister for Finance he raised the funds that kept the rebel Dáil alive. Hunted with a price on his head, “the Big Fellow” became a legend during the war of 1919–1921.

When a truce came, he was sent reluctantly to London as a plenipotentiary and helped negotiate the Anglo-Irish Treaty with David Lloyd George and Winston Churchill. The treaty granted a self-governing Irish Free State but stopped short of a full republic and partitioned the north; signing it, Collins remarked that he had signed his own death warrant.

The bargain split the revolutionary movement. Collins became chairman of the Provisional Government and commander-in-chief of its army, pressed by Churchill to crush the republicans who rejected the treaty. In the civil war that followed he was killed in an ambush at Béal na Bláth in his native Cork in August 1922, only thirty-one years old. The comrade-turned-adversary who led the opponents of his treaty was Éamon de Valera.
