Braccio da Montone

Italian condottiero, lord of Perugia (1368–1424)

Andrea Fortebraccio, known as Braccio da Montone, was the most brilliant and ambitious of the early condottieri, a captain who dreamed not merely of pay but of a kingdom of his own. Born to an exiled noble family near Perugia and schooled under the first great organizer of Italian mercenary companies, he developed a fast, slashing style of cavalry warfare that made his “Bracceschi” the most admired soldiers in Italy.

His lifelong rival was Muzio Attendolo Sforza, whose own school of war, the Sforzeschi, opposed Braccio's in battle after battle; between them the two captains and their pupils dominated the fighting of the age. In 1416 Braccio seized Perugia and made himself its lord, carving out a princely state across Umbria and the Marches and treating with popes as a power in his own right.

The great prize of the south drew him in at the last. In the tangled war for the throne of Naples he took the side of Queen Joanna II and her chosen heir, the King of Aragon, against an Angevin and papal coalition. Laying siege to the mountain city of L'Aquila, he was caught by a relief army—among its commanders the young Sforza—and in 1424 was defeated and mortally wounded.

His rival Muzio had drowned only weeks before; so within a single year the two titans of Italian warfare both perished. The Aragonese king whose cause Braccio had championed in the Neapolitan war, and who would go on to win the throne of Naples for himself, was Alfonso V of Aragon.
