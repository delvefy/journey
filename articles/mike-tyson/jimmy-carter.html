Jimmy Carter

39th President of the United States (1924–2024)

Jimmy Carter was the 39th President of the United States, a one-term leader whose presidency foundered abroad but who became, in his long afterlife, perhaps the most admired ex-president in American history.

A peanut farmer and former naval officer from Plains, Georgia, he rode the post-Watergate desire for honesty into the White House in 1976. His crowning achievement came in 1978, when he brought Anwar Sadat of Egypt and Israel's Menachem Begin together at Camp David and mediated, through thirteen tense days, the framework for peace between their nations. He also concluded the Panama Canal treaties and a strategic-arms agreement with the Soviets.

But economic stagnation, soaring oil prices and a mood of national gloom sapped his standing at home. The decisive blow came from Iran, where a revolution toppled the Shah and a mob seized the American embassy, holding its staff hostage for 444 days; a botched rescue mission only deepened the humiliation. Worn down by the crisis, he lost the 1980 election in a landslide to Ronald Reagan.

Out of office he reinvented himself as a tireless humanitarian and peacemaker—building houses for the poor, monitoring elections, and brokering disputes around the globe—work crowned by the Nobel Peace Prize in 2002. He lived to be a hundred, the longest-lived of all American presidents. The revolutionary cleric whose seizure of power in Iran had wrecked his presidency was the Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini.
