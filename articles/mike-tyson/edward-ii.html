Edward II

King of England (1284–1327)

Edward II was born at Caernarfon, the first English heir to bear the title Prince of Wales, and he inherited from his fearsome father Edward I a strong kingdom that he proved unable to hold. Where the old king had been a soldier and lawgiver, the son was drawn to comforts, to the company of favorites, and to pursuits his barons thought unbecoming a king.

His reign was poisoned by these attachments. The Gascon knight Piers Gaveston, raised high and openly preferred, so enraged the magnates that they seized and beheaded him in 1312. Two years later Edward led a great army north and was humiliated at Bannockburn by Robert the Bruce, throwing away his father's hard-won grip on Scotland.

In his later years he leaned on a new set of favorites, the grasping Despensers, until baronial revolt and royal vengeance filled the land with executions. The final blow came from his own household. His queen, Isabella of France, slighted and embittered, crossed to her homeland, took the exiled baron Roger Mortimer as her ally and lover, and returned at the head of an invading force in 1326.

The kingdom abandoned him. In 1327 Edward was forced to surrender the crown to his young son, Edward III, and was shut up in Berkeley Castle, where he was murdered before the year was out, by tradition with peculiar cruelty. The queen who deposed and destroyed him, and ruled in her son's name with her lover, was Isabella of France.
