Guy Fawkes

English conspirator of the Gunpowder Plot (1570–1606)

Guy Fawkes was born in York in 1570 and raised a Protestant, but converted to Catholicism as a young man. Seeking a cause for his faith, he left England to fight for Catholic Spain in its long war in the Netherlands, where he gained a soldier's hard experience and a working knowledge of gunpowder and siege-craft, and took the Italian form of his name, Guido.

Returning home, he was drawn by Robert Catesby into a desperate conspiracy. The plotters meant to destroy King James I and the Protestant lords at a single stroke by blowing up the House of Lords at the State Opening of Parliament, and so to spark a Catholic rising. Because of his military skill, Fawkes was given charge of the explosives—some three dozen barrels of gunpowder concealed in a cellar beneath the chamber.

The plot was betrayed by an anonymous warning, and in the small hours of 5 November 1605 Fawkes was seized beside his barrels, fuses at the ready. Racked and interrogated, he at last gave up the names of his fellow conspirators, and was condemned for treason.

At his execution early in 1606 he jumped or fell from the scaffold and broke his neck, sparing himself the full horror of the traitor's death. The failure of the plot has been marked in Britain ever since by the bonfires of the fifth of November, and his masked likeness has become a worldwide emblem of defiance.
