Steve McQueen

American actor (1930–1980)

Steve McQueen was the embodiment of screen cool, a laconic, rebellious leading man who at his height was the highest-paid movie star in the world.

After a hard, rootless youth that included reform school, he found his way to acting and to a persona all his own—tough, watchful, and impatient of authority. He rode to fame in The Magnificent Seven and the prisoner-of-war epic The Great Escape, with its unforgettable motorcycle leap, and he sealed his legend with the detective thriller Bullitt, whose roaring chase through the streets of San Francisco set the standard for every car chase to come.

He played the elegant thief of The Thomas Crown Affair, the convict of Papillon alongside Dustin Hoffman, and joined Paul Newman in the disaster spectacle The Towering Inferno. A genuine and gifted racer of cars and motorcycles, he poured that passion into the film Le Mans.

He died of cancer in 1980, only fifty years old, his cool undimmed. His co-star in the grim prison saga Papillon had been Dustin Hoffman.
