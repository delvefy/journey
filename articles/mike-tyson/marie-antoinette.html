Marie Antoinette

Queen of France (1755–1793)

Marie Antoinette, born an archduchess of Austria and youngest daughter of the formidable Empress Maria Theresa, was sent to France at fourteen to marry the heir to the throne and seal an alliance between two old enemies. When her husband became King Louis XVI in 1774, she became queen of a country that never warmed to "the Austrian woman."

At Versailles she became famous, and then notorious, for extravagance—lavish fashions, gambling, and a private pleasure-world at the Petit Trianon—while the royal finances crumbled. Pamphleteers made her the symbol of every royal excess, and though she never uttered the heartless words about cake forever put in her mouth, the slander stuck. The bizarre Affair of the Diamond Necklace dragged her name lower still.

When the Revolution broke in 1789, the hatred of the people fixed upon her. The royal family was dragged from Versailles to Paris, and their desperate flight toward the frontier was halted at Varennes, destroying the last trust between king and nation. The monarchy fell, and after Louis XVI was guillotined in January 1793, Marie Antoinette was tried on charges of treason and worse, and went to the scaffold that October—meeting her death, by all accounts, with a composure that even her enemies remembered.

A decade earlier she had watched a wondrous balloon rise into the sky at Versailles; now she was the doomed emblem of the old order swept away. Her husband, who shared her fall and preceded her to the guillotine, was Louis XVI.
