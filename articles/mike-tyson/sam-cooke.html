Sam Cooke

American soul singer (1931–1964)

Sam Cooke was the “King of Soul,” the smooth, gospel-trained singer who all but invented the genre and used it as a vehicle for dignity and change.

Raised in Chicago, he first found fame in gospel with the Soul Stirrers before crossing over to pop with the effortless “You Send Me” and a string of hits like “Cupid,” “Chain Gang” and “Wonderful World.” A shrewd businessman as well as an artist, he was a pioneer in owning his own recordings and publishing. Moved by the civil rights struggle—and, he said, by hearing Bob Dylan's “Blowin' in the Wind”—he wrote the soaring anthem “A Change Is Gonna Come.”

He was at the very center of a remarkable Black cultural moment: on the February 1964 night that Cassius Clay—soon to be Muhammad Ali—won the heavyweight title, Cooke celebrated with Ali and Malcolm X. His gospel-to-pop path shaped Aretha Franklin and inspired his devoted disciple Otis Redding.

His life ended violently and mysteriously in 1964, shot dead at a Los Angeles motel at just thirty-three. Another member of that famous Miami circle—the greatest running back of his era, who would become an actor and activist—was Jim Brown.
