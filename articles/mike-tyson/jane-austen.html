Jane Austen

English novelist (1775–1817)

Jane Austen was born in 1775 at Steventon in Hampshire, the seventh of eight children of a country clergyman. Educated mostly at home amid her father's books and her family's lively talk, she was devoted to her elder sister Cassandra, with whom she exchanged a celebrated correspondence and to whom she remained closest all her life. Both sisters remained unmarried.

She began writing as a girl, producing burlesques and sketches, and by her twenties had drafted versions of the novels that would make her name. After unsettled years in Bath and Southampton, the family moved in 1809 to a cottage at Chawton, where she revised her manuscripts and wrote steadily. Her books appeared anonymously, attributed only to "a Lady."

In quick succession she published Sense and Sensibility (1811), Pride and Prejudice (1813), Mansfield Park (1814) and Emma (1815), the last dedicated, at royal request, to the Prince Regent. Through sharp irony, precise social observation and the technique of free indirect speech, she turned the courtship and economics of the English gentry into art of the first rank. The admiration of figures such as Sir Walter Scott marked her growing reputation.

Her health failed—probably from a wasting illness—and she died at Winchester in 1817, aged only forty-one, with Northanger Abbey and Persuasion still unpublished. Once a modestly known author, she rose over the following centuries to stand among the most beloved and closely studied novelists in the English language.
