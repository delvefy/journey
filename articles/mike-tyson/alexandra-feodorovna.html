Alexandra Feodorovna

Last Empress of Russia (1872–1918)

Alexandra Feodorovna was the last empress of Russia, a devoted wife and mother whose tragic devotion to a faith healer helped bring down the dynasty she had married into.

Born a princess of the small German state of Hesse, she was a favourite granddaughter of Queen Victoria, who helped raise her after her own mother died young. In 1894 she married the new Tsar Nicholas II in a true love match and embraced her adopted country's Orthodox faith with intense fervour.

Shy and stiff in public, she never won the affection of the Russian court or people, and her German birth made her suspect when war came. She bore four daughters and at last a son, the heir Alexei, whose haemophilia became the secret torment of the family. In her anguish she placed absolute trust in the mystic Rasputin, who alone seemed able to help the boy.

That trust proved fatal to the monarchy. While Nicholas commanded at the front, Alexandra effectively ruled through Rasputin's counsel, and the scandal of his power eroded the crown's last authority.

After the revolution she shared her family's captivity and was murdered with them at Yekaterinburg in 1918. Of her children, the youngest daughter, Anastasia, would pass into legend.
