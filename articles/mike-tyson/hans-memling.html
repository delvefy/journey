Hans Memling

Netherlandish painter of Bruges (c. 1430–1494)

Hans Memling, born around 1430 in the German Rhineland, became the leading painter of Bruges and one of the most prosperous and admired artists of the late fifteenth century. After what was almost certainly a period of training in the Brussels workshop of Rogier van der Weyden, he settled by 1465 in the wealthy Flemish port of Bruges, where he ran a busy studio and grew rich enough to rank among the city's heaviest taxpayers.

Memling refined the achievements of the earlier Netherlandish masters into a style of exceptional serenity and polish. His Madonnas and saints inhabit calm, harmonious spaces; his sitters gaze out with a quiet, dignified composure that made him the most sought-after portraitist of his day. Among his finest works are the jewel-like Shrine of St Ursula, painted for a Bruges hospital, and his great Last Judgment triptych, now in Gdańsk.

His clientele was international. The thriving foreign merchant community of Bruges—Italian bankers, English courtiers, German traders—commissioned altarpieces and portraits from him, carrying his fame across Europe. His serene, devotional manner was widely copied and helped fix the popular image of Flemish painting.

By the time he died in 1494, Memling was the celebrated master of a great commercial city at the height of its splendour. He stands as the culmination of the tradition founded by Jan van Eyck and developed by his own master, the last great painter of Bruges before the city's slow decline.
