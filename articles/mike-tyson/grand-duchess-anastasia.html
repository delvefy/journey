Grand Duchess Anastasia

Youngest daughter of the last Tsar (1901–1918)

Grand Duchess Anastasia Nikolaevna of Russia was the youngest daughter of Tsar Nicholas II and the Empress Alexandra, and the subject of one of the most famous legends of the twentieth century.

By the accounts of those who knew her she was lively, witty, and mischievous, the spirited youngest of the four imperial sisters. When revolution swept away her father's throne in 1917, she shared the family's captivity, which ended in the cellar of a house at Yekaterinburg, where in July 1918 the Bolsheviks shot the Tsar, the Tsarina, and all their children together.

Because the bodies were hidden away and some were long missing from the burial site, a rumour took root and would not die—that Anastasia, alone of the family, had somehow survived the massacre. Over the following decades a number of women came forward claiming to be the lost grand duchess, and their stories filled newspapers, books, and films.

Only after the fall of the Soviet Union were the remains recovered and identified by modern science, proving at last that the whole family, Anastasia among them, had perished in 1918.

Yet for most of a century the legend lived, embodied above all in the most famous and persistent of the pretenders, Anna Anderson.
