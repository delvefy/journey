Wu Zetian

The only female emperor of China (624–705)

Wu Zetian rose from the harem to become the only woman in the long history of China to rule as emperor in her own name. She entered the palace as a young, low-ranking concubine of the great Emperor Taizong. After his death she returned from the convent to which imperial widows were consigned and won the favour of his son and successor, Emperor Gaozong, over whom she gained complete ascendancy, supplanting his empress to take that title for herself.

As Gaozong's health failed, she governed in his name, and after his death she dominated her own sons, raising and deposing them as puppets. Then, in 690, she cast aside all pretence and proclaimed herself emperor, founding a new dynasty she called the Zhou and interrupting the line of the Tang.

Her rule was ruthless—she employed informers, secret police, and bloody purges to destroy rivals among the old aristocracy. Yet she was also a shrewd and effective sovereign. She opened the civil service more widely through the examinations, advanced officials of talent rather than birth, kept the frontiers strong, and lavishly patronized Buddhism, which she used to sanction a woman's claim to the throne.

In 705, old and ill, she was at last pushed from power by a palace coup that restored the Tang, and she died the same year. Modern historians weigh her cruelty against the competence of her long dominance. She had begun her astonishing career as a concubine of the second and greatest of the Tang emperors—Emperor Taizong of Tang.
