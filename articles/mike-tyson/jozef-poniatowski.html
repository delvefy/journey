Józef Poniatowski

Polish prince and Marshal of France (1763–1813)

Prince Józef Poniatowski was the foremost soldier of his fallen nation and one of the great cavalry leaders of the Napoleonic age. Nephew of the last king of Poland, he learned war in the Austrian service before returning home to command Polish troops in the War in Defence of the Constitution of 1792. When his uncle the king submitted to Russia, Józef resigned in protest, and two years later he fought in the national uprising led by Tadeusz Kościuszko.

After Poland's final partition he kept the dream of independence alive through soldiering. When Napoleon created the Duchy of Warsaw, Poniatowski became its war minister and commander, and in 1809 he led its army to victory against Austria, expanding the little Polish state.

He gave Napoleon his complete loyalty. He commanded the Polish corps in the catastrophic Russian campaign of 1812 and fought on into Germany. At the great Battle of Leipzig in 1813, charged with covering the French retreat, he was made a Marshal of France—the only foreigner so honoured. Three days later, wounded and pressed by the enemy as a bridge was blown too soon, he drowned trying to swim the Elster River.

He became Poland's romantic national hero, the “Polish Bayard.” His career had unfolded in the service of Napoleon, and among the marshals at his side in those campaigns was the dashing cavalryman Joachim Murat.
