Rama Raya

Regent of the Vijayanagara Empire (c. 1484–1565)

Rama Raya was the powerful regent who ruled the Vijayanagara Empire of southern India in its last years of greatness, and whose death on the field of Talikota brought the empire crashing down.

He owed his rise to marriage: he was the son-in-law of the renowned emperor Krishnadevaraya, and the epithet by which he is known, Aliya, means just that. After Krishnadevaraya's death the throne fell into weak hands, and Rama Raya, by force and cunning, made himself master of the state, ruling in the name of a puppet king, Sadasiva Raya.

A bold and able statesman, he restored Vijayanagara's dominance over the south and threw himself into the quarrels of the Muslim sultanates that bordered him to the north. Allying now with one and now with another, he played Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, and Bidar against each other and treated their rulers with a high hand.

His interference united his enemies. In 1565 the four sultanates joined in a great confederacy and met the Vijayanagara army at Talikota. The battle was fiercely fought, but when the aged Rama Raya was captured and beheaded in the thick of it, his host dissolved in panic. The victors then fell upon the imperial capital and sacked its temples and palaces for months, leaving the splendid city of Vijayanagara the field of ruins known today as Hampi.

The empire lingered on under his kinsmen but never recovered. Rama Raya's whole career had grown from the greatness of the emperor whose daughter he had married, Krishnadevaraya.
