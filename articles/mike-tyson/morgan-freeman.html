Morgan Freeman

American actor and narrator (born 1937)

Morgan Freeman is among the most beloved and respected actors of the American screen, known for the quiet authority of his presence and the rich, unmistakable depth of his voice. He came to film stardom late, in his fifties, after years of stage and television work, and then sustained it for decades with an extraordinary run of performances.

He brought dignity to a gallery of memorable roles: the proud chauffeur of Driving Miss Daisy, the seasoned soldier of Glory, the weary detective of Se7en, and, most loved of all, the patient lifer “Red” of The Shawshank Redemption. Under the direction of Clint Eastwood he played the loyal trainer of Million Dollar Baby, winning the Academy Award, and he became the studios' favourite figure of wisdom, playing presidents, generals, and even God.

So great was his stature that when a film was made of South Africa's reconciliation, he portrayed Nelson Mandela in Invictus—a role the statesman himself had hoped Freeman would take, the two men having come to know each other. His sonorous narration, meanwhile, lent gravity to countless documentaries and films.

His co-star in the road-trip comedy The Bucket List, one of the most acclaimed and charismatic actors of his generation, was Jack Nicholson.
