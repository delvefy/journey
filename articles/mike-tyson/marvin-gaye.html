Marvin Gaye

American soul singer (1939–1984)

Marvin Gaye was one of the supreme voices of soul music, the Motown star who deepened the genre into an instrument of social conscience and sensual revelation.

The son of a stern preacher in Washington, D.C., he rose through Motown—marrying into the family of its founder—and scored a run of hits, including tender duets and the immortal “I Heard It Through the Grapevine.” But he chafed at the label's formula, and in 1971 he seized artistic control to make What's Going On, a flowing meditation on war, poverty and the environment that transformed what a soul album could be. He was a peer in that golden Motown generation of Stevie Wonder.

He reinvented himself again with the frank sensuality of “Let's Get It On” and, after years of drug troubles and depression, with the 1982 comeback “Sexual Healing.” His silken, multi-tracked voice influenced nearly every R&B singer who followed, and he moved among the Motown royalty alongside the young Michael Jackson.

His life ended in tragedy: in 1984, one day before his forty-fifth birthday, he was shot and killed by his own father. The flamboyant musical genius of the next generation who took his blend of funk, soul and sexuality to dazzling new heights was Prince.
