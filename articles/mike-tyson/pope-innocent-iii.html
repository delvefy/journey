Pope Innocent III

Medieval pope at the height of papal power (c. 1160–1216)

Pope Innocent III, born Lotario dei Conti di Segni around 1160, was elected pope in 1198 at the age of about thirty-seven and became the most powerful pontiff of the Middle Ages. A brilliant lawyer and theologian, he advanced an exalted vision of the papacy as supreme not only over the Church but over the kings and emperors of Christendom, whom he likened to the moon reflecting the sun of papal authority.

He made that claim a reality. He excommunicated King John of England and laid the whole kingdom under interdict, forcing John to surrender England as a fief of the papacy; he became guardian of the young Frederick II of Sicily and secured his election as Holy Roman Emperor. He arbitrated disputed thrones from Germany to the Iberian kingdoms.

Innocent also launched the great crusading enterprises of his age. He called the Fourth Crusade, which infamously went astray and sacked Christian Constantinople in 1204, and the Albigensian Crusade against the Cathar heretics of southern France. Within the Church he was a vigorous reformer, and he gave his approval to two new movements that would transform medieval religion: the Franciscans of Francis of Assisi and the Dominicans of Saint Dominic.

His crowning achievement was the Fourth Lateran Council of 1215, the greatest church council of the Middle Ages, which defined doctrine and reformed clerical life. Innocent III died suddenly in 1216, at the height of his power, having raised the medieval papacy to a peak of authority it would never surpass.
