Fred Astaire

American dancer and actor (1899–1987)

Fred Astaire is widely regarded as the greatest dancer in the history of film, a performer whose effortless elegance concealed a relentless perfectionism and changed how dance was put on screen.

He began in vaudeville as a child, partnering his sister, before finding his destiny in Hollywood. In a glorious run of 1930s musicals he was paired with Ginger Rogers—Top Hat, Swing Time, Shall We Dance—and together they became the very image of romance in motion, gliding through their numbers as if gravity had been suspended.

He transformed the filming of dance, insisting that the camera show the dancer's whole body in long, unbroken takes rather than chopping the movement into fragments—a discipline that made his artistry visible and that every later filmmaker had to reckon with. The greatest songwriters of the age, among them George Gershwin, wrote music expressly for him.

Graceful, modest, and exacting, he was admired even by classical ballet dancers as a master of the body. His name will forever be joined to that of his great partner, Ginger Rogers.
