Margaret of Anjou

Queen of England, Lancastrian leader (1430–1482)

Margaret of Anjou, born in 1430, was the daughter of René of Anjou, a prince rich in crowns but poor in lands. In 1445, as part of a truce in the Hundred Years' War, she was married to the gentle, ineffectual Henry VI of England—a queen of fifteen sent to a court already sliding toward crisis.

When Henry lapsed into spells of madness and the Yorkist faction under Richard, Duke of York, pressed its claim, Margaret emerged as the fierce champion of the Lancastrian cause and of the rights of her infant son, Edward of Westminster. Where her husband was passive, she was implacable. She rallied armies, intrigued tirelessly, and earned from her enemies—and from Shakespeare—the name of the "She-Wolf of France."

Her struggle ran the length of the Wars of the Roses. She won a great victory at Wakefield in 1460, where the Duke of York was killed, and triumphed again at the second battle of St Albans. But the rise of York's son as Edward IV turned the tide, and she was driven into exile in France. There the cunning Louis XI brokered her astonishing reconciliation with her old enemy the Earl of Warwick, who restored Henry VI in 1470.

The reversal was brief. In 1471 the Lancastrians were crushed at Tewkesbury, where her seventeen-year-old son—her last hope—was slain. Henry VI was murdered soon after, and Margaret was taken prisoner. Broken at last, she was ransomed by Louis XI and returned to France, where she lived out her final years in poverty, dying in 1482. No medieval English queen had fought so long or so hard for a crown, and few had lost so utterly.
