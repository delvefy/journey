Mohammed Adil Shah

Sultan of Bijapur, builder of the Gol Gumbaz (r. 1627–1656)

Mohammed Adil Shah succeeded his father Ibrahim Adil Shah II as Sultan of Bijapur and reigned for nearly thirty years, a period in which the kingdom reached its greatest extent even as it fell under the lengthening shadow of the Mughal empire.

His most famous legacy is the Gol Gumbaz, the vast mausoleum he raised over his own tomb at Bijapur. Its dome is among the largest masonry domes ever built, and the gallery beneath it carries a whisper around its whole circuit—a marvel of engineering that remains one of the great monuments of the Deccan.

In his early years he pushed Bijapur's power southward into the Karnataka country, his armies led by able captains including the Maratha general Shahaji, father of Shivaji. But to the north the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan, with his son Aurangzeb commanding in the Deccan, forced the southern sultanates to acknowledge imperial overlordship; by treaty in 1636 Bijapur became a tributary, buying survival with submission and tribute.

Within his own dominions a greater danger was stirring. The young Shivaji, son of his servant Shahaji, began to seize hill-forts in the western country and to carve out an independent Maratha power at Bijapur's expense, defying the sultanate that had employed his family.

Among the great nobles of Mohammed Adil Shah's court was the general Afzal Khan, a commander of the Karnataka wars who would later march against Shivaji in the confrontation that ended in his own death. At the Sultan's passing in 1656 the throne fell to his son Ali Adil Shah II, and Bijapur entered its long decline.
