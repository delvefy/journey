Boris Johnson

British Prime Minister, 2019–2022 (born 1964)

Boris Johnson was the flamboyant journalist-turned-politician who led Britain out of the European Union and through the COVID-19 pandemic before scandal toppled him.

Educated at Eton and Oxford, he made his name as a witty, provocative newspaper columnist and editor, cultivating a deliberately rumpled persona. He admired Winston Churchill above all other statesmen, even writing an admiring biography of him, and consciously cast himself in the same mould of the larger-than-life leader. Two terms as Mayor of London, crowned by the 2012 Olympics, lifted him to national fame.

He became the most charismatic champion of the 2016 campaign to leave the European Union, and in 2019 he rode the promise to “Get Brexit Done” to the premiership and a thumping election victory. He duly took Britain out of the EU, then steered it—erratically, and nearly dying of the disease himself—through the pandemic. After Russia invaded Ukraine he became one of the loudest and earliest Western backers of Volodymyr Zelensky.

But a drip of scandals, above all revelations of lockdown-breaking parties in Downing Street, destroyed his authority, and his own party forced him out in 2022. Throughout his turbulent premiership his head of state—the sovereign to whom he reported and who would die only weeks after he left office—was Queen Elizabeth II.
