Barsine

Persian noblewoman (c. 363–309 BC)

Barsine was a daughter of the great Persian satrap Artabazus, born to one of the highest houses of the empire and raised partly at the Macedonian court during her father's years of exile, so that she knew Greek language and manners as well as Persian. She was married in turn to the two Rhodian brothers who served the Great King, first Mentor and then Memnon, the ablest of Persia's commanders against Alexander.

When Memnon died and the Persian cause crumbled, Barsine was taken among the captives, and her beauty, learning, and royal blood drew the notice of Alexander the Great himself. She became his companion, by many accounts the first woman he loved, and around 327 BC she bore him a son, whom they named Heracles.

After Alexander's sudden death the world he had conquered was torn apart by his generals, and a woman with a royal child became a piece on a deadly board. Barsine and her boy withdrew into quiet obscurity for years, hoping to be forgotten.

They were not. In the merciless politics of the successors, the half-Macedonian son of Alexander was too dangerous to live, and in 309 BC both mother and son were put to death to clear a rival's path. The son she bore to Alexander, raised up as a pretender only to be murdered, was Heracles of Macedon.
