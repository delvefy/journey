Henry Kissinger

American statesman and diplomat (1923–2023)

Henry Kissinger was the most famous and controversial American diplomat of the twentieth century, the practitioner of cold realpolitik who reshaped the Cold War world.

Born to a Jewish family in Germany that fled the Nazis, he became a Harvard scholar of strategy before entering government as national security adviser and then secretary of state under President Richard Nixon. He pursued détente with the Soviet Union, negotiated the accords that ended American involvement in Vietnam—for which he shared a Nobel Peace Prize—and conducted dramatic “shuttle diplomacy” in the Middle East.

His masterstroke was the secret opening to communist China, splitting the great communist powers and transforming the global balance; it culminated in Nixon's famous meeting with Mao Zedong. Yet his record was darkened by the secret bombing of Cambodia, by American complicity in coups and repression—including in the Chile of Augusto Pinochet—and critics charged him with war crimes even as admirers hailed his strategic genius. He remained a sought-after elder statesman until his death at the age of one hundred in 2023.

The breakthrough to China rested on the rapport he struck with its urbane premier Zhou Enlai, while his policy of détente brought him repeatedly across the table from the leader of the Soviet Union. That Soviet leader, with whom he and Nixon negotiated the arms-limitation treaties at the heart of the era's uneasy peace, was Leonid Brezhnev.
