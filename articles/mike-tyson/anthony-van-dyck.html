Anthony van Dyck

Flemish Baroque painter (1599–1641)

Anthony van Dyck was born in Antwerp in 1599 and revealed his gifts so early that while still a youth he became the most brilliant assistant in the workshop of Peter Paul Rubens, the towering figure of Flemish art. From his master he absorbed a fluent, sumptuous Baroque manner, but he soon sought a style of his own.

A long sojourn in Italy in the 1620s, especially in Genoa, brought him the patronage of the nobility and a profound study of Titian, whose warm colour and aristocratic grace marked his art for life. He returned north as a celebrated portraitist before being drawn, in 1632, to the court of England.

There King Charles I made him principal painter, knighted him, and granted him a pension. Van Dyck repaid the favour by creating the enduring image of the Stuart monarchy: elegant, melancholy, effortlessly noble portraits of the king, of Queen Henrietta Maria, of the royal children and the doomed Cavalier aristocracy on the eve of civil war. His equestrian and hunting portraits of Charles set a pattern for royal imagery across Europe.

Working at great speed and with a large studio, he transformed English portraiture and set the model that Lely, Reynolds and Gainsborough would follow for a century and a half. He married into the English aristocracy but his health was poor, and he died in London in 1641, aged only forty-two.
