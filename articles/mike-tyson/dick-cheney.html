Dick Cheney

Vice-President of the United States, 2001–2009 (born 1941)

Dick Cheney was widely regarded as the most powerful vice-president in American history, the driving force behind his country's response to the September 11 attacks.

From Wyoming, he rose young to be Gerald Ford's White House chief of staff, then served in Congress and as Secretary of Defense under George H. W. Bush, helping direct the swift victory of the Gulf War of 1991 against Saddam Hussein. After a spell running the energy giant Halliburton, he returned to power as the vice-president to George W. Bush.

In that office he wielded extraordinary influence. A hawkish proponent of expansive executive power, he was a principal architect of the “war on terror,” the invasion of Iraq, and the harsh interrogation policies that followed, becoming for his admirers the steely guardian of national security and for his critics the dark heart of an overreaching presidency. In retirement he broke bitterly with Donald Trump, whom he and his daughter denounced as a danger to the republic.

The general who had served as chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff during Cheney's time at the Pentagon—and who would later clash with him in the Bush cabinet over the wisdom of the Iraq War—was Colin Powell.
