Ringo Starr

Drummer of the Beatles (born 1940)

Ringo Starr was the drummer of the Beatles, the warm, unflappable everyman whose steady beat and genial humor anchored the most famous band in history.

Born Richard Starkey in working-class Liverpool and frequently ill as a child, he was already a respected local drummer when he joined the Beatles in 1962, replacing their original drummer just as fame arrived. Within the group of John Lennon, Paul McCartney and George Harrison he was the affable heart, beloved by fans and occasionally stepping to the microphone for songs like “Yellow Submarine” and “With a Little Help from My Friends.”

After the band's breakup he enjoyed a string of solo hits, took up acting and narration, and for decades toured the world with his rotating All-Starr Band. With McCartney he remains one of the two surviving Beatles, and he was knighted late in life for his services to music.

The Beatles' sound, however, was shaped in the studio as much as on the stage, by the classically trained producer who arranged their strings, spliced their tapes, and was often called the “fifth Beatle.” That producer was George Martin.
