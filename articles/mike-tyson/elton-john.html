Elton John

British singer-songwriter (born 1947)

Elton John is one of the best-selling and most flamboyant musicians of all time, a piano-playing showman whose melodies have soundtracked half a century of popular music.

Born Reginald Dwight and trained at the Royal Academy of Music, he formed a lifelong songwriting partnership with the lyricist Bernie Taupin, who supplied the words to his soaring tunes. From the early 1970s he poured out classics—“Your Song,” “Rocket Man,” “Tiny Dancer,” “Goodbye Yellow Brick Road”—and dazzled audiences with outlandish costumes and spectacle. He counted among his friends a fellow rock royalty that included John Lennon, with whom he shared a stage at Madison Square Garden in what proved the ex-Beatle's last great concert appearance.

He was also a close friend of Princess Diana, and when she died he rewrote his elegy “Candle in the Wind” to perform at her funeral, the recording—produced by George Martin—becoming the best-selling single in history. In later life he became a tireless campaigner against AIDS, was knighted, and saw his story told in film.

Among the great friends of his world of rock stardom—a flamboyant showman and peerless frontman to rival Elton's own theatricality—was the lead singer of Queen, Freddie Mercury.
