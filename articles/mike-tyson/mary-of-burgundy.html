Mary of Burgundy

Duchess of Burgundy, "Mary the Rich" (1457–1482)

Mary of Burgundy was the heiress of the richest state in Europe, and her marriage carried that vast inheritance into the house of Habsburg, shaping the destiny of the continent for centuries.

She was the only child of Charles the Bold, the last great Valois Duke of Burgundy. When he fell at the Battle of Nancy in 1477, Mary suddenly inherited the Low Countries, Franche-Comté, and the duchy's other lands—and at once found herself prey. The French king Louis XI moved to swallow her inheritance, seizing the duchy of Burgundy itself and pressing hard on the rest.

To hold her people's loyalty she confirmed their ancient liberties, and to find a protector she married, that same year, Maximilian of Austria, son of the emperor and the future Maximilian I. He took up arms in her defence and checked the French advance. Their union joined the Burgundian inheritance to the Habsburgs forever—one of the most fateful marriages in history.

Mary bore two children, Philip the Handsome and Margaret of Austria, before her life was cut short. In 1482, while hawking, she was thrown from her horse and died of her injuries at only twenty-five.

Through her son the Burgundian, Austrian, and Spanish crowns would converge upon her grandson, the emperor Charles V. She was herself the granddaughter of Philip the Good, in whose day Burgundy had reached its splendour. Her daughter Margaret of Austria would govern the Netherlands she left behind.
