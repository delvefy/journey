Artemisia II of Caria

Queen of Caria (died 350 BC)

Artemisia II was the sister and wife of Mausolus and, on his death in 353 BC, the ruler of Caria in her own right. Her brief reign of barely two years became a byword for devotion and for grief. By tradition she mourned Mausolus so extravagantly that she mixed his ashes into her drink and consumed them, making her own body his living tomb, and pined away to follow him within a few years.

Her enduring memorial, however, was made of marble. To honour her husband she raised at Halicarnassus a tomb of unequalled magnificence—the Mausoleum—summoning the foremost Greek sculptors of the age to adorn it with friezes and statues. So splendid was the result that it was reckoned among the Seven Wonders of the ancient world, and she also gathered the most celebrated orators of Greece to a grand contest of eulogies in Mausolus's praise.

Yet she was no mere mourner. When the islanders of Rhodes, scorning the rule of a woman, sent a fleet against her, Artemisia lured it into her harbour, ambushed and seized the ships, and turned the tables to capture Rhodes itself—proving herself a worthy heir to the martial Artemisia who had ruled Halicarnassus before her.

She died around 350 BC, the Mausoleum still rising; its sculptors finished it for their own glory. Foremost among those masters was Scopas.
