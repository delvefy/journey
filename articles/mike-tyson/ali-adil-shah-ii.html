Ali Adil Shah II

Sultan of Bijapur (1638–1672)

Ali Adil Shah II ascended the throne of Bijapur as a youth and reigned through the years in which the sultanate was crushed between two rising powers—the Marathas of Shivaji within and the Mughal empire of Aurangzeb without.

Early in his reign he confronted the swelling revolt of Shivaji. He sent his great general Afzal Khan to subdue the Maratha chief, only to see Afzal Khan killed beneath the walls of Pratapgad and his army scattered. Undaunted, the Sultan dispatched another commander, Siddi Jauhar, who besieged Shivaji in the fort of Panhala; but Shivaji slipped away by night in a celebrated escape, and Bijapur could never again pin him down.

From the north pressed an even graver threat. The Mughal prince Aurangzeb, master of the Deccan and soon emperor, regarded the southern sultanates as ripe for conquest. Ali Adil Shah II was forced into costly wars and uneasy truces, his frontiers gnawed away on both sides at once.

Like his forebears he was also a man of culture, a poet who wrote verse under a pen-name and a patron of the refined Bijapur court. But the energies of the kingdom were spent on mere survival, and the brilliance of Ibrahim Adil Shah II's golden age was not to return.

He died in 1672, leaving the throne to his young son Sikandar Adil Shah, the last of the line, in whose reign Bijapur would at last be swallowed by the empire of Aurangzeb.
