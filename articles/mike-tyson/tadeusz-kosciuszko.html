Tadeusz Kościuszko

Polish military engineer and revolutionary (1746–1817)

Tadeusz Kościuszko was a hero of two worlds, a Polish patriot who fought for liberty in both America and his partitioned homeland. Trained as a military engineer, he crossed the Atlantic to join the American Revolution, where his skill proved invaluable: he fortified the bluffs at Saratoga that helped trap a British army and designed the defences of West Point. He served under George Washington, won a brigadier's commission, and formed a deep, lifelong friendship with Thomas Jefferson.

Returning to a Poland being devoured by its neighbours, he took up its cause against the empire of Catherine the Great. In 1794 he led a national uprising, winning an early victory with peasant volunteers armed with scythes before being overwhelmed, wounded, and captured; the revolt was finally drowned by the armies of Alexander Suvorov.

Freed from Russian prison, he became a living symbol of the struggle for freedom. He drafted an American will entrusting his estate to Jefferson to purchase the liberty and education of enslaved people, and he pointedly refused to lend his name to Napoleon's schemes for a French-controlled Poland, distrusting the emperor's promises.

He died in Switzerland in 1817, mourned as a national hero by Poles, Americans, and others, his name later carried by the highest mountain in Australia. His comrade among the Polish volunteers who crossed the ocean to fight for American independence was Casimir Pulaski.
