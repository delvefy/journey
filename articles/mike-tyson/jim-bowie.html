Jim Bowie

American pioneer and Alamo defender (1796–1836)

James Bowie was a frontier adventurer whose name lives on in the heavy fighting knife he made famous. Raised on the Louisiana frontier, he grew into a large, formidable man with a taste for risk. His legend was born at the Sandbar Fight of 1827, a riverside brawl in which, badly wounded, he killed an opponent with a great blade—and the “Bowie knife” became the must-have weapon of the American backwoods.

His early career mixed daring with fraud: he speculated wildly in disputed land titles and trafficked in enslaved people smuggled into Louisiana through the operations of a notorious Gulf pirate. Seeking fresh fortune, he moved to Mexican Texas, became a citizen, married into a prominent family, and pursued grand land schemes.

When the Texas Revolution broke out he threw in with the rebels and shared command of the small garrison holding the Alamo at San Antonio. Stricken by illness during the siege, he lay bedridden as Santa Anna's army overran the mission in March 1836, and he was killed where he lay.

His death alongside Davy Crockett, in the cause Sam Houston would carry to victory, secured his place among the martyrs of Texan independence. The Gulf pirate from whose smuggling ring he had once bought slaves was Jean Lafitte.
