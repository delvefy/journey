Severus Alexander

Roman emperor, last of the Severans (208–235)

Severus Alexander was the last emperor of the Severan dynasty, a well-meaning young ruler whose murder opened a half-century of chaos. A cousin of the disgraced Elagabalus, he was raised to the throne as a boy of about thirteen in 222, after the Praetorian Guard cut down his predecessor. Throughout his reign real power lay with the formidable women of his family—his mother Julia Mamaea and his grandmother Julia Maesa—who steered the state back toward sobriety and respect for the Senate.

His was a cultivated and unusually tolerant court. He was said to keep in his private chapel images of figures from many faiths, including Christ and Abraham, and his mother, curious about the new religion, summoned the most learned Christian thinker of the age to instruct her.

The wider world, however, grew more dangerous. In the East the vigorous new Sassanid Persian Empire rose under Ardashir I, while Germanic tribes pressed the Rhine frontier. When Severus Alexander tried to buy peace from the Germans with gold rather than win it in battle, his soldiers, contemptuous of such weakness, mutinied. In 235 they murdered the emperor and his mother and hailed a rough soldier in his place.

His death ended the Severan line and plunged Rome into the anarchy of the Third Century. The renowned Christian teacher whom his mother had called to the court—the greatest scholar of the early Church—was Origen.
