Mike Tyson

American boxer, undisputed heavyweight champion (b. 1966)

Michael Gerard Tyson, born June 30, 1966, is an American former professional boxer who reigned as undisputed world heavyweight champion and remains one of the most famous and ferocious fighters in the sport's history. Raised in poverty in Brooklyn, New York, he accumulated dozens of arrests as a boy before his talent for fighting emerged at a reform school.

There the veteran trainer Cus D'Amato discovered him, became his legal guardian, and shaped both his savage peek-a-boo style and his ring psychology. Turning professional in 1985, Tyson overwhelmed opponents with speed, head movement, and concussive power. In November 1986 he knocked out Trevor Berbick for the WBC title at age 20, becoming the youngest heavyweight champion ever, and soon unified the division, demolishing Michael Spinks in 91 seconds in 1988.

His personal life then unraveled. A turbulent marriage, the death of D'Amato, and a split from his handlers preceded a stunning 1990 upset, when the 42-to-1 underdog Buster Douglas knocked him out in Tokyo. In 1992 Tyson was convicted of rape and served three years in prison. He returned to briefly reclaim portions of the title, but his most notorious moment came in 1997, when he was disqualified for biting off part of Evander Holyfield's ear. Decline, defeats, and financial ruin despite hundreds of millions earned followed, and he retired in 2005.

In retirement Tyson reinvented himself as a cultural figure, appearing in films, performing a confessional one-man stage show, hosting a popular podcast, and returning for exhibition bouts. Volatile yet oddly beloved, he endures as boxing's archetype of raw, untamed power.
