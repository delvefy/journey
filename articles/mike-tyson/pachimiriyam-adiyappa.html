Pachimiriyam Adiyappa

Carnatic composer of the Thanjavur court (18th century)

Pachimiriyam Adiyappa was an eighteenth-century composer and court musician of the Maratha kings of Thanjavur, and one of the formative figures of the Carnatic tradition in the generation before its great Trinity. A Telugu musician of the Tanjore court, he is remembered chiefly for a single immortal composition.

That work is the Viriboni, a varnam in the raga Bhairavi set to the slow and elaborate ata tala. Reckoned among the oldest and finest varnams in the whole repertoire, it became the very model of the form—a piece studied by nearly every serious student of Carnatic music as the gateway to advanced practice. Through it his name has outlived almost all else about him.

He flourished at the Maratha court of Thanjavur, long the richest centre of music in the south, where successive kings gathered composers and performers and where the classical art was refined and codified. Adiyappa stood in this milieu as a master of the older, ornate style.

His greatest legacy, beyond his own compositions, was as a teacher. He gave advanced training to the young Syama Sastri, the eldest of the coming Trinity, passing on the disciplined classicism of the Tanjore court to the very musicians who would carry Carnatic music to its height.

Little of Adiyappa's life is documented, and few of his works survive, but the Viriboni alone secures him a place in the history of South Indian music. He marks the bridge between the older court tradition and the golden age that followed at Thanjavur under its Maratha kings, among them Pratapasimha.
