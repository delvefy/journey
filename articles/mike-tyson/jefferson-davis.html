Jefferson Davis

President of the Confederate States of America (1808–1889)

Jefferson Davis was the first and only president of the Confederate States of America, the rebel government formed by the slaveholding South. Born in Kentucky and raised in Mississippi, he was trained at West Point, won distinction as a colonel in the Mexican-American War, and built a career as a planter, congressman, and senator. As Secretary of War under President Franklin Pierce he ably modernised the United States Army.

A firm believer in states' rights in the tradition of John C. Calhoun, he reluctantly followed his state out of the Union when it seceded in 1861, resigning from the Senate. The new Confederacy chose him as its president, and for four years he directed its war effort, struggling with quarrelsome generals and governors, a crumbling economy, and the overwhelming resources of the North.

His great asset was the brilliant commander he appointed to lead the Army of Northern Virginia, in whom he placed near-total trust. But against the relentless pressure applied by the Union, the cause failed.

With the collapse of the Confederacy in 1865, Davis was captured, imprisoned for two years at Fort Monroe, and charged with treason, though never tried. Unrepentant to the end, he wrote a long defence of the Confederate cause and died in New Orleans in 1889. He had waged his war against the Union of Abraham Lincoln and Ulysses S. Grant, and entrusted his armies to Robert E. Lee.
