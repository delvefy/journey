Cosimo de' Medici

Banker and ruler of Florence, “the Elder” (1389–1464)

Cosimo de' Medici inherited from his father a flourishing bank and turned it into the financial engine of an age. Under his direction the Medici Bank became the largest in Europe, with branches from London to Rome and the lucrative role of banker to the Papacy. That wealth he converted, with extraordinary subtlety, into power over his native Florence.

Holding no permanent office and styling himself merely a private citizen, Cosimo governed the republic from behind the scenes through a network of allies, marriages, and well-placed loans. His rivals, the Albizzi, sensed the danger and had him exiled in 1433; within a year the city recalled him, and from 1434 until his death he was the unchallenged master of Florence. Grateful Florentines later carved on his tomb the title Pater Patriae, father of his country.

Cosimo spent his fortune as lavishly on beauty and learning as on politics. He was the patron of Donatello and Fra Angelico, employed the architect Michelozzo to raise the great Medici palace, and paid for the rebuilding of San Marco and San Lorenzo. He gathered manuscripts from across the known world and, late in life, captivated by Greek philosophy, founded the circle that became the Platonic Academy.

From that union of money, art, and ideas grew the Florentine Renaissance, carried on by his grandson Lorenzo the Magnificent. Of all the artists Cosimo favored, none was closer to him than the sculptor whose bronzes still define the early Renaissance, and beside whom Cosimo asked to be buried—Donatello.
