Salvador Allende

President of Chile, 1970–1973 (1908–1973)

Salvador Allende was the Chilean physician and politician who became the first Marxist freely elected president in Latin America, and who died in the coup that overthrew him.

A founder of Chile's Socialist Party, he ran for the presidency again and again before winning at last in 1970. He set out to build socialism by democratic and constitutional means—“the Chilean road to socialism”—nationalizing the copper mines and banks and redistributing land. He drew the support of the Latin American left, the friendship of Fidel Castro, who visited Chile, and the admiration of revolutionaries; Che Guevara had once inscribed a book to him, to one who sought the same goal by other means.

His government was besieged from the start—by economic chaos, fierce domestic opposition, and the covert hostility of the United States, where the administration of Richard Nixon worked to “make the economy scream.” The poet Pablo Neruda, his ambassador and champion, stood among his closest allies.

On 11 September 1973 the military rose against him. As tanks and planes attacked the presidential palace, Allende broadcast a final, defiant address to the nation and then died inside La Moneda rather than surrender, becoming a martyr of the left worldwide. The general who led that coup and seized power for the next seventeen years was Augusto Pinochet.
