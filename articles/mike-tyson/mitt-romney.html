Mitt Romney

American businessman, governor, and presidential nominee (born 1947)

Mitt Romney built a career at the meeting point of business and politics. The son of a Michigan governor who had himself sought the presidency, he made his fortune as a co-founder of the private-equity firm Bain Capital, then won wide praise for rescuing the troubled 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City from financial ruin.

Elected governor of Massachusetts, he enacted a landmark health-care reform that required residents to carry insurance—a model that, ironically, would later shape the national health law passed under his great rival. In 2012 he captured the Republican nomination for president and ran a vigorous campaign, but lost the general election to the incumbent.

He returned to public life as a United States senator from Utah, where he carved out an unusual role as a Republican willing to break with his own party's leader on grounds of conscience, becoming the only member of his party in the Senate to vote to convict the president in his first impeachment trial. A devout member of the Latter-day Saint church, he cultivated an image of probity and independence.

The president who defeated him in the election of 2012, and against whom he had waged that long campaign, was Barack Obama.
