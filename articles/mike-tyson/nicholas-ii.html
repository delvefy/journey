Nicholas II

Last Emperor of Russia (1868–1918)

Nicholas II was the last emperor of Russia, a well-meaning but irresolute autocrat whose reign ended in revolution and in the murder of himself and his family.

He came unprepared to the throne in 1894 and clung stubbornly to the principle of absolute rule even as Russia strained against it. Defeat in the war with Japan and the Revolution of 1905 forced him to concede an elected assembly, the Duma, whose powers he then worked to undermine. A loving husband and father, he was a poor judge of men and of his times.

He had married Alexandra, a granddaughter of Queen Victoria, and their only son and heir suffered from haemophilia. The family's desperation for the boy drew them under the influence of the mystic Grigori Rasputin, whose sway over the empress scandalised the nation and corroded the prestige of the crown.

In 1914 Nicholas led Russia into the First World War against his cousin Wilhelm II of Germany. When he took personal command of the failing army, the government fell to the empress and Rasputin, and discontent swelled into the revolution of February 1917, which compelled his abdication.

The Bolsheviks under Lenin took the family captive, and in July 1918 Nicholas, Alexandra, and their five children were shot at Yekaterinburg. He had been the cousin and the very likeness of George V. His fate is forever bound to the dark figure of Rasputin.
