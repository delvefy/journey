Bill Clinton

42nd President of the United States (born 1946)

Bill Clinton was the 42nd President of the United States, a gifted political talent who presided over a decade of peace and prosperity but saw his second term consumed by scandal.

Raised in modest circumstances in Arkansas, he was a Rhodes Scholar and Yale-trained lawyer who became his state's governor before capturing the White House in 1992, ending the long Republican hold and turning out the incumbent George H. W. Bush. Governing as a centrist “New Democrat,” he reaped the longest economic boom in American history, balanced the budget, signed sweeping trade and welfare-reform measures, and brokered peace efforts from the Balkans to Northern Ireland to the Middle East.

His presidency was marked too by fierce partisan warfare with a resurgent Republican Congress and, in 1998, by a sex scandal that led the House to impeach him, though the Senate acquitted him and he left office with high approval. He handed the presidency to George W. Bush in 2001 and built a sprawling global foundation, later campaigning energetically for Barack Obama and against Donald Trump.

In every chapter of that career stood his wife and partner of a lifetime, a formidable politician in her own right who would become a senator, secretary of state, and the first woman to win a major party's nomination for president—Hillary Clinton.
