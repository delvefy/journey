Mausolus

Satrap and dynast of Caria (died 353 BC)

Mausolus ruled Caria for some quarter-century in the fourth century BC. Though in name a satrap, a provincial governor for the Persian king, in practice he governed his corner of Asia Minor as an all-but-independent prince, expanding his power over Lycia, the Ionian coast, and the offshore islands, and trimming his loyalties with great cunning when the satraps of the empire rose in revolt.

An admirer of Greek culture, he moved his capital to Halicarnassus—the city once ruled by the warrior-queen Artemisia I—and rebuilt it into a strong and splendid harbour city, ringed with walls so formidable that, a generation later, they would cost Alexander the Great a hard siege to overcome. Following the custom of his Carian house, Mausolus married his own sister, Artemisia II.

His name has outlived his realm because of his tomb. When he died in 353 BC, his grieving sister-queen raised over him a monument of such scale and beauty—a temple-like sepulchre crowned by a stepped pyramid and chariot and carved by the greatest Greek sculptors of the day—that it was numbered among the Seven Wonders of the ancient world. From it every later language takes the very word for a grand tomb: the mausoleum.

That monument was the work of his widow and successor, who poured her devotion and her treasury into it—Artemisia II of Caria.
