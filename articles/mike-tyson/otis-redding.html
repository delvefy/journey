Otis Redding

American soul singer (1941–1967)

Otis Redding was one of the greatest soul singers of the 1960s, the raw, impassioned voice of Memphis soul whose career was cut tragically short.

Raised in Macon, Georgia—the hometown of Little Richard, whose songs he sang as a boy—he found his home at Stax Records, where with the house band he cut a string of deeply felt classics: “These Arms of Mine,” “Try a Little Tenderness,” and “Respect,” a song he wrote that Aretha Franklin would soon transform into an anthem. He shared the Georgia soul tradition with the Godfather himself, James Brown.

His electrifying 1967 set at the Monterey Pop Festival won him a vast new audience among rock fans. But that December, at the very height of his powers, he died in a plane crash at the age of twenty-six. The wistful “(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay,” recorded only days earlier, was released after his death and became his first number-one hit.

The smooth, soaring singer he had idolized above all others—the pioneer of soul whose grace and social conscience set the template Otis followed—was Sam Cooke.
