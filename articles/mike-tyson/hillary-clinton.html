Hillary Clinton

American stateswoman and presidential nominee (born 1947)

Hillary Clinton was one of the most prominent and polarizing American politicians of her generation, the first woman to win a major party's nomination for president.

A Yale-trained lawyer and children's advocate, she became a powerful and unconventional First Lady during the presidency of her husband, Bill Clinton, taking the lead on an ambitious but doomed effort to reform health care and weathering the scandals of his second term at his side. Rather than retreat, she launched her own career, winning a Senate seat from New York in 2000.

In 2008 she waged a long, narrow, and bitter primary battle for the presidency against Barack Obama; defeated, she joined his administration as Secretary of State, traveling the globe as America's chief diplomat through the Arab Spring and the raid that killed Osama bin Laden. In 2016 she captured the Democratic nomination at last and won the national popular vote—but lost the presidency in the Electoral College to Donald Trump, a stunning defeat that haunted her party for years.

Through decades at the summit of American public life she worked alongside a fellow Democrat who had served as Obama's vice-president and would himself reach the White House—her longtime Senate colleague, Joe Biden.
