Xi Jinping

Paramount leader of China (born 1953)

Xi Jinping is the most powerful Chinese leader since Mao Zedong. The son of a Communist revolutionary who was purged and later rehabilitated, he came of age during the Cultural Revolution, when he was sent as a youth to labour in the countryside. He climbed steadily through provincial party posts before reaching the summit in 2012 as General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party, adding the presidency and command of the armed forces.

Once in power he gathered authority into his own hands to a degree not seen in decades. A sweeping anti-corruption campaign cleaned house and removed rivals at once; his political doctrine was written into the party charter; and in 2018 he abolished the term limits that might have ended his rule, opening the way to govern indefinitely. Critics saw the revival of a Mao-style cult of the leader.

Abroad he pursued an assertive course—the vast Belt and Road infrastructure program, a harder line over Hong Kong and the South China Sea, and a deepening partnership with Russia—positioning China as a rival superpower to the United States.

Yet the economic colossus he commands was not his creation. It had been built by the leader whose market reforms opened China to the world after Mao, and who had personally restored Xi's disgraced father to favour—Deng Xiaoping.
