Bartolomeu Dias

Portuguese navigator, first to round the Cape of Good Hope (c. 1450–1500)

Bartolomeu Dias was the Portuguese navigator who first rounded the southern tip of Africa, proving that the Atlantic and Indian oceans were joined and opening the long-sought sea-road to the East.

In 1487 King John II of Portugal sent him south with a small squadron to push beyond the furthest point yet reached on the African coast. Carried past the land's end by a fierce storm, Dias turned back and at last sighted the great headland he had unknowingly passed. He named it the Cape of Storms; the king, foreseeing what it promised, renamed it the Cape of Good Hope. The way to India lay open.

When Dias returned to Lisbon in 1488 to report his triumph, among those at the Portuguese court was the Genoese sailor Christopher Columbus, then seeking a patron for a westward voyage of his own. In the years that followed, Dias lent his hard-won knowledge to the next great venture, helping to build and provision the ships of Vasco da Gama and sailing with that fleet as far as the Cape Verde Islands before it bore away for India.

His own end came on the sea he had mastered. In 1500 he sailed in the great armada of Pedro Álvares Cabral, but off the Cape of Good Hope a storm took his ship down with all aboard.

His feat was the indispensable step in the work begun long before by Prince Henry the Navigator, and completed at last when da Gama reached the shores of India.
