George W. Bush

43rd President of the United States (born 1946)

George W. Bush was the 43rd President of the United States, whose two terms were consumed by the attacks of September 11 and the wars that followed.

The eldest son of President George H. W. Bush, he made his career in Texas oil and baseball before serving as governor and winning the presidency in the bitterly disputed election of 2000, decided only by a Supreme Court ruling on the Florida recount. Months into his term, the terrorist attacks of September 11, 2001, transformed his presidency and the world. He launched a global “War on Terror,” invading Afghanistan to topple the Taliban and, in 2003, ordering the invasion of Iraq that overthrew Saddam Hussein—a war justified by weapons that were never found, waged alongside his staunch ally Tony Blair.

Re-elected in 2004, he saw his standing erode amid the grinding Iraqi insurgency, the bungled response to Hurricane Katrina, and the financial crash of 2008. Abroad he became a lightning rod for anti-Americanism, mocked from the United Nations podium by Hugo Chávez. He also mounted a vast and lifesaving anti-AIDS program in Africa that later won quieter praise.

He handed the White House to Barack Obama in 2009 and retired to private life and painting, while his party drifted toward the populism of Donald Trump, who would humiliate his brother Jeb in 2016. The defining shadow over his presidency, the man whose attacks set its whole course and who eluded capture throughout it, was Osama bin Laden.
