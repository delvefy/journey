Justinian I

Byzantine emperor (482–565)

Justinian I was the most ambitious of all the Byzantine emperors, a ruler who dreamed of restoring the Roman Empire to its ancient greatness. Of humble Balkan birth, he rose as the nephew and heir of Emperor Justin and took the throne in 527, determined to make his reign a turning point in history. In nearly every field he succeeded on a grand scale.

His most lasting achievement was in law. The vast compilation he ordered, the Corpus Juris Civilis, gathered and harmonized centuries of Roman jurisprudence into a single body that became the foundation of the legal systems of much of the modern world. In architecture he raised the Hagia Sophia in Constantinople, its colossal dome the supreme glory of Byzantine art.

By war he won back great swaths of the lost West—North Africa from the Vandals, Italy from the Ostrogoths, and a corner of Spain—briefly making the Mediterranean a Roman lake again. At his side stood his formidable wife, the empress Theodora, who saved his throne during the terrible Nika riots by refusing to flee. Yet his reign was also scourged by a devastating plague, and his costly conquests would not long outlive him.

The conquests that nearly restored the empire were won above all by one extraordinary soldier, the commander who shattered the Vandals and the Goths in Justinian's name—Belisarius.
