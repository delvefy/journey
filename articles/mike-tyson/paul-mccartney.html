Paul McCartney

Beatle and songwriter, one of music's most successful (born 1942)

Sir Paul McCartney is, by almost any measure, one of the most successful and gifted songwriters in the history of popular music. A Liverpool boy like his bandmates, he joined forces as a teenager with John Lennon, and together they formed the Lennon–McCartney partnership that wrote the songbook of the Beatles and, in large part, of an era.

Within the band he was the melodist, the bassist, and the most versatile musician, author or co-author of some of the best-loved songs ever recorded—“Yesterday,” perhaps the most covered song of all time, along with “Hey Jude,” “Let It Be,” “Eleanor Rigby,” and “Blackbird.” His tunefulness balanced Lennon's edge, and the friction and friendship between them powered the Beatles to heights no group has matched.

When the band broke apart in 1970 he refused to fade, forming the group Wings with his wife Linda and scoring a fresh run of hits through the 1970s and beyond. Knighted, endlessly honoured, and still touring into his eighties, he became an institution, championing vegetarianism and animal rights along the way.

In the early 1980s he recorded a pair of chart-topping duets with a younger superstar—who then astonished and wounded him by outbidding him to buy the rights to the Beatles' own songs. That superstar was Michael Jackson.
