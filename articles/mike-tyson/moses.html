Moses

Hebrew prophet and lawgiver (traditionally 13th century BC)

Moses is the towering prophet and lawgiver of the Hebrew Bible, revered in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam alike. Tradition places him in the thirteenth century BC, though scholars debate the historical details. According to the book of Exodus, he was born to Hebrew slaves in Egypt at a time when Pharaoh had ordered their male infants killed; set adrift on the Nile in a basket, he was found and raised within the Egyptian royal household.

As a grown man he killed an Egyptian overseer who was beating a Hebrew and fled into the desert of Midian. There, the Bible relates, God spoke to him from a bush that burned without being consumed, commanding him to return and lead the Israelites out of slavery. Moses confronted Pharaoh, and after a series of devastating plagues the people were released—the Exodus—escaping through the waters of the sea that parted before them.

At Mount Sinai, according to scripture, Moses received from God the Ten Commandments and the Law that bound the people of Israel in covenant. For forty years he led them through the wilderness, shaping them into a nation, enduring their rebellions, and transmitting the commandments that would become the heart of Jewish law.

By tradition he glimpsed the Promised Land from Mount Nebo but died before entering it, handing leadership to Joshua. Honored as the great liberator and lawgiver, Moses became a model invoked across the centuries—by Jesus, by Muhammad, and by countless seekers of freedom.
