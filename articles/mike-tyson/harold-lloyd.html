Harold Lloyd

American silent-film comedian (1893–1971)

Harold Lloyd was the third great comedian of the silent screen, the bespectacled go-getter whose “thrill comedies” gave the era some of its most heart-stopping images.

Working with the producer Hal Roach, he developed the “Glasses Character”—an eager, optimistic, ordinary young man scrambling up the ladder of modern American life. Where Charlie Chaplin offered pathos and Buster Keaton offered stoic absurdity, Lloyd offered breathless suspense. His most famous film, Safety Last!, left an indelible picture of him dangling from the hands of a great clock high above a city street—a stunt all the more remarkable because he performed such feats having lost two fingers to an accident.

Through the 1920s he was enormously popular and wealthy, in some years making more films and more money than his two great rivals. Though his fame faded more than theirs after the silent era ended, the daredevil ingenuity of his stunts lived on in the work of later physical comedians like Jackie Chan, who studied the silent masters closely.

Another comedian who came up through the Hal Roach studio in those years, and who with his rotund partner would form the most beloved comedy duo in screen history, was Stan Laurel.
