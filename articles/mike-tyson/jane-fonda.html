Jane Fonda

American actress and activist (b. 1937)

Jane Fonda is an American actress and activist whose long, eventful life has spanned film stardom, fierce political controversy, and a second career as a fitness pioneer.

The daughter of Henry Fonda, she became a star in her own right in the 1960s, first in lighter fare such as the science-fiction romp Barbarella and then in serious dramatic roles that won her two Academy Awards, for the thriller Klute and the veterans' drama Coming Home. She co-starred with her father and Katharine Hepburn in On Golden Pond, and made many films with Robert Redford.

She was as famous for her convictions as for her acting. Her impassioned opposition to the Vietnam War, including a deeply controversial visit to Hanoi, made her a lightning rod for years, and she remained an outspoken voice for feminist and environmental causes. In the 1980s she launched the home-exercise craze with her best-selling workout videos.

Married in turn to a French director, an American activist, and the media magnate Ted Turner, she kept reinventing herself, returning to popularity on television in old age. Her most frequent screen partner across the decades was Robert Redford.
