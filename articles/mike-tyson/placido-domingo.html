Plácido Domingo

Spanish operatic tenor and conductor (born 1941)

Plácido Domingo is among the most versatile and prolific opera singers of all time, an artist whose career has spanned more than half a century and some hundred and fifty roles. Born in Madrid into a family of zarzuela performers and raised largely in Mexico, he began on the light-opera stage of his parents before discovering the depth and stamina of his voice in grand opera.

A tenor of dark, heroic colour, he made the title role of Verdi's Otello his own and ranged across the whole repertory, from the Italian works of Giacomo Puccini to the French masters; unusually for an Italianate tenor, he also embraced the long, punishing roles of Richard Wagner. In later decades he extended his career still further by taking on baritone parts.

His fame reached its widest audience in 1990, when he joined Luciano Pavarotti and José Carreras for the first Three Tenors concert on the eve of the football World Cup final, an event that drew opera to stadium crowds and television viewers by the hundred million. Beyond singing, he became a conductor, an opera-house director, and the founder of a competition for young voices.

His long recording career brought him together with the leading conductors of the age. Foremost among them, in a series of celebrated opera recordings and festival productions, was the Austrian maestro Herbert von Karajan.
