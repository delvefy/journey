Adi Shankara

Indian philosopher, master of Advaita Vedanta (c. 788–820)

Adi Shankara was the most influential philosopher of Hinduism and the great consolidator of Advaita Vedanta, the doctrine of non-duality. Born at Kalady in Kerala, he renounced the world as a boy, and in a life of barely more than thirty years reshaped the religious thought of India.

His system taught that the ultimate reality, Brahman, alone is truly real; that the individual self, the atman, is identical with it; and that the manifold world of experience is maya, an appearance that veils this single truth. Liberation came not through ritual but through knowledge of one's own true nature.

Shankara set out this teaching in masterly commentaries—on the Brahma Sutras of Vyasa, on the principal Upanishads, and on the Bhagavad Gita—works of close argument that remain the foundation of Vedanta. He framed his non-dualism partly against the Buddhists, whose analysis he absorbed even as he refuted it, so sharply that rivals accused him of being a Buddhist in disguise.

He is said to have travelled the length of India in great public disputations, most famously with the ritualist Mandana Mishra, winning followers as he went. To anchor his movement he founded four monastic centres at the cardinal points of the land—Sringeri, Dwarka, Puri, and Jyotirmath—and organized an order of monks that endures to this day.

He also composed hymns of great beauty and devotion. Dying young, by tradition at Kedarnath in the Himalayas, Shankara left a school that would dominate Indian philosophy and provoke the rival Vedantas of Ramanuja and Madhva. He is honoured as a jagadguru, a teacher of the world.
