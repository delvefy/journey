David Dodd

Economist and co-author of Security Analysis (1895–1988)

David Dodd was an American finance scholar whose name is permanently bound to that of Benjamin Graham. A devoted professor at Columbia Business School, he was a younger colleague of Graham's when, in the depths of the Great Depression, the two set out to give the chaotic business of investing a rigorous intellectual foundation.

The result, published in 1934, was Security Analysis, the book that founded the discipline of fundamental analysis and value investing. Graham supplied much of the brilliance and the lecturing; Dodd, by tradition, supplied the patient scholarship and organization, having carefully set down Graham's classroom teaching. Their partnership was so complete that students and investors ever after spoke simply of “Graham and Dodd,” and the approach itself as the gospel of a place Warren Buffett would jokingly call Graham-and-Doddsville.

Dodd remained the quieter, more academic of the pair, content to teach generations of Columbia students the principles he and Graham had set down, while his famous collaborator and their celebrated pupils carried the creed into the wider world.

Decades later their great textbook was revised and championed for a new age by one of the most respected of modern value investors, who wrote the preface to its latest edition—Seth Klarman.
