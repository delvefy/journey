Paul Newman

American actor and philanthropist (1925–2008)

Paul Newman was one of the most admired and enduring stars of American film, famous for his piercing blue eyes, his easy cool, and a decency that reached far beyond the screen.

He gave a string of indelible performances—the pool hustler of The Hustler, the unbreakable prisoner of Cool Hand Luke, the charming outlaw of Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid and the con man of The Sting, both made with his friend Robert Redford. Long passed over by the Academy, he won the Oscar at last for The Color of Money, Martin Scorsese's sequel to The Hustler, in which he played mentor to the young Tom Cruise. Earlier he had matched Elizabeth Taylor in Cat on a Hot Tin Roof.

Off screen he was a man of unusual substance: a fifty-year marriage to the actress Joanne Woodward, a serious career as a racing driver, and a food company whose every dollar of profit he gave to charity.

Among his co-stars in the disaster epics of the 1970s was the other great emblem of screen cool, Steve McQueen.
