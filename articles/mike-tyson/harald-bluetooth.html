Harald Bluetooth

King of Denmark, unifier and Christianiser (c. 910–c. 986)

Harald Bluetooth, born around 910, was a king of Denmark and Norway who united the Danish realm and brought it into the Christian and European world. The son of Gorm the Old—traditionally reckoned the first king of a unified Denmark—Harald consolidated his father's kingdom and extended Danish power into Norway.

His most momentous act was religious. Around 965, by tradition after the missionary Poppo proved the power of the Christian God by carrying red-hot iron unharmed, Harald accepted baptism and made Christianity the religion of the Danes. The conversion came under the long shadow of the powerful German empire to the south—the realm of Otto the Great and his Ottonian successors—whose expansion and missions pressed upon Denmark's borders, and against which Harald strengthened the great frontier rampart, the Danevirke.

Harald proclaimed his achievements on the larger of the two great runestones he raised at Jelling, in memory of his parents: that he had won for himself all Denmark and Norway, and made the Danes Christian. The Jelling stones are among the most treasured monuments of Scandinavia, sometimes called "Denmark's birth certificate."

His reign ended in betrayal by his own blood. His son Sweyn Forkbeard rose in revolt, and Harald was driven out, dying of his wounds around 986. Yet his dynasty endured and triumphed: from Sweyn came Cnut the Great, ruler of a North Sea empire. In the modern age Harald gained an unexpected second fame, for the Bluetooth wireless technology—uniting devices as he had united Denmark—was named in his honour, its symbol formed from his runic initials.
