Rudolf II

Holy Roman Emperor (1552–1612)

Rudolf II, Holy Roman Emperor, was the strange and brilliant ruler who made Prague the capital of European art and learning, a melancholy collector more at home among astronomers and alchemists than among statesmen.

Son of Maximilian II, he was sent as a boy to be raised at the rigid Spanish court of his uncle Philip II, and returned grave, reserved, and steeped in ceremony. On becoming emperor in 1576 he moved the imperial seat from Vienna to Prague and there gathered about him one of the greatest treasuries of art and curiosities the age had seen.

His court drew the finest minds of Europe. He made Tycho Brahe his imperial mathematician, and after Tycho's death raised Johannes Kepler to the post; from the observations gathered under Rudolf's patronage, Kepler would draw the laws of planetary motion. The emperor also delighted in painters such as Arcimboldo, and in alchemists and astrologers, pursuing the secrets of nature with a passion that bordered on obsession.

But the gifts that made him a patron made him a poor ruler. As the quarrel between Catholics and Protestants sharpened, he withdrew into his collections and his melancholy, until his own family forced him to surrender power to his brother Matthias. To hold Bohemia he granted it religious liberty by his Letter of Majesty of 1609.

He died in 1612, his unsolved conflicts soon to explode in the Thirty Years' War. The crown he had worn he had received from his father, Maximilian II.
