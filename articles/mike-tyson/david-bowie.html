David Bowie

British musician and cultural icon (1947–2016)

David Bowie was the great chameleon of rock, a restless innovator who reinvented himself again and again and reshaped popular music, fashion and identity in the process.

Born David Jones in London, he broke through with “Space Oddity” and then, as the flame-haired alien Ziggy Stardust, made glam rock a cultural force. Refusing ever to stand still, he moved through soul, electronic experiment—the “Berlin Trilogy” made with Brian Eno—and sleek 1980s pop, while collaborating with and producing fellow travellers like Iggy Pop. He co-wrote his first American number one, “Fame,” with John Lennon, and his duet with Queen, “Under Pressure,” topped the charts.

He was also a striking actor and a fearless explorer of gender and persona, an icon to outsiders everywhere. He worked almost to the end, releasing the haunting album Blackstar two days before his death from cancer in 2016—a final, deliberate work of art.

Among his great friends and peers in the rock aristocracy, with whom he recorded the exuberant Live Aid–era duet “Dancing in the Street,” was the frontman of the Rolling Stones, Mick Jagger.
