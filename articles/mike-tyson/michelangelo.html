Michelangelo

Italian Renaissance artist (1475–1564)

Michelangelo Buonarroti, born in Tuscany in 1475, was one of the greatest artists in history, a supreme master of sculpture, painting, and architecture whose contemporaries called him simply "the Divine." Raised in Florence, he was taken as a youth into the household of Lorenzo de' Medici, where he steeped himself in classical sculpture and humanist learning.

He first stunned the world with marble. His Pietà, carved when he was barely in his twenties, and his towering David, completed in 1504, set a new standard for the human form in stone. Summoned to Rome by the formidable Pope Julius II, he spent four grueling years on his back painting the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel, covering it with the Creation, the Fall, and the prophets in one of the supreme achievements of Western art. Decades later he returned to the chapel to paint the vast Last Judgment on its altar wall.

Fiercely independent and famously difficult, Michelangelo competed with the older Leonardo da Vinci and the younger Raphael in a Florence and Rome teeming with genius. In his later years he turned increasingly to architecture, crowning his career as chief architect of the new St. Peter's Basilica, for which he designed the great dome.

He was also a gifted poet, pouring private longing and faith into his sonnets. Working almost to the end of his long life, he died in Rome in 1564, his fame already secured by the biographer Vasari and undimmed ever since.
