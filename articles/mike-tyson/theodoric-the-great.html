Theodoric the Great

King of the Ostrogoths, ruler of Italy (454–526)

Theodoric the Great was the Ostrogothic king who ruled Italy for a generation and tried to govern the wreckage of the Western Roman Empire as a Roman would. As a boy he spent some ten years as a royal hostage in Constantinople, where he absorbed the ways and ambitions of the Roman world. The Eastern emperor, eager to turn his restless Goths elsewhere, sent him to take Italy from Odoacer—the Germanic commander who had deposed the last Western emperor in 476.

After a hard war Theodoric defeated his rival and, by tradition, slew him with his own hand at a banquet in 493. For the next thirty-three years he reigned over Italy, nominally as the deputy of Constantinople but in truth as an independent king. His rule brought a remarkable revival: he preserved Roman law, the Senate, and the machinery of government, kept the peace, and adorned his capital of Ravenna with churches and his own great mausoleum.

An Arian Christian ruling a Catholic people, he was for most of his reign notably tolerant. Only in his final, fearful years did suspicion of conspiracy with the Byzantine court lead him to execute his learned minister Boethius and others, staining his memory.

Theodoric died in 526, and within a generation his Gothic kingdom was annihilated by the armies of the Eastern emperor who dreamed of restoring the whole Roman world—Justinian I.
