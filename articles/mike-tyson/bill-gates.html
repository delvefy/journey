Bill Gates

Co-founder of Microsoft and philanthropist (born 1955)

Bill Gates was the dominant figure of the software age and, for much of his life, the richest man in the world. In 1975 he and his boyhood friend Paul Allen founded Microsoft, and through shrewd strategy and relentless drive they made its operating systems—first MS-DOS, then Windows—run on the overwhelming majority of the world's personal computers. Where his rival Steve Jobs prized design and elegance, Gates built ubiquity, and his company's software became the invisible foundation of modern office and home life.

His success was not without controversy: Microsoft's power drew a landmark antitrust prosecution, and his methods earned him a reputation for ferocity in business. Yet in midlife he turned decisively from making money to giving it away.

Through the foundation he established, the largest private charity in the world, he poured billions into global health—fighting malaria, polio, and other diseases of the poor—and into education, becoming one of the most influential philanthropists in history.

In that second career he found a close partner and friend, a man of similar wealth and similar resolve to give it away, with whom he launched a campaign urging the world's billionaires to donate their fortunes. That friend, the legendary investor known as the Oracle of Omaha, was Warren Buffett.
