Che Guevara

Argentine Marxist revolutionary (1928–1967)

Ernesto “Che” Guevara was the Argentine doctor turned guerrilla who became, in death, the most reproduced icon of revolution in the world.

Born into a middle-class family in Rosario and trained as a physician, he was transformed by a youthful motorcycle journey across South America, during which the poverty and injustice he witnessed turned him toward radical politics. Steeped in the writings of Karl Marx and Vladimir Lenin, and drawn to the peasant guerrilla strategy of Mao Zedong, he dreamed of the liberated, united Latin America that Simón Bolívar had once imagined.

In Mexico he met Fidel Castro and joined the expedition that ignited the Cuban Revolution, proving himself a fearless and ruthless commander. After victory he held high office in the new Cuban state, preaching the creation of a selfless “new man” and writing an influential manual of guerrilla warfare. Jean-Paul Sartre, meeting him in Havana, called him “the most complete human being of our age.”

Restless for global revolution, he left Cuba to carry the struggle abroad, first to the Congo and then to Bolivia, where in 1967 he was captured and executed by soldiers aided by the CIA. The photograph of his face, taken years before, became an emblem of rebellion on countless walls and shirts. Among the Latin American leaders who would later wrap themselves in his memory and Bolívar's, proclaiming a new “Bolivarian” revolution, was the Venezuelan Hugo Chávez.
