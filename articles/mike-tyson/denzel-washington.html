Denzel Washington

American actor and director (b. 1954)

Denzel Washington is one of the most commanding actors of his generation, a performer of great dignity and intensity who has carried both serious drama and popular cinema for four decades.

He first won an Academy Award as a proud, rebellious soldier in the Civil War film Glory, and a second, for Best Actor, as a corrupt detective in Training Day—only the second Black actor ever to win that award. In between he gave perhaps his greatest performance in the title role of Malcolm X, embodying the slain civil-rights leader so completely that the portrayal became definitive.

That film was the work of the director Spike Lee, with whom Washington formed a lasting partnership across several pictures. He ranged widely—the boxing drama The Hurricane, the crowd-pleasing Remember the Titans, the crime epic American Gangster—and turned director himself, notably with Fences, drawn from the stage play in which he had also triumphed.

Admired on screen and on the stage alike, he is regarded as a model of craft and gravity. His Oscar-winning turn in Philadelphia had paired him with Tom Hanks.

His most fruitful collaboration was with the filmmaker who directed Malcolm X, Spike Lee.
