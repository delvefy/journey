Khadija

First wife of Muhammad and first Muslim (c. 555–619)

Khadija bint Khuwaylid, born around 555 in Mecca, was the first wife of the Prophet Muhammad and, by the testimony of Islamic tradition, the first person of all to accept Islam. A wealthy widow and a respected merchant in her own right—rare for a woman of her time—she ran caravans of trade between Mecca and Syria and hired the young Muhammad, then known for his honesty, to manage her goods.

Impressed by his character, Khadija proposed marriage to him, though she was some years his senior. The union was happy and devoted, and for as long as she lived Muhammad took no other wife. Together they had several children, including the daughter Fatima, through whom the Prophet's bloodline would descend.

Khadija's greatest role came at the very birth of Islam. When Muhammad returned from the cave of Hira in 610, shaken and terrified by his first encounter with the angel Gabriel, it was Khadija who comforted and reassured him, becoming the first to believe in his prophethood. She sustained him with her faith and her fortune through the lonely early years of his mission and the bitter persecution of the Meccans.

She died around 619, in what Muhammad called the "Year of Sorrow," along with his protective uncle Abu Talib. Muhammad grieved her deeply and honoured her memory for the rest of his life. Revered as one of the greatest women in Islam—and counted among the four perfect women of the faith—Khadija stands as the first believer and the steadfast support of the Prophet at the dawn of his mission.
