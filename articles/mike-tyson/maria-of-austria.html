Maria of Austria

Holy Roman Empress (1528–1603)

Maria of Austria was a Spanish infanta who became Holy Roman Empress, a woman who stood at the very centre of the Habsburg dynasty as daughter, sister, wife, and mother of its greatest rulers.

She was a daughter of the emperor Charles V and Isabella of Portugal, and the elder sister of Philip II of Spain. In 1548 she married her first cousin Maximilian, the future emperor Maximilian II, and the young couple were left to govern Spain as regents while her brother Philip travelled abroad.

Devout to the point of austerity, Maria often found herself uneasy with her more tolerant and easy-going husband amid the religious storms of the age. Yet the marriage was fruitful beyond measure: she bore sixteen children. Among them were two future emperors, Rudolf II and Matthias, and a daughter, Anna, who became the fourth wife of her own uncle, Philip II.

After Maximilian's death in 1576 she left the imperial court and returned to the land of her birth. There she passed her last twenty-seven years in the convent of the Descalzas Reales in Madrid, founded by her sister, living in pious retirement among the nuns while still consulted on the affairs of two dynasties.

She died in 1603, having seen her eldest son installed upon the imperial throne as Rudolf II, the strange and brilliant emperor of Prague.
