Achyuta Pisharati

Astronomer and grammarian of Kerala (c. 1550–1621)

Achyuta Pisharati was an astronomer, mathematician, and grammarian of Kerala, and one of the last great figures of the mathematical school founded by Madhava. A pupil of Jyeshtadeva, he carried the tradition of careful, demonstrated astronomy into the seventeenth century.

His most noted scientific contribution was the method known as the "reduction to the ecliptic," a correction for finding the true longitude of a planet—a refinement that brought computed positions closer to the observed sky. He set out his astronomy in works such as the Sphutanirnaya and the Rasigolasphutaniti, and wrote on the calculation of eclipses.

Achyuta was also a grammarian of standing, the author of a concise Sanskrit grammar, and a man of wide learning at the heart of Kerala's intellectual life. He stood in the direct line of teachers descending from Madhava through Nilakantha and Jyeshtadeva.

His most celebrated role, however, was as a teacher. Among his pupils was Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri, who would become one of the greatest Sanskrit poets of Kerala—the link by which the austere world of Kerala astronomy touched the flowering of its devotional literature.

With Achyuta the creative period of the Kerala school neared its close, though his methods continued in use. He is remembered as a meticulous astronomer and as the master who trained a poet whose fame would in time outshine his own.
