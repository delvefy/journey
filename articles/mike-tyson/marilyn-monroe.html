Marilyn Monroe

American actress and cultural icon (1926–1962)

Marilyn Monroe was the most famous film star of her age and remains one of the enduring icons of the twentieth century, a symbol of glamour and beauty shadowed by vulnerability and an early death.

After a hard childhood spent largely in foster homes, she began as a photographic model and worked her way into films, where she played comic blonde roles with a gift and a tenderness that transcended them. She lit up Gentlemen Prefer Blondes and The Seven Year Itch, gave a dazzling comic turn in Some Like It Hot, and showed real dramatic depth in The Misfits, written for her by her husband Arthur Miller.

She married the baseball hero Joe DiMaggio and then the playwright Miller, fought the studios for better parts, and studied seriously at the Actors Studio. She co-starred with Laurence Olivier in The Prince and the Showgirl, and in 1962 famously sang “Happy Birthday, Mr. President” to John F. Kennedy.

Beneath the dazzle she suffered from deep anxiety and dependence, and in August 1962 she died of a drug overdose at only thirty-six.

Her face outlived her as few have: the artist Andy Warhol turned her image into a series of silkscreen portraits that became emblems of fame itself.
