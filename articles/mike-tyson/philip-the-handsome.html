Philip the Handsome

Duke of Burgundy, King of Castile (1478–1506)

Philip the Handsome was the first Habsburg to wear a Spanish crown, and though he reigned only a few months and died at twenty-eight, through his sons he made his dynasty the master of Europe.

He was the son of the emperor Maximilian I and of Mary of Burgundy, and on his mother's early death he inherited the wealthy lands of the Burgundian Netherlands. In 1496, as part of a great web of marriages binding the Habsburgs to Spain, he married Joanna, a daughter of the Catholic Monarchs.

Fortune then made Joanna the heiress of Castile and Aragon, and Philip determined to rule in her right. When Isabella I of Castile died, he came to Spain to claim the throne, and there fell into a bitter contest with his father-in-law, Ferdinand II of Aragon, over who should govern. By a hard-driven agreement Philip won recognition as king alongside Joanna, pushing Ferdinand aside.

His triumph was brief. Within months he was dead of a sudden fever, leaving Joanna distracted with grief and the regency of Castile once more in dispute.

Yet his line endured beyond all expectation. His sons became the emperors Charles V and Ferdinand I, heirs to Burgundy, Spain, Austria, and the imperial crown. Philip's own great inheritance had come down to him from his mother, Mary of Burgundy, the heiress of Charles the Bold.
