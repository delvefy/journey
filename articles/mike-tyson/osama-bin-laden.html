Osama bin Laden

Founder of al-Qaeda (1957–2011)

Osama bin Laden was the founder of the terrorist network al-Qaeda and the architect of the September 11 attacks, the most wanted man of his era.

Born in Riyadh into a wealthy Saudi construction dynasty, he was drawn in the 1980s to Afghanistan, where Arab volunteers joined the war against the Soviet occupation. Out of those networks of fighters and money he built al-Qaeda at the decade's end. After the Gulf War he turned his fury on the United States, enraged by the presence of American troops in Saudi Arabia, and issued declarations calling for attacks on Americans everywhere.

His organization carried out the 1998 bombings of two American embassies in East Africa and the attack on the USS Cole, and then, on September 11, 2001, the hijackings that destroyed the World Trade Center and killed nearly three thousand people. The atrocity launched the War on Terror of President George W. Bush, who invaded Afghanistan to destroy al-Qaeda and its Taliban hosts. Bin Laden slipped away into the mountains and vanished for nearly a decade.

He was finally tracked to a compound in Abbottabad, Pakistan, and killed there in May 2011 by American special forces in a raid ordered by President Barack Obama. His murderous ideology had been shaped by the radical Islamist thought of an earlier Egyptian writer—the theorist whose call for a revolutionary vanguard against the modern order influenced a whole generation of jihadists—Sayyid Qutb.
