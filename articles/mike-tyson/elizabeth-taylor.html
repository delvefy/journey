Elizabeth Taylor

Hollywood film legend and icon of glamour (1932–2011)

Elizabeth Taylor was one of the last and greatest stars of Hollywood's golden age, famous as much for her violet-eyed beauty and turbulent life as for her acting. A child star who captivated audiences in National Velvet, she grew into a commanding screen presence in A Place in the Sun, Giant, and Cat on a Hot Tin Roof, and won two Academy Awards, the second for the ferocious Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf?

Her 1963 epic Cleopatra was the most expensive film yet made and nearly ruined its studio, but it changed her life, for on its set she met the actor who would become her great obsession. Their love affair, conducted while both were married to others, scandalized the world and made “Liz and Dick” the most watched couple on earth.

She married eight times to seven men—twice to that same actor—and her jewels, her diamonds, and her dramas filled the newspapers for decades. Yet behind the glamour was a woman of fierce loyalty and courage. After the death of her friend Rock Hudson she became one of the first celebrities to fight publicly against AIDS, founding a major research foundation and raising enormous sums when few others dared speak of the disease.

Devoted to her friends—among them Michael Jackson, whom she defended to the last—she remained a legend until her death in 2011. The actor who was the great love of her life, her co-star and twice her husband, was Richard Burton.
