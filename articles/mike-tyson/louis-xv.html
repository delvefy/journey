Louis XV

King of France (1710–1774)

Louis XV was born at Versailles in 1710, the great-grandson of Louis XIV. A succession of deaths in the royal family left him heir, and he became king at the age of five in 1715, under the regency of Philippe d'Orléans. After the regency, the long and frugal ministry of Cardinal Fleury gave France a generation of relative peace and recovery, and the young king, handsome and admired, was hailed as Louis le Bien-Aimé, the Beloved.

That affection drained away over a reign of fifty-nine years. France fought the War of the Austrian Succession to little lasting gain, and then, in the Diplomatic Revolution of 1756, reversed centuries of policy to ally with Maria Theresa of Austria against Frederick the Great of Prussia. The Seven Years' War that followed was a catastrophe: France lost Canada and its position in India to Britain, and the monarchy's prestige with it.

At home the court revolved around the king's mistresses, above all Madame de Pompadour, whose intelligence and patronage of the arts made her a power in government for two decades. The treasury sank deeper into debt, and the parlements grew defiant. The philosopher Voltaire, who chronicled the age, embodied the rising criticism the throne could no longer silence.

Louis died of smallpox in 1774, leaving an exhausted treasury and a discredited crown to his grandson Louis XVI and the young Marie Antoinette. The failures of his reign did much to prepare the ground for the Revolution that would consume his successors.
