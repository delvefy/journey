Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri

Sanskrit poet and grammarian of Kerala (1560–1646)

Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri was the greatest Sanskrit poet of Kerala and the author of the Narayaniyam, one of the most beloved devotional poems of southern India. Born near Tirur into a Namboodiri Brahmin family, he was a prodigy who mastered grammar, logic, and the sciences while still young.

He studied under the astronomer and grammarian Achyuta Pisharati, from whom he received both his learning in the exact sciences and his deep grounding in Sanskrit grammar. From this training came his great grammatical work, the Prakriya-sarvasvam, an original and comprehensive reorganization of the system of Panini.

His fame, however, rests on the Narayaniyam, composed in 1587. In one thousand and thirty-four verses it distils the vast Bhagavata Purana—the cycle of Vishnu's descents and the life of Krishna, traditionally ascribed to the sage Vyasa—into a single sustained hymn. By tradition Melpathur composed it at the temple of Guruvayur, where, stricken by paralysis, he was healed canto by canto through his devotion.

The poem is prized for its compression, its musicality, and its theological depth, each canto closing with a prayer to the Lord. It is recited daily at Guruvayur and across Kerala four centuries later.

Melpathur was honoured at the court of the Zamorin of Calicut and ranked among the foremost scholars of his age. In him the learning of Kerala's astronomers flowered into a poetry that has carried his name far beyond the world of the schools.
