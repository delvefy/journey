Gaspard de Coligny

Admiral of France and Huguenot leader (1519–1572)

Gaspard de Coligny was born in 1519 into the noble Châtillon family and rose to become Admiral of France. A capable soldier in the Italian Wars of Henry II, he was taken prisoner at the disaster of Saint-Quentin in 1557, and during his captivity moved toward the Reformed faith. Around 1560 he openly embraced the Calvinism of John Calvin and emerged as one of the foremost leaders of the French Protestants.

Through the savage Wars of Religion he fought beside the Prince of Condé, and after Condé's death became the supreme political and military chief of the Huguenots. The peace of 1570 brought him to the court of the young King Charles IX, over whom he gained a remarkable personal ascendancy, urging the king to lead France into war with Spain in support of the Dutch revolt.

This policy, and the trust the king placed in him, terrified Catherine de' Medici and the Catholic house of Guise, who had long blamed him for an earlier assassination. In August 1572, with the Protestant nobility gathered in Paris for the wedding of Henry of Navarre, a gunman wounded Coligny in the street.

Two days later, in the St Bartholomew's Day Massacre, his enemies finished the work: Coligny was killed in his lodgings and his body cast from the window, the first victim of a slaughter that spread across France. He was thereafter revered as a martyr of the Protestant cause.
