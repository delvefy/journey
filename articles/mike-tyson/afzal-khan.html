Afzal Khan

General of the Sultanate of Bijapur (d. 1659)

Afzal Khan was one of the foremost generals of the Sultanate of Bijapur, remembered for his dramatic and fatal encounter with the Maratha leader Shivaji, an episode that became a turning point in the history of the Deccan.

He rose to prominence in the service of the Adil Shahi sultans, commanding armies in the long wars by which Bijapur extended its power into the Karnataka south. A noble of the court of Mohammed Adil Shah, he was esteemed as a hard and capable soldier in a kingdom hard pressed on every side.

In 1659 the sultanate, alarmed by the rapid rise of Shivaji, who was seizing hill-forts and defying his overlords, dispatched Afzal Khan at the head of a great army to crush him. Marching into the western hills, he sought to draw the elusive Maratha into open battle. By tradition he provoked Shivaji by despoiling temples along his route, but Shivaji refused to be lured down from his strongholds.

At last the two agreed to meet for a parley at the foot of the fortress of Pratapgad. The meeting ended in violence: according to the famous account, Afzal Khan attempted to stab Shivaji as they embraced, but Shivaji, wearing hidden armour and the steel claws called bagh nakh, killed the general on the spot. The leaderless Bijapuri army was then routed.

The death of Afzal Khan made Shivaji's name across India and shattered Bijapur's prestige. The Sultanate that had sent him, now ruled by the young Ali Adil Shah II, never recovered the initiative against the Marathas.
