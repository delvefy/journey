George V

King of the United Kingdom and Emperor of India (1865–1936)

George V was King of the United Kingdom and Emperor of India through the upheavals of the early twentieth century, a plain and dutiful sovereign who steered the monarchy through war and revolution into the modern age.

A grandson of Queen Victoria, he was a naval officer by training and became heir only after the early death of his elder brother. Reserved, conscientious, and a devoted stamp collector, he came to the throne in 1910 amid a constitutional struggle over the power of the House of Lords.

His reign was dominated by the First World War. With feeling against all things German running high, he in 1917 changed the name of his royal house from Saxe-Coburg-Gotha to the English-sounding Windsor. The war set him against his own first cousin, the German emperor Wilhelm II, and it doomed another first cousin, Tsar Nicholas II of Russia, whom he closely resembled and whom, when revolution came, Britain declined to shelter.

He weathered the rise of Labour, the troubles of Ireland, and the strains of empire, and he brought the crown closer to his people, beginning the royal Christmas broadcasts by radio.

He was the grandfather of Elizabeth II, who would carry the house of Windsor through the rest of the century. He died in 1936. His tragic cousin and look-alike was Nicholas II.
