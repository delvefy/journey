Nero

Roman emperor (37–68 AD)

Nero, born in 37 AD, was the fifth Roman emperor and the last of the Julio-Claudian dynasty descended from Augustus. He came to the throne at sixteen, after his scheming mother Agrippina poisoned the emperor Claudius, who had adopted Nero as his heir. For a few years the young ruler was steadied by wise counselors—above all his old tutor, the philosopher Seneca—and Rome was governed well.

Power soon corrupted him. Nero murdered his mother, his stepbrother Britannicus, and his wife Octavia, and indulged a craving to perform in public as a singer, charioteer, and actor, to the horror of traditional Romans. When the Great Fire of 64 AD devastated the city, rumor accused the emperor himself, and he in turn fixed the blame on the small sect of Christians, unleashing the first imperial persecution; tradition holds that the apostles Peter and Paul died in Rome at about this time.

On the cleared ground Nero raised his enormous, gilded pleasure-palace, the Golden House. His extravagance, heavy taxation, and cruelty alienated the provinces, the Senate, and finally the army.

In 68 AD, declared a public enemy and deserted on all sides, Nero fled the city and took his own life, reportedly lamenting, "What an artist dies in me!" His fall plunged Rome into the chaos of the Year of the Four Emperors, and his name became a byword for tyranny and decadence.
