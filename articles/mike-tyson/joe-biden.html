Joe Biden

46th President of the United States (born 1942)

Joe Biden was the 46th President of the United States, a fixture of American public life for half a century who reached the White House only at its very end.

Elected to the Senate from Delaware in 1972 at the age of twenty-nine, he endured the loss of his wife and daughter in a car crash that same year and built a long career in foreign affairs and the law, weathered by further personal grief. After two failed runs for the presidency he served eight years as the loyal vice-president to Barack Obama. In 2020, amid a pandemic, he defeated the incumbent Donald Trump to become, at seventy-eight, the oldest person ever sworn in as president.

His single term brought a vast pandemic-recovery program and major laws on infrastructure and climate, but also a painful withdrawal from Afghanistan and stubborn inflation. Abroad he organized the Western response to Russia's full invasion of Ukraine, leading sanctions and arms shipments against Vladimir Putin, while steering the long rivalry with the China of Xi Jinping. Faltering in his bid for re-election in 2024, he withdrew, and his party lost the office back to Trump.

The defining foreign cause of his presidency was the defense of Ukraine, and his closest partner in it was the wartime leader who rallied his besieged nation and the world—Volodymyr Zelensky.
