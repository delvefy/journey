Madonna

American singer, the "Queen of Pop" (b. 1958)

Madonna is the most successful female recording artist in history and one of the defining figures of modern popular culture, the self-made “Queen of Pop” whose constant reinvention reshaped the music industry.

Born in Michigan, she made her way to New York in the late 1970s and into its restless downtown world of dance clubs and artists. Her breakthrough came in the early 1980s, and across the following decade she ruled the charts with a string of hits—Like a Virgin, Material Girl, Like a Prayer, Vogue—each accompanied by a striking new image.

She courted controversy as deliberately as fame, provoking outrage over religion and sexuality, and she turned every scandal to her advantage. Forever remaking her sound and persona, she stayed at the summit of pop far longer than any rival, while also acting—winning praise for the title role in Evita—and building a formidable business empire.

She was the great female counterpart to Michael Jackson in the age when pop conquered the world, and her influence runs through nearly every pop star who has followed her.

Among the most publicised chapters of her life was her turbulent marriage to the actor Sean Penn.
