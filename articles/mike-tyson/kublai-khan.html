Kublai Khan

Mongol emperor, founder of the Yuan dynasty (1215–1294)

Kublai Khan, born in 1215, was a grandson of Genghis Khan and the fifth Great Khan of the Mongol Empire, who completed the Mongol conquest of China and founded the Yuan dynasty. After a struggle for the succession following his brother Möngke's death, he secured the throne and, in 1279, crushed the Southern Song—becoming the first foreign ruler to govern all of China.

He moved the center of his power into China, building a magnificent capital at Dadu, the site of modern Beijing, and at his summer city of Shangdu, the "Xanadu" that would later haunt European imagination. Ruling as a Chinese emperor while remaining a Mongol khan, he adopted Chinese institutions, patronized Tibetan Buddhism, extended the Grand Canal, and presided over a cosmopolitan realm in which merchants, missionaries, and adventurers moved freely along the protected trade routes of the Mongol peace.

Among the foreigners welcomed to his court was the Venetian Marco Polo, whose account of the Khan's wealth and splendor made Kublai a figure of legend in the West. Yet not all his ambitions prospered: his great seaborne invasions of Japan, in 1274 and 1281, were shattered by storms the grateful Japanese hailed as the "divine wind," the kamikaze.

By his death in 1294 the once-unified Mongol Empire had fragmented into rival khanates, but the dynasty Kublai founded would rule China for nearly another century.
