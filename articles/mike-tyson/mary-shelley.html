Mary Shelley

English novelist, author of Frankenstein (1797–1851)

Mary Shelley was the author of Frankenstein, one of the most famous and influential novels ever written and a founding work of science fiction. She was born into intellectual royalty—the daughter of the feminist Mary Wollstonecraft, who died days after her birth, and the philosopher William Godwin—and grew up amid books and radical ideas. At sixteen she fell in love with the married poet Percy Bysshe Shelley, and the two ran away together to the continent.

In the rainy summer of 1816, lodging near Lake Geneva in the company of Percy, Lord Byron, and Byron's physician Polidori, the friends amused themselves by competing to write ghost stories. Out of that famous gathering, the eighteen-year-old Mary conceived her tale of Victor Frankenstein and the wretched creature he assembles and abandons. Published in 1818, Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus became a myth for the modern age, a meditation on science, creation, and responsibility.

Her life was shadowed by grief: the deaths of several of her children and, in 1822, the drowning of Percy off the Italian coast. Widowed young, she devoted herself to raising their surviving son, to editing and championing her husband's poetry, and to writing further novels of her own.

Mary Shelley died in 1851, her reputation secured by a single immortal book. The Romantic poet she had loved, eloped with, and married—and whose verse she preserved for posterity—was Percy Bysshe Shelley.
