Henry II of France

King of France (1519–1559)

Henry II was born in 1519, the second son of King Francis I. As a boy he spent years as a hostage in Spain, surrendered in exchange for his captured father, an ordeal that left him grave and reserved. In 1533 he was married to Catherine de' Medici, though his heart and his trust belonged for life to his mistress Diane de Poitiers, who wielded great influence over him.

He came to the throne in 1547 and pressed on with the long Habsburg–Valois struggle, waging the Italian Wars against the Emperor Charles V and then against Charles's son, Philip II of Spain. He recovered Calais from the English and seized the bishoprics of Metz, Toul and Verdun, while at home he persecuted the growing Protestant movement with severity.

The exhausting wars were finally closed by the Peace of Cateau-Cambrésis in 1559, sealed with marriages—his daughter to Philip II, and the young Mary, Queen of Scots, already wed to his heir Francis. To celebrate, Henry held a great tournament in Paris.

There disaster struck: a splintered lance pierced the king's eye through his visor, and after ten days of agony he died in July 1559. His death, later said to have been foretold by Nostradamus, left the throne to a series of weak young sons and threw France, under Catherine's regency, into decades of religious civil war.
