Robert De Niro

American actor, master of total immersion (born 1943)

Robert De Niro emerged from the same New York Method tradition as his great contemporary Al Pacino and became, like him, one of the defining screen actors of his age. Famous for the totality of his immersion in a part—learning to box, to drive a cab, to speak a dialect, even reshaping his own body—he disappeared so completely into his roles that the man behind them remained a mystery.

He won his first Academy Award as the young Vito Corleone in The Godfather Part II, taking up a character the older Brando had created. But his deepest work came in a long partnership with one director, in a series of films about violence, guilt, and the American underworld: the lonely, menacing cabbie of Taxi Driver, the self-destructive boxer Jake LaMotta of Raging Bull—which won him a second Oscar—and the gangsters of Goodfellas and Casino.

Across other films—The Deer Hunter, Once Upon a Time in America, and Heat, where he at last faced Pacino across a diner table—and in a later, unexpected gift for comedy, he proved himself among the most versatile of actors. He also built the Tribeca Film Festival to revive his beloved downtown New York.

The director who was his lifelong collaborator, with whom he made Taxi Driver, Raging Bull, and Goodfellas, was Martin Scorsese.
