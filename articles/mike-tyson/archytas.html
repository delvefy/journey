Archytas

Pythagorean philosopher and statesman of Tarentum (c. 435–347 BC)

Archytas of Tarentum was the greatest of the later Pythagoreans and a rare embodiment of the ideal of the philosopher in power. In his native Tarentum, the foremost Greek city of southern Italy, he was elected general again and again and, by tradition, was never beaten in the field—a ruler famed for justice, moderation, and learning who governed by reason rather than force.

As a mathematician he was among the most original of his age. He produced a celebrated solution to the ancient problem of doubling the cube, by a bold construction in three dimensions, and he advanced the theory of proportions and of the musical scale, carrying forward the Pythagorean faith that number underlies all harmony. He is also reckoned a founder of mechanics, and legend credited him with building a self-moving wooden dove.

His closest tie was to Plato, his friend and admirer. When Plato fell into danger at the court of the tyrant of Syracuse, it was Archytas who intervened to rescue him, sending help to bring the philosopher safely away. Through that friendship, Archytas helped fix mathematics at the heart of Plato's vision of education and the well-ordered state, deepening the legacy of Pythagoras.

Among the pupils who carried his mathematics forward was one of the supreme geometers and astronomers of antiquity, Eudoxus of Cnidus.
