Lionel of Antwerp

Duke of Clarence, English prince (1338–1368)

Lionel of Antwerp, named for the city of his birth, was the third son of King Edward III of England and Queen Philippa of Hainault, a brother of the famous Black Prince and of John of Gaunt. A giant of a man by report, he was married while still a boy to Elizabeth de Burgh, heiress of Ulster, a match that made him one of the greatest landholders in Ireland and, in time, Duke of Clarence.

In the 1360s his father sent him to Ireland as royal lieutenant, charged with restoring an English authority that had badly decayed. His chief monument there was the Statutes of Kilkenny of 1366, which tried, with little success, to forbid the English settlers from adopting Irish law, language, and custom. In his household in these years served a young page named Geoffrey Chaucer, then at the very start of his long career.

Widowed, Lionel was offered a glittering second marriage by the Visconti, who sought English prestige for their house. In 1368 he traveled to Milan and wed Violante, daughter of Galeazzo II, in a feast remembered for its fabulous extravagance.

The triumph was brief. Within months Lionel fell ill and died at Alba in Piedmont, some whispering of poison. Through his daughter Philippa his blood would pass to the Mortimers and at last to the House of York, furnishing the claim that lit the Wars of the Roses. The king his father, victor of Crécy and founder of the Order of the Garter, was Edward III.
