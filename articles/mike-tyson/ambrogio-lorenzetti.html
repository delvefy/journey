Ambrogio Lorenzetti

Sienese painter of the Good Government frescoes (c. 1290–1348)

Ambrogio Lorenzetti, born in Siena around 1290, was one of the most original and intellectually ambitious painters of the fourteenth century. The younger brother of the painter Pietro Lorenzetti, he absorbed both the lyrical grace of the Sienese tradition descending from Duccio and the solid, space-filling naturalism of the Florentine Giotto, fusing them into an art of remarkable observation.

His masterpiece, and one of the supreme achievements of medieval painting, is the great fresco cycle of the Allegory and Effects of Good and Bad Government, painted between 1338 and 1339 in the council chamber of Siena's Palazzo Pubblico. On its walls a serene, prosperous city and a fertile countryside under just rule are set against the desolation wrought by tyranny. The sweeping panorama of Siena and its hills is among the first true landscapes in Western art, and the whole cycle is a profound visual argument about the common good.

Ambrogio was a learned man, interested in geometry and the writings of the ancients, and his paintings show an unusual concern with perspective and the natural world. He worked in Florence as well as Siena and was admired by his contemporaries for the depth of his thought as much as the beauty of his hand.

His career, and that of his brother, was cut short by catastrophe: both Lorenzetti are believed to have perished in the Black Death that devastated Siena in 1348. Long undervalued, Ambrogio Lorenzetti is now recognised as a painter of extraordinary vision, whose meditation on good government still speaks across seven centuries.
