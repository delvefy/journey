Kalidasa

The supreme poet and dramatist of classical Sanskrit (c. 4th–5th century AD)

Kalidasa is universally regarded as the greatest poet and playwright of the classical Sanskrit tradition—the figure later admirers would call the Shakespeare of India. Almost nothing certain is known of his life. Legend makes him a simpleton transformed into a master of language by the grace of a goddess, and tradition places him among the “jewels” of the court of a king Vikramaditya, often identified with one of the great Gupta emperors during whose golden age learning and art flourished.

His surviving works are few but supreme. In drama, his Recognition of Shakuntala—the tale of a king, a forest maiden, a lost ring, and a curse—is the most celebrated of all Sanskrit plays. In epic he wrote the Raghuvamsha, on the storied dynasty of Raghu, and the Kumarasambhava, on the birth of the war-god. In lyric his Meghaduta, the “Cloud Messenger,” sends a banished spirit's love across the whole landscape of India on the back of a passing monsoon cloud.

His poetry is prized for its luminous imagery, its tenderness, and its descriptions of nature and the seasons of love. When his Shakuntala was translated into English at the end of the eighteenth century it caused a sensation in Europe, and Goethe saluted it in a famous verse of wonder.

The Raghuvamsha sang the line of Rama, whose ancient story had first been told in the great epic ascribed to the sage revered as the very first of poets—Valmiki.
