Joseph Smith

Founder of the Latter Day Saint movement (1805–1844)

Joseph Smith was the founding prophet of Mormonism and one of the most influential religious figures in American history. Born in Vermont and raised amid the fervent revivals of upstate New York, he announced in his youth a series of heavenly visions, and declared that an angel had directed him to buried golden plates which he translated as the Book of Mormon, published in 1830. That same year he organised the church that would become the Latter-day Saints.

He gathered converts and led them westward in search of a promised gathering place—to Kirtland in Ohio, then to Missouri, where mobs and the state government drove them out with savage violence, and finally to Illinois, where his followers built the prosperous city of Nauvoo. Continuing revelations shaped a distinctive faith, and in private he introduced the practice of plural marriage.

Smith sought justice for his persecuted people from the highest levels of government, traveling to Washington to lay their grievances before the president, only to be turned away. In 1844 he even launched a campaign for the presidency himself.

That year his order to destroy a dissident newspaper in Nauvoo brought his arrest. Jailed at Carthage, he and his brother were killed when an armed mob stormed the building. His martyrdom set off the struggle for leadership won by his apostle Brigham Young. The president who had earlier refused to help him was Martin Van Buren.
