Tom Cruise

American actor and global movie star (born 1962)

Tom Cruise has been one of the biggest film stars in the world for four decades, a byword for box-office power and relentless dedication. He burst into stardom as a cocky young pilot in Top Gun and never relinquished his place at the summit of Hollywood, drawing audiences across the globe to one blockbuster after another.

He was more than an action idol. He held his own against Paul Newman in The Color of Money and Dustin Hoffman in Rain Man, earned acclaim as a paralyzed veteran in Born on the Fourth of July, faced down Jack Nicholson in the courtroom of A Few Good Men, and bared a darker intensity in Magnolia. But it was the Mission: Impossible series that defined his later career, famous for the death-defying stunts—clinging to airplanes, scaling towers—that he insisted on performing himself.

Off screen he became as known for his fervent and controversial devotion to the Church of Scientology as for his films, and for an almost superhuman commitment to his craft and to the survival of the cinema itself.

The director who guided him through the science-fiction thrillers Minority Report and War of the Worlds, the most commercially successful filmmaker in the history of the movies, was Steven Spielberg.
