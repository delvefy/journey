Duke of Wellington

British general and statesman (1769–1852)

Arthur Wellesley, the first Duke of Wellington, was the British commander who finally brought down Napoleon, and one of the most celebrated soldiers in his nation's history. Born in Ireland in 1769—the same year as his great adversary—he first made his reputation in India before returning to win lasting fame in Europe.

In the long Peninsular War he led British, Portuguese, and Spanish forces against the French in Spain and Portugal, grinding them down through victories such as Salamanca and Vitoria and slowly driving them back over the Pyrenees. His patient, defensive generalship earned him the nickname "the Iron Duke."

His supreme moment came in 1815. When Napoleon escaped from Elba and returned to power, Wellington took command of an allied army in Belgium and met the Emperor at the Battle of Waterloo. Through a desperate day's fighting he held his ground until the arrival of Blücher's Prussians turned the battle into a decisive allied victory, ending the Napoleonic Wars for good and making Wellington a hero across Europe.

He turned afterward to politics, serving as Tory Prime Minister from 1828, a tenure that saw the contentious passage of Catholic Emancipation, and remaining a revered elder statesman thereafter. His name passed into everyday life—in the Wellington boot and Beef Wellington—and on his death in 1852 he was honored with a magnificent state funeral.
