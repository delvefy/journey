Pietro Perugino

Umbrian Renaissance painter (c. 1448–1523)

Pietro Vannucci, called Perugino after his adopted city of Perugia, was for a time the most admired painter in Italy, master of a sweet, serene, perfectly balanced manner that seemed to many the height of grace. He learned his art in Florence, in the bustling workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio, where among his fellow apprentices was the young Leonardo da Vinci.

His reputation carried him to Rome, where Pope Sixtus IV chose him among the painters of the Sistine Chapel. There he created the Delivery of the Keys to St. Peter, a composition of such lucid perspective and calm order—figures spaced like notes across a sunlit pavement before a domed temple—that it became a textbook of the new art.

From his workshops in Perugia and Florence flowed a stream of gentle Madonnas and tranquil altarpieces, and his designs set patterns that others, above all his greatest pupil, would build upon. His Marriage of the Virgin gave Raphael the model for one of that young master's first triumphs.

In time his manner came to seem repetitive, and the rising generation turned against it; Michelangelo mocked his art as clumsy and outmoded, an insult so stinging that Perugino took him to court for slander. He died of plague in 1523, his fame already eclipsed by those he had helped to form. The master in whose Florentine workshop he had first learned to paint, alongside Leonardo, was Andrea del Verrocchio.
