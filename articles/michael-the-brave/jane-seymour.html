Jane Seymour

Queen of England, c.1508–1537

Jane was born about 1508 or 1509 at Wolf Hall in Wiltshire to Sir John Seymour, a court soldier of Henry VII and Henry VIII, and Margery Wentworth. Her education was the ordinary domestic and religious instruction of a gentry daughter, with none of the humanist polish of an Anne Boleyn or a Catherine Parr; she could just barely sign her name. She entered the royal household in the late 1520s as maid of honour to Catherine of Aragon, then served Anne Boleyn in the same office after 1533, and her grave, quiet manner contrasted sharply with the older queens.

Henry VIII began to court her openly during the autumn of 1535 and especially after Catherine's death in January 1536. Coached by her brothers Edward and Thomas Seymour and by the conservative councillors who hated Boleyn, she returned the king's gifts and made it plain she would not be a mistress. She was betrothed the morning after Anne's execution on 19 May 1536 and married Henry privately at Whitehall on 30 May. As queen she pressed for the restoration of the Princess Mary to her father's favour, which was achieved in June, and she dared, during the Pilgrimage of Grace that autumn, to plead for the dissolved monasteries; Henry warned her sharply not to meddle.

On 12 October 1537, after a long and dangerous labour at Hampton Court, she gave birth to the long-awaited male heir, the future Edward VI. The christening was magnificent, and she received envoys propped up in bed. Within days childbed fever set in, and she died on 24 October. Henry mourned her in unfeigned black, called her his only true wife, and arranged that he should be buried beside her at St George's Chapel, Windsor, where his coffin was indeed lowered into her vault in 1547.
