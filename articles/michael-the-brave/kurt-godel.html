Kurt Gödel

Austrian-American logician, 1906–1978

Kurt Gödel was born in 1906 in Brünn in Moravia (now Brno) into a German-speaking family of textile manufacturers, and as a boy was so persistent in his questions that his family called him "Herr Warum." He studied physics and then mathematics at the University of Vienna under Hans Hahn and through the seminars of the Vienna Circle around Moritz Schlick and Rudolf Carnap, though he privately rejected their logical positivism.

His doctoral thesis of 1929 proved the completeness of first-order predicate logic. Then, in 1931, at twenty-five, he published "On Formally Undecidable Propositions of Principia Mathematica and Related Systems" and overturned the foundations of mathematics. The first of his two incompleteness theorems showed that any consistent formal system rich enough to contain arithmetic must contain true statements it cannot prove. The second showed that such a system cannot prove its own consistency. Hilbert's formalist program, then at its height, was at a stroke shown to be impossible in the strict sense Hilbert had wanted.

In 1938 he proved the relative consistency of Cantor's continuum hypothesis with the axioms of set theory; Paul Cohen completed the picture in 1963 by showing its independence. As the Nazis annexed Austria and stripped him of his post, Gödel emigrated to Princeton in 1940. He spent the rest of his life at the Institute for Advanced Study, where he and Albert Einstein became inseparable; the elderly Einstein, by his own account, came to the Institute "to have the privilege of walking home with Gödel."

His later thought turned to the philosophy of mind, to the relativistic cosmology in which he constructed solutions of Einstein's field equations permitting closed time-like curves, and to a careful ontological argument for the existence of God. Increasingly paranoid in old age, he came to believe his food was being poisoned, refused to eat unless his wife Adele tasted his meals, and starved himself to death in 1978 when she was hospitalised. Among the Vienna philosophers whose seminars he had attended in his youth, the leading systematiser of logical empiricism, who carried the program of the Vienna Circle to America, was Rudolf Carnap.
