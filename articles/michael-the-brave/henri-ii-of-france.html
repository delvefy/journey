Henri II of France

King of France, 1519–1559

Henri was born at Saint-Germain-en-Laye in March 1519, the second son of Francis I and Claude of France. After Francis’s defeat at Pavia in 1525 the boy and his elder brother the dauphin were sent into Spain as hostages of Charles V, in exchange for their father’s release. Four years of comfortless captivity at Pedraza and Villalpando left a permanent mark of grimness on his character; he learned Spanish, hated his hosts, and on his return at the age of eleven was visibly the changed son. In 1533 he was married, at fourteen, to Catherine de’ Medici. When his elder brother died unexpectedly in 1536 he became heir to the French throne.

He came to the crown in 1547 on the death of his father. His policies were continuity with a hardening edge. The Italian Wars resumed at once: in 1552 he seized the three bishoprics of Metz, Toul and Verdun from the Empire, an annexation never seriously reversed thereafter. He allied with the Protestant princes of Germany against Charles V even while persecuting Huguenots at home, founded the Chambre Ardente of the Parlement of Paris in 1547 to prosecute heresy, and signed the Edict of Châteaubriant in 1551 to tighten that programme. Abroad the war went on with the Habsburgs, against both Charles V and his son Philip II, with mixed results: Calais was recaptured from the English in January 1558.

The Peace of Cateau-Cambrésis, signed in April 1559, ended the long Habsburg-Valois conflict and recognised the bishoprics. Two royal marriages sealed it: Henri’s daughter Elisabeth to Philip II of Spain, his sister Marguerite to the Duke of Savoy. At the tournament held to celebrate them, in the rue Saint-Antoine on 30 June 1559, a splinter from the Scottish captain Gabriel de Montgomery’s lance pierced the king’s gilded visor and entered his eye. He lingered ten days in agony and died on 10 July. His three minor sons would each be king in turn under the regency of their mother, Catherine de’ Medici.

The road continues to Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor.
