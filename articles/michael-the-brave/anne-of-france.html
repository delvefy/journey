Anne of France

Regent of France, 1461–1522

Anne was born in 1461, the eldest daughter of the cunning king Louis XI, who is said to have prized her shrewd judgement above that of anyone at his court. When he died in 1483 he left the crown to her young brother Charles VIII, and Anne, together with her husband Pierre de Bourbon, took up the government of France as regents for the boy king.

For most of a decade she ruled with a firm and subtle hand, earning the title "Madame la Grande." She outmanoeuvred the great nobles who challenged her authority in the conflict known as the Mad War, defeating among them the future Louis XII, and she secured the eventual union of Brittany with France by arranging her brother’s marriage to Anne of Brittany. Her court became a celebrated school of manners where many highborn children, among them Margaret of Austria, were raised.

When Charles VIII came of age Anne gradually relinquished power, but as Duchess of Bourbon she remained one of the greatest magnates in the realm and composed a book of instruction for her daughter Suzanne. The vast Bourbon inheritance she helped consolidate would later, through her son-in-law the Constable, become the source of a dangerous quarrel with the crown. She died in 1522, remembered as one of the most capable rulers France ever had.
