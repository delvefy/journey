Antiochus III the Great

Seleucid king, reigned 222–187 BC

Antiochus III came to the Seleucid throne as a youth in 222 BC and devoted his long reign to restoring the sprawling empire of his ancestors. He put down dangerous revolts in the east and in Asia Minor, then turned to the recurring struggle with Ptolemaic Egypt for the rich lands of Syria and Palestine. Defeated early at Raphia, he persevered, and at the battle of Panium around 200 BC he wrested Coele-Syria from Egypt, bringing Judea under Seleucid rule.

His most celebrated achievement was a vast eastern expedition, an anabasis that carried his armies through Parthia and Bactria to the borders of India, reasserting Seleucid suzerainty and winning him the title "the Great." At its height his empire stretched from the Aegean to central Asia, the most powerful state of the Hellenistic world.

His ambitions then collided with Rome. He extended his power into Asia Minor and crossed into Greece, welcoming to his court the exiled Carthaginian general Hannibal, who urged him to make war on the republic. But the venture ended in disaster: thrown back at Thermopylae, Antiochus was decisively crushed at the battle of Magnesia in 190 BC by a Roman army in which Scipio Africanus served. The Treaty of Apamea stripped him of Asia Minor, burdened him with a crushing indemnity, and took his younger son as a hostage to Rome. To pay his debts Antiochus plundered a temple in the east, where he was killed by an enraged populace in 187 BC.
