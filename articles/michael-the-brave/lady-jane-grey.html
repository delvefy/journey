Lady Jane Grey

Queen of England for nine days, c. 1537–1554

Jane was born in the autumn of 1537, almost certainly at Bradgate in Leicestershire, the eldest of three daughters of Henry Grey, Marquess of Dorset and later Duke of Suffolk, and of Frances Brandon, the niece of Henry VIII through her mother Mary Tudor, Queen of France. The Grey claim to the throne ran through that descent and stood, after Henry VIII's third Succession Act and his will, immediately behind the three children of the king. Jane was given a serious humanist education: she read Latin and Greek with the chaplain John Aylmer, learned Hebrew and Italian, and at thirteen so impressed the visiting Roger Ascham, found at home reading Plato in Greek while her household hunted, that he wrote of her in the Scholemaster as the ideal Protestant pupil.

From 1547 she was placed in the household of the queen-dowager Catherine Parr at Chelsea and at Sudeley; after Catherine's death in childbirth in 1548 she returned home and resumed her studies. In May 1553 her parents and John Dudley, Duke of Northumberland, the chief minister of the dying Edward VI, married her, against her will, to Northumberland's son Guildford Dudley. Edward, sick of tuberculosis and determined to keep England Protestant, set aside the claims of his half-sisters in a "Devise for the Succession" that made Jane heir. He died on 6 July 1553. Four days later, at the Tower of London, Jane was proclaimed queen.

Her reign collapsed within nine days. Mary Tudor raised East Anglia, the privy council changed sides, Northumberland's army melted away in Cambridgeshire, and on 19 July 1553 Mary was proclaimed queen in London. Jane and Guildford remained in the Tower while Mary, at first inclined to spare her, weighed the danger. Sir Thomas Wyatt's rising of January 1554, raised against the Spanish marriage and invoking Jane's name without her knowledge, decided the question. She refused her father-confessor's invitation to convert to Rome and was beheaded on Tower Green on 12 February 1554, sixteen years old, after watching her husband led out to die before her on Tower Hill.

The road continues to Edward VI.
