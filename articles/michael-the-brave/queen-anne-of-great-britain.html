Queen Anne

Last Stuart monarch; first sovereign of Great Britain, 1665–1714

Anne was the last monarch of the House of Stuart and the first to reign over a united Great Britain. Born in 1665, the younger daughter of the future James II, she was raised a firm Protestant at the insistence of her uncle Charles II, and that upbringing shaped her destiny when her father's Catholicism cost him his throne. She married Prince George of Denmark and bore many children, but a long series of miscarriages and infant deaths left her without an heir; the death of her last surviving son at the age of eleven was the central sorrow of her life.

Childless, she assented to the settlement that would carry the crown to the Protestant House of Hanover after her. She came to the throne in 1702 on the death of her brother-in-law William III, and her reign at once became a great one.

Abroad, the armies of the Duke of Marlborough won victory after victory in the War of the Spanish Succession. At home, the Act of Union of 1707 joined the kingdoms of England and Scotland into a single realm, making Anne its first sovereign and reshaping the island's history. Her court was riven by the rivalry of Whig and Tory and by the famous breach between the Queen and her domineering friend Sarah Churchill.

Plagued by ill health and grief, Anne died in 1714, and with her the direct Stuart line of sovereigns ended. The throne passed quietly to Hanover. The elder sister who had reigned before her, jointly with William of Orange, was Mary II.

The road continues to Mary II of England.
