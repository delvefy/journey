Wilhelm II of Germany

1859–1941, last German Emperor and King of Prussia

Friedrich Wilhelm Viktor Albert was born in the Crown Prince's Palace, Berlin, the eldest child of the Crown Prince Friedrich and Victoria, Princess Royal of the United Kingdom and eldest daughter of Queen Victoria. A breech birth left his left arm shrivelled, a defect he spent his life concealing. Educated by the strict Calvinist Georg Hinzpeter and at the University of Bonn, he grew into a clever, restless, sharp-tongued man whose enthusiasms outran his patience.

His ninety-nine-day father reigned only between the deaths of Wilhelm I in March 1888 and his own from throat cancer in June. Wilhelm II thus came to the throne at twenty-nine, in the prime of life, with views formed by his Prussian military entourage rather than by his liberal-English mother. In March 1890 he forced the resignation of Otto von Bismarck, ended the Reinsurance Treaty with Russia, and steered Germany into the Tirpitz Naval Plan and the construction of a battle fleet to rival Britain's. The new course alarmed his uncle King Edward VII of the United Kingdom, his cousin Tsar Nicholas II, and ultimately produced the Triple Entente.

The Daily Telegraph affair of 1908, his telegrams to Paul Kruger, the Tangier and Agadir crises over Morocco, and a personal style alternating between bluster and self-pity all undermined Germany's standing. He signed the mobilization order in August 1914 in the belief, half-shared with his ministers, that Britain would not fight; the Willy–Nicky telegrams he exchanged in those days with Nicholas II could not stop the catastrophe. Through the war the army under Hindenburg and Ludendorff increasingly governed in his name.

On 9 November 1918 he abdicated under pressure from the high command and crossed into the Netherlands. He bought the manor of Doorn near Utrecht and lived there twenty-two years in fastidious retirement, chopping wood, blaming Jews and Freemasons for the empire's fall, watching the rise of Adolf Hitler with mixed hope and disdain, refusing Churchill's offer of asylum in 1940 and politely declining to return when the Wehrmacht overran Holland. He died at Doorn in June 1941 and was buried there, by his own wish, until the monarchy should be restored.

The road continues to Edward VII.
