Pope John Paul II

1920–2005, supreme pontiff of the Roman Catholic Church, born Karol Wojtyła

Karol Józef Wojtyła was born in Wadowice in southern Poland, the younger son of a Polish-army administrative officer and a schoolteacher of Lithuanian descent. He lost his mother at eight, his elder brother at twelve, and his father at twenty. During the German occupation he worked in a stone quarry and at the Solvay chemical plant in Kraków, acted in the underground Rhapsodic Theatre, and from 1942 studied for the priesthood at the clandestine seminary run by Archbishop Adam Sapieha. He was ordained in November 1946.

He took a doctorate in Rome under the Dominican Reginald Garrigou-Lagrange and a second in moral theology at the Catholic University of Lublin, and worked as a parish priest, university chaplain, and lecturer in ethics. Pope Paul VI made him auxiliary bishop of Kraków in 1958, archbishop in 1964, and cardinal in 1967. He attended every session of the Second Vatican Council and helped to draft Gaudium et Spes on the Church and the modern world. On 16 October 1978, after the brief reign of John Paul I, the cardinals elected him at fifty-eight, the first non-Italian pope since Adrian VI in 1522 and the first Slav ever.

His twenty-six-year pontificate, the second-longest in history, was conducted at sustained motion. He undertook 104 foreign trips to 129 countries; addressed the United Nations twice; spoke at Auschwitz, at Yad Vashem and at the Western Wall in Jerusalem; and convoked the World Youth Days. His 1979 pilgrimage to communist Poland helped ignite the Solidarity movement of Lech Wałęsa, and his correspondence with Ronald Reagan and his meeting with Mikhail Gorbachev in 1989 placed him at the heart of the Catholic dimension of the Cold War's end. He survived the assassination attempt of Mehmet Ali Ağca in St Peter's Square in May 1981 and forgave him in person in Rome's Rebibbia prison.

He canonized 482 saints, far more than all his predecessors combined, opened a dialogue with the Jewish, Orthodox and Muslim communities, and worked closely with the future Pope Benedict XVI and with Mother Teresa, whom he beatified in 2003. Parkinson's disease bent and silenced him through his last years. He died at the Apostolic Palace on 2 April 2005 and was canonized in 2014.
