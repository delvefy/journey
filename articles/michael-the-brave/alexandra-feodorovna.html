Alexandra Feodorovna

1872–1918, last Empress consort of Russia

Princess Alix Viktoria Helene Luise Beatrice of Hesse and by Rhine was born in Darmstadt in June 1872, the sixth child of Ludwig IV, Grand Duke of Hesse, and of Princess Alice of the United Kingdom, the second daughter of Queen Victoria. Diphtheria in 1878 took her mother and her youngest sister within a fortnight. The four surviving children were raised much under Victoria's eye at Osborne and Windsor, and Alix was one of her grandmother's particular favourites.

In 1884 she travelled to St Petersburg for her sister Elisabeth's marriage to the Grand Duke Sergei and there met the heir to the Russian throne, the diffident sixteen-year-old Nicholas. The attachment that followed required two ducal refusals on her part to abandon Lutheranism for Orthodoxy before, in April 1894, at her cousin Wilhelm II's prompting, she at last consented at Coburg. The dying Alexander III recalled them to Russia, and within ten days of his death and Alix's reception into the Orthodox Church she married Nicholas in St Petersburg in November 1894. As empress she took the name and patronymic Alexandra Feodorovna.

The Russian court did not warm to her. Reserved, English in manners, and given to mystical religiosity, she retreated with Nicholas to Tsarskoe Selo, gave birth between 1895 and 1901 to four daughters — Olga, Tatiana, Maria and Anastasia — and in August 1904 to the long-awaited heir Alexei, who proved to suffer the haemophilia transmitted through the female line from Queen Victoria. Her search for relief for him brought into the imperial household the wandering starets Grigori Rasputin, on whose presence she came to depend.

From the summer of 1915, with Nicholas at army headquarters at Mogilev, she effectively ran the home government from Tsarskoe Selo, and her appointments at Rasputin's prompting helped destroy the dynasty's last credit. After the abdication of March 1917 she shared her husband's house arrest at Tsarskoe Selo, Tobolsk and Ekaterinburg, and was shot with him and the children in the cellar of the Ipatiev House on 17 July 1918. The Russian Orthodox Church canonized the family as passion-bearers in 2000.

The road continues to Wilhelm II of Germany.
