Marguerite of Navarre

Queen of Navarre and author, 1492–1549

Marguerite was born in 1492, the daughter of Louise of Savoy and the elder sister of the future Francis I. Exceptionally well educated for a woman of her time, fluent in several languages and learned in scripture and philosophy, she married first the Duke of Alençon and, after his death, Henry d’Albret, the king of the small Pyrenean realm of Navarre.

She was the devoted sister and trusted counsellor of Francis I; after his capture at Pavia she undertook the difficult journey to Madrid to negotiate with the Emperor Charles V for his release. As the most influential woman at the French court, she made herself the great protector of humanists, poets, and evangelical reformers, sheltering men in danger of persecution and seeking, like many in her circle, a renewal of faith that need not break with Rome.

Marguerite was also a writer of real distinction. She composed devotional verse and, most famously, the Heptameron, an unfinished collection of stories modelled on Boccaccio and rich in moral and religious reflection. Her patronage sustained authors such as Rabelais, and her household nurtured the new learning of the French Renaissance. Through her daughter, Jeanne d’Albret, she became grandmother to the future Henry IV of France. She died in 1549, mourned as the brightest ornament of her brother’s court.
