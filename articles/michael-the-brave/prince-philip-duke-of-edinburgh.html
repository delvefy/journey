Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh

Royal consort, 1921–2021

Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark was born on a dining-room table at the Mon Repos villa on Corfu in 1921, the only son of Prince Andrew of Greece and Princess Alice of Battenberg. He was a year old when his uncle King Constantine I was forced to abdicate and the family was bundled out of Greece on the British warship HMS Calypso, in an orange crate. He grew up between France, England, and Germany, with a fractured family—his mother committed to a Swiss sanatorium, his father an exile in Monte Carlo, his four sisters all married into German aristocratic houses, two of them to Nazi officers.

His mother's brother, Louis Mountbatten, took charge of his English education and steered him into the Royal Navy. He attended Gordonstoun under Kurt Hahn, then the Royal Naval College at Dartmouth, where in 1939 the young Princess Elizabeth, on a visit with her father George VI, fell for him; she was thirteen, he eighteen. He served with distinction in the Second World War in the Mediterranean and the Pacific, won mention in despatches at the Battle of Cape Matapan, and was on the deck of HMS Whelp in Tokyo Bay for the Japanese surrender.

He renounced his Greek and Danish titles, took British citizenship and the surname Mountbatten, and on 20 November 1947 married Princess Elizabeth in Westminster Abbey. He was created Duke of Edinburgh, Earl of Merioneth and Baron Greenwich. Her unexpected accession in 1952 ended his naval career and turned him into the modern world's longest-serving royal consort. He found a role for himself in active patronage of more than 800 organisations; in environmental causes through the World Wildlife Fund; in industry through the Duke of Edinburgh's Award scheme, which has involved millions of young people in physical and voluntary challenges in more than 140 countries.

He retired from public duties in 2017 at ninety-six and died at Windsor Castle in April 2021, two months short of his hundredth birthday. The naval uncle who had brought him out of statelessness into the Royal Navy, who orchestrated his marriage to the future Queen, and who himself became the last Viceroy of India and Chief of the Defence Staff, was Louis Mountbatten.
