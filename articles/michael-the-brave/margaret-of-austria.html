Margaret of Austria

Governor of the Habsburg Netherlands, 1480–1530

Margaret was born in 1480, the daughter of Maximilian I and Mary of Burgundy. As a small child she was sent to France, betrothed to the dauphin who would become Charles VIII and raised at the French court, only to be cast off when the king chose instead to marry Anne of Brittany. The slight returned her to her own family and gave her an early, hard lesson in the uses and cruelties of dynastic politics.

Two further marriages ended in early widowhood: first to Juan, the heir of Ferdinand and Isabella of Spain, who died within months, and then to Philibert of Savoy, in whose memory she raised the splendid church of Brou. Refusing to marry a fourth time, she turned her formidable energies to government, and her father and afterwards her nephew, the future Charles V, entrusted her with the rule of the Habsburg Netherlands.

For most of two decades she governed the Low Countries with skill and authority, and she took charge of raising the orphaned imperial children, including Charles V himself. A gifted diplomat, she helped negotiate the so-called Ladies’ Peace of Cambrai in 1529, and she made her court at Mechelen a brilliant centre of art and music. She died in 1530, mourned as one of the ablest rulers, male or female, of her generation.
