Craterus

Macedonian general, c. 370–321 BCE

Craterus was among the most distinguished and best-loved of Alexander the Great's generals, a Macedonian of the old stock who commanded the infantry phalanx in the great battles of the eastern conquest. Brave, conservative, and devoted to traditional Macedonian ways, he stood high in Alexander's trust and in the affection of the common soldiers.

His standing brought him into rivalry with Hephaestion, Alexander's dearest companion; the two quarreled so bitterly that, by one famous account, the king had to intervene to keep them from drawing their swords, declaring that he loved them both but for different reasons. In 324 BCE Alexander chose Craterus to lead the discharged veterans home to Macedon and to take over the regency of the homeland from the aging Antipater.

He was still on this journey when Alexander died, and he threw in his lot with Antipater, whose daughter Phila he married. When war broke out among the successors, Craterus crossed into Asia Minor to help crush the regent Perdiccas's ally Eumenes.

There, in 321 BCE, his great reputation availed him nothing: in a hard-fought cavalry battle his horse fell and he was trampled and killed, mourned even by his enemy Eumenes. His widow Phila would later marry the young Demetrius Poliorcetes, carrying her line into the next generation of the wars.
