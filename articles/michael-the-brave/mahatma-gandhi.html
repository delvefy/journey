Mahatma Gandhi

Leader of Indian independence, 1869–1948

Born in coastal Gujarat and trained as a lawyer in London, Gandhi found his vocation in South Africa, where over twenty years he fought the humiliations imposed on its Indian community and forged his method of satyagraha—the disciplined, nonviolent refusal to cooperate with injustice. Returning to India in 1915, he transformed the independence movement from an elite debating society into a mass campaign embracing the poorest villager.

Wrapped in homespun cloth and armed only with moral authority, he led great acts of civil disobedience against British rule: the boycott of foreign goods, the famous 1930 march to the sea to make salt in defiance of the salt tax, and the “Quit India” movement of the war years. Jailed again and again, he fasted to shame his followers into peace and championed Hindu–Muslim unity and the dignity of those branded untouchable, whom he called the children of God.

Independence came in 1947, but with it the agony of partition and savage communal bloodshed that broke his heart; he toured the riot-torn districts on foot, fasting to halt the killing. On 30 January 1948 he was shot dead by a Hindu extremist who hated his tolerance. Mourned as the Father of the Nation and revered worldwide as the Mahatma, or “great soul,” he became the model for nonviolent movements everywhere—above all for the American preacher Martin Luther King Jr.
