Louis Mountbatten

British admiral and last Viceroy of India, 1900–1979

Louis Francis Albert Victor Nicholas Mountbatten, 1st Earl Mountbatten of Burma, was born in 1900 at Frogmore House on the Windsor estate, a great-grandson of Queen Victoria, son of Prince Louis of Battenberg, First Sea Lord. He was christened with the names of the European monarchs who attended. In 1917, in the anti-German feeling of the First World War, the family Anglicised its name from Battenberg to Mountbatten and gave up its German titles.

He entered the Royal Navy at thirteen and rose through it with the deliberate, sometimes ruthless ambition that became his style. As Captain of HMS Kelly in 1939 he was sunk twice off Norway and once off Crete; appointed Chief of Combined Operations in 1941, he planned the disastrous Dieppe raid of 1942 and learned from it the doctrines that would make the 1944 Normandy landings possible. From 1943 he served as Supreme Allied Commander of the South East Asia Command, retaking Burma from Japan and accepting the Japanese surrender at Singapore in 1945.

In February 1947 the Labour government of Clement Attlee sent him to Delhi as the last Viceroy of India with a mandate to transfer power. He brought the date forward to 15 August 1947, partitioned British India into the two new dominions of India and Pakistan, presided over the chaos and the communal massacres that followed, and then served the new Indian state for ten months as its first Governor-General. His wife Edwina formed a close, much-discussed friendship with the first Indian Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru.

He returned to the Royal Navy as First Sea Lord, his father's old post; presided over the cancellation of British naval aviation and the difficult Suez crisis; was Chief of the Defence Staff from 1959 to 1965. As uncle to Prince Philip and great-uncle to the young Prince Charles, he carried much weight inside the British Royal Family. He was assassinated by a remote-controlled IRA bomb on his fishing boat at Mullaghmore in County Sligo on 27 August 1979. The Indian Prime Minister with whom he, and especially Edwina, brokered the chaotic partition of 1947 was Jawaharlal Nehru.
