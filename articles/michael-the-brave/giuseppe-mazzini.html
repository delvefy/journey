Giuseppe Mazzini

Italian patriot and revolutionary, 1805–1872

Mazzini was born in Genoa and as a young man joined the secret society of the Carbonari, for which he was imprisoned. In exile at Marseille he founded in 1831 the movement Young Italy, dedicated to forging a single, free, and republican Italian nation through popular insurrection and moral regeneration. More than a conspirator, he was a prophet, preaching that every people had a God-given mission and binding nationalism to duty under the banner "God and the People."

For most of his life a hunted exile, he settled for long periods in London, from which he directed plots, poured out political writings, and inspired a generation of patriots, among them the young Garibaldi. His vision reached beyond Italy: he founded a wider association, Young Europe, and became the most influential apostle of national self-determination of his century, a figure the conservative order built by Metternich regarded as among the most dangerous men in Europe.

In 1849 he briefly governed the revolutionary Roman Republic as a triumvir before French troops crushed it. When unification finally came, it took the form he had fought against, a monarchy engineered by the diplomacy of Cavour rather than the republic of his dreams, and he died in 1872 still in semi-clandestine exile within his own country. Yet as the conscience and spiritual founder of the Risorgimento, his influence on movements for liberty far outlasted him.
