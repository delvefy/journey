Barsine

Persian noblewoman, c. 363–309 BCE

Barsine was a Persian noblewoman whose life touched both the old empire and its conqueror. A daughter of the powerful satrap Artabazus, she was raised partly in exile at the Macedonian court when her father took refuge there, and so knew the Greek world from childhood. She married in turn the two Rhodian brothers in Persian service, Mentor and then the great mercenary general Memnon.

After Memnon's death and the Persian defeat at Issus in 333 BCE, Barsine was among the captives taken by the Macedonians. Cultivated and high-born, she became the mistress of Alexander the Great and bore him a son named Heracles—Alexander's only acknowledged child during his lifetime, though never accepted as his heir.

After Alexander's death Barsine withdrew with the boy to Pergamon, far from the center of the wars. But a child of Alexander's blood, however irregular, remained a danger to the ambitions of the generals.

Around 309 BCE the regent Polyperchon, prompted by Cassander, had both Barsine and the young Heracles put to death, closing yet another path back to Alexander's line.
