Francis I of France

King of France, 1494–1547

Francis was born on 12 September 1494 at Cognac, the only son of Charles, Count of Angoulême, of a younger branch of the House of Valois, and of Louise of Savoy. His cousin Louis XII had no surviving sons, and from his fourth year Francis was raised as heir presumptive. He was educated under the influence of his formidable mother and his sister Marguerite, the future Queen of Navarre, and he succeeded Louis XII on 1 January 1515. Within nine months of his accession he had crossed the Alps and crushed the Swiss pikemen of the Holy League at Marignano on 13 and 14 September 1515, recovering the Duchy of Milan.

From 1519 his reign was dominated by his rivalry with Charles of Habsburg, who beat him in the imperial election of that year and as Charles V hemmed France in on every land frontier. Four wars followed. The first ended in disaster at Pavia on 24 February 1525, where Francis was unhorsed, captured, and carried to Madrid; his release in 1526 was bought with the Treaty of Madrid, which he repudiated on French soil. Successive wars closed with the Peace of Cambrai in 1529, the Truce of Nice in 1538, and the Treaty of Crépy in 1544. To strengthen himself he made the famous "impious alliance" with Sultan Suleiman, and met Henry VIII on the Field of Cloth of Gold in 1520.

At home he centralised the kingdom: the Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts in 1539 imposed French in place of Latin in legal acts and required parish registers, and the Concordat of Bologna with Leo X in 1516 gave the crown the nomination of bishops. He invited Italian artists to France — Leonardo da Vinci spent his last three years under his patronage at Clos Lucé and died in his arms by tradition in 1519 — and rebuilt Chambord and Fontainebleau as monuments to the new style. He died at Rambouillet on 31 March 1547 and was succeeded by his second son, Henri II.
