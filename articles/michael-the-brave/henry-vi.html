Henry VI

King of England and France, 1421–1471

Henry was born at Windsor on 6 December 1421, the only son of Henry V and Catherine of Valois. His father's death at Bois de Vincennes in August 1422 made him king of England at nine months old; the death of his maternal grandfather Charles VI of France two months later made him, under the Treaty of Troyes, king of France as well. The English realm was governed by a regency council in which his uncle Humphrey of Gloucester took the protectorate; the French war was managed from Paris by his elder uncle John, Duke of Bedford. The boy-king was solemnly crowned at Westminster in 1429 and at Notre-Dame de Paris in 1431.

The French inheritance crumbled. Joan of Arc relieved Orléans and saw Charles VII crowned at Reims in 1429; Bedford died in 1435; Paris fell in 1436; the Truce of Tours of 1444, sealed by Henry's marriage to Margaret of Anjou, ceded Maine; and Normandy was lost in 1450, Gascony at Castillon in 1453. At home Henry was pious, vague, and easily led, founding Eton College in 1440 and King's College, Cambridge, in 1441, but unable to govern. The fall of his minister William de la Pole, Duke of Suffolk, and Jack Cade's rebellion of 1450 weakened the regime. Henry collapsed into catatonic stupor in 1453, and Richard, Duke of York, was made Protector. When the king recovered, the two factions of court (Lancaster) and York drifted into open war.

The first battle of St Albans in 1455 opened the Wars of the Roses. Henry was captured at Northampton in 1460, deposed by York's son Edward in 1461, briefly restored by Warwick in 1470, and recaptured after Edward IV's return. He was murdered in the Tower of London on the night of 21 May 1471, after his son Edward of Westminster had been killed at Tewkesbury. Devotion to him as an uncrowned saint persisted into the Tudor age.
