Bram Stoker

Irish novelist, author of Dracula, 1847–1912

Abraham “Bram” Stoker was born on 8 November 1847 in Clontarf, near Dublin. A sickly child kept bedridden for years — an experience he later credited with stirring his imagination — he recovered to become an athlete and honours graduate at Trinity College Dublin, then entered the Irish civil service while moonlighting as an unpaid theatre critic. A glowing notice of the actor Henry Irving’s Hamlet won him Irving’s friendship, which would shape the rest of his life.

In 1878 Stoker married Florence Balcombe, a celebrated Dublin beauty who had earlier been courted by his fellow Dubliner Oscar Wilde, and moved to London to manage Irving’s Lyceum Theatre. For nearly three decades he ran the great actor’s affairs, touring, organizing and corresponding on his behalf, while writing fiction in his spare hours. He had also, as a young man, poured out his admiration in letters to the American poet Walt Whitman, whom he revered and at last met.

In 1897 Stoker published Dracula, an epistolary novel of letters, diaries and newspaper cuttings that fixed the modern image of the vampire in the sinister Transylvanian Count. For his villain’s name and a few grim details he reached back to the fifteenth-century Wallachian prince Vlad the Impaler. The book sold modestly in his lifetime, but its afterlife on stage and screen made Dracula one of the most enduring characters in fiction. Stoker wrote other romances and a memoir of Irving, but none rivalled his masterpiece. He died in London on 20 April 1912, his fame still to come.
