Otho

Roman emperor, 32–69 CE

Marcus Salvius Otho was the second of the four emperors of 69 CE, a man whose reign lasted barely three months and whose death won him more honour than his life. Born in 32 CE into a family raised to the nobility under the early emperors, he became a close companion of the young Nero and shared in the extravagance of his court.

His fortune turned on his wife, the beautiful Poppaea Sabina. When Nero desired her for himself, Otho was conveniently removed from Rome and packed off to govern the distant province of Lusitania, which, to general surprise, he ruled with restraint and competence for some ten years. Resentment, however, smouldered.

In 68 he attached himself to Galba's rising against Nero, expecting to be adopted as the old emperor's heir. Passed over in favour of Piso, Otho bribed the Praetorian Guard, who hailed him emperor and slaughtered Galba in the Forum in January 69. Scarcely had he secured the city when the armies of the Rhine declared for their own commander, Vitellius, and marched into Italy.

The decision fell at the first battle of Bedriacum, near Cremona, where Otho's troops were beaten in April 69. Though he still commanded fresh forces and might have fought on, Otho chose instead to spare Rome a longer civil war and took his own life. Ancient writers who despised his earlier frivolity, Tacitus and Plutarch among them, praised the calm courage of his end.
