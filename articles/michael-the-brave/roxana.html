Roxana

Wife of Alexander the Great, c. 340–310 BCE

Roxana was a noblewoman of Bactria who became the wife of Alexander the Great and the mother of his posthumous son and heir. The daughter of the Sogdian baron Oxyartes, she was taken when Alexander stormed the mountain fortress where her family had sheltered, and in 327 BCE the conqueror married her—remembered as a love match as much as a politic gesture toward his new eastern subjects.

She accompanied Alexander on the long march into India and back. When he died suddenly at Babylon in 323 BCE, Roxana was pregnant; the child she bore was recognized as King Alexander IV, joint heir with Alexander's half-brother. To secure her son's position she is said to have had Alexander's other wife, Stateira, put to death.

But a woman and an infant could not hold their own amid the ambitions of Alexander's generals. Passed from the protection of one powerful figure to another, Roxana and her son were at last seized by Cassander, who held them at Amphipolis and, around 310 BCE, had them quietly killed—extinguishing the last legitimate hope of Alexander's dynasty.
