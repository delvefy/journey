Piero the Gouty

Piero di Cosimo de’ Medici, ruler of Florence, 1416–1469

Piero de’ Medici, born in 1416, was the only surviving son of Cosimo de’ Medici, the banker who had made his family the unofficial masters of Florence. He inherited the gout that ran in the line — it gave him his nickname, “il Gottoso” — and with it, on Cosimo’s death in 1464, the harder inheritance of guiding the family bank and the republic without holding any crown or formal office.

His five years of leadership were brief but decisive. In 1466 a group of powerful citizens, weary of Medici pre-eminence and led by Luca Pitti, plotted to break the family’s grip. Piero, though often confined to his bed, moved faster than the conspirators, rallied support, and faced them down; the failed coup left the Medici position stronger than before and showed that the family could survive the loss of its founder. He had married Lucrezia Tornabuoni, an accomplished poet and a shrewd manager of the household’s affairs and marriages, whose influence reached deep into the family’s fortunes.

Like his father, Piero was a careful patron and collector, fond of illuminated manuscripts, carved gems and fine objects, and it was he who had Benozzo Gozzoli fresco the family chapel with its glowing procession. He died in 1469, having held the inheritance steady and passed it, intact and unchallenged, to his sons — the young Lorenzo, soon called the Magnificent, and Lorenzo’s brother Giuliano.
