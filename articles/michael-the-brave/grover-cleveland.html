Grover Cleveland

1837–1908, twenty-second and twenty-fourth President of the United States

Stephen Grover Cleveland was born in Caldwell, New Jersey, the son of a Presbyterian minister. After his father's death he abandoned plans for college, read law in a Buffalo office, and was admitted to the New York bar in 1859. During the Civil War he hired a substitute and remained at his practice. He served as sheriff of Erie County and then, in 1881, was elected reform mayor of Buffalo, where his vetoes of corrupt appropriations earned him the nickname “Veto Mayor.”

Within a year he was governor of New York and within two years, in 1884, the Democratic candidate for president. He defeated James G. Blaine in a campaign coloured by his admission of an out-of-wedlock child and by Thomas Nast's cartoon defection from the Republican party. As president he vetoed more bills than all his predecessors combined, opposed pensions for fraudulent Civil War claims, married Frances Folsom in the White House in 1886, and called for tariff reduction. Benjamin Harrison defeated him in 1888.

Cleveland returned in 1892, the only president to serve two non-consecutive terms. The Panic of 1893 dominated his second administration. He repealed the Sherman Silver Purchase Act over the bitter opposition of his party's western wing, and in 1895 issued bonds through a syndicate led by J.P. Morgan to replenish the Treasury's gold reserve. In 1894 he sent federal troops to break the Pullman strike, breaking with the labour vote, and reappointed the young Theodore Roosevelt to the Civil Service Commission.

His insistence on the gold standard alienated his party, and in 1896 the Democrats nominated his enemy William Jennings Bryan on a silver platform. Cleveland retired to Princeton, lectured occasionally at the university, served on a trust commission for life-insurance reform, and was widely regarded by Woodrow Wilson and later admirers as the model of a Bourbon Democrat. He died at Princeton in 1908.
