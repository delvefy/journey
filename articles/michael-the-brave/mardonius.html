Mardonius

Persian general, d. 479 BCE

Mardonius was the Persian commander who pressed hardest for the conquest of Greece and who died trying to achieve it. A nephew of Darius the Great and married to one of the king's daughters, he belonged to the innermost circle of the Achaemenid court. Under Darius he led an early expedition that restored Persian control over Thrace and Macedon, though his supporting fleet was wrecked in a storm off Mount Athos.

When the young Xerxes came to the throne, Herodotus presents Mardonius as the chief advocate, against more cautious voices, of the great invasion of 480 BCE. He marched with the king and shared in its early successes. But after the naval disaster at Salamis, Xerxes returned to Asia, leaving Mardonius behind with a hand-picked army to finish the conquest the following year.

Through the winter he tried diplomacy as well as force, sending Alexander I, the king of Macedon, to offer the Athenians generous terms if they would change sides; they refused him, and he occupied and burned the evacuated Athens a second time. In 479 BCE the Greek states massed their full strength against him.

At the battle of Plataea, the allied army under the Spartan regent Pausanias broke his forces, and Mardonius himself was cut down in the fighting. His death ended the Persian bid to subjugate Greece and closed the great invasion he had done so much to set in motion.
