Leonardo da Vinci

Florentine painter, engineer, and natural philosopher, 1452–1519

Leonardo was born out of wedlock on 15 April 1452 in or near the village of Vinci, in the lower valley of the Arno, the son of the Florentine notary Ser Piero da Vinci and a peasant woman, Caterina. Raised first in his grandfather's household and then in his father's, he was sent in his mid-teens to Florence and apprenticed in the workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio, where he learned painting, bronze casting, and the mathematics of perspective alongside the young Botticelli and Perugino. He was admitted to the painters' guild in 1472 but remained for years in Verrocchio's bottega, contributing the kneeling angel to the master's Baptism of Christ.

About 1482 he left Florence for the court of Ludovico Sforza in Milan, offering himself in a famous letter as a military and civil engineer, with painting added almost as an afterthought. He stayed seventeen years. There he painted the Virgin of the Rocks and, on the refectory wall of Santa Maria delle Grazie, the Last Supper; modelled the great clay horse for a monument to Francesco Sforza, never cast in bronze; and filled the notebooks with anatomical dissections, designs for flying machines, weapons, locks, and water-works, written in mirror script. Driven out by the French invasion of 1499, he served Cesare Borgia in 1502 as military architect, returned to Florence to paint the Mona Lisa and to begin the lost Battle of Anghiari opposite Michelangelo's Battle of Cascina, and then divided his last decade between Milan, Rome under Leo X, and France.

In 1516 Francis I brought him across the Alps and installed him at the manor of Clos Lucé near Amboise as Premier Painter, Engineer, and Architect to the King. There, his right hand crippled, he ordered the notebooks, dictated treatises, and designed pageants and canal schemes for the young court. He died on 2 May 1519 and was buried in the cloister of Saint-Florentin at Amboise. The Mona Lisa, the Last Supper, and roughly seven thousand pages of notebooks are the surviving heart of his work.
