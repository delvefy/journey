Alexander I of Russia

Emperor of Russia, victor over Napoleon, 1777–1825

Alexander I was the Russian emperor who, after years of defeat, broke Napoleon and rode at the head of Europe's armies into Paris. Born in 1777, he was the favourite grandson of Catherine the Great, who supervised his upbringing herself. He came to the throne in 1801 on the murder of his father, Paul I—a palace coup he had guiltily allowed—and began his reign full of liberal hopes and projects of reform.

Those hopes were soon swallowed by the wars with France. He was beaten beside the Austrians at Austerlitz and again at Friedland, and at Tilsit in 1807 he met Napoleon on a raft in the Niemen and made a startling peace and alliance with him.

The friendship could not last. In 1812 Napoleon invaded with the largest army Europe had seen, and Alexander, refusing to treat even when Moscow lay in ashes, watched the Grande Armée destroy itself in the Russian winter. Then he carried the war westward, and in 1814 entered the French capital as a conqueror.

At the Congress of Vienna he stood among the makers of the new Europe and, in his deepening religious mysticism, founded the Holy Alliance to bind its monarchs in Christian solidarity. His final years grew withdrawn and reactionary. He died suddenly in the south in 1825, so unexpectedly that legend whispered he had staged his death to live on as a hermit. The throne passed to his younger brother, Nicholas I.
