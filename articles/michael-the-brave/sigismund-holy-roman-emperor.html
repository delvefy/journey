Sigismund, Holy Roman Emperor

Holy Roman Emperor, King of Hungary and Bohemia, 1368–1437

Sigismund was born at Nuremberg on 15 February 1368, the second son of Emperor Charles IV by his fourth wife Elizabeth of Pomerania. His father betrothed him at ten to Mary, the heiress of Louis I of Hungary, and sent him to be brought up in the Hungarian court at Buda. When Louis died in 1382 a chaotic regency around the young queen brought civil war; Sigismund won her hand and his crown by Polish and Bohemian arms in March 1387. The first phase of his reign turned on the Ottoman frontier. A great crusading host he led south through Hungary was annihilated by Bayezid I at Nicopolis on the lower Danube on 25 September 1396; he escaped down the river to Constantinople and only re-entered Hungary in 1401 to face arrest by his own barons.

From 1410 he was king of the Romans, elected during a fragmented and partly disputed contest. He used the title to summon a general council to end the Great Schism, which had three popes. The Council of Constance (1414–1418) was his political masterpiece and his most notorious failure: it deposed John XXIII, accepted the resignation of Gregory XII, deposed Benedict XIII, and elected Martin V, restoring a single pope. But it also tried, convicted, and burned the Bohemian reformer Jan Hus on 6 July 1415, despite the imperial safe-conduct Sigismund had granted. Bohemia rose; on his brother Wenceslaus IV's death in 1419 Sigismund inherited a kingdom in armed revolt. Five Hussite crusades between 1420 and 1431 were beaten, mostly by the blind Jan Žižka, and a settlement came only at Basel in 1436 with the Compactata.

He was crowned emperor at Rome in 1433. He treatied with Henry V of England at Canterbury in 1416 and with the Greek emperor John VIII at Buda. He died at Znaim in Moravia on 9 December 1437, the last Luxembourg, leaving Bohemia and Hungary to his Habsburg son-in-law Albert.
