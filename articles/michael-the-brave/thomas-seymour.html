Thomas Seymour

Baron Seymour of Sudeley, Lord High Admiral, c.1508–1549

Thomas was born about 1508 at Wolf Hall in Wiltshire, the younger of the two surviving sons of Sir John Seymour and Margery Wentworth. His career stood entirely on his sister Jane's marriage to Henry VIII in 1536 and the birth of the future Edward VI in 1537. Where his elder brother Edward, the future Duke of Somerset, was cautious and bureaucratic, Thomas was glamorous, charming, and almost compulsively reckless. Henry sent him on embassies to France and the imperial court in the 1530s and 1540s and made use of him as a naval and military commander, and at his death named him to the regency council that was to govern Edward VI.

Once the new reign opened in January 1547 Thomas was created Baron Seymour of Sudeley and Lord High Admiral, but he refused to accept his brother's elevation as Protector and lord governor. Within months he secretly married Henry's widow Catherine Parr and brought into the household at Chelsea and Hanworth the king's half-sister Elizabeth, then about fourteen, and the young Lady Jane Grey. His behaviour toward Elizabeth — early-morning visits to her bedchamber, romping that Kat Ashley felt forced to interrupt — became a scandal. After Catherine died of childbed fever in September 1548, Thomas pressed his suit for Elizabeth and bought up gunpowder, ordnance, and the loyalty of pirates from his vice-admiral Sir William Sharington.

On the night of 16 January 1549 he was discovered in the privy garden of Hampton Court, pistol in hand, near the king's bedchamber, after shooting Edward's spaniel. Arrest followed, then thirty-three articles of treason concerning the Bristol mint, his designs on the king's person, and a marriage scheme for Elizabeth. Parliament passed an act of attainder; his brother the Protector reluctantly signed the warrant; and Thomas was beheaded on Tower Hill on 20 March 1549.

The road continues to Jane Seymour.
