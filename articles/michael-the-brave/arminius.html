Arminius

Chieftain of the Cherusci, liberator of Germania, c. 18 BC–AD 21

Arminius was a nobleman of the Germanic Cherusci who, like many sons of allied chieftains, was taken young to Rome as a hostage. There he received a Roman military education, gained citizenship and equestrian rank, and served as a commander of Germanic auxiliaries. This intimate knowledge of Roman methods made him uniquely dangerous when he returned to his homeland and secretly forged a coalition of tribes against the expanding empire.

In AD 9 he sprang the trap that made his name. Feigning loyalty, he lured the Roman governor Publius Quinctilius Varus, marching with three legions, into the broken country of the Teutoburg Forest and destroyed them utterly over several days of ambush. Varus took his own life, and the eagles of legions XVII, XVIII, and XIX were lost. The disaster shattered Roman ambitions east of the Rhine; the aged Augustus is said to have beaten his head against a door, crying, "Quinctilius Varus, give me back my legions!"

Rome struck back through the campaigns of Germanicus, who ravaged the German lands and carried off Arminius's pregnant wife Thusnelda, surrendered by her own father Segestes, a rival who opposed Arminius. Yet no lasting reconquest followed. Arminius then warred with Maroboduus, king of the Marcomanni, to win supremacy among the tribes, but his reach for kingly power turned his kinsmen against him, and around AD 21 he was murdered by his own relations. Tacitus called him without doubt the liberator of Germany, and in later ages he was revered as a German national hero.
