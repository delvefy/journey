Aurelian

Roman emperor, 214–275 AD

Aurelian was born around 214 in the Danube provinces, of humble stock, and rose through the army as a hard-driving cavalry officer in the desperate years when the Roman Empire seemed to be disintegrating under barbarian invasion, plague, and breakaway states. On the death of Claudius Gothicus in 270 he was proclaimed emperor by the legions.

He inherited an empire split into three, and in a few astonishing years he put it back together. He first beat back the Germanic invaders—Juthungi, Vandals, and Alemanni—who had broken into Italy itself, and to guard the capital he began the great circuit of walls around Rome that still bears his name. Judging the province of Dacia indefensible, he withdrew its garrisons and settlers behind the Danube.

He then turned east against the Palmyrene Empire of Queen Zenobia, which had seized Syria, Egypt, and much of Asia Minor. In a swift campaign in 272 he defeated her armies, captured Palmyra, and took the queen prisoner; when the city later revolted, he razed it. Wheeling west, he reconquered the breakaway Gallic Empire by 274 and celebrated a magnificent triumph in Rome, where a grateful Senate hailed him Restitutor Orbis, “Restorer of the World.”

He reformed the debased coinage and promoted the worship of Sol Invictus, the Unconquered Sun, as a unifying state cult. Stern and uncompromising, he made many enemies, and in 275, while preparing a Persian campaign, he was murdered in Thrace by officers tricked into believing themselves marked for death. Though his reign was brief, he had pulled the empire back from collapse.

The road continues to Zenobia.
