Khadija

First wife of the Prophet and first Muslim, c.555–619

Khadija bint Khuwaylid was born about 555 into the Quraysh of Mecca and became, through inheritance and her own ability, one of the wealthiest merchants of the city, dispatching caravans to Syria and beyond. Twice widowed, she hired a young kinsman named Muhammad to take charge of her trade, and his honesty so impressed her that she proposed marriage. Though she was older than he, the union was long and devoted, and while she lived he took no other wife. Their daughter Fatima would carry the Prophet’s line into the future, and the boy Ali grew up in their household.

Her place in history rests on a single act of faith. When Muhammad returned trembling from the cave of Hira after his first encounter with the angel, fearing for his sanity, it was Khadija who wrapped him in comfort and believed him without hesitation — the first of all Muslims. She brought him to her cousin Waraqa ibn Nawfal, a man learned in the scriptures, who declared that Muhammad had received the same revelation as Moses before him.

Through the bitter early years, as the Quraysh mocked and boycotted the small band of believers, Khadija stood by her husband as his refuge and his strength, spending her fortune in the cause. She died about 619, in the season the tradition calls the Year of Sorrow, before the faith she had first embraced rose to power. Islam remembers her among the greatest of all women, the Mother of the Believers who believed before any other.
