Catherine Parr

Queen of England and Ireland, 1512–1548

Catherine was born in 1512 to Sir Thomas Parr of Kendal, a courtier in the household of Henry VIII, and Maud Green, who supervised her unusually thorough humanist education after her father's death in 1517. She married twice while young — first to Edward Burgh, who died about 1533, and then to John Neville, Lord Latimer, a northern magnate caught up in the Pilgrimage of Grace in 1536. Through these years she ran great country households in Yorkshire and Northamptonshire and met the leading reform-minded clerics of the north.

After Latimer's death in 1543 Henry VIII chose her as his sixth wife; they were married at Hampton Court on 12 July 1543. She drew the king's three estranged children, Mary, Elizabeth, and Edward, around her and pressed for an act of Parliament that restored Mary and Elizabeth to the succession in 1544. In the same year Henry left her regent-general while he campaigned at Boulogne, the last time an English queen consort wielded that authority. She corresponded in Latin with Mary and supervised Elizabeth's studies under John Cheke and Roger Ascham. In 1545 she published Prayers or Meditations, the first book printed in England by a queen under her own name; her Lamentation of a Sinner of 1547 was a forthright Protestant confession.

Her conservative enemies on the council nearly secured a warrant for her arrest in 1546 over her advanced religious views, but she talked her way back into the king's favour. After Henry's death in January 1547 she quickly married Thomas Seymour, brother of the Protector, and took the young Lady Jane Grey and Princess Elizabeth into her Chelsea household, where Seymour's behaviour toward Elizabeth caused a scandal. Catherine died at Sudeley Castle on 5 September 1548 of puerperal fever, days after the birth of her only child, Mary Seymour.
