Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor

Habsburg ruler of an empire on which the sun never set, 1500–1558

Charles was born at Ghent on 24 February 1500, the eldest son of Philip the Handsome and Joanna of Castile, and the grandson on his father’s side of the Emperor Maximilian I and Mary of Burgundy, on his mother’s of the Catholic Monarchs of Spain. By the accidents of inheritance he assembled in his single person territories that no later Habsburg would ever again hold together: the Burgundian Netherlands at six, Castile and Aragon with their Italian and American dependencies at sixteen, the Austrian lands and the imperial crown at nineteen. Elected emperor in June 1519 against the rival candidacy of Francis I, he ruled in person from Brussels, Madrid, Augsburg and the road.

His reign turned on three irreducible struggles. The first was with the Reformation. At the Diet of Worms in April 1521 he confronted Martin Luther, refused to lift the imperial ban, and spent the next thirty years trying to keep Germany Catholic. He won the brief military victory of Mühlberg in 1547 but lost the political war, accepting in the Peace of Augsburg of 1555 the principle of cuius regio, eius religio. The second was the long duel with France. In 1525 his army captured Francis I at Pavia; in 1527 mutinous imperial troops sacked Rome; the Habsburg-Valois conflict continued, with intermissions, until almost the end of his life. The third was the Ottoman advance under Suleiman the Magnificent: Vienna besieged in 1529, Tunis taken by Charles himself in 1535, Algiers lost disastrously in 1541.

Hernán Cortés and Francisco Pizarro added Mexico and Peru to his treasury, but the silver vanished into the wars almost as fast as it crossed the Atlantic. Exhausted and gout-ridden, Charles abdicated by stages in 1555 and 1556, splitting his inheritance: Spain, the Netherlands, Italy and the Indies to his son Philip II, the Austrian lands and the imperial title to his brother Ferdinand. He withdrew to a small house attached to the monastery of Yuste in Estremadura, where he kept clocks and ate too much, and died there on 21 September 1558.
