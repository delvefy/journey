William Gladstone

1809–1898, four-time Prime Minister of the United Kingdom

William Ewart Gladstone was born in Liverpool, the fourth son of John Gladstone, a Scottish-born corn and sugar merchant whose West Indian plantations rested on slave labour. He was educated at Eton and at Christ Church, Oxford, where he took a rare double first in classics and mathematics, was president of the Union, and seemed to many a future archbishop. Instead he entered Parliament as a high-church Tory in 1832 and devoted the remaining sixty-three years of his life to politics.

Sir Robert Peel made him a junior minister, and in 1846 Gladstone followed Peel out of the Conservative party over the repeal of the Corn Laws. Through the 1850s as Chancellor of the Exchequer under Lord Aberdeen and then Lord Palmerston he turned free trade into orthodoxy, abolished the duties on paper and on hundreds of articles, and produced budget speeches that ran for four hours and were read in pamphlet form afterwards. In 1859 he joined the new Liberal party and from 1868 led it for the rest of his career.

His four ministries — 1868–74, 1880–85, 1886, and 1892–94 — disestablished the Irish Church, opened the universities to non-Anglicans, reformed the army and the civil service, introduced the secret ballot, and twice attempted Home Rule for Ireland. The second attempt, with Charles Stewart Parnell as his Irish partner, split his party and ushered in long Conservative rule under Lord Salisbury. He warred publicly with Benjamin Disraeli for thirty years, the “people's William” against the imperial showman; Queen Victoria thought him an oratorical bore and addressed him, she said, “as if I were a public meeting.”

He stumped the country in the Midlothian Campaigns of 1879–80, the first modern stump tour. In his last years he campaigned against Ottoman atrocities in Bulgaria and Armenia, wrote on Homer and on biblical doctrine, and translated Latin and Greek for relaxation. He resigned at eighty-four over a naval estimate he refused to sign, and died at Hawarden in May 1898.
