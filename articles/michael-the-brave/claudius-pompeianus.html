Claudius Pompeianus

Roman general, c.125–193

Tiberius Claudius Pompeianus was born around 125 at Antioch in Syria, of modest provincial stock, and rose by sheer ability to become the most trusted general of the emperor Marcus Aurelius. Through the long and grinding Marcomannic Wars on the Danube he served as the emperor’s right hand, and in 169 Marcus rewarded and bound him to the dynasty by marrying him to his widowed daughter Lucilla — a union the proud princess and her mother are said to have thought beneath them.

Pompeianus became a model of the loyal servant who refused the supreme prize. Under Commodus, Marcus’s unworthy son, he sat for a time on the imperial council, but as the reign curdled into tyranny he withdrew into retirement, pleading age and failing sight, and so survived — even after his own wife Lucilla was put to death for conspiring against her brother.

When Commodus was at last assassinated and the upright Pertinax raised to the throne in 193, the new emperor summoned the venerable Pompeianus back to court, honoured him, and is said to have offered to share his power. Pompeianus declined, as he declined again amid the turmoil that followed Pertinax’s swift murder, content to end his days a private man. He died around that year, a bridge between the golden age of the Antonines and the violent scramble for empire that came after.
