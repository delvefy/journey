Anne of Austria

Queen and regent of France, 1601–1666

Eldest daughter of Philip III of Spain and a Habsburg by blood despite her title, Anne was married at fourteen to the young Louis XIII of France. The marriage was long unhappy and barren, deepened by the suspicion of Cardinal Richelieu, who watched the Spanish queen for any sign of intrigue with France’s enemies. Only after more than two decades did she bear a son, the future Louis XIV, hailed as a gift of God, followed by a second, Philippe.

When Louis XIII died in 1643 the four-year-old king inherited the throne, and Anne secured for herself the full powers of regent. To the surprise of those who expected her to favour Spain, she entrusted the government to Richelieu’s heir, the Italian Cardinal Mazarin, whom she supported with a loyalty so complete that contemporaries whispered of a secret marriage. Together they steered France through the dangerous noble and parlementary revolts of the Fronde, twice fleeing Paris with the boy-king before crushing the rebels.

A proud and pious woman, devoted to her sons and to the Church, she preserved the authority of the crown intact and handed it to Louis XIV strengthened by Mazarin’s diplomacy. She withdrew from politics after the cardinal’s death in 1661 and ended her days at the convent of Val-de-Grâce, whose great church she had built, dying of cancer in 1666.
