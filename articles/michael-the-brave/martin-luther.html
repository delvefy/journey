Martin Luther

German reformer, 1483–1546

Luther was born at Eisleben in Saxony on 10 November 1483, the son of a copper miner. His father pushed him through the universities of Magdeburg, Eisenach and Erfurt towards a career in law. A famous summer thunderstorm in 1505, in which the young Luther was thrown from his horse near Stotternheim, settled his vocation instead: he entered the Augustinian friars at Erfurt, was ordained two years later, and in 1511 was sent on as professor of biblical theology to the new university of Wittenberg, where he would remain almost the rest of his life.

The dispute that defined him began as a quarrel over the papal indulgence preached by Johann Tetzel in Saxony to finance the rebuilding of St Peter’s in Rome. On 31 October 1517 Luther sent his ninety-five theses against indulgences to the Archbishop of Mainz, and probably also posted them on the door of the Wittenberg castle church. The intended academic disputation became, with the help of the new printing press, a German-wide controversy. Excommunicated by Pope Leo X in 1521, Luther was summoned by Charles V to the Diet of Worms; refusing to recant, he was placed under imperial ban. The Elector Frederick the Wise hid him at the Wartburg, where in eleven weeks he translated the New Testament into German.

From his return in 1522 he organised, with Philip Melanchthon at his side, the reformed church of Saxony: a vernacular liturgy, a vernacular Bible (the complete Old Testament followed in 1534), congregational hymns of his own composition (“A Mighty Fortress”), and the renunciation of clerical celibacy that he himself acted on in 1525 by marrying the runaway nun Katharina von Bora. He broke with Erasmus over free will, with the Swiss reformer Zwingli over the Eucharist, with Thomas Müntzer and the peasants of 1525 over revolution, and at the end of his life with the Jews of Europe in a violent late pamphlet. He died at Eisleben on 18 February 1546, in the town where he was born.
