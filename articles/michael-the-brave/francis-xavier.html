Francis Xavier

Jesuit missionary, “Apostle of the Indies”, 1506–1552

Francis Xavier was born in 1506 in the castle of Xavier in Navarre, of a noble family caught up in the wars that cost their kingdom its independence. As a student at the University of Paris he shared lodgings with Ignatius of Loyola, who slowly won him over from worldly ambition, and he became one of the small band that vowed itself to God at Montmartre in 1534. When King John III of Portugal asked Rome for missionaries to his sprawling eastern empire, Xavier was chosen, armed with the authority of a papal nuncio, and in 1541 he sailed from Lisbon on a voyage of more than a year.

Reaching Goa in 1542, he threw himself into a mission of extraordinary range. He preached and baptized among the pearl-fishing villages of southern India, learning to teach the faith through simple translated prayers, then pushed on to Malacca and the Spice Islands of the Moluccas. Restless and tireless, he was always drawn to the next horizon.

In 1549 he opened the first Christian mission to Japan, where he spent more than two years and concluded that the road to converting the East ran through China, the country the Japanese themselves revered. He set out to enter the closed Chinese empire, but in December 1552 he died of fever on the island of Shangchuan, in sight of the mainland he was never allowed to reach. His body was carried back to Goa, where it is still venerated, and in 1622 he was canonized alongside Ignatius, remembered as one of the greatest missionaries in the history of the Church.

The road continues to John III of Portugal.
