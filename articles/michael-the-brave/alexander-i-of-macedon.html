Alexander I of Macedon

King of Macedon, r. c. 498–454 BCE

Alexander I, king of Macedon from about 498 to 454 BCE, earned the surname Philhellene for his lifelong effort to bind his half-Greek kingdom to the wider Hellenic world. He ruled at a moment when Macedon lay in the shadow of the Persian Empire, and for years he was obliged to serve as a Persian vassal.

During Xerxes's invasion of Greece he marched, of necessity, with the Persian host, and the general Mardonius employed him as an envoy to Athens, trusting his standing among the Greeks to carry an offer of alliance; the Athenians refused it. Yet Herodotus also records that Alexander secretly favored the Greek cause, slipping across the lines on the eve of Plataea to warn the allied commanders of Mardonius's plan of attack.

To assert his Greek identity he pointed to the legendary Argead descent of his house and, by tradition, won admission to compete at the Olympic Games, which were open only to Greeks. After the Persian withdrawal he enlarged his kingdom, exploited its rich silver mines, and strengthened Macedon's standing among its neighbors.

He was the ancestor of the line that would in time produce Philip II and Alexander the Great, and his reign marks the beginning of Macedon's long emergence from the margins toward the center of Greek affairs. He was succeeded by his son Perdiccas II.
