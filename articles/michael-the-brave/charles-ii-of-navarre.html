Charles II of Navarre

King of Navarre, “the Bad”, 1332–1387

Charles, born in 1332, became king of the small Pyrenean realm of Navarre in 1349, but his ambitions lay far to the north, for he was also count of Évreux, held rich estates in Normandy, and through his mother carried a claim to the crown of France itself. Clever, charming and utterly faithless, he spent his life intriguing between the Valois kings and the English, earning the lasting name “the Bad.”

His feud with the French crown opened in 1354, when he had the king’s favourite and constable, Charles of Spain, murdered. King John II struck back, seizing him at a banquet in Rouen in 1356 and throwing him into prison; but the catastrophe of Poitiers, where John himself was captured, set Charles free into a France in chaos. He seized his chance, allying with Étienne Marcel and the rebels of Paris against the young Dauphin, the future Charles V, and for a moment seemed poised to master the kingdom.

The moment passed. In 1364 the royal captain Bertrand du Guesclin shattered his army at Cocherel, and the new King Charles V steadily stripped him of his Norman lands and his influence. Reduced once more to his mountain kingdom, the “Bad” king went on plotting with England and in Castile for another twenty years to no great effect. He died in 1387, and the chroniclers told with grim satisfaction how, wrapped for his illness in cloths soaked in spirits, he was accidentally burned alive.

The road continues to Étienne Marcel.
