Thibron

Mercenary captain, d. 322 BCE

Thibron was a soldier of fortune who, for a brief and violent career at the end of the fourth century BCE, made himself a power in North Africa. He first appears as an officer in the service of Harpalus, the runaway treasurer of Alexander the Great; when Harpalus took refuge on Crete in 323 BCE, Thibron murdered him and seized his hoard of money and his army of some six thousand mercenaries.

With this force Thibron sailed to Cyrenaica and threw himself into the quarrels of the wealthy Greek city of Cyrene, hoping to carve out a dominion of his own. He won early successes and took the city's harbor, but the Cyreneans and their allies resisted stubbornly, and his army was worn down by hard campaigning and by treachery within his own ranks.

The struggle at last drew in Ptolemy, the new master of Egypt, who dispatched his general Ophellas to settle the region. Ophellas crushed Thibron's forces; Thibron himself was captured and put to death in 322 BCE, and Cyrene passed under Ptolemaic control.
