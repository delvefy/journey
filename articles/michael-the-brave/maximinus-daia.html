Maximinus Daia

Roman emperor, c. 270–313 AD

Maximinus Daia was a nephew of Galerius, raised like his uncle from peasant origins in the Balkans to high military command. In 305, when Diocletian and Maximian abdicated, Galerius made him Caesar of the East, giving him charge of Syria and Egypt. Hungry for the higher rank, he had himself proclaimed Augustus in 310.

He was the most determined persecutor of Christians of his generation. Even after Galerius's deathbed edict of toleration in 311, Maximinus renewed the attack within his domains, putting to death bishops such as Peter of Alexandria, sponsoring a revived and tightly organised pagan priesthood to rival the Church, and circulating forged anti-Christian propaganda such as the so-called Acts of Pilate.

When Galerius died, Maximinus and Licinius divided the East between them along the Bosporus. He allied himself with Maxentius against the opposing bloc of Constantine and Licinius. But after Constantine destroyed Maxentius at the Milvian Bridge in 312, the web of alliances collapsed.

In 313 Maximinus struck westward against Licinius but was decisively beaten at the Battle of Tzirallum near Adrianople. He fled across Asia Minor to Tarsus, where he died the same year amid reports of poison and disease. His death left Licinius sole master of the East, and his persecutions, recorded in detail by the church historian Eusebius, became a byword for the last great pagan reaction against the rising faith.
