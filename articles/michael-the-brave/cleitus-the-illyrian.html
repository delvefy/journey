Cleitus the Illyrian

Dardanian king, fl. 4th century BCE

Cleitus the Illyrian was a Dardanian king of the fourth century BCE, a son of the great Illyrian ruler Bardylis, who inherited his father's ambition to resist the expanding power of Macedon. He is remembered chiefly for the war he waged at the very start of Alexander the Great's reign.

In 335 BCE, while the young Alexander was still securing his northern frontiers before turning against Persia, Cleitus rose in revolt, holding the frontier stronghold of Pelium. He was joined by Glaucias, king of the neighboring Taulantii, and together they hoped to trap the Macedonian army in the difficult mountain country.

But Alexander, in a campaign that the historian Arrian later praised as a model of generalship, extricated his forces by a series of swift and disciplined maneuvers, then fell upon the allied Illyrians by surprise and scattered them. Cleitus burned Pelium and fled into the territory of Glaucias.

With his defeat the Illyrian threat to Macedon was broken for a generation, and Alexander was left free to march east against the Persian Empire.
