Margaret Tudor

Queen of Scots, 1489–1541

Margaret was born in 1489, the eldest daughter of Henry VII and Elizabeth of York and the elder sister of the future Henry VIII. In 1503, at the age of thirteen, she was married to James IV of Scotland in a union hailed by poets as the joining of the Thistle and the Rose, the centrepiece of a treaty of perpetual peace meant to end centuries of war between the two kingdoms.

That peace did not outlive her husband, who fell at Flodden in 1513, leaving Margaret regent for their infant son, James V. Her authority quickly crumbled after she made a hasty and unpopular second marriage to the Earl of Angus, which alienated the Scottish lords and drew her into years of bitter faction, flight, and intrigue played out among the courts of Scotland, England, and France. She sought to steer Scottish policy, by turns, toward and away from her formidable brother in London.

Her later life was tangled in further marriage and scandal, including her efforts to obtain a divorce, and she never fully regained her early power. Her lasting importance lay in her blood: it was through Margaret Tudor that the Stuart line gained its claim to the English throne, so that in 1603 her great-grandson James VI of Scotland succeeded as James I of England, uniting the two crowns at last. She died in 1541.
