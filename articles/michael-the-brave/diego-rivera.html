Diego Rivera

Mexican muralist, 1886–1957

Diego María de la Concepción Juan Nepomuceno Estánislao de la Rivera y Barrientos Acosta y Rodríguez was born in 1886 at Guanajuato, the son of a liberal schoolteacher. He drew from infancy, was sent at ten to the Academy of San Carlos in Mexico City, and in 1907 took a state scholarship to Spain and then to Paris. He spent the next decade in the city as a working Cubist alongside Picasso, Modigliani, Juan Gris, and Robert Delaunay, and absorbed first Cubism and then, through long looking at Cézanne, the architectural geometries of late Cézanne's apples and Mont Sainte-Victoires.

A 1920 journey through Italy to study the early Renaissance fresco cycles of Giotto, Masaccio, and Piero della Francesca changed his ambition. He returned to a Mexico in the aftermath of revolution and joined the new education minister José Vasconcelos's programme of public murals on the walls of the country's government buildings, with José Clemente Orozco and David Alfaro Siqueiros, founding the Mexican muralist movement. His vast cycles at the Ministry of Public Education in Mexico City, the National Palace, the Palace of Cortés at Cuernavaca, and the Detroit Institute of Arts narrated the history of Mexico from the Mayan codices through the Conquest to the modern industrial worker.

He joined the Mexican Communist Party in 1922 and remained, broadly, a Marxist for the rest of his life, though his expulsions and re-entries to the Party kept time with international Marxism's own quarrels. From 1937 to 1939 he and Frida Kahlo hosted the exiled Leon Trotsky at the Casa Azul in Coyoacán, until a quarrel sent Trotsky to a separate Mexico City house where Stalin's assassin found him.

He married Kahlo twice, took many other lovers, and outlived her by three years; he died in Mexico City in 1957. The American patron who in 1933 commissioned him to paint Man at the Crossroads on the lobby wall of the new RCA Building at Rockefeller Center, and who chiselled it from the wall once Rivera refused to remove a portrait of Lenin from it, was Nelson Rockefeller.
