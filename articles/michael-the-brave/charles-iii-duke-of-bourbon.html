Charles III, Duke of Bourbon

Constable of France, 1490–1527

Charles of Bourbon was born in 1490 into a branch of the French royal house, and his marriage to his cousin Suzanne gathered into his hands the immense Bourbon estates, making him the greatest nobleman in France. A soldier of high ability, he was named Constable of France and won renown fighting for the young king Francis I, notably at the great victory of Marignano in 1515.

Royal favour soon curdled into enmity. When his wife died in 1521, the king’s mother, Louise of Savoy, advanced rival claims to the Bourbon inheritance, and a lawsuit threatened to strip Charles of nearly everything he possessed. Embittered by what he saw as the crown’s ingratitude and greed, the Constable did the unthinkable for a prince of the blood: in secret he went over to Francis I’s great rival, the Emperor Charles V.

Fighting now against his own country in the Italian Wars, he was among the commanders of the imperial army that shattered the French and captured Francis I at the Battle of Pavia in 1525. Two years later he led a vast, unpaid, and mutinous host of Spaniards and German mercenaries down upon Rome. He was killed at the walls in the first assault, and his leaderless troops poured into the city to commit the terrible Sack of Rome of 1527, an atrocity that made his name a byword for treason.

The road continues to Louise of Savoy.
