Bardylis

King of the Illyrians, c. 448–358 BCE

Bardylis was the Illyrian king who, for a generation, made the tribes of the western Balkans a deadly threat to Macedon. By tradition he rose from humble beginnings—later writers called him a charcoal-burner—to weld the Dardanians and neighboring Illyrian peoples into a single powerful kingdom, through a reputation for the fair division of plunder and for stern leadership.

From this base he raided and dominated the Macedonian frontier for decades. His greatest success came in 359 BCE, when his army destroyed the forces of the Macedonian king Perdiccas III, killing the king and some four thousand of his men and leaving Macedon on the brink of collapse.

But his triumph summoned a greater adversary. The new Macedonian ruler, Philip II, rebuilt the shattered army and in 358 BCE met Bardylis in a hard-fought pitched battle. The Illyrians were routed with heavy loss, and Philip drove them back from the lands they had seized, securing his western border and beginning the long rise of Macedon.

Bardylis, by then a very old man, did not long outlive his defeat, and the Illyrian power he had built soon passed into decline.
