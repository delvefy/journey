Henry V of England

King of England, victor at Agincourt, 1386–1422

Henry of Monmouth was born on 16 September 1386 in the gatehouse of Monmouth Castle, the eldest son of Henry Bolingbroke and Mary de Bohun. The deposition of Richard II in 1399 and his father's accession as Henry IV made him at thirteen Prince of Wales, Duke of Cornwall, Earl of Chester, and lieutenant of Wales against the Welsh rising under Owain Glyndŵr. He commanded at Shrewsbury in 1403, where he was wounded in the face by an arrow, and spent the next decade fighting an attritional war in the Welsh marches that taught him siegecraft and military logistics. After 1408 he took an increasing role in the council and quarrelled with his ailing father.

He succeeded as king on 21 March 1413. His first year saw the suppression of the Lollard rising under his old companion John Oldcastle and the conciliation of former opponents, including the reburial of Richard II at Westminster. In August 1415 he reopened the war with France, took Harfleur after a costly siege, and on 25 October 1415 destroyed a much larger French army at Agincourt — the rolling longbow volleys on the muddy field killed thousands of the French nobility and made Henry's reputation across Europe. He returned in 1417 with a methodical plan to conquer Normandy: Caen, then Falaise, then a long siege of Rouen which surrendered in January 1419. The Burgundian murder at Montereau later that year drove Duke Philip into his alliance.

The Treaty of Troyes of May 1420 named Henry regent and heir of the mad Charles VI, married him to Catherine of Valois, and disinherited the Dauphin. A son, the future Henry VI, was born to them in December 1421. Henry died of dysentery at the Château de Vincennes on 31 August 1422, aged thirty-five, leaving an unfinished conquest, a nine-month-old heir, and a posthumous reputation that the Tudors and Shakespeare would burnish into a national legend.
