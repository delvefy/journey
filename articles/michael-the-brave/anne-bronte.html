Anne Brontë

1820–1849, English novelist and poet

Anne Brontë was born at Thornton in Yorkshire on 17 January 1820, the youngest of the six children of the Reverend Patrick Brontë and his wife Maria Branwell. Within a year the family had moved to the parsonage at Haworth on the moors, where Maria and the two eldest sisters soon died. Anne grew up the smallest of the surviving children, attached to her aunt Elizabeth Branwell and to Emily, with whom she invented the imaginary kingdom of Gondal that supplied the matter of her early poems.

Her schooling was largely at home with her sisters; only a brief period at Roe Head, in 1835, was spent away. From 1839 she earned her own living, first as governess to the Ingham children at Blake Hall and then, from 1840 to 1845, in the Robinson household at Thorp Green near York. The post placed her near to her brother Branwell, employed in the same family as tutor, and ended in scandal when his affair with Mrs Robinson was discovered. Anne returned to Haworth with materials enough for two novels.

In 1846 she contributed twenty-one poems to the volume Poems by Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell, published with her sisters under male pseudonyms. The novel Agnes Grey, drawn closely from her years as a governess, appeared in 1847 in the same volume as Emily's Wuthering Heights. Her second and more ambitious novel, The Tenant of Wildfell Hall (1848), broke new ground in its unsparing portrait of marital abuse and alcoholism, much of it drawn from Branwell's deterioration. Charlotte, after Anne's death, suppressed its republication, judging the subject matter unsuited to her sister's gentle temper, and the book waited decades to be re-evaluated.

Anne survived her brother by ten weeks and her sister Emily by less than six months. Suffering from tuberculosis, she insisted on a final journey to Scarborough in May 1849 in the hope of a recovery, and died there on 28 May, aged twenty-nine. She is the only Brontë buried away from Haworth; her grave overlooks the sea. Elizabeth Gaskell, in the 1857 Life of Charlotte Brontë, gave her, almost in passing, the place she has held since.
