Lorenzo Ghiberti

Florentine sculptor and goldsmith, 1378–1455

Lorenzo Ghiberti was born in Florence in 1378 and trained as a goldsmith, the discipline of fine metalwork that underlay so much early Renaissance sculpture. His career was made in a single contest. In 1401 the city held a competition to design new bronze doors for the Baptistery, and Ghiberti and Filippo Brunelleschi each cast a trial relief of the Sacrifice of Isaac. The judges favoured Ghiberti; Brunelleschi, by tradition, turned away from sculpture toward architecture, and the two surviving panels became a famous emblem of the moment the Renaissance found its footing.

The commission consumed much of his life. The first set of doors, covered with Gospel scenes, occupied him for some twenty years. He then began a second pair, ten gilded panels of Old Testament stories rendered with a new mastery of relief and perspective, which Michelangelo is said to have judged fit to be the Gates of Paradise — the name they have carried ever since. Between them he also cast over-lifesize bronze saints for the guild niches of Orsanmichele.

His workshop was the training ground of a whole generation: the young Donatello and Paolo Uccello passed through it, and assistants such as Benozzo Gozzoli ground their skills on his doors. Late in life he composed the Commentarii, part history of art, part technical manual, and one of the earliest autobiographies left by any artist. He died in Florence in 1455, his great bronze doors standing as the threshold of the city’s sacred heart.
