Vitellius

Roman emperor, 15–69 CE

Aulus Vitellius was the third and most short-lived ruler of the year of the four emperors, remembered chiefly for the gluttony and indolence that the ancient sources heap upon him. Born in 15 CE into a family that had climbed to the heights under Claudius—his father Lucius held the consulship three times and the censorship—he made his own way as a courtier, winning the favour of one emperor after another by sharing their pleasures: the chariot-track with Caligula, the dice-table with Claudius, the music and flattery dear to Nero.

Late in 68 Galba, judging him too lazy to be dangerous, gave him command of the army of Lower Germany. It was a fatal misjudgement. In January 69 the discontented Rhine legions hailed Vitellius emperor, and his generals led them into Italy, crushing Otho's forces at the first battle of Bedriacum and leaving Vitellius master of Rome.

His rule dissolved into feasting and waste. He neither disciplined his troops nor steadied the treasury, and within months the legions of the East and the Danube proclaimed their own candidate, Vespasian. Vespasian's energetic lieutenant Antonius Primus invaded Italy, broke the Vitellians at the second battle of Bedriacum, and burned and sacked Cremona before advancing on the capital.

Vitellius tried to bargain away the throne, but his soldiers would not let him abdicate. In the fighting the Capitol itself went up in flames. When the Flavian army stormed Rome in December 69, Vitellius was hauled from hiding, paraded through the streets, and butchered—the last obstacle before Vespasian founded a new dynasty.

The road continues to Antonius Primus.
