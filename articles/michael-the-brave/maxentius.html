Maxentius

Roman emperor, c. 278–312 AD

Maxentius was the son of the emperor Maximian and the son-in-law of Galerius, yet when the Tetrarchy was reshuffled in 305 he was pointedly passed over. In 306, exploiting discontent in Rome and the loyalty of the Praetorian Guard, he had himself proclaimed emperor in the old capital, seizing control of Italy and Africa.

His position was precarious and bitterly contested. He beat back two invasions sent to unseat him—first by the emperor Severus, who was captured and killed, then by Galerius himself, who was forced to withdraw. For a time his father Maximian shared power with him before the two fell out, and he crushed a revolt in Africa that had threatened Rome's vital grain supply.

Maxentius was a great builder who cast himself as the restorer of Rome's ancient glory. He raised the colossal basilica on the Forum that still bears his name, later completed by Constantine, and built a circus and a dynastic mausoleum on the Via Appia, together with a temple to his deified young son Romulus.

His rule ended when Constantine invaded Italy in 312. The armies met north of the city at the Milvian Bridge on 28 October. Constantine broke the enemy line, and as Maxentius tried to flee across a pontoon bridge it gave way and he drowned in the Tiber. His head was paraded through Rome, his victor hailed the triumph as the work of the Christian God, and Maxentius was branded ever after a tyrant.

The road continues to Galerius.
