Louis II of Hungary

King of Hungary and Bohemia, 1506–1526

Louis was born in 1506, the son of Vladislaus II of the Jagiellonian dynasty, and inherited the crowns of Hungary and Bohemia as a young boy when his father died in 1516. His long minority left real power in the hands of quarrelling magnates, and the kingdom he would rule was militarily feeble, financially exhausted, and dangerously exposed on the Ottoman frontier.

His marriage tied him closely to the rising House of Habsburg: Louis wed Mary, the sister of Charles V and Ferdinand I, while his own sister Anna married Ferdinand. This double alliance, harmless enough in itself, would prove momentous, for it gave the Habsburgs their claim to the central European crowns should Louis die without an heir.

That moment came with terrible speed. When Suleiman the Magnificent led a great army up the Danube, the young king gathered what forces he could and met the Ottomans on the field of Mohács in August 1526. In less than two hours the Hungarian army was destroyed, and Louis, only twenty years old, drowned in a swollen stream as he fled the rout. His death extinguished the Jagiellonian line in Hungary, tore the kingdom three ways between Habsburg, Ottoman, and native claimants, and ranks among the greatest catastrophes in Hungarian history.

The road continues to Mary of Hungary.
