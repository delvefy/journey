Prince Eugene of Savoy

Habsburg field marshal and statesman, 1663–1736

Prince Eugene of Savoy was the supreme general of the Habsburg monarchy and one of the finest commanders in European history. Born in Paris in 1663 to a cadet branch of the House of Savoy, with the blood of Cardinal Mazarin in him through his mother, he was small, plain, and meant for the Church. When Louis XIV refused him a commission, the slighted youth left France in 1683 and offered his sword to the Emperor, reaching Vienna in time to fight in its relief.

He never forgave France, and he never lost his gift for war. Against the Ottomans he won the annihilating victory of Zenta in 1697 and, twenty years later, stormed Belgrade. In the War of the Spanish Succession he joined the Duke of Marlborough in one of the great partnerships of arms, sharing the triumphs of Blenheim, Oudenarde, and Malplaquet, while on his own he relieved Turin and swept the French from Italy.

Soldier and statesman both, he served three emperors in turn as field marshal and president of the war council, the steadiest pillar of the dynasty for half a century. Yet he was also a man of deep cultivation, who raised the splendid Belvedere palaces in Vienna, gathered a renowned library and gallery, and corresponded with the philosophers of his day.

He died in Vienna in 1736, honoured across the continent as the “noble knight” of song. The last of the emperors he had served so long, and the father of Maria Theresa, was Charles VI.
