John Hawkwood

English condottiero in Italy, c. 1323–1394

John Hawkwood was born around 1323 in Essex, of modest family, and learned soldiering in the French campaigns of Edward III during the Hundred Years’ War, in whose service tradition says he was knighted. When the peace of 1360 threw thousands of veterans out of work, these masterless soldiers banded into free companies and went looking for paymasters. Hawkwood led one such band, the White Company, across the Alps into Italy, and there he stayed for the rest of his life.

For three decades he was the most renowned mercenary captain in the peninsula, known to Italians as Giovanni Acuto. He sold his services in turn to the papacy, to Milan, to Pisa and to Florence, changing employers as contracts and pay dictated, and his name was attached both to feats of generalship and to the brutal sack of Cesena in 1377. That same year he married Donnina, an illegitimate daughter of Bernabò Visconti, tying the English adventurer into the ruling house of Milan.

In his later years he settled into steady service as captain-general of Florence, defending the republic against the swelling power of Gian Galeazzo Visconti. He died in Florence in 1394, honoured by the city he had served. The Florentines long remembered him: in 1436 they had Paolo Uccello paint a great trompe-l’oeil equestrian monument to him on the wall of their cathedral, an image of a mercenary raised to the dignity of a bronze hero. By tradition his bones were returned to his native England at the request of King Richard II.
