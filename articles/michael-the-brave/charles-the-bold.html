Charles the Bold

Duke of Burgundy, 1433–1477

Charles the Bold succeeded his father, Philip the Good, in 1467 as ruler of the Burgundian state, a glittering and immensely wealthy patchwork stretching from the duchy of Burgundy through Flanders and the Low Countries. The richest court in Europe, it lacked only one thing its proud and headstrong duke craved: a royal crown to bind its scattered territories into a kingdom in its own right.

To that end Charles waged almost ceaseless war and high-stakes diplomacy, dreaming of reviving a middle kingdom between France and the Empire. His relentless ambition brought him into a long duel with the cunning French king Louis XI, who patiently financed his enemies, and into negotiations with the Emperor Frederick III for the royal title that always slipped from his grasp. He strengthened his Yorkist ties by marrying Margaret of York, sister of Edward IV.

In the end he overreached fatally. After a costly siege of Neuss he turned on the Swiss confederates, who shattered his magnificent army at Grandson and Morat in 1476. The following winter, besieging Nancy, he was killed in battle, his body found frozen days later. His death extinguished Burgundian independence; through the marriage of his daughter and heiress, Mary of Burgundy, the great inheritance passed to the Habsburgs and reshaped the destiny of Europe.
