Lucius Verus

Roman co-emperor, r. 161–169

Lucius Verus was born in 130, the son of Lucius Aelius, whom the emperor Hadrian had first chosen as his heir but who died before him. To preserve the line, Hadrian arranged that his eventual successor Antoninus Pius should adopt both the young Lucius and Marcus Aurelius, and so the two grew up as brothers and heirs. When Antoninus died in 161, Marcus Aurelius refused to rule alone and raised Lucius to share the throne as his equal — the first time Rome was governed by two emperors together.

Lucius was always the junior partner, and tradition contrasts his easy, pleasure-loving nature with the grave self-discipline of Marcus. His chief task was the great war against Parthia that broke out in the East at the start of the joint reign. Lucius travelled to Antioch to oversee it, but the victories — the recovery of Armenia, the sack of the Parthian capital Ctesiphon — were won in the field by his generals, above all the able Avidius Cassius.

The triumph carried a curse: the returning legions brought with them a devastating pestilence, the Antonine Plague, which swept through the empire for years. Lucius himself married Marcus’s daughter Lucilla, and in 169, while accompanying Marcus on campaign against the Germanic tribes of the Danube, he died suddenly — struck down, it was said, by the plague or a stroke. Marcus Aurelius ruled alone thereafter.
