Xerxes I

King of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, r. 486–465 BCE

Xerxes I, king of the Achaemenid Persian Empire from 486 to 465 BCE, is remembered above all as the great king who led Persia's massive second invasion of Greece. He was the son of Darius the Great and of Atossa, a daughter of Cyrus the Great, and so united the two founding lines of the dynasty. On his accession he crushed revolts in Egypt and Babylon before turning to the unfinished business his father had left in the west.

In 480 BCE he assembled one of the largest armies and fleets of antiquity and crossed the Hellespont on bridges of boats, having cut a canal through the peninsula at Mount Athos to spare his ships. He overwhelmed the Spartan king Leonidas and his small force at the pass of Thermopylae and burned the evacuated city of Athens. But the allied Greek fleet shattered his navy at the battle of Salamis later that year, and Xerxes withdrew to Asia, leaving his general Mardonius to be defeated and killed at Plataea in 479.

At home he was a great builder, completing and enlarging the royal palaces at Persepolis, among them the monumental Gate of All Nations and the Hall of a Hundred Columns. The Persian king Ahasuerus of the biblical Book of Esther is commonly identified with him.

His later reign grew troubled, and in 465 BCE he was assassinated in a palace conspiracy and succeeded by his son Artaxerxes I. Through Herodotus's vivid portrait, Xerxes became for the Greeks the very image of imperial pride humbled by a free people, a reading that has colored his memory ever since.
