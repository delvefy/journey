Moses

Hebrew prophet and lawgiver

Moses is the towering figure of the Hebrew scriptures, revered as prophet and lawgiver in Judaism, Christianity and Islam alike, though his life is known only through religious tradition and no independent record of him survives. According to the Book of Exodus he was born to an Israelite family during their bondage in Egypt and, to escape a decree that Hebrew boys be killed, was set adrift on the Nile in a basket of reeds, where Pharaoh’s daughter found him and raised him as her own in the royal court.

Grown to manhood, the story tells, he killed an Egyptian who was beating a Hebrew slave and fled into the desert of Midian, where God spoke to him from a bush that burned without being consumed and charged him to deliver his people. Returning to confront Pharaoh, he called down ten plagues upon Egypt until the king relented, then led the Israelites out in the great Exodus, the waters of the sea parting before them and closing upon the pursuing chariots.

At Mount Sinai, by tradition, he received the Law — the Ten Commandments and the covenant that bound Israel to its God — and for forty years he led the people through the wilderness, enduring their rebellions, the golden calf and their longing for Egypt. He brought them at last to the edge of the Promised Land but was not permitted to enter it, dying within sight of it on Mount Nebo and leaving Joshua to lead them in. As the giver of the Law, Moses became the measure of every prophet who came after — the model invoked for both Jesus and Muhammad.
