Agesilaus II

King of Sparta, c. 444–360 BCE

Agesilaus II was king of Sparta for some four decades, from about 400 to 360 BCE, and the dominant figure of his city through the years of its greatest power and the beginning of its decline. Lame from birth, he was not expected to reign, but on his half-brother's death he was raised to the Eurypontid throne with the backing of the ambitious admiral Lysander.

Energetic and intensely patriotic, he led a Spartan army into Asia Minor in 396 BCE to free the Greek cities there from Persia, winning victories until the Great King's gold stirred up war against Sparta in Greece itself and he was recalled. He fought his way home through hostile country and defeated the allied Greeks at Coronea in 394.

For the next thirty years his aggressive, Sparta-first policy guided the city, but it bred enemies, above all a resurgent Thebes. When Spartan power was shattered at the battle of Leuctra in 371, Agesilaus was not himself in command, yet it was his policy that had brought Sparta so low; soon the Theban Epaminondas was invading Laconia itself, and the aged king organized the desperate defense of an unwalled Sparta.

To raise money for his impoverished state he hired himself out late in life as a mercenary captain in Egypt, and died on the voyage home around 360 BCE. His friend Xenophon, who had served with him, left an admiring portrait of a plain, dutiful, and stubborn soldier-king.
