Vladimir the Great

Grand Prince of Kiev, c.958–1015

Vladimir, grand prince of Kiev, was a grandson of the Christian princess Olga and a son of the warrior Sviatoslav, and he seized the throne of the Kievan Rus after a bloody struggle among his brothers. At first a fervent pagan, who set up idols on the hills of Kiev and was remembered for his many wives, he ruled over a sprawling realm of Slavic and Norse peoples that he set about consolidating and expanding.

His great act, and the turning point of his people’s history, was his conversion. The chronicles tell that he sent envoys to weigh the faiths of the world and that they returned dazzled from the worship in Hagia Sophia at Constantinople, unable to say whether they had stood in heaven or on earth. The choice was sealed by politics: Vladimir aided the emperor Basil II against a dangerous rebel, and in return received the emperor’s sister Anna in marriage and accepted baptism into the Orthodox Church about the year 988.

Returning home, he had the idols thrown down and the people of Kiev baptized in the waters of the Dnieper, making Byzantine Christianity the faith of the Rus. From that conversion flowed the religious and cultural identity of Russia, Ukraine and Belarus for a thousand years. Honoured as a saint and equal of the apostles, Vladimir died in 1015, and after another contest among his sons the throne passed to Yaroslav the Wise, under whom Kievan Rus reached its height.
