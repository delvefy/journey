Shah Ismail I

Founder of the Safavid dynasty of Iran, 1487–1524

Ismail was born on 17 July 1487 at Ardabil, the youngest son of Sheikh Haydar, hereditary master of the Safaviyya Sufi order, and of Halima Begum, a daughter of Uzun Hasan of the Aq Qoyunlu confederation. His father was killed in battle against the Shirvanshah a year after his birth, his eldest brother died in prison, and Ismail himself was hidden in Gilan, on the Caspian shore, for six years while the Aq Qoyunlu princes hunted him. He emerged in 1499, a boy of twelve, at the head of the Qizilbash, the red-capped Turkmen tribesmen who venerated the Safavid sheikhs as living saints.

In 1501, after defeating the Aq Qoyunlu at Sharur, he entered Tabriz at the age of fourteen and proclaimed himself Shah of Iran. He proclaimed Twelver Shia Islam the religion of his state, a stroke that severed Iran culturally from the Sunni Ottoman and Uzbek worlds on either side and that has shaped Iranian identity ever since. Over the next decade he subdued Hamadan, Shiraz, Yazd, Baghdad, and Herat, drove the Uzbek Muhammad Shaybani Khan to his death at Merv in 1510, and sent the defeated khan's skull, set in gold, as a drinking cup to his neighbor Babur.

His luck broke at Chaldiran on 23 August 1514, where the Ottoman Selim I, with cannon and matchlock janissaries, destroyed the Qizilbash cavalry and briefly occupied Tabriz. Ismail, who had ridden into battle in person and barely escaped, never again took the field. He spent his last decade in melancholy and in poetry, composing in Azeri Turkish under the pen name Khata'i. He died at Tabriz on 23 May 1524 and was buried at Ardabil, leaving his ten-year-old son Tahmasp the throne and a Shi'a Iran that would outlast his dynasty by centuries.
