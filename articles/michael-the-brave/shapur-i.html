Shapur I

Sasanian King of Kings, reigned c. 240–270 AD

Shapur I was the son of Ardashir I, founder of the Sasanian Empire of Persia, and he carried his father's work to its height. Crowned King of Kings around 240, he proved one of the most formidable rulers of late antiquity, waging a series of victorious wars against Rome that humbled the great power of the West.

In three campaigns he shattered Roman armies in the East. He defeated the forces of the young emperor Gordian III, who died on the march; he compelled the emperor Philip the Arab to buy peace with an enormous tribute; and in 260, at Edessa, he won an unprecedented triumph by capturing the emperor Valerian himself and holding him prisoner for life. Shapur commemorated these victories in monumental rock reliefs at Naqsh-e Rostam, showing the Roman emperors humbled before him, and in a great trilingual inscription recording his deeds.

He was also a builder and patron. Using Roman captives as labour, he founded cities such as Bishapur and Gundeshapur—the latter destined to become a famous centre of learning—and raised the great bridge-dam at Shushtar. Religiously tolerant, he welcomed the prophet Mani to his court and allowed him to preach the new faith of Manichaeism throughout the empire.

His ambitions in Syria were eventually checked by Odaenathus of Palmyra, who drove his forces back across the Euphrates. Yet Shapur remained the dominant figure of his age, and he died around 270 having made the Sasanian Empire the equal and rival of Rome for the next four centuries.
