Jefferson Davis

President of the Confederate States, 1808–1889

Born in Kentucky and raised on a Mississippi plantation, Davis graduated from West Point and married the daughter of his commander, the future president Zachary Taylor, only to lose her to malaria within months. He won fame as a colonel at the Battle of Buena Vista in the Mexican War, then entered politics as a fierce defender of slavery and states’ rights, serving as a United States senator and as an able Secretary of War under President Pierce.

When the Southern states seceded in 1861 he was chosen president of the new Confederacy. He brought genuine military experience to the role, but his prickly temperament, quarrels with his generals and governors, and inability to inspire the broad population made him a less effective war leader than his Northern counterpart. He leaned heavily on Robert E. Lee while the cause slowly bled to death.

Captured by Union cavalry in 1865 as the Confederacy collapsed, Davis was imprisoned for two years and indicted for treason, though he was never brought to trial. Released, he remained unrepentant to the end, defending secession in his memoirs and becoming a revered figure of the Lost Cause. He died in 1889. The statesman whose doctrines of states’ rights and nullification he had taken as his political creed was John C. Calhoun.
