Philip IV of Spain

King of Spain, 1605–1665

Coming to the throne in 1621, Philip IV presided over both the dazzling cultural climax and the political decline of Habsburg Spain. For two decades he left the burden of government to his masterful favourite, the Count-Duke of Olivares, who strove to reverse Spain’s waning fortunes, renew the war against the Dutch, and bind the kingdoms of the monarchy more tightly to Castile. The effort overreached: revolts erupted in Catalonia and in Portugal, which broke away for good in 1640, and Olivares fell from power in 1643.

Spain’s long wars ended in retreat—the recognition of Dutch independence at Westphalia and a humbling peace with France at the Pyrenees, sealed by the marriage of Philip’s daughter Maria Theresa to Louis XIV. Yet amid this ebbing power the king sustained an incomparable artistic golden age. He was the devoted patron of Diego Velázquez, who served him for decades and immortalised the royal family in Las Meninas, and he welcomed Peter Paul Rubens to his court.

A connoisseur who assembled one of history’s great picture collections, Philip was personally cultivated, melancholic, and burdened by guilt over his realm’s misfortunes. His many children mostly died young; the sickly son who survived him, Charles II, would be the last of the Spanish Habsburgs. Philip died in 1665, leaving an empire still vast but exhausted.
