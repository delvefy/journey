Perdiccas II of Macedon

King of Macedon, r. c. 448–413 BCE

Perdiccas II ruled Macedon for roughly thirty-five years, from about 448 to 413 BCE, through the turbulent decades of the great war between Athens and Sparta. The son of Alexander I, he inherited a kingdom still weak and exposed, hemmed in by powerful Thracian and Illyrian neighbors and by turns courted and threatened by the two great powers to the south.

His long reign was a masterclass in survival through shifting alliances. He quarreled and reconciled with Athens repeatedly over the cities of the Chalcidice, and encouraged the people of several towns to abandon their coastal sites and concentrate at Olynthus, which grew into a formidable power. When it suited him he welcomed Spartan intervention: he supported the brilliant Spartan commander Brasidas when he marched into the Thraceward region, only to fall out with him soon after.

He was repeatedly at war with the Thracian king Sitalces, who once invaded Macedon with a vast army, and he balanced the demands of Athens and Sparta with a flexibility his enemies called treachery. Through all these reversals he kept both his throne and his kingdom intact.

The historian Thucydides, who served as an Athenian commander in the same region, records his maneuvers in close detail. Perdiccas was succeeded by his son Archelaus I, under whom Macedon would take a further step toward the prominence it would reach under Philip II.
