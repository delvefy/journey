Paul McCartney

English musician and founding member of the Beatles, born 1942.

Born James Paul McCartney in Liverpool to a cotton-salesman father who led a local jazz band, McCartney lost his mother to cancer when he was fourteen — an event "Let It Be" would later commemorate. The next summer at the Woolton church fête he met John Lennon, joined his skiffle group the Quarrymen on guitar, and over the next two years brought in George Harrison and discarded the original line-up. By 1962, with Ringo Starr installed at the drums and Brian Epstein managing them, the Beatles had a recording contract at EMI under George Martin.

Across the band's eight working years his songwriting partnership with Lennon produced melodic anthems — "Yesterday," "Hey Jude," "Let It Be," "Eleanor Rigby," "Penny Lane," "The Long and Winding Road" — that became the most-covered songs of the second half of the twentieth century. McCartney was the band's organizer, the one who pushed them into Sgt. Pepper's costume conceit, the orchestral overdubs of Magical Mystery Tour, and the rooftop concert in January 1969. When the Beatles broke up in April 1970 it was McCartney's solo announcement and the lawsuit he filed against his bandmates that made the dissolution legally final.

With his wife Linda Eastman, whom he had married in 1969, he formed Wings, scoring through the 1970s with Band on the Run, "Live and Let Die," "Mull of Kintyre," and "Maybe I'm Amazed." He outlived three Beatles by surviving Lennon's murder, Harrison's death in 2001, and Linda's death in 1998, remarried twice, and continued through forty years of solo records, classical compositions, and stadium tours that worked the back catalogue. Queen Elizabeth II knighted him in 1997, and he remained, alongside the surviving Ringo, the principal living curator of the Beatles' legend.

The road continues to Michael Jackson.
