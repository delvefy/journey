Galerius

Roman emperor, c. 258–311 AD

Galerius rose from peasant origins in Dacia, where he is said to have herded cattle in his youth, to become one of the foremost soldiers of his age. In 293 Diocletian appointed him Caesar of the East and gave him his daughter Galeria Valeria in marriage, binding him to the imperial house.

His greatest achievement was the Persian war. After an early reverse, in 298 he routed the Sasanian king Narseh, captured the royal family and treasury, and imposed the Treaty of Nisibis, which won Rome territory beyond the Tigris and decades of dominance in the East—one of the most complete victories Rome ever achieved over Persia.

A devoted pagan and enemy of the new faith, Galerius is reported to have been the chief voice pressing the aging Diocletian to launch the Great Persecution in 303. When Diocletian abdicated in 305 he became Augustus of the East and the dominant figure in the Tetrarchy, installing his nephew Maximinus Daia and his colleague Severus as Caesars.

Yet the system fractured almost at once, as Maxentius and Constantine seized power outside it, and Galerius's own invasion of Italy ended in retreat. Struck down by a gruesome and agonising disease, he issued from his deathbed in 311 the Edict of Serdica, ending the persecution and granting Christians toleration in return for their prayers. He died days later, his great Persian triumph long overshadowed by the religious war he had urged.
