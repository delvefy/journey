Raphael

Painter and architect of the High Renaissance, 1483–1520

Raffaello Sanzio was born on Good Friday, 6 April 1483, at Urbino, the son of the court painter Giovanni Santi and Magia di Battista Ciarla. His mother died when he was eight, his father three years later, leaving him in the household of an uncle, but he had already had a workshop training from his father. The court of Federico da Montefeltro and his son Guidobaldo, then one of the most refined small courts in Italy, was the air he breathed. About 1500 he became a pupil and assistant of Pietro Perugino, almost certainly in Perugia, and by his late teens he was signing altarpieces of his own, including the Marriage of the Virgin of 1504.

From 1504 to 1508 he worked in Florence, where the Madonnas of these years — the Madonna of the Goldfinch, the Madonna del Prato, the Belle Jardinière — absorbed the pyramidal compositions of Leonardo and the sculptural musculature of Michelangelo and transformed both into something graver and more lucid. In 1508 Pope Julius II called him to Rome, almost certainly on the recommendation of his kinsman Donato Bramante. He was given the papal apartments to fresco. Over the next twelve years he and an enormous workshop covered the walls of the Stanza della Segnatura with the Disputa and the School of Athens, then the Stanza d'Eliodoro, the Stanza dell'Incendio, and finally the Sala di Costantino, executed after his death from his cartoons.

Under Leo X he became also painter of portraits — Castiglione, Julius II, Leo X with his cardinals — designer of the tapestry cartoons for the lower walls of the Sistine Chapel, and from 1514 the chief architect of the new St Peter's, in succession to Bramante. He directed surveys of the antiquities of Rome and was building the Villa Madama when, after a brief fever, he died on his thirty-seventh birthday, Good Friday 6 April 1520. By his own wish he was buried in the Pantheon, where his tomb still bears Pietro Bembo's epitaph.
