Boccaccio

Italian writer and humanist, author of the Decameron, 1313–1375

Giovanni Boccaccio was born in the summer of 1313, probably in Certaldo or Florence, the illegitimate son of Boccaccino di Chellino, a representative of the Bardi banking house. In 1327 his father took him south to Naples to be trained in commerce in the Bardi branch there, and from about 1330 to switch to canon law at the studium. Naples — the cosmopolitan, French-flavoured court of Robert the Wise — was the formative scene of his youth. There he met the woman he called Fiammetta, traditionally identified with Maria d'Aquino, and there he composed his earliest works in the Italian vernacular: the prose romance Filocolo, the verse Filostrato of Troilus and Criseyde (Chaucer's source two generations later), and the Teseida of Theseus and the rival lovers.

The Bardi failure of 1340 forced him back to Florence. The Black Death of 1348 killed his father and stepmother and gave him the frame for his greatest work. Composed between about 1349 and 1353, the Decameron describes ten young Florentines who flee the plague to a country villa and tell a hundred stories over ten days: bawdy, sober, satirical, tragic, an encyclopaedia of fourteenth-century life and the foundation stone of European prose fiction. He met Petrarch at Florence in 1350 and the friendship that followed reshaped his ambitions toward Latin scholarship. In the years afterward he composed the great Latin reference-works De casibus virorum illustrium, De mulieribus claris, and the immense Genealogia deorum gentilium, the first systematic dictionary of classical mythology.

Florence used him on a string of diplomatic embassies — to Padua, Ravenna, Avignon, and in 1351 to Emperor Charles IV at Forlì. He sponsored the Calabrian Leonzio Pilato in the first Latin translation of Homer at his own house in Florence in 1360–62, and gave the public lectures on Dante's Inferno that founded the lecturae Dantis tradition in 1373–74. Ill and lonely, he retired to Certaldo, where he died on 21 December 1375 and was buried in the parish church.

The road continues to Charles IV, Holy Roman Emperor.
