Inês de Castro

The murdered lady “crowned after death”, c. 1325–1355

Inês de Castro was born around 1325 into the powerful Galician house of Castro and came to Portugal as a lady-in-waiting to Constanza of Castile, the bride of the heir to the throne, Prince Pedro. Pedro fell helplessly in love with her, and after Constanza died in 1345 the two lived openly together and had children. To the old king, Afonso IV, the attachment was a danger: Inês’s Castilian kinsmen were powerful and ambitious, and her sons threatened the place of his legitimate grandson. After years of mounting unease he resolved to be rid of her.

In 1355, while Pedro was away, the king’s agents came to Inês at Coimbra and killed her. The grief-stricken prince rose in armed revolt against his own father before the two were uneasily reconciled. But Pedro did not forget. When he came to the throne as Pedro I in 1357 he hunted down the men who had done the deed and put them to death with notorious cruelty, and he declared that he had secretly married Inês, so that she had been his lawful wife and queen all along.

From this came the most famous of Portuguese legends: that Pedro had her body exhumed and enthroned in royal robes, and compelled his court to kneel and kiss the hand of the dead queen. Whatever the truth of that scene, the story of love and vengeance became one of the great themes of Iberian literature, mourned by Camões in his epic and retold on stages across Europe for centuries.

The road continues to Pedro I of Portugal.
