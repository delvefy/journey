Jawaharlal Nehru

First Prime Minister of India, 1889–1964

Jawaharlal Nehru was born in 1889 at Allahabad, the only son of Motilal Nehru, a wealthy and Anglicised barrister of the Indian National Congress. He was tutored at home by an Irish governess, sent to Harrow at fifteen, read natural sciences at Trinity College, Cambridge, and was called to the Bar at the Inner Temple. He returned to India in 1912 a polished young Brahmin lawyer, with no immediate intention of politics.

The 1919 Jallianwala Bagh massacre at Amritsar turned him toward Mahatma Gandhi, whose call for non-cooperation he and his father took up. He spent more than nine years in British prisons over the next two and a half decades, and used them to write a long correspondence with his daughter that became Glimpses of World History, his Autobiography, and the imaginative The Discovery of India. By the late 1920s he was the leading figure of the Congress's left, pushing it toward an outright demand for complete independence rather than dominion status.

When Britain at last conceded independence in 1947, he negotiated the partition with Mountbatten in a few brutal months and became, at midnight on 15 August 1947, the first Prime Minister of an independent India. The speech he gave that night—"At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom"—is the founding text of the modern republic. He held the office for seventeen years until his death.

As Prime Minister he built the institutions of the world's largest secular democracy, founded the planning commissions and the public-sector industrial base, expanded universal franchise across an enormous and largely illiterate electorate, and on the world stage co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement with Tito, Nasser, and Sukarno. The 1962 border war with China, which his army lost humiliatingly, broke his health. He died in office in Delhi on 27 May 1964. The daughter who would, in 1966, herself become Prime Minister and dominate Indian politics for a generation was Indira Gandhi.
