James V of Scotland

King of Scots, 1512–1542

James V was born in 1512, the son of James IV and Margaret Tudor, the sister of Henry VIII of England. His father’s death at the catastrophic Battle of Flodden in 1513 left James an infant king, and his long minority was consumed by the feuds of the great nobles, above all the Douglas earls of Angus, who held the boy king as a virtual prisoner until he escaped to claim power for himself.

Ruling in his own right from the late 1520s, James worked to tame the nobility, strengthen royal justice through a new College of Justice, and fill his treasury, in part by heavy taxation of the wealthy Scottish church. In foreign affairs he held firmly to the alliance with France, marrying first Madeleine, the daughter of Francis I, who died within months of reaching Scotland, and then the formidable Mary of Guise.

A lover of building and display, he raised splendid Renaissance palaces at Stirling and Falkland and won a reputation, embroidered by later legend, as a friend of the common folk. A loyal Catholic, he resisted his uncle Henry VIII’s pressure to break with Rome, and the resulting war ended in the rout of his army at Solway Moss in 1542. James died days later at Falkland, a broken man, leaving his throne to his newborn daughter, Mary, Queen of Scots.
