Chandragupta Maurya

Founder of the Maurya Empire, c. 350–295 BCE

Chandragupta Maurya rose from obscure origins to found the first empire to unite almost all of the Indian subcontinent. By tradition he was discovered and tutored by the brilliant and ruthless strategist Chanakya, also called Kautilya, who became the architect of his rise. Together they overthrew the powerful but hated Nanda dynasty and seized the throne of Magadha at Pataliputra around 321 BCE.

His ascent coincided with the aftermath of Alexander the Great's invasion of the northwest. As the Macedonian garrisons fell into disarray following Alexander's departure and death, Chandragupta extended his power over the Punjab and the Indus lands, welding a vast territory into a single centralised state served by an elaborate bureaucracy, a huge standing army, and a far-reaching network of spies.

Around 305 BCE he came into conflict with Seleucus I Nicator, the general who had inherited Alexander's eastern conquests. Chandragupta prevailed, and by treaty gained the provinces beyond the Indus; in return Seleucus received five hundred war elephants and sealed the bargain with a marriage alliance. Seleucus sent the ambassador Megasthenes to the Mauryan court, whose account gave the Greek world its fullest picture of India.

By tradition Chandragupta, late in life, embraced the ascetic faith of Jainism, abdicated in favour of his son Bindusara, and journeyed south as a wandering monk, ending his days by the ritual fast unto death at Shravanabelagola. He left behind the mighty empire that his grandson Ashoka would carry to its greatest height.
