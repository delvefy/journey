Darius III

Last king of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, r. 336–330 BCE

Darius III was the last king of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, the ruler whose defeat at the hands of Alexander the Great ended more than two centuries of Persian dominion over the Near East. A distant member of the royal house, he was raised to the throne in 336 BCE by the powerful courtier Bagoas, after a series of palace murders had thinned the line of succession.

He had scarcely secured his crown when the young king of Macedon crossed into Asia. At the river Granicus in 334 Alexander shattered the satraps' army; at Issus in 333 Darius himself was put to flight, abandoning his mother, wife, and daughters to capture—captives whom Alexander, famously, treated with honor. Darius offered generous terms, even the cession of the western half of his empire, but Alexander refused them.

The decisive trial came at Gaugamela in 331 BCE, where Darius gathered the full strength of his empire on ground of his own choosing. Yet once more, at the crisis of the battle, his nerve failed and he fled the field, and with him fled the empire's last hope.

A hunted fugitive, Darius was at last seized and murdered by his own kinsman Bessus, the satrap of Bactria, in 330 BCE. Alexander, finding the dead king, is said to have covered the body with his own cloak and sent it for royal burial—an end that turned the defeated Darius into an object of pity rather than scorn.
