Pedro I of Portugal

King of Portugal, “the Just”, 1320–1367

Pedro, born in 1320, was the son and heir of Afonso IV of Portugal. Married for policy to Constanza of Castile, he gave his heart instead to her lady-in-waiting, Inês de Castro, and after Constanza’s death lived with Inês and their children. When his father, fearing her Castilian kin, had Inês put to death in 1355, the prince rose in armed rebellion against the old king before a grudging peace was patched together. He nursed the wound until power was his own.

He came to the throne in 1357. By a treaty with Peter of Castile to exchange one another’s fugitives, he secured the surrender of two of the men who had killed Inês, and he took his revenge upon them with a ferocity that became notorious. He then declared that he had secretly married Inês in her lifetime, proclaiming the dead woman his lawful queen.

For all the darkness of that story, Pedro was remembered by his people as “the Just.” He rode his kingdom dispensing swift, personal and often brutal justice, curbing the great nobles, protecting humbler folk, and keeping Portugal at peace and in plenty through a decade of good government. He had the tombs of himself and Inês carved and placed facing each other at the abbey of Alcobaça, so that, by legend, they might rise to see one another at the Last Judgment. He died in 1367, leaving the crown to his son Ferdinand, while another son, born out of wedlock, would one day found a new royal line.
