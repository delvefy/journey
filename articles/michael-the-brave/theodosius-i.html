Theodosius I

Roman emperor, 347–395 AD

Theodosius, later called the Great, was born in 347 in Hispania, the son of the able general Count Theodosius. He campaigned with his father on the Danube and in Britain, but withdrew to Spain after the elder Theodosius was executed in a political purge. In 379, in the catastrophe following the Battle of Adrianople—where the eastern emperor Valens and most of his army had been destroyed by the Goths—the western emperor Gratian raised Theodosius to rule the East.

He spent years stabilising the Gothic war, and in 382 settled the Goths inside the empire as autonomous allies, the first such concession on Roman soil. He overthrew the usurper Magnus Maximus in 388, and at the Battle of the Frigidus in 394 crushed the usurper Eugenius and the Frankish general Arbogast, reuniting the whole Roman world under a single ruler for the last time in history.

A fervent Nicene Christian, Theodosius made orthodox Christianity the official religion of the empire by the Edict of Thessalonica in 380, summoned the First Council of Constantinople in 381 to reaffirm the Nicene Creed, and issued laws suppressing pagan sacrifice. When his soldiers massacred thousands of civilians at Thessalonica in 390, Bishop Ambrose of Milan barred him from communion until he performed public penance.

At his death in Milan in 395 he divided the empire between his sons—Arcadius in the East and Honorius in the West—a partition that became permanent. Founder of the Theodosian dynasty, he was the emperor who fixed Christianity as the enduring faith of Rome.
