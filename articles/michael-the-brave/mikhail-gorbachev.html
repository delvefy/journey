Mikhail Gorbachev

1931–2022, last General Secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union

Mikhail Sergeyevich Gorbachev was born in the village of Privolnoye in Stavropol Krai, the son of peasants whose families had been deported and starved under Stalin's collectivization. He drove a combine harvester through high-school summers, joined the party as a teenager, and read law at Moscow State University, where he met and married the philosophy student Raisa Titarenko. He returned to Stavropol, rose through the regional komsomol and party committees, and in 1978 was brought to Moscow as Central Committee secretary for agriculture by Yuri Andropov, who became his protector and patron.

The brief secretariats of Andropov and Konstantin Chernenko ended in March 1985, and Gorbachev was elected General Secretary at fifty-four, the youngest Politburo member by a decade. He inherited an economy starved of investment, an exhausted army in Afghanistan, and a society lying to itself. His twin programmes were perestroika, the restructuring of central planning and ownership, and glasnost, the opening of the press, the past, and political competition. Andrei Sakharov was recalled from exile in Gorky in 1986. The 1989 elections to the Congress of People's Deputies were the first contested in seven decades.

Abroad he negotiated with Margaret Thatcher (“a man one can do business with”) and conducted the four Reykjavik, Geneva, Washington and Moscow summits with Ronald Reagan that produced the INF treaty of 1987. He met Pope John Paul II at the Vatican in December 1989, withdrew the Red Army from Afghanistan in February 1989, refused to send tanks against the East European revolutions of that autumn, and negotiated German reunification with Helmut Kohl. He shared the 1990 Nobel Peace Prize.

The August 1991 coup against him by hardliners failed, and within four months the Soviet Union itself had been dissolved by Russia, Ukraine and Belarus over his protest. Boris Yeltsin took the Kremlin; Gorbachev resigned on 25 December 1991. He spent the next thirty years foundering for political relevance, running an ineffectual 1996 presidential campaign, founding the Gorbachev Foundation and the journal Novaya Gazeta, and watching with public dismay as Vladimir Putin reversed his openings. He died in Moscow in August 2022, four months into the Russian invasion of Ukraine.

The road continues to Ronald Reagan.
