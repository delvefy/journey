Benozzo Gozzoli

Florentine fresco painter, c. 1421–1497

Benozzo Gozzoli was born in Florence about 1421 and began, like many of his generation, in metalwork, helping Lorenzo Ghiberti finish the gilded Baptistery doors. He then attached himself to the painter-friar Fra Angelico, serving as his assistant on the frescoes of the Vatican chapel of Pope Nicholas V and at Orvieto, and absorbing from him a sweetness of colour and devotional grace that he never lost even as his own taste ran to splendour.

His enduring masterpiece is the small chapel of the Palazzo Medici in Florence, frescoed in 1459 with the Procession of the Magi. The three kings and their immense retinue wind through an enchanted Tuscan landscape of cypress and castle, and among the riders Gozzoli set the faces of his patrons and of dignitaries who recalled the great Council of Florence of two decades before, where Latin and Greek churchmen had met. It is at once a religious image and a glittering group portrait of the Medici world at its height.

He was a tireless decorator of walls. At San Gimignano he painted an extensive cycle on the life of Saint Augustine, and at Pisa he covered the long walls of the Camposanto with Old Testament scenes, a vast labour of his later years that was largely destroyed only in the twentieth century. Prolific, genial and much in demand across Tuscany, Gozzoli worked almost to the end of his life and died at Pistoia in 1497.
