Helmut Kohl

1930–2017, Chancellor of Germany 1982–1998

Helmut Josef Michael Kohl was born in Ludwigshafen on the Rhine in April 1930, the youngest son of a Bavarian-born tax inspector and a devout Catholic mother. His elder brother died on the Eastern Front in 1944, and Helmut himself was conscripted in the war's last weeks into a flak emplacement. He read history and political science at Frankfurt and Heidelberg, took his doctorate on the political parties of the Palatinate in 1958, and worked briefly for a chemical industry trade association before politics absorbed him entirely.

He rose through the Rhineland-Palatinate Christian Democrats to become, at thirty-nine, the youngest minister-president of any West German state. He took over the federal CDU in 1973, lost the 1976 federal election to Helmut Schmidt, and on 1 October 1982 unseated Schmidt in a constructive vote of no confidence in coalition with the Free Democrats. He held the chancellorship for sixteen years, longer than anyone since Bismarck.

The decisive years were 1989–91. When the Berlin Wall opened on 9 November 1989 he was in Warsaw and broke off his visit to fly to Berlin. He produced within ten days a ten-point plan for confederation; over the following year, with Mikhail Gorbachev's restraint, George H.W. Bush's support, François Mitterrand's hesitating consent and Margaret Thatcher's open opposition, he carried German reunification through to its formal accomplishment on 3 October 1990. With Mitterrand he then drove the Maastricht Treaty of 1992, the single currency, and the European Union itself. Ronald Reagan had spoken at the Brandenburg Gate; Kohl was the chancellor who walked through it.

The financial scandals of the late 1990s caught up with him after his defeat by Gerhard Schröder in 1998. The CDU donations affair, in which Kohl admitted to receiving unrecorded contributions but refused to name the donors, broke his honorary chair of the party in 2000 and was the occasion on which his pupil Angela Merkel publicly disowned him. He retired to Oggersheim, was honoured by Pope John Paul II and named an honorary citizen of Europe in 1998, and died there in June 2017, eighty-seven years old.

The road continues to Bill Clinton.
