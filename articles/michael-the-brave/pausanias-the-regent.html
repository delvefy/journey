Pausanias the Regent

Spartan regent and general, d. c. 470 BCE

Pausanias was the Spartan regent and general who led the Greeks to their decisive land victory over Persia at Plataea, and whose later career ended in disgrace and a grim death. A member of the Agiad royal house and a nephew of Leonidas, he governed Sparta as regent for Leonidas's young son Pleistarchus.

In 479 BCE he commanded the combined Greek army—the largest the city-states had yet fielded—against the Persian forces left in Greece under the general Mardonius. At Plataea in Boeotia he won a complete victory; Mardonius was killed and the invasion finally broken. The next year Pausanias led the allied fleet to free Byzantium and to campaign on Cyprus.

But success went to his head. His high-handed, almost despotic manner alienated the allied Greeks, who turned instead to Athens for leadership—the moment from which the Athenian-led Delian League grew. Worse, he was suspected of secret dealings with King Xerxes, even of scheming to make himself master of Greece with Persian backing. Recalled to Sparta and tried, he was acquitted for want of proof, but suspicion clung to him.

At last, accused also of intriguing with the discontented helots, he took sanctuary in the temple of Athena of the Brazen House. The authorities walled up the doors and left him to starve, carrying him out only as he neared death, around 470 BCE. His fall, recounted by Thucydides alongside that of Themistocles, became a classic study of a hero ruined by his own ambition.
