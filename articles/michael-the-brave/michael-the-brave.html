Michael the Brave

Voivode of Wallachia, c. 1558–1601

Michael was born around 1558, an illegitimate son of the Wallachian prince Pătrașcu the Good. He grew up among the boyar gentry of the lower Danube, accumulating estates and offices, and by 1593 he had secured, through influence at the Sublime Porte and heavy borrowing in Constantinople, the throne of Wallachia as a tributary of the Ottoman Empire.

Within a year he turned on his suzerain. In November 1594 he ordered the slaughter of the Ottoman creditors and the Turkish garrison in Bucharest, joining the anti-Ottoman Holy League sponsored by the Habsburgs and the Papacy. The campaign that followed made his name. In August 1595 at Călugăreni, on a marshy ford of the Neajlov, his small Wallachian and Transylvanian army staggered the much larger force of Sinan Pasha, the Ottoman grand vizier. Though Michael had to retreat afterwards, the autumn counter-offensive recovered Bucharest and Târgoviște, and the war stabilised the Danube frontier for years.

His most audacious achievement came at the end of the century. In 1599 he crossed the Carpathians and crushed the army of Andrew Báthory at Șelimbăr, taking Transylvania for himself. In May 1600 he marched into Moldavia and unseated Ieremia Movilă, briefly holding all three of the principalities later associated with modern Romania—a personal union, never formalised, that would echo in nineteenth-century national memory.

The union lasted only months. Hungarian nobles revolted, the Poles invaded Moldavia, and Michael lost Transylvania at Mirăslău in September 1600. He went to Vienna and Prague to plead his case before Emperor Rudolf II, who outfitted him with fresh troops. Returning in 1601, he defeated the Transylvanian rebels at Guruslău alongside the imperial general Giorgio Basta—and a few weeks later, on Basta’s orders, was murdered in his tent on the Câmpia Turzii.

The road continues to Rudolf II, Holy Roman Emperor.
