Pope Francis

1936–2025, supreme pontiff of the Roman Catholic Church, born Jorge Mario Bergoglio

Jorge Mario Bergoglio was born in the Flores neighbourhood of Buenos Aires in December 1936, the eldest of five children of an Italian railway accountant who had emigrated from Piedmont and an Argentine homemaker. He worked as a chemical technician and briefly as a nightclub bouncer before, after a bout of pneumonia that cost him part of a lung, entering the Society of Jesus in 1958. He took final vows in 1973 and was that same year, at thirty-six, named Jesuit provincial of Argentina during the country's slide into the “dirty war,” the conduct of which under military rule shadowed his later election.

He taught and reformed the Colegio Máximo in San Miguel, was appointed auxiliary bishop of Buenos Aires in 1992, archbishop in 1998, and cardinal in 2001. He travelled to the slums by subway, refused the archbishop's palace, and at the 2005 conclave that followed John Paul II's death was the runner-up to Joseph Ratzinger. When Pope Benedict XVI resigned in February 2013, the second conclave elected Bergoglio at seventy-six. He took the name Francis after Francis of Assisi, the first pope ever to do so, the first Latin American, and the first Jesuit.

His pontificate of twelve years pressed two recurring themes: the Church's care for the poor and the planet, and an internal reform of the curia. The encyclicals Evangelii Gaudium (2013), Laudato Si' on the environment (2015), and Fratelli Tutti on fraternity (2020) framed the programme. He travelled to Lampedusa to address the migrant crisis, brokered the December 2014 thaw between the United States under Barack Obama and Raúl Castro's Cuba, met Donald Trump at the Vatican in 2017, received Vladimir Putin and protested the invasion of Ukraine, and canonized Mother Teresa in St Peter's Square in 2016.

He convened the Synod on Synodality in two sessions from 2023 to 2024, opening more lay and female participation in Church governance, while resisting pressure to alter doctrine on the priesthood or marriage. Long-standing illness and the loss of mobility narrowed his last public appearances. He died at the Casa Santa Marta on Easter Monday, 21 April 2025, and was succeeded by the American cardinal Robert Prevost as Pope Leo XIV.
