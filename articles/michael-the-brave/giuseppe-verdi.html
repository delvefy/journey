Giuseppe Verdi

Italian opera composer, 1813–1901

Verdi was born in a village near Busseto to a family of modest means and suffered early the loss of his young wife and both their children. His career was rescued from despair by the triumph of Nabucco (1842), whose chorus of exiled Hebrews, "Va, pensiero," was taken up by Italians chafing under foreign rule. Verdi himself became a living emblem of the movement for national unification, his very name made into the patriotic cry "Viva VERDI."

In the 1850s came the trio of works that secured his immortality: Rigoletto, drawn from a play by Victor Hugo, Il Trovatore, and La Traviata. With an unerring sense of the stage and an unmatched gift for melody, he made the human voice the vehicle of raw dramatic truth, and operas such as Don Carlos, after Schiller, and the Egyptian spectacle Aida spread his fame across the world.

A committed patriot, Verdi accepted a seat in the first parliament of a united Italy and composed his majestic Requiem in memory of the writer Manzoni. After a long silence many thought final, he returned in old age to crown his life’s work with two operas after Shakespeare, the tragic Otello and the radiant comedy Falstaff. He devoted his last years and fortune to charity, founding a home for aged musicians, and died in 1901 amid national mourning.
