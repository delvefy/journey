Vespasian

Roman emperor, founder of the Flavian dynasty, 9–79 AD

Vespasian rose from modest Sabine stock, the son of a tax-gatherer, and owed everything to his own ability rather than to noble birth. He made his name as a soldier in the invasion of Britain under Claudius, commanding a legion with distinction. His career later faltered—by one story he gave offence by dozing during one of Nero's recitals—but in 66 Nero entrusted him with the hard task of suppressing the great revolt in Judea, and he set about it with steady competence.

When Nero fell and the empire dissolved into the civil wars of the Year of the Four Emperors, the legions of the East proclaimed Vespasian their candidate. His forces overthrew Vitellius, and at the end of 69 he entered Rome as emperor, leaving his son Titus to finish the war and take Jerusalem. After the ruin of the civil wars, Vespasian's chief work was restoration: he repaired the shattered finances with shrewd, frugal, sometimes mocking economy, secured the frontiers, and rebuilt the burned Capitol.

He began the greatest of Roman monuments, the Colosseum, and raised the Temple of Peace, stamping the city with Flavian grandeur. A plain-spoken man famous for his earthy humour, he made the throne respectable and, above all, hereditary, founding a dynasty that passed to his sons Titus and Domitian. He died in 79, reportedly joking as the end came that he supposed he was now becoming a god, and was duly deified by the state he had set back on its feet.
