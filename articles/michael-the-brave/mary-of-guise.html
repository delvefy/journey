Mary of Guise

Queen consort and regent of Scotland, 1515–1560

Mary was born in 1515 into the house of Guise, one of the greatest and most ambitious noble families of France. Widowed young after a first marriage, she was courted by the recently widowed Henry VIII of England, but she declined the English king and chose instead to marry James V of Scotland in 1538, becoming queen consort of the Scots and binding her fortunes to the old alliance between Scotland and France.

When James V died suddenly in 1542, only days after the birth of their daughter, the infant became Mary, Queen of Scots, and Mary of Guise gave her life to defending the child’s crown. Against the campaign of pressure and invasion by which Henry VIII sought to seize the baby queen for his own son, the so-called Rough Wooing, she secured her daughter’s future by sending her to be raised in France and betrothed to the heir to the French throne.

From 1554 Mary governed Scotland as regent in her absent daughter’s name, leaning on French soldiers and counsellors to hold the realm. Her rule, however, ran headlong into the swelling Protestant movement of the Lords of the Congregation and the fiery preaching of John Knox. Worn down by rebellion and illness, she died in Edinburgh Castle in 1560, just as the Scottish Reformation triumphed and the long French ascendancy in Scotland came to an end.
