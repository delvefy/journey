James Madison

Fourth President of the United States, 1751–1836

A small, scholarly Virginian of immense learning, Madison earned the title “Father of the Constitution” for his role at the Philadelphia Convention of 1787. He arrived with the Virginia Plan that framed the debate, argued tirelessly for a strong federal union, and kept the fullest record of the secret proceedings. With Alexander Hamilton and John Jay he then wrote The Federalist to win ratification, and in the first Congress he drafted the amendments that became the Bill of Rights.

Soon, however, Madison broke with Hamilton’s financial nationalism and joined his close friend Thomas Jefferson to organise the opposition that became the Democratic-Republican Party. As Jefferson’s Secretary of State he helped manage the diplomacy of an age dominated by the wars of revolutionary and Napoleonic Europe.

Elected president in 1808, Madison led the nation into the War of 1812 against Britain over maritime rights and national honour. The conflict went badly at first—British troops burned the new capital, forcing the president and his celebrated wife Dolley to flee—but it ended without disaster, and a surge of patriotic feeling followed. Retiring to his estate at Montpelier, the last of the constitutional founders lived on into the 1830s, and was succeeded in the presidency by his friend and fellow Virginian James Monroe.
