Bartolomeu Dias

Portuguese navigator, first to round the Cape of Good Hope, c. 1450–1500

Bartolomeu Dias was born around 1450, almost certainly in the Algarve, into the lesser nobility of the Portuguese sea-coast. Of his early life little is recorded beyond a fact that mattered: he served in the household of the Infante Dom João, and when that prince became King John II in 1481, Dias was one of the captains the king relied on to extend the African discoveries already pushed past the Congo by Diogo Cão. He commanded a caravel on the voyage that brought slaves home from the Guinea coast in 1481, and managed the royal warehouses in Lisbon thereafter.

In August 1487 John II sent him south with three small ships, two caravels and a supply vessel, to find the southern end of Africa and the way through to the Indian Ocean. Dias sailed past every previous landfall, planted his padrões on stone columns at strategic capes, and at the Bay of Angra Pequena ran into a strong south-easterly that drove him offshore for thirteen days. When he turned east he found no land; turning north he made his landfall at Mossel Bay, well east of any cape. He had been blown around the southern tip of Africa without seeing it. He sailed on as far as the Great Fish River before his exhausted crews compelled him to turn back, and on the return voyage in May 1488 he sighted at last the great promontory he had missed. He named it Cabo das Tormentas, the Cape of Storms; in Lisbon, on his triumphant arrival in December 1488, King John renamed it Cabo da Boa Esperança, the Cape of Good Hope.

His subsequent career was less glittering. He helped fit out Vasco da Gama’s expedition and sailed in support as far as the Cape Verdes. In 1500 he commanded one of the ships of the great fleet of Pedro Álvares Cabral, the second voyage to India, that touched accidentally on the Brazilian coast at Porto Seguro in April. Off the Cape of Good Hope on 29 May 1500, a sudden storm wrecked four of the ships and Dias was lost with his vessel and crew within sight of the headland he had named.

The road continues to Pedro Álvares Cabral.
