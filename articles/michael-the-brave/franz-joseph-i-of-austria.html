Franz Joseph I of Austria

Emperor of Austria-Hungary, 1830–1916

Franz Joseph I reigned for sixty-eight years, one of the longest tenures any European monarch has held, and his life spanned the whole arc of his empire's decline. Born in 1830, he was raised to the throne at eighteen in the turmoil of 1848, when revolution forced his uncle to abdicate. He restored order with an iron conservatism and a soldier's sense of duty that never left him.

Yet the world moved against him. In 1859 the French emperor Napoleon III beat his armies at Solferino and tore Lombardy away; in 1866 the Prussia of Bismarck crushed Austria at Königgrätz and shut it out of Germany forever. To save the monarchy, Franz Joseph struck the Compromise of 1867 that created the Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary, and had himself crowned king in Budapest.

He governed as a tireless bureaucrat, rising before dawn to his papers, but tragedy stalked his house. His brother Maximilian was shot as a puppet emperor in Mexico; his only son died by his own hand at Mayerling; and his beautiful, melancholy wife, the Empress Elisabeth, was murdered by an anarchist in 1898.

Bound at last in alliance with Wilhelm II's Germany, the old emperor faced one final blow. When his heir was assassinated at Sarajevo in 1914, his ultimatum to Serbia lit the fuse of the First World War. He died in 1916, two years before the empire he personified dissolved. The murdered heir who set the catastrophe in motion was Archduke Franz Ferdinand.
