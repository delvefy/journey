Livia Drusilla

Roman empress, 58 BCE–29 CE

Livia Drusilla was the wife of Augustus and the matriarch of the Julio-Claudian dynasty, the most powerful woman of the early Roman Empire. Born in 58 BCE into the proud patrician house of the Claudii, she was first married to Tiberius Claudius Nero, by whom she bore two sons, the future emperor Tiberius and Nero Claudius Drusus.

In 38 BCE the young Octavian, soon to be Augustus, fell in love with her and compelled her husband to give her up, marrying her even while she was pregnant. The union lasted fifty-one years. Livia became his closest confidante, a trusted adviser whose counsel he is said to have valued above all others, while presenting to Rome the image of the ideal matron—modest, dignified, and devoted to wool-working and the old virtues.

Behind that image, later writers, above all the historian Tacitus, painted her as a relentless dynast who cleared the path to power for her son. Rumour laid at her door the deaths of Augustus's preferred heirs—Marcellus, Gaius, and Lucius—and even the end of Augustus himself, though such charges are reported as suspicion rather than proven fact.

When Augustus died in 14 CE his will adopted her into his own family and gave her the title Julia Augusta. She remained a commanding presence into the reign of her son Tiberius, with whom her relations grew strained, until her death in 29 CE. Her grandson, the emperor Claudius, completed her ascent by having her formally deified.
