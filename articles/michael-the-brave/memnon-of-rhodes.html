Memnon of Rhodes

Greek mercenary general in Persian service, c. 380–333 BCE

Memnon of Rhodes was a Greek soldier of fortune who became the ablest commander in the service of the Persian king Darius III, and the one general who might have stopped Alexander the Great. Born around 380 BCE on the island of Rhodes, he and his brother Mentor sold their military skill to the Persian court, and Memnon married Barsine, a daughter of the great Persian noble Artabazus.

When Alexander invaded Asia in 334 BCE, Memnon urged the Persians to avoid open battle and instead lay waste the country before the advancing Macedonians, starving them out—sound advice that the proud satraps rejected, to their ruin at the river Granicus. Memnon then conducted a stubborn defense of the great fortress-city of Halicarnassus, holding Alexander at bay for months before withdrawing by sea.

Darius now gave him supreme command and a fleet, and Memnon opened a bold counteroffensive in the Aegean, seizing islands and threatening to carry the war into Greece itself and cut Alexander off from home.

But at the height of this campaign, while besieging Mytilene on Lesbos in 333 BCE, Memnon fell ill and died. His death removed the gravest strategic threat to Alexander's expedition, and the initiative passed for good to the Macedonians.

The road continues to Barsine.
