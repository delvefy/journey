Angela Merkel

b. 1954, Chancellor of Germany 2005–2021

Angela Dorothea Kasner was born in Hamburg in July 1954, the elder child of a Lutheran pastor and an English teacher. Weeks after her birth her father accepted a parish in Quitzow in the German Democratic Republic, and the family moved east. She grew up at the Waldhof seminary in Templin, won prizes in Russian and mathematics in school, and read physics at the University of Leipzig, where she met her first husband Ulrich Merkel. She took her doctorate in quantum chemistry at the East German Academy of Sciences in 1986 and spent the following years at the Central Institute for Physical Chemistry in Berlin.

The fall of the Wall in November 1989 turned her toward politics. She joined Democratic Awakening, then the Christian Democratic Union, and after reunification was elected to the Bundestag for Stralsund-Rügen-Grimmen in December 1990. Helmut Kohl, recognizing in her a useful eastern Protestant woman in a party of western Catholic men, made her Minister for Women and Youth, then Environment Minister, and the press took to calling her “Kohl's Mädchen.” She broke with Kohl in 1999 over the CDU donations affair, and in 2000 succeeded him as party chair.

In November 2005 she narrowly defeated Gerhard Schröder and became the first woman chancellor and the first eastern German to hold the office. Through sixteen years and four governments — two grand coalitions with the Social Democrats, one with the Free Democrats — she steered Germany through the 2008 financial crisis, the euro sovereign-debt crisis from 2010, the 2015 admission of close to a million refugees from Syria and elsewhere, the post-Fukushima exit from nuclear power, and the slow widening fissure with the United States under Donald Trump.

She spoke Russian to Vladimir Putin and English to Barack Obama, was a fluent Atlanticist who frustrated Washington and a sceptical European who frustrated Brussels, and exchanged careful exchanges with her fellow German Pope Benedict XVI. She declined to seek a fifth term, handed the chancellery to Olaf Scholz on 8 December 2021, and retired to Berlin and her Uckermark cottage. Her memoir, Freiheit, appeared in 2024.

The road continues to Helmut Kohl.
