Nicholas II of Russia

1868–1918, last Emperor of Russia

Nicholas Alexandrovich Romanov was born at Tsarskoe Selo in May 1868, the eldest son of the future Alexander III and Maria Feodorovna. His schooling, the most thorough ever given a tsarevich, included the legal lectures of Konstantin Pobedonostsev, the strategic instruction of Mikhail Dragomirov, and a long Asian tour in 1890–91, on which he was attacked by a sword-wielding policeman at Otsu in Japan. In 1894, days before his father's death, he became engaged to Princess Alix of Hesse, who took the names Alexandra Feodorovna on conversion to Orthodoxy.

He inherited at twenty-six an empire whose tasks were beyond his temperament: industrialization under Sergei Witte, peasant land-hunger, the borderland nationalities, and the spread of socialism. The disastrous coronation crush at Khodynka Field, the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–05 ending in the loss of the fleet at Tsushima, and the revolutionary year 1905 forced him in October of that year to grant the October Manifesto, conceding a State Duma and civil liberties. He took back as much as he could in the Fundamental Laws of 1906, and lived in private retirement at Tsarskoe Selo with his wife, four daughters, and the haemophiliac heir Alexei.

The boy's illness brought the Siberian peasant healer Grigori Rasputin into the imperial intimacy, and from 1907 his presence at court eroded the dynasty's prestige. The Stolypin land reforms attempted to stabilize the peasantry; Stolypin himself was assassinated in 1911. The First World War, into which Nicholas took the country in defence of Serbia in 1914, exposed the army's want of shells and rifles. In September 1915 he assumed personal command at the front, leaving Petrograd to Alexandra and Rasputin.

The February Revolution of 1917 forced his abdication in a railway carriage at Pskov on 15 March. His cousin King George V declined asylum. Held first at Tsarskoe Selo, then at Tobolsk, and finally at Ekaterinburg, he and his entire family were shot by the local Bolshevik soviet on the night of 17 July 1918. Lenin and Trotsky, with Stalin in the wings, had buried the Romanov three-hundred-year reign.
