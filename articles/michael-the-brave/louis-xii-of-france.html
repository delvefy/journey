Louis XII of France

King of France, “Father of the People,” 1462–1515

Louis was born at Blois on 27 June 1462, son of Charles, Duke of Orléans — the captive poet of Agincourt — and Marie of Cleves. Through his father he was the great-grandson of Charles V, head of the cadet line of Valois-Orléans, and the heir presumptive of his cousin Louis XI’s direct line. Louis XI, distrusting the Orléans, forced him at fourteen to marry the king’s own pious, hunchbacked daughter Jeanne of France in 1476. He led the rebellious nobles of the Mad War against the regent Anne of Beaujeu, was beaten and captured at Saint-Aubin-du-Cormier in 1488, and spent three years in the king’s prison at Bourges before Charles VIII restored him to favour and to the campaigns in Italy.

The death of Charles VIII without a son in April 1498 brought him the throne. He had the marriage to Jeanne annulled by Alexander VI in December — at the price of the cardinalate for Cesare Borgia and the Valentinois duchy for him — and married Anne of Brittany on 8 January 1499 to keep her duchy with the crown. He revived the Angevin claim to Naples and, through his Visconti grandmother, the claim to Milan. Milan was occupied in October 1499; Lodovico Sforza was sent in irons to die in the dungeon of Loches; Naples was divided with Ferdinand of Aragon by the Treaty of Granada in 1500 and then lost in 1503 to Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba at Cerignola and the Garigliano. France held Milan and lost it twice in his reign.

At home he passed for a moderate. He cut the taille by a third, codified provincial custom-law, set in motion a great purge of the parlements, and was hailed by the Estates of Tours in 1506 as Père du Peuple. His diplomacy was less happy: the League of Cambrai of 1508 against Venice, designed by Pope Julius II, ended with Julius turning the same Holy League of 1511 against France; Gaston de Foix died winning Ravenna for Louis in April 1512, but the army that won could not hold Lombardy without him, and Massimiliano Sforza re-entered Milan that autumn under Swiss escort. Anne of Brittany died in January 1514. In October he married, for diplomatic peace, the eighteen-year-old Mary Tudor, sister of Henry VIII; he died at the Hôtel des Tournelles in Paris on 1 January 1515, exhausted by the bridal nights, and was succeeded by his cousin and son-in-law Francis of Angoulême.
