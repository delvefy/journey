Caracalla

Roman emperor, 188–217 AD

Caracalla—a nickname drawn from a hooded Gallic cloak he liked to wear—was the elder son of Septimius Severus and Julia Domna, and bore the official name Marcus Aurelius Antoninus to bind him to the old dynasty. Raised to co-emperor as a boy, he inherited the throne jointly with his younger brother Geta on their father's death in 211. The brothers detested one another, and within months Caracalla had Geta cut down, by report in the very arms of their mother, and slaughtered his brother's supporters in their thousands.

His reign mixed lasting reform with savagery. In 212 he issued the Antonine Constitution, granting Roman citizenship to almost every free inhabitant of the empire—a measure of vast significance, whether prompted by pride or by the wish to widen the tax rolls. In Rome he raised the colossal baths that still bear his name. But he was also brutal and unstable, debasing the coinage to pay his beloved soldiers and ordering a massacre of the people of Alexandria for mocking him.

He dreamed of rivalling Alexander the Great, whom he idolised and imitated in dress and manner, even raising a phalanx in the old Macedonian style, and he marched east to make war on Parthia. There, near Carrhae in 217, he was murdered by a soldier at the prompting of his praetorian prefect Macrinus, who seized the throne. Caracalla's grief-stricken mother, the formidable Julia Domna, soon starved herself to death.
