Frederick Barbarossa

Holy Roman Emperor, c. 1122–1190

Frederick I of Hohenstaufen, Duke of Swabia, was elected King of the Romans in 1152 and crowned emperor at Rome in 1155. The Italians gave him the name Barbarossa, red-beard, and it was in Italy that the great drama of his reign unfolded. Convinced that the wealthy, self-governing cities of Lombardy had usurped rights that belonged to the empire, he crossed the Alps again and again to reassert what he called the honour of the empire, and in 1162 he razed rebellious Milan to the ground.

His ambitions set him against the papacy. For nearly two decades he backed a succession of antipopes against Pope Alexander III, until the cities banded together in the Lombard League and, at Legnano in 1176, their infantry broke the imperial cavalry and Frederick himself was unhorsed and feared dead. The defeat forced a reckoning: at Venice in 1177 he knelt to Alexander, and at the Peace of Constance in 1183 he conceded the cities their liberties while saving the form of his overlordship.

Closer to home he crushed the overmighty: his cousin Henry the Lion, the greatest of the German princes, was stripped of Saxony and Bavaria in 1180. By marrying his son to the heiress of Sicily he drew the southern kingdom into his house’s orbit. Then, an old man crowned with prestige, he took the cross for the Third Crusade. Leading a vast army overland in 1190, he drowned crossing the Saleph river in Anatolia, and his host melted away in grief and disorder. Germany would not let him die: legend laid him asleep beneath a mountain, red beard growing through the stone table, waiting to wake in the hour of his people’s need.
