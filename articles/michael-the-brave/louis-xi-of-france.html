Louis XI of France

King of France, 1423–1483

Louis XI was born in 1423, the son of Charles VII. Impatient and conspiratorial even as heir, he joined a revolt of the nobles against his father and was eventually driven into exile at the Burgundian court, biding his time until he succeeded to the throne in 1461. The king he became was unlike the chivalric ideal of his age: plainly dressed, devout to the point of superstition, and endlessly calculating.

He preferred the weapons of bribery, espionage, and patient diplomacy to the battlefield, weaving such tangled intrigues that contemporaries dubbed him the "Universal Spider." When the great feudatories combined against him in the League of the Public Weal, he survived by dividing and buying off his enemies, and he bought peace with the invading English of Edward IV at Picquigny in 1475 rather than fight.

His central struggle was with Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, whose vast holdings were the last serious feudal challenge to the crown. Louis encircled his rival with alliances, encouraging the Swiss and the duke’s other foes, and when Charles fell at the Battle of Nancy in 1477 the king moved swiftly to absorb much of the Burgundian inheritance. By taming the nobility, fostering trade, and building up a professional administration, Louis laid the foundations of the centralized French monarchy before his death in 1483.
