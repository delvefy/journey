Georges Danton

French Revolutionary leader, 1759–1794.

Georges Jacques Danton was born in Arcis-sur-Aube, a market town in Champagne, the fourth child of a small-time prosecutor's clerk. Disfigured in childhood by a bull's horn, a smallpox attack, and a fall in a pig-pen, he was educated by the Oratorians at Troyes, came to Paris in 1780, and after eating his way through the bar bought, in 1787, the practice of the King's Council of Estates. The Revolution found him a thirty-year-old advocate with a powerful platform voice, a deeply popular touch, and very little money of his own — deficiencies he would never satisfactorily explain to his later accusers.

From the Cordeliers district where he lived he made himself the orator of the radical Parisian sections. After the king's flight to Varennes he led the Champ-de-Mars petition for the deposition of Louis XVI; after the storming of the Tuileries on 10 August 1792 he was made Minister of Justice and at the height of the September prison massacres pronounced his most famous line, "De l'audace, encore de l'audace, toujours de l'audace," urging citizens to march against the invading Prussian army. As founding member of the Committee of Public Safety in April 1793 he steered the early Terror but pressed, after his second wife's death and his own retreat to Arcis, for moderation, an end to dechristianization, and peace negotiations with Britain — positions that put him on a collision course with Robespierre.

Arrested on the night of 30 March 1794 with his closest friend Camille Desmoulins and the Indulgents, he was denied effective defence at the Revolutionary Tribunal, condemned in three days, and guillotined on the Place de la Révolution on 5 April. He was thirty-four. Tradition has him saying to the executioner Sanson, "Show my head to the people. It is worth it." Robespierre followed him to the same scaffold less than four months later. Marat had been stabbed in his bath the summer before. The Revolution's first generation of orators had cancelled each other out.
