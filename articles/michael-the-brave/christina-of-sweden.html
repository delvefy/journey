Christina of Sweden

Queen of Sweden, 1626–1689 (reigned 1632–1654)

Christina was the only surviving legitimate child of Gustavus Adolphus, and on his death in battle in 1632 she became queen of Sweden at the age of six. A council of regency led by the chancellor Axel Oxenstierna governed until she came of age, while she received an education usually reserved for a prince, learning languages, philosophy, and statecraft. Precociously intellectual and indifferent to the conventional role of a queen, she grew into one of the most learned women of her century and gathered scholars, books, and works of art to her court in Stockholm.

Her hunger for learning led her to summon the philosopher René Descartes to Sweden in 1649 to instruct her; the early-morning lessons in the cold contributed to his death there a few months later. Christina presided over the final phase of the Thirty Years’ War and the Peace of Westphalia, but she refused to marry and increasingly chafed against the duties of rule. In 1654 she stunned Europe by abdicating in favour of her cousin, Charles X Gustav.

Soon afterward she made a still greater break: the daughter of the Protestant champion of the Thirty Years’ War converted to Roman Catholicism and settled in Rome, entering the city in ceremonial triumph. There she became a celebrated and controversial patron of music, theatre, and the arts, founding academies and supporting composers, though her reputation was stained by her ordering the killing of her servant Monaldeschi. Restless to the end, she twice sought other thrones in vain. She was buried in St Peter’s Basilica, one of the few women so honoured.
