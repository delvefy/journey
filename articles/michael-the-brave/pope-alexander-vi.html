Pope Alexander VI

Borgia pope of the Italian Wars, 1431–1503

Rodrigo de Borja y Borja was born at Xàtiva, in the kingdom of Valencia, on 1 January 1431, into a minor Aragonese noble family. His maternal uncle Alonso de Borja, Bishop of Valencia and a power at the Aragonese court of Naples, was elected pope in 1455 as Calixtus III; within twelve months Rodrigo, twenty-five years old, was made cardinal-deacon and the following year vice-chancellor of the Roman church, the second-greatest office of the curia. He held it for thirty-five years under five successive popes, accumulating benefices, building one of the great Roman fortunes, and fathering a family — first by various mistresses, then by Vannozza dei Cattanei — that included Cesare, Juan, Lucrezia, and Jofré Borgia.

On 11 August 1492, after a conclave in which his fellow cardinals received gifts famous even by Roman standards, he was elected pope and took the name Alexander VI. The same year Christopher Columbus reached the Caribbean and Granada fell to Castile. By the bulls Inter caetera of May 1493 and the Treaty of Tordesillas that followed in 1494 he drew the meridian dividing Castile's and Portugal's spheres of discovery west of the Cape Verde Islands — a stroke of the pen that shaped the colonial world for centuries. He confirmed Ferdinand and Isabella as Reyes Católicos, the Catholic Monarchs, in 1496.

His pontificate in Italy was inseparable from his family. He survived the descent of Charles VIII in 1494 and the brief French occupation of Rome; backed the Holy League that drove the French out; presided over the show trial and execution of Girolamo Savonarola in 1498; and then, in alliance with Louis XII, used his son Cesare to carve a hereditary Borgia state out of the Romagna. He patronised Pinturicchio in the Appartamento Borgia of the Vatican palace and admitted Jews expelled from Spain to Rome. He died on 18 August 1503, after a fever that struck him and Cesare together at supper, and was buried in St Peter's, his body so swollen and disfigured that the sextons could barely close the coffin.

The road continues to Ferdinand II of Aragon.
