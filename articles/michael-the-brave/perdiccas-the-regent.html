Perdiccas the Regent

Macedonian general and regent, d. 321 BCE

Perdiccas was the senior Macedonian general who briefly held Alexander the Great's empire together after the conqueror's death. A nobleman of the highland canton of Orestis, he rose into the inner circle of Alexander's officers, and when Alexander lay dying at Babylon in 323 BCE he is said to have handed Perdiccas his signet ring—a gesture taken to mark him as guardian of the realm.

As regent for the two helpless kings, the simple-minded Philip III and the infant Alexander IV, Perdiccas tried to govern the whole of Alexander's conquests. But the other marshals chafed under his authority. His refusal to let Alexander's body be carried home—Ptolemy seized it and bore it off to Egypt instead—became the spark of open war.

Against the coalition that formed around Antipater, Craterus, Antigonus, and Ptolemy, Perdiccas relied on his loyal ally Eumenes to hold Asia Minor while he himself marched on Egypt to crush Ptolemy.

The campaign was a disaster. His army failed to force a crossing of the Nile, losing many men to the river, and in the recriminations that followed his own officers—among them the future king Seleucus—murdered him in his tent in 321 BCE. With his death the unity of Alexander's empire was gone for good, and the long wars of the successors began in earnest.
