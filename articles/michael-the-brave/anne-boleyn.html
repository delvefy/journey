Anne Boleyn

Queen of England, c. 1501–1536

Anne Boleyn was born around 1501 (some sources prefer 1507) at Blickling Hall in Norfolk or Hever Castle in Kent, second daughter of Thomas Boleyn, an ambitious diplomat, and Elizabeth Howard. From 1513 her father’s court connections placed her abroad: first in the household of Margaret of Austria at Mechelen, then from 1515 in the train of Mary Tudor and Queen Claude of France. The seven years she spent at the French court polished her dancing, her French, her musical taste and her religious sympathies—she came home a quiet evangelical, friendly to reform. She returned to the English court late in 1521.

Henry VIII began openly to court her around 1525. Her elder sister Mary had been his mistress; Anne refused to take that road, holding out for the queen’s place. The seven-year diplomatic and theological struggle to annul his marriage to Catherine of Aragon—the “king’s great matter”—was driven by his determination to make her his wife. When Pope Clement VII refused, Henry broke with Rome. He married Anne secretly in January 1533; Thomas Cranmer pronounced the first marriage null in May; she was crowned at Westminster, visibly pregnant, on 1 June. On 7 September she gave birth to a daughter, the future Elizabeth I.

Her reign as queen was three years long and politically barren. She quarrelled with Thomas Cromwell over the use of monastic revenues, alienated her uncle the Duke of Norfolk, and miscarried a male child in January 1536. Cromwell, with the king’s tacit consent, assembled a charge of adultery with five men, including her brother George. Five days of arrests and interrogations, a rapid show trial at the Tower presided over by her uncle, and Cranmer’s annulment of the marriage followed each other within a fortnight. She was beheaded on the green within the Tower by a swordsman summoned from Calais on 19 May 1536, and buried in the chapel of St Peter ad Vincula. Henry was betrothed to Jane Seymour the next day.

The road continues to Catherine of Aragon.
