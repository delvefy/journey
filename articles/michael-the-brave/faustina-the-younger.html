Faustina the Younger

Roman empress, c.130–175

Faustina the Younger was the daughter of the emperor Antoninus Pius and his wife Faustina the Elder, born into the heart of the Antonine dynasty around 130. In 147 she married her kinsman Marcus Aurelius, and when he came to the throne in 161 she became empress at his side. Over the years she bore him a great many children — perhaps thirteen or fourteen, though few survived — among them the future emperor Commodus and the daughter Lucilla, who was given in marriage to Marcus’s co-emperor Lucius Verus.

She was no distant consort. She accompanied Marcus on his long campaigns along the Danube frontier, and for sharing the hardships of the legions she was honoured with the title Mother of the Camps. In the first book of his Meditations, Marcus gave thanks for a wife so affectionate and unaffected.

Yet hostile writers spread darker tales: of infidelities, and even of her complicity in the revolt of Avidius Cassius in 175, said to have been hatched when she feared her husband near death and her son’s succession in doubt. Most of this is probably slander. She died soon after, in 175 or 176, while travelling with Marcus in the East. The grieving emperor had her deified, named a town and a charity for poor girls in her memory, and mourned her as the companion of his reign.
