Eusebius of Caesarea

Bishop of Caesarea and father of church history, c. 260–339 AD

Eusebius was born around 260 in Roman Palestine and became the devoted pupil and friend of the learned presbyter Pamphilus, who maintained at Caesarea the great library founded on the writings of Origen. From this inheritance Eusebius drew the immense erudition that shaped all his work. He lived through the Great Persecution, in which Pamphilus was martyred, and around 314 became bishop of Caesarea.

His enduring monument is the Ecclesiastical History, the first attempt to narrate the Christian Church from the apostles to his own day, preserving countless documents and traditions that would otherwise have perished; for it he is called the father of church history. He also compiled the Chronicle, a synchronised table of world events later translated and extended by Jerome, and wrote the Preparation for the Gospel and the Onomasticon of biblical places.

Theologically he stood in the tradition of Origen and leaned toward a subordinationist view of the Son. At the Council of Nicaea in 325 he was at first under suspicion and put forward a creed of his own, but in the end subscribed, with reservations, to the Nicene definition. He remained wary of its strictest defenders and took part in the synods that deposed Athanasius.

Above all he was the confidant and admirer of Constantine, whose reign he celebrated as the providential triumph of Christianity and whose life he wrote in a famous, idealising panegyric. He died around 339, leaving a body of historical and apologetic writing without which the early Church would be far darker to us.
