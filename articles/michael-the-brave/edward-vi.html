Edward VI

King of England and Ireland, 1537–1553

Edward was born at Hampton Court on 12 October 1537, the only legitimate son of Henry VIII and the king's third wife Jane Seymour, who died of puerperal fever twelve days later. He was raised in his own household at Hatfield and Ashridge under the eye of his nurse Sybil Penn, then trained by an elaborate succession of Cambridge humanists — Richard Cox, John Cheke, and later Roger Ascham — in Latin, Greek, French, and a Protestant theology that he absorbed thoroughly. His relations with his half-sister Mary cooled steadily over the Mass, while with Elizabeth he kept a close intellectual companionship. He succeeded his father on 28 January 1547, nine years old.

For the first two and a half years of the reign his uncle Edward Seymour, Duke of Somerset, governed as Lord Protector, leading an invasion of Scotland that won the field at Pinkie Cleugh in 1547 and prosecuted a forward Reformation: the dissolution of the chantries in 1547, the first Book of Common Prayer of Thomas Cranmer in 1549, the abolition of clerical celibacy. The risings of 1549, Robert Kett's in Norfolk and the Prayer Book Rising in the south-west, broke Somerset. He was overthrown and eventually executed by John Dudley, who as Duke of Northumberland led the council from 1549 to 1553 and pressed the Reformation further: a stricter, second Prayer Book of 1552, the Forty-Two Articles, and the dismantling of altars and images.

Edward kept the political journal that survives in the British Library and increasingly took a hand in council. From early 1553 he was visibly ill with tuberculosis; rather than let Mary undo his religious settlement, he drafted by his own hand the "Devise for the Succession" that disinherited his sisters and named his cousin Lady Jane Grey heir, and forced his judges and councillors to subscribe it on 21 June 1553. He died at Greenwich on 6 July 1553, fifteen years old, and was buried in Henry VII's chapel at Westminster Abbey. Within weeks the country had repudiated his last act of state and accepted his sister Mary.
