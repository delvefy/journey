Charles III of the United Kingdom

King of the United Kingdom, born 1948

Charles Philip Arthur George Mountbatten-Windsor was born at Buckingham Palace in 1948, the elder son of Princess Elizabeth and Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh. He was four when his mother became Queen, and from that moment he was heir apparent. He was the first heir to the British throne educated outside the palace, at Cheam School, then at Gordonstoun in Scotland and one term at Geelong in Australia, before reading archaeology, anthropology, and history at Trinity College, Cambridge. He served as a helicopter pilot in the Royal Navy through the 1970s.

In July 1981 he married Lady Diana Spencer in St Paul's Cathedral, and the births of Prince William in 1982 and Prince Harry in 1984 anchored a marriage that the public would later be told had begun unhappily. The Waleses formally separated in 1992 and divorced in 1996. Diana's death in 1997 left Charles to raise their two teenage sons largely on his own. In 2005 he married his long-standing partner Camilla Parker Bowles, who took the title Duchess of Cornwall and, on his accession, Queen Camilla.

For most of his decades as Prince of Wales he made an unusual public role for himself. The Prince's Trust, founded in 1976, supported many tens of thousands of disadvantaged young people; he became a forceful early advocate for organic farming, sustainable architecture, and the protection of historic British towns, and was prepared to write directly to ministers—the "black spider memos" later disclosed by court order—on issues he believed mattered. His Hay-on-Wye model village at Poundbury became a working test of his planning convictions.

On 8 September 2022, on the death of his mother Queen Elizabeth II at Balmoral, he acceded to the throne as Charles III. He was formally crowned with Queen Camilla at Westminster Abbey on 6 May 2023. In February 2024 his cancer diagnosis was announced, the first such public disclosure for a reigning British monarch. The man whose long, often eccentric career at his mother's side, as the longest-serving royal consort in British history, set the template of the modern British prince was his father—Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh.
