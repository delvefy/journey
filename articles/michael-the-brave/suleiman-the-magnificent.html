Suleiman the Magnificent

Tenth Sultan of the Ottoman Empire, 1494–1566

Suleiman was born on 6 November 1494 at Trabzon on the Black Sea coast, the only surviving son of the prince who would become Selim I. He was educated in the palace school at Istanbul, served as governor of Kaffa in the Crimea and of Manisa in western Anatolia, and on his father's death in September 1520 mounted the throne unopposed. He inherited an empire that Selim had doubled in size by conquering Syria, Egypt, and the holy cities of Mecca and Medina, and he commanded its army and its treasury at the peak of their strength.

His first campaigns were European. In 1521 he took Belgrade, the gate of central Europe; in 1522 he reduced Rhodes and drove the Knights of St John from the Aegean. At Mohács on 29 August 1526 his guns broke the Hungarian army in under two hours and killed King Louis II, opening Hungary to Ottoman occupation and Habsburg counter-claim. He laid siege to Vienna in 1529 and again threatened it in 1532, and through admiral Hayreddin Barbarossa carried Ottoman naval power across the western Mediterranean, defeating a Holy League fleet at Preveza in 1538. To the east he campaigned three times against the Safavid shah Tahmasp, taking Tabriz in 1534 and securing Baghdad and Iraq under the Peace of Amasya in 1555.

Within the empire he was Kanuni, the lawgiver: he codified secular and fiscal law, reorganised the timar system, and patronised the architect Sinan, whose Süleymaniye mosque in Istanbul and Selimiye in Edirne crown his reign. His wife Hürrem (Roxelana), the first slave-concubine raised to legal marriage, reshaped palace politics; their son Selim II succeeded him after the executions of the elder princes Mustafa and Bayezid. Suleiman died in his tent on 6 September 1566 before the walls of Szigetvár in Hungary, his death concealed until the fortress fell.
