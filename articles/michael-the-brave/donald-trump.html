Donald Trump

b. 1946, forty-fifth and forty-seventh President of the United States

Donald John Trump was born in the borough of Queens, New York, the fourth child of Fred Trump, a real-estate developer of German descent who had built outer-borough rental housing through the New Deal mortgage programmes, and Mary MacLeod, a Scottish immigrant. He attended the New York Military Academy and the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, took over his father's company in 1971, and renamed it the Trump Organization. The lawyer and McCarthy-era operator Roy Cohn became his early mentor and taught him the public combativeness he carried for life.

From the 1980s he built a series of Manhattan towers and Atlantic City casinos, four of which went through bankruptcy reorganizations between 1991 and 2009. His name became a luxury brand licensed onto golf courses, neckties and bottled water, and from 2004 the network reality series The Apprentice broadcast a constructed image of the dealmaking executive to a national audience. In 2015 he descended a Trump Tower escalator to announce a candidacy for the Republican presidential nomination, attacking Mexican immigrants and the Republican establishment with equal ferocity.

He defeated sixteen primary opponents and, in November 2016, the Democratic nominee Hillary Clinton, despite losing the popular vote by nearly three million ballots. His first term saw a large corporate tax cut, the appointment of three Supreme Court justices, a trade war with Xi Jinping's China, a withdrawal from the Paris and Iran agreements, two summits with Kim Jong-un, a Helsinki meeting with Vladimir Putin, two impeachments in the House, and acquittal both times in the Senate. The COVID-19 pandemic ended his bid for re-election in November 2020 against Joe Biden.

He refused to concede, and on 6 January 2021 his supporters stormed the Capitol. Four federal and state indictments followed, on charges including election interference and the retention of classified documents, and in May 2024 a New York jury convicted him of falsifying business records, the first criminal conviction of an American president. He survived an assassination attempt at a rally in Butler, Pennsylvania, in July 2024, defeated Kamala Harris that November, and was inaugurated for a second, non-consecutive term in January 2025.
