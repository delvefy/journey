James IV of Scotland

King of Scots, 1473–1513

James IV came to the throne in 1488 at the age of fifteen, after rebel nobles in whose camp he stood defeated and killed his father, James III, at Sauchieburn. Stricken with remorse, he is said to have worn an iron chain about his waist ever after in penance. He grew into the ablest of the Stewart kings, energetic, cultured, and curious about everything from medicine to alchemy.

His reign was a golden age for Scotland. He brought the fractious nobility and the Highlands and Isles under firmer royal control, patronised poets such as William Dunbar, encouraged learning and the new art of printing, and built a powerful navy crowned by the great warship Michael. A true Renaissance prince, he made his court a centre of splendour and ambition.

In 1503 he married Margaret Tudor, daughter of Henry VII of England, in a match celebrated as the union of the Thistle and the Rose and sealed by a treaty of perpetual peace; through it the Stuarts would one day inherit the English crown. Yet the old alliance with France proved stronger than the new bond with England. When Henry VIII invaded France, James led a great army across the border in support of his ally, only to be cut down with much of the Scottish nobility at the disastrous Battle of Flodden in 1513, the last king in the British Isles to die in battle.

The road continues to Margaret Tudor.
