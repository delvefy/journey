Gaiseric

King of the Vandals, c.389–477

Gaiseric became king of the Vandals and Alans in 428 and ruled them for nearly half a century, the shrewdest and most dangerous of the barbarian leaders who tore apart the Western Roman Empire. Lamed by a fall in his youth but tireless in ambition, he led his whole people — tens of thousands of warriors and their families — across the Strait of Gibraltar in 429 to invade the rich Roman provinces of North Africa. As his army pressed eastward it besieged the city of Hippo, within whose walls the aged Augustine lay dying.

In 439 Gaiseric seized Carthage and made it his capital, and from its harbours he built a fleet that turned the western Mediterranean into a Vandal sea. He raided Sicily and the Italian coast at will, and in 455 he descended upon Rome itself, sacking the city over fourteen days; only the intercession of Pope Leo I, by tradition, spared its people from massacre. An ardent Arian, he persecuted the Catholic Christians of his African kingdom throughout his reign.

No power could dislodge him. He shattered a vast naval expedition sent against him from Constantinople in 468, and he died in his bed in 477, master still of his realm, having even, it was said, helped stir Attila against the empire’s other enemies. The kingdom he founded outlasted him by two generations, until the armies of Justinian under Belisarius overthrew his grandson and restored Africa to Roman rule. From his people’s name later ages drew the word vandalism.
