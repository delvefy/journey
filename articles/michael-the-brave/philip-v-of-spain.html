Philip V of Spain

First Bourbon king of Spain, 1683–1746

Philip V was the first of the Bourbon kings of Spain and the founder of the dynasty that rules there still. Born at Versailles in 1683 as the Duke of Anjou, he was a grandson of Louis XIV. When the childless Charles II, last of the Spanish Habsburgs, named him heir to the whole Spanish inheritance, his accession in 1700 raised the spectre of France and Spain united under one house.

The great powers would not allow it, and the result was the War of the Spanish Succession, which consumed Europe for more than a decade. A grand alliance fought to place a Habsburg archduke on the Spanish throne, and Philip's armies suffered crushing defeats at the hands of allied commanders. Yet he kept the loyalty of most of Spain, and the Peace of Utrecht in 1713 confirmed him as king on condition that he renounce any claim to France. Spain surrendered its possessions in the Netherlands and Italy, and Gibraltar to Britain.

At home Philip remade his kingdom on the French model, sweeping away the old liberties of Aragon and Catalonia and centralizing the state. Abroad, guided by his forceful second wife Elisabeth Farnese, he pursued thrones in Italy for their sons and bound Spain to Bourbon France.

His reign was shadowed by a deep and recurring melancholy. He abdicated in 1724 in favour of a son who died within months, then resumed the crown against his will, and in his last years found relief only in the nightly singing of Farinelli. He died in 1746. The most formidable of the allied generals who had fought to deny him his throne was the Duke of Marlborough.
