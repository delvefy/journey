Rudolf II, Holy Roman Emperor

Habsburg emperor, 1552–1612

Rudolf was born in Vienna in 1552, the eldest son of the future emperor Maximilian II and Maria of Spain. From the age of eleven he was raised at the court of his maternal uncle Philip II in Madrid, where seven years among Spanish grandees and Jesuit tutors gave him the rigid Catholicism and ceremonial reserve that would mark him for the rest of his life. He returned to the Empire in 1571, was crowned King of Hungary in 1572, King of Bohemia in 1575, and on his father’s death in 1576 succeeded as Holy Roman Emperor.

In 1583 he moved his court from Vienna to Prague, and there made of the Hradčany castle one of the strangest capitals in Europe. Astronomers, alchemists, painters and clockmakers filled its halls. Tycho Brahe brought his Danish observatory in 1599; on his death Johannes Kepler inherited the post and the data that would yield the laws of planetary motion. Giuseppe Arcimboldo painted the emperor as a heap of fruit. Arcane manuscripts, automata, narwhal horns and the famous Kunstkammer accumulated around a sovereign whose interest in governing the Empire was, by all accounts, intermittent.

His reign coincided with two long wars he could not finish. The Long Turkish War on the Hungarian frontier dragged on from 1593 to 1606 without decisive result, and it was during its desperate phase that the Wallachian voivode Michael the Brave fought and died in the Habsburg interest. At home, growing tensions between Catholics and Protestants, and Rudolf’s erratic moods, eroded his authority. In 1606 his Habsburg relatives, led by his younger brother Matthias, forced him to cede Hungary, Austria and Moravia. The Letter of Majesty of 1609, granting religious freedom in Bohemia, was his last meaningful act. Stripped of Bohemia too in 1611, he died the following year in Prague.
