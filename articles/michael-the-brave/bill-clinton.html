Bill Clinton

b. 1946, forty-second President of the United States

William Jefferson Blythe III was born in Hope, Arkansas, three months after his father had been killed in a road accident on the way to fetch his pregnant wife. He took the surname of his stepfather Roger Clinton, was raised by his grandparents and then in Hot Springs, won a place at Georgetown School of Foreign Service and a Rhodes Scholarship to University College, Oxford. In July 1963 he attended the American Legion Boys Nation in Washington as a delegate from Arkansas and shook hands in the Rose Garden with President John F. Kennedy; the snapshot fixed his ambition for life.

He read law at Yale, met Hillary Rodham there, and married her in 1975. Two years later he became attorney general of Arkansas and in 1978 the youngest governor in the country. Defeated for re-election in 1980, he learned what he later called the lesson of moderating his pace, and from 1983 served five further terms. In 1992 he ran a centrist Democratic campaign against George H.W. Bush in a three-way contest with Ross Perot, and won with 43 percent of the vote.

His presidency presided over the longest peacetime expansion in American history, ended the federal deficit for the first time in thirty years, signed the North American Free Trade Agreement and the welfare reform of 1996, and pressed expansion of NATO into central Europe over Russian objections. At Camp David in 1993 he stood behind the Oslo handshake of Yitzhak Rabin and Yasser Arafat. He intervened belatedly to halt the Bosnian war and led the Kosovo air campaign of 1999. He was impeached by the House in December 1998 over the Monica Lewinsky affair and acquitted by the Senate the following February.

He left office in January 2001 with high approval and threw himself into the Clinton Foundation, the Clinton Global Initiative, and a series of joint relief missions with George H.W. Bush after the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami and the 2010 Haiti earthquake. He travelled often to Mandela's South Africa as a personal friend, watched his wife Hillary serve as Secretary of State under Barack Obama and run twice for president, and gave the keynote speech for Donald Trump's eventual 2016 opponent. He suffered quadruple bypass surgery in 2004 and stents in 2010 but remains in active public life.
