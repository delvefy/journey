Bernardo O'Higgins

Chilean independence leader and Supreme Director, 1778–1842.

Born in the central Chilean town of Chillán, the natural son of the Spanish-Irish soldier Ambrosio O'Higgins — later viceroy of Peru — and the criolla Isabel Riquelme, Bernardo was sent at fifteen to Lima and from there to England, where in the schools of Richmond he learned mathematics, history, and the harpsichord. In London in 1798 he fell in with the Venezuelan exile Francisco de Miranda and entered the Logia Lautaro, the masonic conspiracy whose graduates — Miranda, San Martín, Bolívar, O'Higgins — would within a generation dismantle the Spanish empire in South America.

He returned home in 1802, inherited his father's estate near Los Ángeles in 1801, and after the Chilean junta of 1810 began to organize and finance the patriot militia. The first phase of independence, the Patria Vieja, collapsed at the Battle of Rancagua in October 1814; O'Higgins escaped over the cordillera with the remnants of the army into Argentina, where he placed himself under José de San Martín's command at Mendoza. He commanded the second division at the Battle of Chacabuco in February 1817, took Santiago, and was acclaimed Supreme Director of Chile; the victory at Maipú on 5 April 1818 finished the royalist field army and made independence permanent.

As head of state from 1817 to 1823 he created the Chilean navy under Lord Cochrane, sent it north to escort San Martín's expedition to Peru, abolished entailed estates and noble titles, founded the National Institute, the National Library, and the cemetery of Santiago, and laid out the Alameda. The aristocratic resistance to those liberal reforms forced his abdication in January 1823, and he sailed for Peru, where Bolívar granted him an estate at Montálvan. He lived in exile twenty years, watched Chilean politics from a distance, drafted unsent memoirs, and died of heart failure in Lima in 1842. His remains were repatriated in 1869 to Santiago, where they lie in the Altar de la Patria on the Alameda that bears his name.
