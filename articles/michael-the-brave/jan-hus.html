Jan Hus

Bohemian priest, reformer, and martyr, c.1372–1415

Hus was born about 1372 in Husinec, in the forested south of Bohemia, the son of poor peasants. He worked his way through Charles University at Prague, taking his BA in 1393 and his MA in 1396, and entered the philosophy faculty as a regent master. He was ordained priest in 1400 and became dean of the faculty in 1401 and rector of the university in 1402. The fashionable theology in Prague's Czech-speaking masters was the writings of the Oxford theologian John Wycliffe, smuggled into Bohemia partly through the marriage of Anne of Bohemia to Richard II of England in 1382. Hus copied, translated, and defended Wycliffe's books on church and Eucharist, while distancing himself from the more radical realist metaphysics.

From 1402 he preached at the Bethlehem Chapel in Prague's Old Town, a foundation explicitly licensed for preaching in the vernacular. Twice a day, in Czech, before audiences of citizens and the royal court of Wenceslaus IV, he denounced simony, the sale of indulgences, clerical concubinage, and the wealth of the prelates. In 1409 he and the Czech masters secured, through the Decree of Kutná Hora, control of Charles University from the German masters. But in 1410 Archbishop Zbyněk Zajíc burned Wycliffe's books and excommunicated him. In 1412 his open preaching against the indulgence of Pope John XXIII forced him to leave Prague for the countryside, where he wrote De ecclesia, his treatise that defines the true church as the body of the predestined and rejects papal supremacy.

Summoned in 1414 to the Council of Constance under an imperial safe-conduct from Sigismund, he travelled in good faith. Within weeks he was arrested. His trial, which began in earnest in June 1415, demanded an unqualified retraction of articles drawn from his books; he refused. He was condemned as a relapsed heretic and burned at the stake outside the city walls on 6 July 1415, his ashes thrown into the Rhine. The Czech kingdom rose; the Hussite Wars that followed kept Sigismund out of Bohemia for fifteen years and prefigured the Reformation by a century.
