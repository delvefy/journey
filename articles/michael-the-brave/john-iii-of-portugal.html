John III of Portugal

King of Portugal, “the Pious”, 1502–1557

John III, born in 1502, was the son of Manuel I and inherited in 1521 a crown at the summit of its fortunes. Portugal under his rule commanded the sea-lanes of the Indian Ocean through a chain of fortresses from Hormuz to Malacca, controlled the European spice trade, and pushed its ships on to China and Japan. It was John who appointed the aging Vasco da Gama viceroy of India, and John who turned his attention across the Atlantic, dividing Brazil into hereditary captaincies and then planting a royal governor at Salvador to make the colony a true dominion of the crown.

He bound his house tightly to the Habsburgs. He married Catherine of Austria, sister of the Emperor Charles V, while his own sister Isabella became Charles’s empress, a double alliance that placed Portugal beside the greatest power in Christendom. A devout and earnest king, he requested missionaries from the new Society of Jesus and dispatched Francis Xavier to the East, and he brought the Inquisition into Portugal in the 1530s while generously endowing and reforming the University of Coimbra.

For all the splendour, the reign darkened toward its close. The costs of empire strained the treasury, and a long series of family deaths carried off almost all his many children before him. When John III died in 1557 the throne passed to a small grandson, Sebastian, on whose shoulders the future of the dynasty now precariously rested.
