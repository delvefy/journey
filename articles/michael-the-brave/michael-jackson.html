Michael Jackson

American singer and dancer, 1958–2009.

Born the seventh of nine children to a steelworker father and Jehovah's Witness mother in Gary, Indiana, Michael Joseph Jackson sang from infancy at his mother's side and from five was drilled by his father Joseph into the family vocal group with his older brothers. The Jackson 5 signed to Berry Gordy's Motown in 1968, and their first four singles — "I Want You Back," "ABC," "The Love You Save," "I'll Be There" — all reached number one. He was the ten-year-old falsetto lead, already a polished stage performer, already at the centre of a family act he would never escape.

His solo career, begun while still with the brothers, accelerated in 1979 with Off the Wall, produced by Quincy Jones, and exploded in 1982 with Thriller, which sold more than sixty million copies, broke the colour line on MTV with the videos for "Billie Jean," "Beat It" (featuring an Eddie Van Halen solo), and "Thriller" itself, and made him the most famous entertainer alive. Reagan welcomed him to the White House; he debuted the moonwalk on the 1983 Motown 25 television special; the follow-ups Bad, Dangerous, and HIStory sustained him through the 1980s and 1990s. In 1985 he outbid Paul McCartney for the ATV catalogue containing most of the Lennon-McCartney songs.

From the early 1990s his face altered visibly through surgery and his skin lightened; in 1993 the first allegation of child sexual abuse was settled out of court, and a second criminal trial in 2005 ended in acquittal. He married Elvis Presley's daughter Lisa Marie in 1994 and divorced her in 1996, then married and divorced Debbie Rowe, the mother of his two eldest children. Burdened with debt and reclusive at his Neverland Ranch, he agreed in 2009 to a fifty-night London comeback residency and died in Los Angeles in the rehearsal phase of an overdose of the anaesthetic propofol, administered by his personal physician.

The road continues to Madonna.
