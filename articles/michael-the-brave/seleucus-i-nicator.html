Seleucus I Nicator

Macedonian general and founder of the Seleucid Empire, c. 358–281 BCE

Seleucus was a Macedonian nobleman who served as a senior officer under Alexander the Great, commanding the elite infantry guard during the conquest of the Persian Empire. When Alexander died in 323 BCE without a settled heir, his generals carved up the vast empire among themselves, and in the arrangements that followed Seleucus was assigned the great satrapy of Babylon.

Driven out by his rival Antigonus, he took refuge with Ptolemy in Egypt, then fought his way back to Babylon in 312 BCE—a return so decisive that it marked the beginning of the Seleucid era of dating. From this base he built one of the largest empires of the age, reaching from the coast of Asia Minor across Mesopotamia and Persia toward India.

In the east he clashed with Chandragupta Maurya and, unable to prevail, ceded the Indian provinces in exchange for five hundred war elephants. Those elephants proved decisive in 301 BCE at the Battle of Ipsus, where Seleucus and his allies destroyed Antigonus, the most powerful of the successors. He founded great cities to anchor his realm, above all Antioch and Seleucia, which became flourishing centres of Greek civilisation in the East.

In 281 BCE he defeated the last of his rivals, Lysimachus, at Corupedium, leaving him master of nearly all of Alexander's former empire. Crossing into Europe to claim Macedonia itself, he was murdered by Ptolemy Keraunos, a man he had sheltered. His son Antiochus succeeded him, and the dynasty he founded ruled the Hellenistic East for more than two centuries.
