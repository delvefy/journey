Afonso de Albuquerque

Second Governor of Portuguese India, 1453–1515

Afonso de Albuquerque was born in 1453 at Alhandra, just north of Lisbon, the second son of Gonçalo de Albuquerque, lord of Vila Verde dos Francos, and great-grandson, through illegitimate lines, of King Diniz. Raised as a page at the court of Afonso V, he served in North Africa from his early twenties, fought at Asilah and Tangier in 1471, and crossed Castile in 1476 with the army sent against the Catholic Monarchs. Under John II he was made master of the horse; under Manuel I he sailed for India in 1503 on his first voyage with his cousin Francisco. He returned a captain marked by an icy intelligence and a violent temper.

His second voyage, in 1506, took him to East Africa under the command of Tristão da Cunha. The orders he carried, sealed against the day of his promotion, named him Captain-General of the Indian Ocean from 1508. He spent the next year cruising the Arabian Sea and, on his own authority, seizing the strategic island of Hormuz at the mouth of the Persian Gulf. In November 1509 he formally succeeded Francisco de Almeida as second Governor of the Estado da Índia. From that post he built, in five years, the Portuguese empire in Asia.

In February and November 1510 he twice attacked and finally took Goa from the Adil Shahi sultan, making it the capital of Portuguese India and the seat of its archbishopric. In July 1511 he stormed Malacca and broke the eastern terminus of the Muslim spice trade; from there his captains opened the way to the Moluccas and to China. In 1513 he failed before Aden but cruised the Red Sea, and in 1515 he returned to Hormuz to consolidate it as a vassal kingdom, exchanging embassies and rich gifts with Shah Ismail I of Persia. Sailing back to Goa in December 1515 he learned that the king, persuaded by his enemies at court, had recalled him. He died on the deck of his ship within sight of Goa harbour on 16 December 1515, dictating his last bitter letter to Manuel I.

The road continues to Shah Ismail I.
