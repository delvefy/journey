Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick

English magnate, "the Kingmaker", 1428–1471

Richard Neville became, through inheritance and a great marriage, the wealthiest and most powerful nobleman in England, commanding a vast network of estates, retainers, and castles. When the dynastic quarrel known as the Wars of the Roses broke into open conflict, he threw the weight of his house behind the Yorkist cause against the Lancastrian king Henry VI.

It was Warwick’s armies, alliances, and money that placed the young Edward IV on the throne in 1461, an achievement that won him his enduring nickname, the Kingmaker. For a few years he governed almost as the power behind the crown. But Edward’s secret marriage to Elizabeth Woodville, and the favour shown to her grasping relatives, cut Warwick out of the influence he believed his due, and the two men fell into bitter enmity.

Driven at last into open revolt, Warwick fled to France, where the cunning Louis XI brokered an extraordinary reconciliation with his former foe, the Lancastrian queen Margaret of Anjou. Returning at the head of an invasion, he drove out Edward IV and briefly restored the hapless Henry VI in the episode known as the Readeption of 1470. The triumph was short-lived: Edward came back, and Warwick was killed at the Battle of Barnet in 1471, the very model of the overmighty subject undone by his own ambition.

The road continues to Louis XI of France.
