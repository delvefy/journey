Ahmose I

Pharaoh of Egypt, founder of the New Kingdom, r. c. 1550–1525 BC

Ahmose I was the founder of Egypt’s Eighteenth Dynasty and the man who began the age of empire known as the New Kingdom. He belonged to a line of Theban princes — the son of Seqenenre Tao and brother of Kamose — who had taken up arms against the Hyksos, the Asiatic rulers who for over a century had governed Lower Egypt from their stronghold at Avaris. Coming to the throne while still young, Ahmose carried that war to its conclusion.

He stormed Avaris and drove the Hyksos out of Egypt, pursuing them into the southern Levant, and so reunited the country under a single native crown for the first time in generations. He went on to restore Egyptian power in Nubia and to reorganize the kingdom’s administration, founding a dynasty that would include Hatshepsut, Thutmose III and the great conquerors who carried Egypt’s borders to the Euphrates. His queen and sister Ahmose-Nefertari was honoured for centuries afterward as a goddess.

The memory of his great achievement — the expulsion of a Semitic people from Egypt — passed down through the Egyptian priest-historian Manetho, and from him to the Jewish historian Josephus, who identified those expelled Hyksos with the ancestors of the Israelites. That identification has ever since haunted debate over whether a historical memory lies behind the biblical Exodus of Moses. Ahmose died about 1525 BC, the founder of Egypt’s most glorious age.
