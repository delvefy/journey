Claudius

Fourth Roman emperor, 10 BC–AD 54

Claudius was born in 10 BC, a son of Drusus and Antonia and the younger brother of the celebrated Germanicus. A limp, a stammer, and other afflictions led his family to treat him as a simpleton and keep him from public life—a judgement that, ironically, preserved him through the murderous reigns of Tiberius and his nephew Caligula, since no one thought him worth killing. He turned instead to scholarship, writing histories of the Etruscans and Carthaginians that have not survived.

When Caligula was assassinated in AD 41, the Praetorian Guard discovered Claudius hiding in the palace and proclaimed him emperor, the first to owe his throne to the army. Against all expectation he ruled ably for thirteen years. He reformed and expanded the imperial administration, leaning on a corps of powerful freedmen secretaries, built the great harbour at Ostia and new aqueducts, drained the Fucine Lake, extended Roman citizenship, and admitted leading Gauls to the Senate.

His most celebrated achievement was the conquest of Britain in AD 43, which he joined in person and which added a rich new province to the empire and delivered the captured British king Caratacus to Rome. His private life was less fortunate. His wife Messalina was executed in 48 for conspiracy, and he then married his niece Agrippina the Younger, adopting her son Nero ahead of his own boy Britannicus. In 54 Claudius died—by widespread belief poisoned by Agrippina—and was deified, leaving the empire to the teenaged Nero.
