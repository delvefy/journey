Philip II of Spain

King of Spain, Portugal, Naples, and the Indies, 1527–1598

Philip was born at Valladolid on 21 May 1527, the only surviving son of the Emperor Charles V and Isabella of Portugal. He was raised at the Spanish court under his mother's eye until her death in 1539 and then trained for government by Juan de Zúñiga and by his father's letters. At sixteen he was made regent of Spain. He was married four times: to Maria Manuela of Portugal in 1543, to Mary I of England in 1554, to Elisabeth of Valois in 1559, and to his niece Anna of Austria in 1570. From 1556, when Charles V abdicated, he ruled Castile, Aragon, the Italian states, the Netherlands, and the empire in the Indies; from 1580, after the death of his nephew Sebastian and the cardinal-king Henry, he added Portugal and its overseas possessions.

His was an empire on which the sun never set, and the reign was an unbroken effort to govern it from a single desk. He built the Escorial near Madrid, settled the court there in 1561, and worked at the dispatches that arrived from every continent. In 1571 the Holy League fleet under his half-brother Don Juan of Austria broke the Ottoman navy at Lepanto. In the Netherlands the imposition of the Inquisition and new bishoprics provoked from 1568 the Dutch revolt under William the Silent; the war ran on until after Philip's death and severed the seven northern provinces from Spain. The Armada he sent against Elizabeth of England in 1588, with the express object of putting a Catholic on her throne, was shattered by storm and the English fleet.

In Spain itself he crushed the Morisco rising of the Alpujarras in 1568–71, deported the survivors, and put down the rebellion of his secretary Antonio Pérez in Aragon. He patronised Titian, El Greco, and Antonio Moro, founded the Escorial library, and built up the cult of his great-grandparents the Catholic Monarchs. Suffering for years from gout, he died of cancer at the Escorial on 13 September 1598, having dictated, the day before, the regulations for his funeral. His son succeeded him as Philip III.
