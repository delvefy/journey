Maximinus Thrax

Roman emperor, r. 235–238

Gaius Julius Verus Maximinus, called Thrax for his Thracian origin, was born about 173 to humble provincial stock and rose entirely through the army, a soldier of legendary size and strength who had begun his career, it was said, in the reign of Septimius Severus. In 235 the legions on the Rhine mutinied against the young Alexander Severus, cut down the last of the Severan emperors together with his mother, and hailed the rough soldier Maximinus in his place.

He was the first of the so-called barracks emperors — a man raised by the troops rather than the Senate, who reportedly never once entered Rome during his reign. He fought hard and successfully against the Germans and the Danube tribes, but his heavy taxation to pay for war turned the propertied classes against him.

In 238 the storm broke. Africa revolted and proclaimed the elderly Gordian and his son; the Senate, hating Maximinus, backed the rebels and then, when they fell, raised emperors of its own. Maximinus marched on Italy, but his army was held up before the walls of Aquileia, and as hunger and frustration mounted his own soldiers turned on him and killed him. His violent rise and fall are usually taken to mark the beginning of the Crisis of the Third Century, the half-century of near-anarchy from which the empire only barely recovered.
