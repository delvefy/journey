Pelopidas

Theban general and statesman, c. 410–364 BCE

Pelopidas was the Theban general and statesman who, together with his friend Epaminondas, raised Thebes to the leadership of Greece. Born to a wealthy family around 410 BCE, he distinguished himself first as a patriot in exile: in 379 he led a daring band of conspirators who slipped back into Spartan-occupied Thebes in disguise, killed the puppet rulers, and freed the city.

He became the most trusted soldier of the Theban revival and the commander of its elite corps, the Sacred Band, a body of three hundred picked men. At Tegyra in 375 he won a startling victory over a far larger Spartan force, foreshadowing the greater triumph to come, and he fought at the decisive battle of Leuctra in 371 at Epaminondas's side.

Beyond the battlefield he served Thebes as a diplomat, journeying to the Persian court to win recognition of Theban primacy and intervening in the affairs of Thessaly and Macedon—where his dealings brought the young Philip, the future Philip II, to Thebes as a hostage. In Thessaly he made himself the enemy of the cruel tyrant Alexander of Pherae, who once held him prisoner.

In 364 BCE, leading an army against that tyrant at Cynoscephalae, Pelopidas won the field but was killed in the fighting, having charged recklessly to reach his foe. Plutarch paired his life with that of the Roman Marcellus as a study in valor.
