Mary of Burgundy

Duchess of Burgundy, 1457–1482

Mary was born in 1457, the only child of Charles the Bold. When her father was killed at Nancy in 1477 she became, at nineteen, the richest heiress in Europe and sole ruler of the Burgundian lands of the Low Countries. At once she faced mortal danger: the French king Louis XI invaded to swallow the inheritance and schemed to bind her in marriage to the French dauphin, while her own subjects pressed her for concessions.

To secure her cities’ loyalty she granted the Great Privilege, restoring liberties to the Estates, and to defend her realm she chose a husband from outside France, marrying Maximilian of Habsburg, the future Holy Roman Emperor, in 1477. Maximilian took up arms in her cause and checked French expansion, and the union, by repute a genuine love match, proved one of the most fateful in European history.

By joining the wealthy Burgundian inheritance to the rising House of Habsburg, Mary’s marriage set in motion the dynasty’s rise to continental dominance; their grandson would rule as the Emperor Charles V over lands on which the sun never set. Mary herself did not live to see it. In 1482, still in her mid-twenties, she suffered a fall from her horse while hawking and died of her injuries, leaving two young children, Philip the Handsome and Margaret of Austria.
