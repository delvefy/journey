Domenico Scarlatti

Composer of the keyboard sonata, 1685–1757

Domenico Scarlatti was a Neapolitan composer who, working far from the centres of European fashion, transformed the keyboard sonata into something wholly new. Born in Naples in 1685—the same year as Bach and Handel—he was the sixth child of the great opera composer Alessandro Scarlatti, in whose shadow he laboured for years before finding his own path.

As a young man in Rome he served the leading patrons of the city and took part in a celebrated friendly contest with Handel, the two judged equal at the harpsichord while Handel was thought the finer organist. The episode sealed a lifelong mutual respect. For a time Scarlatti directed music at St Peter's, but his true career began when he left Italy.

In 1719 he travelled to Lisbon as music master to the Portuguese royal family, above all to the gifted princess Maria Barbara. When she married into the Spanish royal house in 1729, he followed her to Spain and remained there, in Seville and Madrid, for the rest of his life. In the service of his royal pupil, now queen, he composed the bulk of the works on which his fame rests.

These are the more than five hundred single-movement sonatas, written for the harpsichord, that pour out an inexhaustible variety of invention: daring harmonies, crossed hands, hammered repeated notes, and the strummed guitars and clattering dance rhythms of his adopted Spain. Unknown to most of Europe in his lifetime, they became a cornerstone of the keyboard repertoire. At the Madrid court his closest colleague was the most famous singer of the age, the castrato Farinelli.
