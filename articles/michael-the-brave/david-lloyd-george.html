David Lloyd George

1863–1945, "the man who won the war," British Prime Minister 1916–1922

David Lloyd George was born in Manchester to Welsh-speaking parents, lost his father at one year old, and was raised by his uncle Richard Lloyd, a master cobbler and Baptist lay preacher in Llanystumdwy, Caernarvonshire. He qualified as a solicitor in 1884 and won the Caernarvon Boroughs seat for the Liberals in 1890, taking it to Westminster as a fluent Welsh-language orator with a grudge against the established Church and the landlord interest. He opposed the Boer War from the floor and barely escaped a Birmingham mob in 1901.

As Chancellor of the Exchequer from 1908 he introduced old-age pensions, sickness and unemployment insurance, and the "People's Budget" of 1909, whose rejection by the House of Lords led to the Parliament Act of 1911 that broke the upper chamber's veto. He moved to the Ministry of Munitions in 1915 to address the shell shortage, replaced Kitchener at the War Office, and in December 1916 displaced Herbert Asquith as prime minister of a coalition government.

He centralised war direction in a small cabinet of five, imposed convoys on the Admiralty over the objections of its admirals and so beat the U-boat campaign, oversaw the deployment of conscript armies on the Western Front, and accepted unified command under Foch in March 1918. At the Paris Peace Conference of 1919 he occupied the middle ground between Wilson's idealism and Clemenceau's vengeance, and at Versailles secured a settlement he was first to call a "Carthaginian peace." His coalition fractured over the Chanak crisis of 1922 and the Conservatives walked out; the Liberals never again formed a government. He retired to his books and his constituency, was ennobled as Earl Lloyd-George of Dwyfor in his last months, and died at Llanystumdwy in March 1945.
