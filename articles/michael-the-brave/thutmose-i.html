Thutmose I

Pharaoh of Egypt, r. c. 1506–1493 BC

Thutmose I, third pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty, was a soldier of apparently non-royal birth who came to the throne after Amenhotep I, his claim strengthened by marriage to a princess of the royal house. Though he reigned little more than a decade, he set Egypt firmly on the path of empire and founded the line of warrior-kings who would carry it to its height.

He was above all a conqueror. Marching south, he pushed deep into Nubia and broke its resistance, extending Egyptian control far up the Nile; turning north, he led his armies through the Levant into Syria and reached the river Euphrates, where he set up a boundary marker at the very edge of the world he knew — a feat no pharaoh had accomplished before. The plunder and tribute of these campaigns enriched the temples, and he enlarged the great sanctuary of Amun at Karnak with pylons and obelisks.

Thutmose I is also remembered as the first king known to have been buried in the Valley of the Kings, the hidden royal cemetery that would serve Egypt’s rulers for centuries. His daughter Hatshepsut and his grandson Thutmose III would each, in turn, wear the double crown and surpass even his renown, but it was Thutmose I who first made the Thutmosid name a byword for conquest. He died about 1493 BC.
