Pescennius Niger

Roman usurper, c.135–194

Gaius Pescennius Niger was a Roman senator and soldier whom the emperor Commodus had appointed governor of Syria. When Commodus was murdered, the upright Pertinax raised and then himself struck down, and the throne shamefully auctioned to Didius Julianus in 193, the legions of the East would have none of it. At Antioch they hailed their popular governor Niger as emperor — and in Rome too the crowds had cried out his name — making him one of the three great claimants in the Year of the Five Emperors.

But he was slow to move, while his rival Septimius Severus was swift. Severus seized Italy, secured his rear by naming the third claimant, Clodius Albinus, as his Caesar, and then turned the full weight of the Danube armies against the East. In a series of battles Niger’s forces were driven back, and at Issus in Cilicia — the same ground where Alexander had once broken the Persians — he was decisively defeated in 194.

Niger fled toward the Parthian frontier, hoping to find refuge, but he was overtaken and killed, and his severed head was carried back as proof of Severus’s victory. With Niger destroyed, only Albinus remained between Severus and sole mastery of the Roman world. Niger is remembered as the East’s candidate in the scramble of 193, undone by hesitation and by a faster, harder rival.
