Barack Obama

b. 1961, forty-fourth President of the United States

Barack Hussein Obama II was born in Honolulu in August 1961, the son of an American mother from Kansas and a Kenyan father who left the family when Barack was two. He spent his early school years in Indonesia with his mother and her second husband before returning to Honolulu to live with his maternal grandparents and attend the Punahou School. His first political act was a speech in 1981 at Occidental College in Los Angeles in support of South African divestment, his earliest tribute to the imprisoned Nelson Mandela.

He transferred to Columbia, took a degree in political science, worked three years as a community organizer in Chicago's South Side, and entered Harvard Law School, where he was elected the first black president of the Harvard Law Review in 1990. He returned to Chicago, taught constitutional law at the University of Chicago, married Michelle Robinson in 1992, served three terms in the Illinois State Senate, and in 2004 won an open United States Senate seat after a keynote address to the Democratic National Convention had made him a national figure overnight.

He defeated Hillary Clinton for the Democratic nomination in 2008 and, with Joe Biden as running mate, defeated John McCain in November to become the first African-American president. His administration enacted the Affordable Care Act and the Dodd–Frank reforms, ordered the operation that killed Osama bin Laden in Abbottabad in May 2011, restored diplomatic relations with Cuba, concluded the Paris Climate Agreement and the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action with Iran, and ended the American combat mission in Iraq. He was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in October 2009. In 2012 he defeated Mitt Romney to win a second term.

He delivered the eulogy at Nelson Mandela's memorial in Johannesburg in 2013, paid the first sitting-presidential visit to Hiroshima in 2016, and at the end of his term was succeeded by Donald Trump, whose campaign had centred on attacking Obama's birth certificate and his legacy. He retired to writing and to the Obama Foundation, published the first volume of a presidential memoir, A Promised Land, in 2020, and lives between Washington and Martha's Vineyard with his wife and their two daughters.

The road continues to Donald Trump.
