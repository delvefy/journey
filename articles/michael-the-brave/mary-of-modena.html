Mary of Modena

Italian princess, queen consort of James II, 1658–1718

Mary of Modena, born Maria Beatrice d'Este in 1658, was the Italian princess whose motherhood helped topple a dynasty. The daughter of the Duke of Modena, she was pious to the point of longing for the cloister when, at the age of fifteen, she was married instead to James, Duke of York, the widowed Catholic heir to the English throne. The union of an English prince with a Catholic Italian, pressed on by Louis XIV, was deeply unpopular from the start.

When James succeeded his brother in 1685 she became queen, and her devout Catholicism sharpened the nation's fears for its Protestant religion. Several of her children had died in infancy, and it seemed the throne would pass to her Protestant stepdaughters. Then, in the summer of 1688, she bore a healthy son.

The birth of a Catholic heir transformed a temporary grievance into a permanent threat, and it triggered the Glorious Revolution. Malicious rumour even denied the child was hers, claiming he had been smuggled into the bedchamber in a warming-pan. As William of Orange advanced, Mary fled to France with the infant prince in her arms.

She passed the rest of her life in exile at Saint-Germain, the château granted by Louis XIV, presiding over the melancholy court of the Jacobites. Widowed in 1701, she lived on in devotion as the mother of the pretender to three crowns. Through that son the lost cause descended to her grandson, the most romantic of all the Stuart claimants, Charles Edward Stuart.
