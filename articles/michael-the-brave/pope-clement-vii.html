Pope Clement VII

Giulio de’ Medici, pope 1523–1534

Giulio de’ Medici was born in 1478, the illegitimate son of the murdered Giuliano de’ Medici, and was raised in the household of his uncle Lorenzo the Magnificent beside Lorenzo’s son Giovanni. When Giovanni became Pope Leo X, he showered his cousin with office, making him archbishop of Florence, cardinal, and the most trusted minister of the papal court. In 1523 Giulio was himself elected pope as Clement VII, a cultivated and capable administrator whose pontificate would nonetheless become a byword for catastrophe.

His ruin was indecision in an age that punished it. Trapped between the Emperor Charles V and Francis I of France as their armies fought up and down Italy, Clement leaned now to one side, now to the other, and after the French collapse joined a league against the emperor. The reckoning came in 1527, when an unpaid imperial army, swollen with Lutheran mercenaries, mutinied and stormed Rome. For months the soldiers sacked the holy city in an orgy of looting and slaughter while the pope cowered behind the walls of the Castel Sant’Angelo, emerging at last a humiliated near-prisoner.

Bound now to the victorious emperor, Clement crowned Charles V at Bologna in 1530, the last time a pope set the imperial crown on a Habsburg head, and with imperial help crushed the restored Florentine republic to plant his family back in power as dukes. But his subjection to Charles had a fateful price elsewhere: he could not grant the annulment Henry VIII of England demanded from Catherine of Aragon, who was the emperor’s aunt. Henry broke with Rome, and England was lost to the papacy.

Amid these disasters Clement remained a Medici and a patron, keeping Michelangelo at work on the family chapel and library in Florence. He arranged the French marriage of his young kinswoman Catherine de’ Medici and died in 1534, his reputation buried under the ruin of Rome.
