Muawiya I

Founder of the Umayyad caliphate, c.600–680

Muawiya was born about 600 in Mecca, the son of Abu Sufyan, the Quraysh leader who had fought Muhammad for years before submitting at the conquest of the city. Muawiya became a Muslim at that surrender and served the Prophet as a secretary. Under the caliphs Umar and Uthman he was made governor of Syria, where over twenty years he built a loyal army, the first Arab fleet, and a power base that would change the course of Islam.

When his kinsman Uthman was murdered in 656 and Ali became caliph, Muawiya refused allegiance and demanded vengeance for the dead caliph’s blood. The two armies met at Siffin in 657 in a battle that ended not in victory but in an arbitration that fatally undermined Ali. After Ali was assassinated, his son Hasan came to terms and abdicated, and in 661 — the Year of Unity — Muawiya stood as sole and undisputed caliph, the first of the Umayyad line, ruling from Damascus.

A master of patience and politic guile, he held the vast empire together, pushed its frontiers outward by land and sea, and governed with a pragmatism his admirers called forbearance. But his most fateful decision was to name his own son Yazid as his heir, converting the caliphate from an office of consent into a hereditary throne. He died in 680, and within months that bequest would bring the Prophet’s grandson Husayn to his death at Karbala, a wound in Islam that has never closed.
