Caligula

Third Roman emperor, AD 12–41

Gaius Caesar was born in AD 12, the son of the beloved general Germanicus and Agrippina the Elder and a great-grandson of Augustus. As a small boy he accompanied his father on campaign in Germany, where the soldiers nicknamed him Caligula, "little boots," after the miniature army sandals he wore. When the treason trials of Tiberius's later years destroyed his mother and brothers, the young Caligula survived, taken to live with the old emperor on Capri, and on Tiberius's death in 37 he succeeded to the throne amid widespread rejoicing.

His first months were marked by generosity—he recalled exiles, ended hated taxes, and staged lavish games. But a severe illness later that year seemed to mark a turning point, after which the sources describe a ruler of mounting cruelty, extravagance, and megalomania. He demanded to be honoured as a living god, humiliated the Senate, and drained the treasury with reckless spending and building.

The ancient writers preserved a string of notorious anecdotes—that he meant to make his favourite horse Incitatus a consul, that he ordered soldiers to gather seashells as spoils of a mock campaign against the sea—tales whose literal truth historians still debate but which fixed his reputation for madness. After less than four years of rule, he was cut down in a palace corridor by officers of the Praetorian Guard under Cassius Chaerea in 41, and his wife and infant daughter were killed with him. The guard then raised his overlooked uncle, Claudius, to the purple.
