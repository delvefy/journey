Mary II of England

Joint sovereign with William III, 1662–1694

Mary II reigned over England, Scotland, and Ireland jointly with her husband William of Orange, the only such partnership in the nation's history. Born in 1662, she was the elder daughter of the future James II and, by the command of her uncle Charles II, was brought up a Protestant despite her father's Catholic faith. At fifteen she was married to her cousin William, Prince of Orange, and went to live in the Dutch Republic.

When her father's policies drove England to revolution in 1688, William sailed against him, and Parliament resolved the crisis by offering the crown to the couple together. Though her own hereditary claim came first, Mary refused to reign without her husband and insisted that the real authority of government rest in his hands.

The arrangement worked because Mary proved an able ruler in her own season. While William was abroad at his wars, she governed the kingdom, and her good sense, piety, and evident Englishness did much to reconcile the nation to the new order that her foreign husband alone could never have won. She took a keen interest in building and gardens, lending her name to projects that still stand.

Her life was short. In 1694 she died of smallpox at the age of thirty-two, childless, and was mourned with a grief that astonished observers; the normally cold William was broken by the loss. The Catholic stepmother whose newborn son had been the spark of the revolution that made Mary a queen was Mary of Modena.
