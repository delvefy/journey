Cimon

Athenian general and statesman, c. 510–450 BCE

Cimon was the leading Athenian soldier and statesman of the generation after the Persian Wars, and the son of Miltiades, the victor of Marathon. Inheriting his father's prestige along with his debts, he rose to prominence as Athens organized the Delian League, the maritime alliance formed to carry the war against Persia into the Aegean and beyond.

As its foremost commander he won a long string of victories, clearing Persian garrisons from the Thracian coast and taking the island of Skyros, from which he brought back to Athens bones revered as those of the legendary hero Theseus. His greatest triumph came around 466 BCE at the river Eurymedon in Asia Minor, where on a single day he is said to have destroyed both a Persian fleet and the land army drawn up to support it, ending the Persian threat in the Aegean for a generation.

At home Cimon led the conservative, pro-Spartan faction and spent his great wealth lavishly for the public good. But his policy of friendship with Sparta collapsed when a force he led to help suppress a helot revolt was pointedly dismissed and sent home; in the reaction that followed, the reformers Ephialtes and the young Pericles stripped power from the aristocratic council, and Cimon was ostracized around 461 BCE.

Later recalled, he took up the Persian war once more and died around 450 BCE while besieging Kition on Cyprus. With him passed the last great champion of the older, aristocratic Athens.
