Charles VI, Holy Roman Emperor

Last male Habsburg of the direct line, 1685–1740

Charles VI was the Habsburg emperor whose every effort was bent on a single problem: how to pass his inheritance to a daughter. The younger son of Leopold I, he was put forward by the Grand Alliance as the rightful king of Spain against the Bourbon Philip V, and he spent years in Spain pressing that claim during the War of the Spanish Succession.

The death of his elder brother Joseph in 1711 changed everything. Charles became Holy Roman Emperor, and his allies, unwilling to see Spain and the Empire joined in one hand as in the days of Charles V, made peace and left Spain to his rival. Charles kept the old Spanish lands in Italy and the Netherlands, and with the victories of Prince Eugene he warred against the Turks with mixed fortune.

But his abiding labour was the succession. Having no surviving son, he issued the Pragmatic Sanction to secure the undivided Habsburg dominions for his daughter Maria Theresa, and he spent the rest of his life purchasing the solemn guarantees of Europe's powers that they would honour it.

A lover of music and stiff Spanish ceremony, he made his court a centre of opera and kept Metastasio as his poet. He died in 1740—and within weeks the guarantees dissolved, as Frederick the Great seized Silesia and the War of the Austrian Succession broke over his daughter's head. To anchor her claim, Charles had wed Maria Theresa to a duke of Lorraine, who would reign beside her as the emperor Francis I.

The road continues to Francis I, Holy Roman Emperor.
