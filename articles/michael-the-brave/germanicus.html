Germanicus

Roman general and prince, 15 BC–AD 19

Germanicus was born in 15 BC to Drusus the Elder and Antonia Minor, a daughter of Mark Antony and Octavia, which placed him at the heart of the imperial house. At the direction of Augustus he was adopted by his uncle Tiberius in AD 4, marking him as a future heir, and he married Agrippina the Elder, a granddaughter of Augustus. Their large family included the future emperor Caligula and Agrippina the Younger, mother of Nero.

Handsome, eloquent, and generous, Germanicus became the most beloved figure of his generation. On Augustus's death in AD 14 he was commanding the legions of the Rhine, and he swiftly mastered a dangerous mutiny among the troops. He then led a series of campaigns deep into Germany to avenge the catastrophe of the Teutoburg Forest, where Arminius had destroyed three legions under Varus in AD 9. Germanicus recovered two of the lost legionary eagles, gave honourable burial to the bones of Varus's dead, and fought Arminius in hard battles, though Rome won no lasting conquest beyond the Rhine.

Recalled by a wary Tiberius, he celebrated a magnificent triumph in AD 17 and was dispatched with wide powers to the eastern provinces. There he fell into bitter conflict with the governor of Syria, Gnaeus Calpurnius Piso. In AD 19, at Antioch, Germanicus died at thirty-three, convinced he had been poisoned; the suspicion fell upon Piso. His death plunged Rome into extraordinary public mourning and cast a long shadow over the reign of Tiberius.

The road continues to Arminius.
