Richard III

King of England, last Yorkist king, 1452–1485

Richard was born on 2 October 1452 at Fotheringhay Castle, the youngest surviving son of Richard, Duke of York, and Cecily Neville. He spent the second phase of the Wars of the Roses entirely on the Yorkist side, brought up in part in the household of his cousin Warwick the Kingmaker. After his brother Edward IV took the throne in 1461, Richard was created Duke of Gloucester and, after Warwick's revolt and the brief Lancastrian restoration of 1470–71, fought conspicuously at Barnet and Tewkesbury. He married Anne Neville, the kingmaker's younger daughter and widow of the Lancastrian Prince of Wales, in 1472, and inherited her share of the Warwick estates in the north.

For the next twelve years he governed the north of England from Middleham and Sheriff Hutton as the king's lieutenant. His Council of the North dispensed cheap justice, his Scottish campaign of 1482 recovered Berwick-upon-Tweed, and contemporaries from outside the south praised his lordship. Edward IV's sudden death on 9 April 1483 made him Lord Protector for his twelve-year-old nephew Edward V. Within ten weeks Richard had intercepted the new king at Stony Stratford, lodged him and his younger brother Richard, Duke of York, in the Tower, executed the queen's brother Earl Rivers and his own old ally William Hastings, and obtained a parliamentary declaration that Edward IV's marriage to Elizabeth Woodville was invalid. He was crowned on 6 July 1483; the princes were never seen alive again.

The Buckingham rebellion of October 1483 failed but rallied Lancastrian exiles around Henry Tudor in Brittany. Anne Neville and Richard's only legitimate son Edward both died in 1484. When Henry landed at Milford Haven in August 1485, Richard met him at Bosworth Field on 22 August. Abandoned at the crisis by the Stanleys, he was killed charging Henry's standard — the last English king to die in battle. His remains lay under what became a Leicester car park until their identification in 2013.

The road continues to Edward IV.
