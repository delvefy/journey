Charlotte Brontë

1816–1855, English novelist

Charlotte Brontë was born at Thornton in West Yorkshire, the third of six children of an Irish-born Anglican curate, Patrick Brontë. The family moved in 1820 to the parsonage at Haworth on the moors, where Charlotte's mother and her two eldest sisters soon died. With her surviving sisters Emily and Anne and their brother Branwell she invented in childhood the elaborate imaginary kingdoms of Angria and Gondal, recorded in tiny hand-stitched booklets that anticipated her later prose.

She was sent in 1824 to the Clergy Daughters' School at Cowan Bridge, the model for the bleak Lowood of Jane Eyre. After a period as a governess in Yorkshire, she travelled with Emily in 1842 to Brussels to study French at the pensionnat of Constantin and Zoë Heger. An unrequited attachment to the married M. Heger marked her deeply and supplied the emotional core of her last novels.

In 1846, with sisters and brother, she published a volume of poems under the names Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell. It sold two copies. The following year, under the same pseudonyms, the three sisters issued Jane Eyre, Wuthering Heights and Agnes Grey. Jane Eyre was a sensation, and its second edition (1848) was dedicated, before they had met, to William Makepeace Thackeray. Shirley (1849) and Villette (1853) followed. Branwell, Emily and Anne all died of consumption between September 1848 and May 1849, leaving Charlotte alone with her father at Haworth.

She was drawn cautiously into the London literary world, met Elizabeth Gaskell, who would write her biography, and corresponded with the critic George Henry Lewes. In 1854 she married her father's curate, Arthur Bell Nicholls, and within a year died, in the early stages of pregnancy and worn down by chronic illness, at thirty-eight. Mrs Gaskell's Life of Charlotte Brontë (1857) fixed her, and the Brontë parsonage, in the Victorian and modern imagination, and Virginia Woolf and many later critics ranked Jane Eyre among the central novels of the nineteenth century.
