Christopher Columbus

Genoese navigator in Spanish service, c. 1451–1506

Cristoforo Colombo was born in or near Genoa in the late summer or autumn of 1451, the eldest son of a weaver named Domenico Colombo. He went to sea young, traded along the Mediterranean and as far as Iceland, and was shipwrecked off Cape St Vincent in 1476, swimming ashore into Portugal. In Lisbon, then the centre of Atlantic exploration, he married Filipa Moniz, fathered his son Diego, sailed to Madeira and the Guinea coast, and read deeply in geography and prophecy. The idea that the Indies could be reached by sailing west across the Atlantic he assembled from Ptolemy, Pierre d’Ailly and a confident underestimate of the earth’s circumference. King John II of Portugal rejected the plan in 1484; his Spanish suit took eight years.

In April 1492, at Santa Fe outside the newly conquered Granada, Isabella I of Castile and Ferdinand II of Aragon signed the capitulations that promised him the rank of admiral, the viceroyalty of any lands he discovered, and a tenth of all revenues. On 3 August he sailed from Palos with three small ships; on 12 October a lookout aboard the Pinta sighted an island in the Bahamas. He went on to Cuba and Hispaniola, where the Santa María grounded on Christmas Eve and her timbers were used to build the first European fort in the Americas, La Navidad. He returned to Spain in March 1493 with parrots, gold and seven captive Taino.

Three further voyages followed, in 1493, 1498 and 1502, each on a vaster scale and each more disastrous in its administration. He brought back word of Trinidad and the Venezuelan coast, of Jamaica and the rivers of Central America. As governor of Hispaniola he proved cruel and incompetent; in 1500 the royal investigator Francisco de Bobadilla sent him home in chains. Restored to his ranks but never to the viceroyalty, he died at Valladolid on 20 May 1506, still convinced that the islands he had found lay off the coast of Asia and not, as his contemporary Amerigo Vespucci would soon argue, on a separate continent.
