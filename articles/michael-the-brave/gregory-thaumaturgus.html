Gregory Thaumaturgus

Bishop of Neocaesarea and missionary, c. 213–270 AD

Born around 213 into a prominent pagan family of Neocaesarea in Pontus, the young man—then called Theodore—set out to study law. On his journey his path crossed that of the great Christian teacher Origen at Caesarea in Palestine, and he remained for years as Origen's pupil, abandoning his legal ambitions and embracing Christianity. His farewell oration of thanks to Origen survives as a precious portrait of that master's way of teaching.

Returning home, he was made bishop of Neocaesarea around 240, reportedly finding only a handful of Christians in the city. Through tireless preaching, pastoral care, and a reputation for miracles, he is said to have won almost the entire population to the faith. His wonders—calming a flooding river, draining a marsh, casting out demons—earned him the surname Thaumaturgus, “the Wonderworker.”

He was credited with a concise creed of the Trinity, said to have been revealed to him in a vision, which long guided the church of Pontus. He also took part in the synods at Antioch that examined the doctrines of Paul of Samosata, lending his authority to the defence of orthodox belief.

His influence reached far beyond his lifetime: the grandmother of Basil the Great and Gregory of Nyssa had been raised on his teaching, so that the great Cappadocian Fathers looked back to him as the founder of their family's faith. Gregory of Nyssa later wrote his Life, and he was remembered as one of the most successful missionary bishops of the early Church.
