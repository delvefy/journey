Lysander

Spartan admiral and general, d. 395 BCE

Lysander was the Spartan commander who at last won the long Peloponnesian War and made Sparta, for a brief season, the master of Greece. A man of obscure birth but boundless ambition, he rose through the navy in the war's final phase, when sea power had become decisive.

His success rested on a shrewd partnership with the Persian prince Cyrus the Younger, whose subsidies let him build and pay a first-rate fleet. After patient maneuvering he caught the Athenian navy unprepared on the beach at Aegospotami in 405 BCE and destroyed it almost without a fight. With Athens' fleet gone, he blockaded the city by land and sea until starvation forced its surrender in 404, and he presided over the demolition of its Long Walls.

In the settlement that followed he installed narrow pro-Spartan oligarchies in the former subject cities, among them the brutal regime of the Thirty Tyrants at Athens, and for a time wielded almost personal dominion over the Aegean. But his arrogance and ambition came to alarm even his own city.

He helped place Agesilaus II on the Spartan throne, then quarreled with him over the management of affairs in Asia. When war flared again in Greece, Lysander was killed in 395 BCE at the Battle of Haliartus in Boeotia, his dream of a Spartan empire already coming undone.
