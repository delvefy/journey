Mary, mother of Jesus

Mother of Jesus of Nazareth, first century

Mary was a Jewish woman of Nazareth in Galilee, remembered as the mother of Jesus. The Gospels of Matthew and Luke present her as a young woman betrothed to Joseph, a carpenter of the line of David. According to Luke, the angel Gabriel announced to her that she would bear a son—the episode known as the Annunciation—and Christians hold that she conceived Jesus while remaining a virgin, a belief central to the faith from its earliest creeds.

The infancy narratives describe her journey to Bethlehem, where Jesus was born, and her visit to her kinswoman Elizabeth, the mother of John the Baptist, on which occasion tradition places her song of praise, the Magnificat. The Gospels show her again at the wedding at Cana, where she prompts her son's first sign, and standing at the foot of the cross, where the dying Jesus is said to have entrusted her to the care of the apostle John.

Little more about her life is recorded, but her place in Christianity grew immense. The Council of Ephesus in 431 affirmed her title Theotokos, "God-bearer" or Mother of God, and later centuries elaborated doctrines such as her Immaculate Conception and Assumption within the Catholic and Orthodox traditions. Honoured also in Islam as Maryam, she became the most venerated woman in Western religion and the subject of more painting, sculpture, music, and devotion than perhaps any other figure, an enduring emblem of motherhood and faith.
