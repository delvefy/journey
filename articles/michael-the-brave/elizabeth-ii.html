Elizabeth II

1926–2022, Queen of the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth realms

Elizabeth Alexandra Mary Windsor was born at 17 Bruton Street, Mayfair, in April 1926, the elder daughter of Prince Albert, Duke of York, and Lady Elizabeth Bowes-Lyon. She was ten when her uncle King Edward VIII abdicated in December 1936 and her father became King George VI, making her heir presumptive to the throne. During the Second World War she insisted on serving in uniform as an Auxiliary Territorial Service driver and mechanic, the only female member of the royal family to enter the armed forces. In November 1947 she married her third cousin Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark, and on 6 February 1952, while she was on a royal tour at Treetops Hotel in Kenya, her father died and she became Queen at twenty-five.

Her coronation in Westminster Abbey the following June was the first televised, over her ministers' objections, and her reign coincided almost exactly with the dismantling of the British Empire that her great-great-grandmother Queen Victoria had ruled. From the independence of the Gold Coast in 1957 through the handover of Hong Kong in 1997 she presided over a Commonwealth of nations that she did much to hold together as a voluntary association; she was head of state of fifteen realms at her death, from Canada to Tuvalu. She held over a hundred state visits, met every American president from Harry Truman to Joe Biden except Lyndon Johnson, and conducted a long diplomatic friendship with Nelson Mandela after his release from prison in 1990, the two of them on first-name terms in their correspondence.

She read State papers every morning, met fifteen Prime Ministers in audience — from Winston Churchill at the start of her reign to Liz Truss two days before her death — and remained scrupulously above party while exercising a private influence whose details her constitutional convention required to remain hidden. The annus horribilis of 1992 brought the Windsor Castle fire and the public collapse of three of her children's marriages; the death of her former daughter-in-law Diana in 1997 produced the gravest crisis of public confidence of her reign. The Golden, Diamond and Platinum Jubilees of 2002, 2012 and 2022 each renewed her place in British public life.

She survived Prince Philip in 2021 and died at Balmoral on 8 September 2022 at the age of ninety-six, the longest-reigning monarch in British history. Her son became King Charles III. Of all the human figures of her reign, none cast a larger or more painful shadow over its later years than the former wife of her heir, the global icon whose death in a Paris underpass forced the monarchy itself to its knees — Diana, Princess of Wales.
