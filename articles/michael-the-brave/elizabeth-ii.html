Elizabeth II

1926–2022, Queen of the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth realms

Elizabeth Alexandra Mary Windsor was born at 17 Bruton Street, Mayfair, in April 1926, the elder daughter of the Duke and Duchess of York. She lived in private retirement until her uncle Edward VIII abdicated in December 1936, putting her father on the throne as George VI and her at ten years old next in line. In the Second World War she trained, at seventeen, as a junior officer in the Auxiliary Territorial Service, the only female member of the royal family to enter the armed forces. In November 1947 she married her cousin Prince Philip of Greece and Denmark.

She acceded on the death of her father at Sandringham on 6 February 1952, while she was on tour in Kenya, and was crowned at Westminster Abbey in June 1953 in a ceremony televised over her ministers' objections. Her first prime minister was Winston Churchill, her last Liz Truss, and over seventy years she received in private weekly audience fifteen British prime ministers including Harold Macmillan, Harold Wilson, Margaret Thatcher and Tony Blair. She held over a hundred state visits and met every American president from Harry Truman to Joe Biden except Lyndon Johnson.

The reign saw the dissolution of the British Empire into the modern Commonwealth, of which she was head; the granting of independence to scores of former colonies; and a long diplomatic friendship with Nelson Mandela after his release from prison in 1990, the two of them on first-name terms in their correspondence. She met Pope John Paul II in 1980 and 1982 and again at the Vatican in 2000. The annus horribilis of 1992 saw Windsor Castle burn and three of her four children's marriages fail, and the death of Diana, Princess of Wales, in August 1997 obliged her to broadcast a tribute against her instinct for privacy.

The Golden, Diamond and Platinum Jubilees of 2002, 2012 and 2022 marked the rare longevity of her reign, the longest of any British sovereign. She continued to perform constitutional duties to within two days of her death, accepting the resignation of Boris Johnson and appointing his successor at Balmoral. She died there on 8 September 2022, aged ninety-six, and her eldest son acceded as Charles III.
