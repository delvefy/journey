Antonius Primus

Roman general, c. 30–after 81 CE

Marcus Antonius Primus was the daring general who won the throne for Vespasian in the civil war of 69 CE. Born at Tolosa in southern Gaul, he had a chequered early career: condemned and exiled under Nero for involvement in a forgery, he was recalled by Galba and given command of a legion in the Danubian province of Pannonia.

When the eastern and Danubian armies declared for Vespasian against Vitellius, Antonius Primus became the boldest of his partisans. Rather than wait for Vespasian or for the cautious senior commander Mucianus to direct the campaign, he seized the initiative and led the legions over the Alps into Italy. At the second battle of Bedriacum, near Cremona, he shattered the Vitellian army, then let his soldiers storm and sack the wealthy city in a night of notorious brutality.

Pressing on to Rome, he captured the capital in December 69 and brought down Vitellius, securing the empire for the absent Vespasian. Yet his very success undid him. His impetuous independence and the horrors of Cremona earned him the suspicion of Mucianus and of Vespasian's son Domitian, who eased him out of power once the fighting was over.

He withdrew to his native Tolosa to a long and comfortable retirement. There, decades later, the poet Martial addressed him in affectionate epigrams, praising the serenity of his honoured old age. The historian Tacitus, meanwhile, made him one of the most vivid characters of his Histories—eloquent, energetic, and grasping in equal measure.
