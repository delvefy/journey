Duke of Wellington

British general and statesman, 1769–1852

Born Arthur Wellesley into the Anglo-Irish aristocracy, the future Duke of Wellington first made his name as a commander in India, winning a hard fought victory at Assaye and breaking the power of hostile princely states. Returning to Europe, he was given command in the Peninsular War, where over six years of campaigning in Portugal and Spain he wore down and ultimately expelled the armies of Napoleon’s marshals, earning a dukedom and a reputation as the finest defensive general of the age.

His supreme moment came in 1815. After Napoleon’s return from exile, Wellington led an allied army at the Battle of Waterloo and, with the timely arrival of Blücher’s Prussians, shattered the emperor’s forces in a single bloody day, bringing the Napoleonic Wars to a close. The victory made him the most celebrated soldier in Europe and fixed his nickname, the "Iron Duke."

He then entered politics, serving as Tory prime minister from 1828 to 1830. Though a staunch conservative, he carried Catholic Emancipation through Parliament to avert civil strife in Ireland, even as he resisted the broader Reform movement, which made him for a time deeply unpopular. He remained a revered elder statesman and commander-in-chief of the army until his death in 1852, which was marked by one of the grandest state funerals Britain had ever seen.
