Giuliano de' Medici

Medici prince, victim of the Pazzi conspiracy, 1453–1478

Giuliano de’ Medici, born in 1453, was the younger son of Piero the Gouty and the cherished brother of Lorenzo the Magnificent. Handsome and athletic, he was the ornament of Medici Florence at its most brilliant. The tournament he held in 1475 became legend, its queen of beauty the lovely Simonetta Vespucci and its memorial a celebrated poem by Angelo Poliziano; the painters of the Medici circle, Botticelli among them, fixed his features and his world on panel.

Behind the festivity lay deadly rivalry. The Pazzi, a great banking house, and their allies resented the Medici monopoly of power and money, and they found backing in Rome among the kinsmen and partisans of Pope Sixtus IV. On 26 April 1478, as the brothers attended High Mass in the cathedral of Florence, the conspirators struck at the most sacred moment. Giuliano was set upon at the altar steps and stabbed to death; Lorenzo, slashed across the neck, fought free and barricaded himself in the sacristy.

The coup collapsed within hours. Florence rallied to the surviving brother, and the vengeance was ferocious: conspirators and the archbishop of Pisa were hanged from the windows of the public palace, and the assault bound the city more tightly than ever to Lorenzo. Giuliano left an infant illegitimate son, Giulio, whom Lorenzo took into his own household and raised; that boy would grow up to wear the tiara himself as Pope Clement VII.
