Tiberius

Second Roman emperor, 42 BC–AD 37

Tiberius was born in 42 BC to the noblewoman Livia and her first husband; when his mother married Augustus, the boy entered the orbit of the new imperial house. He proved one of Rome's ablest generals, campaigning in the Alps, Pannonia, Illyricum, and Germany, and recovering by diplomacy the legionary standards lost to Parthia. Compelled by Augustus to divorce his beloved wife Vipsania and marry the emperor's daughter Julia, he grew embittered and withdrew for years into retirement on Rhodes.

The deaths of Augustus's grandsons left no other heir, and Tiberius was adopted and groomed for power, succeeding to the empire on Augustus's death in AD 14. His early rule was careful and frugal: he managed the treasury soundly, avoided needless wars, and preserved the structure his predecessor had built. Yet he was a reluctant and suspicious ruler, never at ease with the role.

From AD 26 he withdrew to the island of Capri, leaving Rome in the hands of his ambitious praetorian prefect Sejanus, whose grasp for power ended only when Tiberius destroyed him in 31. The reign darkened into an age of treason trials and fear that later historians such as Tacitus painted in grim colours. It was during these years, under his prefect Pontius Pilate, that Jesus was crucified in Judea, and his image on the denarius lay behind the famous saying to render unto Caesar what is Caesar's. Tiberius died in 37 and was succeeded by his great-nephew Caligula.
