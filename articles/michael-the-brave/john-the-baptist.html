John the Baptist

Jewish prophet and forerunner of Christ, first century

John the Baptist was a Jewish preacher who appeared in the wilderness of Judea in the early first century, an ascetic figure clothed in camel hair who lived on locusts and wild honey. The Gospel of Luke names him the son of the priest Zechariah and Elizabeth, and a kinsman of Jesus. His existence is confirmed not only by the Gospels but by the Jewish historian Josephus, who describes him as a good man who urged the Jews to virtue and to baptism.

John summoned crowds to repentance, baptising them in the river Jordan as a sign of the forgiveness of sins and proclaiming the imminent arrival of one greater than himself. Among those who came to him was Jesus of Nazareth, whom John baptised; the event marks the beginning of Jesus's public ministry in all the Gospel accounts, and several of Jesus's first followers are said to have been disciples of John.

His preaching brought him into collision with Herod Antipas, the tetrarch of Galilee, whom John denounced for marrying Herodias, his brother's former wife. Herod imprisoned the prophet, and according to the Gospels he was beheaded after the daughter of Herodias, by tradition named Salome, danced before the king and was prompted to demand John's head on a platter. Venerated as the last of the prophets in Christianity and honoured in Islam as Yahya, John remained the great forerunner who pointed beyond himself.
