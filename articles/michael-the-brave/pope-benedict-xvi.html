Pope Benedict XVI

1927–2022, supreme pontiff of the Roman Catholic Church, born Joseph Aloisius Ratzinger

Joseph Ratzinger was born at Marktl am Inn in Upper Bavaria on the morning of Holy Saturday 1927, the youngest of three children of a Bavarian gendarme. The family moved several times between police postings; the Ratzingers were anti-Nazi, but Joseph and his elder brother Georg were nevertheless enrolled, like all schoolboys of their generation, in the Hitler Youth, and Joseph was conscripted at sixteen to anti-aircraft batteries near a BMW factory and to an Austrian infantry post from which he eventually deserted home. He and Georg were ordained priests together at Freising in June 1951.

He took his doctorate on Augustine in 1953 and his habilitation on Bonaventure, taught dogmatic and fundamental theology at Bonn, Münster, Tübingen, and Regensburg, and served as a peritus expert at the Second Vatican Council with Cardinal Frings of Cologne. After 1968 he moved decisively from the council's reforming wing toward a more conservative reading. In 1977 Pope Paul VI made him Archbishop of Munich and Freising; in 1981 Pope John Paul II called him to Rome as Prefect of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, where he served for nearly twenty-four years, disciplining liberation theologians and shaping the doctrinal landscape of his pope's reign.

The cardinals elected him on 19 April 2005, his seventy-eighth birthday, the oldest pope at election since 1730. As Benedict XVI he wrote the encyclicals Deus Caritas Est, Spe Salvi and Caritas in Veritate, the trilogy Jesus of Nazareth, and lifted in 2007 most restrictions on the older Latin Mass. The 2006 Regensburg address on faith and reason provoked Muslim protest by quoting a Byzantine emperor; the lifting of the excommunication of the Holocaust-denying bishop Richard Williamson the next year angered Chancellor Angela Merkel and the German bishops.

On 11 February 2013 he announced in Latin, before a startled consistory, his resignation on grounds of declining strength, the first papal abdication since Gregory XII in 1415. He retired to the Mater Ecclesiae monastery in the Vatican gardens with the title pope emeritus, supported the pontificate of his successor Francis, and remained largely out of public view. He died there on 31 December 2022, ninety-five years old.
