Mariamne

Hasmonean princess, second wife of Herod the Great, c. 54–29 BC

Mariamne was a princess of the Hasmonean royal house, a granddaughter of the high priest and ethnarch Hyrcanus and daughter of the ambitious Alexandra. When Herod the Great captured Jerusalem in 37 BC, he married her, both because of her famed beauty and because her ancient lineage lent his upstart Idumean kingship the legitimacy of the old priest-kings. The match bound the new ruler to the dynasty he had supplanted.

It was a marriage of passion and poison. Mariamne despised Herod as a low-born usurper and could not forgive the violence he visited on her family, while Herod's sister Salome and his mother detested the proud princess and worked ceaselessly to destroy her. Tensions deepened when Herod, departing twice to account for himself before his Roman patrons, left secret orders that Mariamne be killed should he fail to return; when she discovered this, her contempt hardened into open estrangement.

Her family was steadily consumed by Herod's suspicion. Her teenage brother Aristobulus, made high priest at the urging of Alexandra, was drowned on Herod's orders once his popularity seemed a threat. At last, in 29 BC, charges of infidelity and conspiracy pressed by Salome gave Herod the pretext to have Mariamne tried and executed. The king was afterward tormented by grief and guilt, and her sons by him were also eventually put to death. Remembered through the pages of Josephus, the doomed queen became a favourite tragic heroine of later drama.
