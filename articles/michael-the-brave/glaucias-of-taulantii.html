Glaucias of the Taulantii

Illyrian king, fl. c. 335–302 BCE

Glaucias was a king of the Taulantii, one of the larger Illyrian peoples along the Adriatic coast, who flourished around the end of the fourth century BCE. He first appears in history as an ally of the Dardanian king Cleitus, joining him in the revolt against Alexander the Great in 335 BCE; when Alexander broke the rising, Glaucias gave the defeated Cleitus refuge in his lands.

His most lasting act came a generation later. When the royal house of Epirus was overthrown and the infant prince Pyrrhus was carried to safety, it was Glaucias who took the child in and raised him among his own family. The Macedonian regent Cassander, fearing a future claimant, is said to have offered Glaucias a large sum to give up the boy, but the Illyrian king refused.

Around 306 BCE Glaucias intervened with an army to set the young Pyrrhus on the Epirote throne. That act of protection shaped the career of one of antiquity's most famous warrior-kings, and it is the deed for which Glaucias himself is chiefly remembered.
