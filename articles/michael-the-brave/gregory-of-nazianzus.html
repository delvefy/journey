Gregory of Nazianzus

Cappadocian Father and Doctor of the Church, c. 329–390 AD

Gregory was born around 329 near Nazianzus in Cappadocia, the son of the local bishop. He received the finest education of his age, studying at Caesarea, Alexandria, and Athens, where he formed a deep and lifelong friendship with Basil. Contemplative by nature, he longed for a life of quiet study, but was repeatedly drawn against his will into the turmoil of the Church.

His father, and later Basil, ordained him almost by force, Basil naming him bishop of the wretched little town of Sasima—a slight Gregory never forgot. Together with Basil and Basil's brother Gregory of Nyssa, he became one of the three great Cappadocian Fathers, who completed the defence of Nicene orthodoxy and the doctrine of the Trinity.

His finest hour came at Constantinople around 380, where he led a tiny, beleaguered Nicene congregation. In a series of brilliant sermons, the Five Theological Orations, he expounded the full divinity of the Son and the Holy Spirit so powerfully that he was ever after called “the Theologian,” a title shared in the East only with the apostle John.

When Theodosius I entered the city and restored the Nicene cause, Gregory was made archbishop of Constantinople and briefly presided over the First Council of Constantinople in 381. But weary of ecclesiastical intrigue, he soon resigned and returned home to Cappadocia, where he spent his last years writing letters, autobiography, and poetry. He died around 390, honoured as one of the greatest theologians and orators of the Greek Church.
