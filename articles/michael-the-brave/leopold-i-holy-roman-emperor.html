Leopold I, Holy Roman Emperor

Habsburg emperor, 1640–1705

Leopold I was the Habsburg emperor whose long reign held the line of his dynasty against enemies in both east and west. Born in 1640, a younger son destined at first for the Church, he became heir on his brother's death and was elected Holy Roman Emperor in 1658, ruling for forty-seven years. Devout, patient, and a genuine lover and composer of music, he governed through war after war.

In the east loomed the Ottoman Empire. In 1683 a vast Turkish army besieged his capital, and Vienna was saved only at the last hour by the relief led by John III Sobieski. From that deliverance the tide turned: in the campaigns that followed, the imperial armies drove the Turks from Hungary, and by the Peace of Karlowitz the Habsburgs became masters of a great new realm in the Danube basin.

In the west loomed the France of Louis XIV. Through three long conflicts Leopold stood at the head of the coalitions that resisted French expansion, culminating in the War of the Spanish Succession, which he entered to claim the vacant Spanish throne for his younger son.

He made Vienna a baroque capital, advanced the Counter-Reformation, and fathered two future emperors, Joseph and Charles, so becoming grandfather to the great Maria Theresa. He died in 1705 with the war for Spain still raging. Throughout these struggles the soldier who won his battles and secured his dynasty was the brilliant commander Prince Eugene of Savoy.
