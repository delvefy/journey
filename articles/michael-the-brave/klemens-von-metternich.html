Klemens von Metternich

Austrian statesman, 1773–1859

Metternich was born at Koblenz into a family of the Rhenish nobility and rose through the diplomatic service of the Habsburg empire, serving as ambassador to the France of Napoleon before becoming Austrian foreign minister in 1809. Coolly pragmatic, he sought at first to accommodate the all-conquering emperor, even arranging Napoleon’s marriage to the Austrian archduchess Marie Louise to win his country a breathing space.

As Napoleon’s power crumbled, Metternich brought Austria into the final coalition and emerged as the presiding genius of the peace. At the Congress of Vienna in 1814–15 he redrew the map of Europe to restore a balance of power and the principle of legitimate monarchy, and the settlement and the "Concert of Europe" that maintained it bore his stamp for a generation.

For the next three decades he was the foremost conservative statesman of the continent, the architect of an order designed to hold back the twin forces of liberalism and nationalism that he believed would plunge Europe into chaos. Through measures such as the Carlsbad Decrees he policed the press and the universities and earned the hatred of revolutionaries everywhere. So completely did he embody the age that it is often named for him. The revolutions of 1848 at last swept him from office, and he fled to England before returning to die in Vienna in 1859.
