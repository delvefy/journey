Ferdinand II of Aragon

King of Aragon, Castile, Sicily, and Naples, 1452–1516

Ferdinand was born at Sos in Aragon on 10 March 1452, the only surviving son of John II of Aragon by his second wife, Juana Enríquez. From his eighth year he was at the side of his father in the Catalan civil war and was made king of Sicily in 1468 at sixteen. The following October, in a secret ceremony at Valladolid, he married his cousin Isabella, the heiress presumptive of Castile, in defiance of her half-brother Henry IV; the dispensation was supplied by a forged papal bull. Civil war and a Portuguese invasion followed her accession in 1474, but by 1479 they had won, and the union of Aragon and Castile that they jointly ruled became the foundation of the Spanish monarchy.

From 1482 to 1492 he and Isabella prosecuted the war against Granada, capping a long Reconquest with the surrender of Boabdil on 2 January 1492. The same year they expelled the Jews from their kingdoms by the Alhambra Decree and gave Christopher Columbus the contract that took him to the Indies. Pope Alexander VI conferred on them in 1496 the title of Reyes Católicos, the Catholic Monarchs. To Castile Ferdinand brought the Inquisition under Tomás de Torquemada; to Italy he sent Gonzalo de Córdoba, whose tercios destroyed the French army at Cerignola and Garigliano in 1503 and made him master of Naples.

Isabella's death in 1504 broke their joint government: their daughter Joanna and her husband Philip the Handsome inherited Castile, and Ferdinand was reduced to Aragon, regaining Castile only after Philip's death in 1506 and the formal incapacity of Joanna. He married Germaine de Foix in 1505, took Navarre in 1512, and at his death at Madrigalejo on 23 January 1516 left the joined crowns of Castile, Aragon, Navarre, Sicily, Sardinia, Naples, and the new Indies to his grandson Charles, who as Charles V would inherit the Habsburg lands beyond.

The road continues to Joanna of Castile.
