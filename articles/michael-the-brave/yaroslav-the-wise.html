Yaroslav the Wise

Grand Prince of Kiev, c.978–1054

Yaroslav, a son of Vladimir the Great, came to the throne of Kiev in 1019 only after a long and bitter war of succession among Vladimir’s sons, in which his rival Sviatopolk earned the name “the Accursed” for the murder of their brothers Boris and Gleb, who became the first saints of the Rus. Backed by the men of Novgorod and by Varangian warriors, Yaroslav at last secured the great principality and ruled it for thirty-five years, carrying the Kievan Rus to the summit of its power and culture.

He earned his title, the Wise, by his works of peace. He issued the Russkaya Pravda, the first written code of Rus law; he raised the magnificent cathedral of Saint Sophia in Kiev; and he gathered scholars and scribes, founding libraries and fostering the translation of Greek books into Slavonic, so that learning took root among his people. He strengthened the Church and saw a native son raised as metropolitan of Kiev.

So great was his prestige that he became known as the father-in-law of Europe: his daughters were wedded to kings — one to the king of France, another to the king of Hungary, and Elisabeth to the Norwegian warrior-king Harald Hardrada, who had served in his own guard. When Yaroslav died in 1054, he divided his realm among his sons, and the unity he had built soon began to fracture — but the golden age of Kievan Rus had borne his name.
