Diocletian

Roman emperor, reigned 284–305 AD

Born around 244 to a humble family in Dalmatia, Diocletian rose through the ranks of the army to command the imperial bodyguard. After the deaths of the emperors Carus and Numerian, the troops proclaimed him emperor in 284, and he defeated his rival Carinus to take sole power, bringing to an end the half-century of invasion and civil war known as the Crisis of the Third Century.

Judging the empire too vast for one ruler, he created the Tetrarchy in 293: he governed the East as senior Augustus while Maximian held the West, each supported by a junior Caesar—Galerius and Constantius. He divided the provinces into smaller units grouped in dioceses, greatly enlarged the army and bureaucracy, separated civil from military authority, and tried to curb runaway inflation with his Edict on Maximum Prices in 301.

A traditionalist who placed himself under the protection of Jupiter, he launched in 303 the last and most severe persecution of Christians, ordering churches demolished, scriptures burned, and clergy imprisoned across the empire. It failed to halt the faith's spread and left a bitter memory.

In 305, in an act almost without precedent, Diocletian and Maximian abdicated together, and he retired to the vast fortified palace he had built at Spalatum, modern Split. When his carefully designed system collapsed into fresh civil war, he refused all appeals to return, reportedly preferring to tend his garden. He died around 311. His reforms reshaped the late Roman state and laid the foundations on which Constantine would build.
