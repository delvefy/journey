Nefertari

Great Royal Wife of Ramesses II, c.1290–1255 BC

Nefertari, whose name is often given in full as Nefertari Meritmut, “the beautiful companion, beloved of Mut,” was the first and most cherished of the many wives of Ramesses II. Her origins are obscure — she does not appear to have been of royal blood, perhaps a daughter of the Theban nobility — and she married Ramesses before he ascended the throne, standing beside him through the early years of his long reign.

She was no mere ornament of the court. As queen she took part in the ceremonial and diplomatic life of the kingdom, and her name appears in correspondence with the Hittite royal house at the time of the great peace between the two empires. Above all she was honoured by her husband on a scale almost unknown for an Egyptian queen: at Abu Simbel, beside the colossal temple Ramesses raised for himself, he carved a second temple dedicated to Nefertari and the goddess Hathor, its façade bearing statues of the queen as tall as those of the king, proclaimed a temple for which the sun itself would shine.

When she died, around the twenty-fourth year of Ramesses’s reign, she was laid to rest in the Valley of the Queens in a tomb whose vivid painted walls, rediscovered in the twentieth century, are reckoned among the most exquisite to survive from ancient Egypt. Through them and through Abu Simbel, Nefertari remains the most famous of all the queens of the Ramesside age.
