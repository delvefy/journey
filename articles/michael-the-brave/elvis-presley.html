Elvis Presley

American singer, 1935–1977.

Born in a two-room shotgun house in Tupelo, Mississippi, the surviving twin of a stillborn brother, Elvis grew up poor and devout in the Pentecostal Assembly of God, where he absorbed the white gospel quartets and the Black sanctified singing that would meet in his own voice. The family moved to Memphis in 1948, where the teenage Elvis hung around the radio stations on Beale Street and the storefront recording studio of Sun Records. In July 1954 the Sun proprietor Sam Phillips, looking for a white singer who could deliver the rhythm-and-blues feel, recorded him in an after-hours session of Arthur "Big Boy" Crudup's "That's All Right," and the result — a country-blues hybrid that radio could not classify — launched rock and roll into the American mainstream.

With the manager Colonel Tom Parker, a former carnival man with a doubtful immigration status, Elvis moved from Sun to RCA, where "Heartbreak Hotel," "Hound Dog," "Don't Be Cruel," and "Jailhouse Rock" topped the 1956–57 charts; his swivelling hips on the Milton Berle and Ed Sullivan shows scandalized parents and consecrated him as the first American teen idol. Drafted in 1958, he served two unglamorous years as an army sergeant in West Germany — where he met the fourteen-year-old Priscilla Beaulieu, later his wife — and returned to a film career that Parker steered into a long run of mediocre musicals.

The 1968 NBC television special, dressed in black leather and accompanied by his original sidemen, rescued his reputation; the Memphis sessions that followed produced "Suspicious Minds" and "In the Ghetto," and he reinvented himself as a Las Vegas headliner in the white-jumpsuited International Hotel residency. The Beatles visited him at his Bel Air house in August 1965, John Lennon would later credit him with everything, and in December 1970 Elvis walked unannounced into the Nixon White House to ask for a federal narcotics badge. His own dependence on prescription drugs accelerated through the 1970s, and he was found dead on the bathroom floor at Graceland in August 1977, forty-two years old and irreversibly the King.

The road continues to John Lennon.
