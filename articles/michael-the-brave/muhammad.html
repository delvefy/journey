Muhammad

Prophet of Islam, c.570–632

Muhammad was born about 570 in Mecca, a trading town of western Arabia, into the clan of Hashim of the powerful Quraysh tribe. Orphaned in childhood and raised by his grandfather and then his uncle Abu Talib, he grew up to be a merchant of such honesty that men called him al-Amin, the trustworthy. He entered the service of a wealthy widow named Khadija and married her, and the marriage gave him both security and his first devoted believer.

About the year 610, while meditating in a cave on Mount Hira outside Mecca, he experienced the first of the revelations that he and his followers held to be the word of God conveyed by the angel Gabriel. He began to preach an uncompromising monotheism — submission to the one God — against the idolatry of his city, gathering a small band of converts but rousing the fierce hostility of the Quraysh, who saw their gods and their trade threatened.

In 622 he and his followers withdrew to the town of Medina, an emigration, the Hijra, that Muslims took as the beginning of their era; there he became the leader of a community bound by faith and law. Years of conflict with Mecca followed — the battles of Badr, Uhud and the Trench — until in 630 he entered his native city in triumph and cleansed the Kaaba of its idols. By his death in 632 he had united much of Arabia under the new faith.

He taught that he was the last in a line of prophets reaching back through Jesus, Moses and Abraham — the Seal of the Prophets — and the Quran gathered from his revelations became the scripture of a religion that, carried by his successors, would within a century stretch from Spain to India. His death left the question of who should lead, and the dispute that set his companion Abu Bakr above his cousin Ali would in time divide Islam itself.
