Henry VII of England

King of England, founder of the Tudor dynasty, 1457–1509

Henry Tudor was born on 28 January 1457 at Pembroke Castle, the posthumous son of Edmund Tudor, Earl of Richmond, and Margaret Beaufort. His claim to the throne ran through his mother's descent from John of Gaunt's legitimised Beaufort line, and through his father's half-blood kinship to Henry VI. The Yorkist victory at Tewkesbury in 1471 sent him into exile with his uncle Jasper Tudor, and he spent fourteen years as the guest, prisoner, and pawn of Duke Francis II of Brittany. When Richard III's usurpation in 1483 alienated former Yorkists, Henry's claim suddenly drew Lancastrian and dissident-Yorkist support; he landed at Milford Haven on 7 August 1485.

At Bosworth Field on 22 August 1485 Richard III charged toward him and was killed; the crown, retrieved from a thornbush, was placed on Henry's head on the field. He dated his reign from the day before the battle, attainted the Yorkist dead, and on 18 January 1486 married Edward IV's eldest daughter Elizabeth of York, joining the two houses. Two early Yorkist conspiracies tested him — the impostor Lambert Simnel, defeated at Stoke Field in 1487, and Perkin Warbeck, claiming to be Edward's younger son and finally hanged in 1499. Henry was thorough rather than vindictive: he restored the Crown's finances through the Council Learned in the Law, the chamber system administered by Edmund Dudley and Richard Empson, and a network of bonds and recognisances that put the great families under perpetual financial supervision.

His diplomacy married his eldest son Arthur to Catherine of Aragon in 1501 to bind Spain, and his daughter Margaret to James IV of Scotland in 1503 to bind the north — the latter union eventually delivered the Crown of England to the Stuarts. He sponsored John Cabot's 1497 Atlantic voyage, kept England out of war after 1497, and died at Richmond on 21 April 1509, leaving a full treasury and an unchallenged succession.

The road continues to Richard III.
