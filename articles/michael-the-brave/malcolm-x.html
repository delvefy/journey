Malcolm X

American Black nationalist leader, 1925–1965

Malcolm X was born Malcolm Little on 19 May 1925 in Omaha, Nebraska, the seventh child of Earl Little, an itinerant Baptist preacher and a local organizer for Marcus Garvey’s Universal Negro Improvement Association, and Louise Little, of Grenadian birth. His father was killed in Lansing, Michigan, in 1931 — officially a streetcar accident, almost certainly murder by white supremacists — and his mother was committed to a state mental hospital in 1939. The children were scattered to foster homes. Malcolm finished the eighth grade with the best marks in his class and dropped out.

The next ten years took him through small jobs in Boston and Harlem and into the half-organized criminal underworld; he was arrested in 1946 for burglary and sentenced to ten years in Massachusetts state prison. Behind bars he read his way through the prison library, encountered the Nation of Islam through his siblings, and converted. On parole in 1952 he discarded his “slave name” for the letter X and threw himself into the work of Elijah Muhammad’s movement, becoming within a decade its most articulate public voice and the founding minister of Mosque No. 7 in Harlem.

He broke with the Nation in March 1964 over Elijah Muhammad’s private conduct and over what he saw as the movement’s political quietism. A pilgrimage to Mecca that April, and travel in Africa and the Middle East, persuaded him that orthodox Islam offered a wider brotherhood than the racial theology he had preached, and he founded the Organization of Afro-American Unity. He was at work with Alex Haley on the Autobiography that would carry his life into the next century when, on 21 February 1965, three gunmen from his old organization shot him dead on the stage of the Audubon Ballroom, in upper Manhattan. He was thirty-nine.
