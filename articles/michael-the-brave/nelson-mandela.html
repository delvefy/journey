Nelson Mandela

1918–2013, South African anti-apartheid leader and first democratically elected president

Rolihlahla Mandela was born at Mvezo in the Transkei, the son of a Thembu chief deposed by the South African colonial authorities. After his father's death he was raised in the household of the Thembu regent at Mqhekezweni, given the English name Nelson at primary school, and educated at the Methodist Healdtown college and the University of Fort Hare, from which he was expelled for student protest. He read law in Johannesburg and in 1944, with Oliver Tambo and Walter Sisulu, founded the African National Congress Youth League.

The Nationalist victory of 1948 imposed apartheid in statute. Mandela rose in the ANC, drafted with Tambo the Programme of Action of 1949, and after the Sharpeville massacre of March 1960 helped form Umkhonto we Sizwe, the armed wing, of which he was the first commander-in-chief. He travelled abroad, was inspired by both Gandhi's earlier South African campaigns and the example of Jawaharlal Nehru, and on his return was arrested in August 1962. The Rivonia trial of 1963–64 sentenced him to life imprisonment for sabotage; from the dock he closed his statement with the words “I am prepared to die.”

He spent eighteen years on Robben Island, then six at Pollsmoor, and finally fourteen months at Victor Verster, where secret negotiations with the National Party government of P.W. Botha and F.W. de Klerk produced the political settlement of 1990. He was released on 11 February 1990, shared the 1993 Nobel Peace Prize with de Klerk, and on 10 May 1994 was inaugurated President of South Africa after the country's first non-racial election. He set up the Truth and Reconciliation Commission under Desmond Tutu and used the 1995 Rugby World Cup to seal the new South African idea.

He served a single term, retired to his village of Qunu, married Graça Machel on his eightieth birthday in 1998, and through his foundation worked on HIV/AIDS, conflict mediation, and the children's welfare causes of his later years. He befriended Queen Elizabeth II, was admired by Margaret Thatcher's successors in their turn, and inspired the younger Barack Obama, whose first act of student protest had been against apartheid. He died at his home in Johannesburg in December 2013, ninety-five years old.
