Louise of Savoy

Regent of France, 1476–1531

Louise was born in 1476 into the House of Savoy and raised, like so many highborn girls of her day, at the court of Anne of France. Married young to the Count of Angoulême and widowed at nineteen, she poured all her ambition into her two children, the future writer Marguerite and a son she pointedly nicknamed "my Caesar," watching over his prospects with a fierce and calculating devotion recorded in her own journal.

That son became, against the odds, King Francis I in 1515. Louise at once emerged as the real power behind the throne, and twice she governed France as regent while Francis pursued his wars in Italy. Her finest hour came after the catastrophe of Pavia in 1525, when the king was taken prisoner: she held the shaken kingdom together, rallied its defences, and worked tirelessly to secure his release.

A politician of rare nerve and cunning, she pressed the claims that drove the Constable de Bourbon into treason, and in 1529 she met Margaret of Austria to negotiate the so-called Ladies’ Peace of Cambrai, which brought a pause to the long struggle with the Emperor Charles V. A discerning patron of Renaissance art and letters, she was mother to Francis I and to the gifted Marguerite of Navarre. She died in 1531.
