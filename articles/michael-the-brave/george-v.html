George V

1865–1936, King of the United Kingdom and Emperor of India

Prince George Frederick Ernest Albert was born at Marlborough House in June 1865, the second son of the Prince and Princess of Wales, the future Edward VII and Alexandra of Denmark. As a younger son destined for the navy, he served as a cadet on HMS Britannia and then around the world on HMS Bacchante with his elder brother Albert Victor. The death of Albert Victor of influenza in 1892 made him heir presumptive at twenty-six, and the following year he married his brother's fiancée, Princess Mary of Teck.

He succeeded his father in May 1910 in the middle of a constitutional crisis over Lloyd George's “People's Budget,” and within fifteen months had given Asquith a guarantee to create enough Liberal peers to pass the Parliament Act of 1911, breaking the absolute veto of the House of Lords. He was the only British sovereign to be crowned a second time, at the Delhi Durbar of December 1911, as Emperor of India.

The First World War set him against his cousin Wilhelm II of Germany and beside, but ultimately unable to save, his cousin Nicholas II of Russia, to whose family his government declined asylum in 1917 from fear of republican feeling at home. In July of that year he renounced the dynasty's German titles and took the surname Windsor. He visited the Western Front five times, opened the Cenotaph in 1920, and conducted the broadcasts that began the Christmas-Day royal address in 1932.

His reign saw the Easter Rising and the Irish Free State settlement, the Statute of Westminster of 1931, the Round Table Conferences with Mohandas Gandhi over Indian self-rule, and the long parliamentary careers of Stanley Baldwin and Ramsay MacDonald, with Winston Churchill on the back benches. Bronchial illness and his First World War injuries weakened him steadily after 1928. He died at Sandringham on the evening of 20 January 1936, helped on by his physician Lord Dawson, succeeded by his eldest son as Edward VIII. His granddaughter the future Elizabeth II, then nine, was the favourite child of his last years.
