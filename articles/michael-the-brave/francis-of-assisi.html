Francis of Assisi

Founder of the Franciscan order, c. 1181–1226

Francis was born about 1181 in the Umbrian town of Assisi, the son of a prosperous cloth merchant, and spent his youth in pleasure and dreams of knightly glory. War with neighbouring Perugia brought him a year in prison and a long illness, and from that crisis grew a slow, shattering conversion. He began to nurse lepers and to pray in ruined churches, and at last, summoned before the bishop by a father enraged at his charity, he stripped off his fine clothes in the public square and renounced his inheritance to wed what he called Lady Poverty.

Barefoot and penniless, he drew others to him, and around 1209 he sought out Pope Innocent III, who, despite misgivings, blessed the little brotherhood that became the Friars Minor. Francis preached repentance across Italy and beyond; with Clare of Assisi he founded an order for women, and during the Fifth Crusade he crossed the lines into Egypt to preach peace before the sultan al-Kamil. His tenderness toward all creation—the sermon to the birds, the Canticle of the Sun that praised Brother Sun and Sister Moon, the living Nativity he staged at Greccio—gave Christianity a new and gentler face.

In 1224, praying on the mountain of La Verna, he is said to have received the stigmata, the first recorded bearer of the wounds of Christ. Worn out and nearly blind, he died in 1226 at the little chapel of the Porziuncola, and was canonised within two years. His life so seized the imagination of the age that the greatest painters and poets returned to it again and again, and he remains among the best loved of all the saints, patron of Italy and of the natural world he embraced as kin.
