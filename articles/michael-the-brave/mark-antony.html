Mark Antony

Roman general and triumvir, 83–30 BC

Marcus Antonius rose as a cavalry commander and trusted lieutenant of Julius Caesar, serving in Gaul and through the civil war, and holding Italy while Caesar campaigned. He was consul in 44 BC, sharing office with the dictator when Caesar was struck down on the Ides of March. Antony’s funeral oration over the body, turning the Roman crowd against the assassins, made him for a moment the master of Rome.

The arrival of Caesar’s teenaged heir, Octavian, complicated everything. After fighting and then reconciling, the two joined with Lepidus to form the Second Triumvirate in 43 BC, sealing their pact with bloody proscriptions in which Cicero was killed. At Philippi in 42 BC they destroyed the armies of Brutus and Cassius, avenging Caesar, and then divided the Roman world. Antony took the wealthy East.

There he met Cleopatra, queen of Egypt, and their alliance became one of the most famous love affairs in history. He married Octavian’s sister Octavia for the sake of peace, but his open devotion to Cleopatra and his grants of Roman territory to her children—the Donations of Alexandria—gave Octavian the propaganda to brand him a traitor enslaved by a foreign queen. War followed. At the naval battle of Actium in 31 BC their fleet was broken, and the lovers fled to Egypt. The next year, with Octavian closing in, Antony took his own life, dying in Cleopatra’s arms as the Republic gave way to the empire.
