Robert Schumann

German Romantic composer, 1810–1856

Schumann was born in Zwickau, the son of a bookseller, and grew up steeped in literature as much as music. Sent to study law, he abandoned it for the piano, but an injury to his hand, possibly from a misguided strengthening device, ended his hopes of a virtuoso career and turned him toward composition and criticism. In 1834 he founded the Neue Zeitschrift für Musik, which he edited with brilliance, using it to champion the gifted young musicians of his day.

His personal life centred on Clara Wieck, the daughter of his teacher and one of the foremost pianists of the century. Only after a long and bitter lawsuit against her opposing father were the two able to marry, in 1840. That year released in him a flood of song; there followed symphonies, a beloved piano concerto, chamber music, and the intimate piano cycles, Carnaval, Kinderszenen, and the great Fantasie, that capture the poetic, inward spirit of musical Romanticism.

As a critic Schumann possessed an unrivalled ear for genius. He acclaimed the young Chopin with the cry "Hats off, gentlemen, a genius!", unearthed Schubert’s long-lost "Great" C-major Symphony, and near the end of his life hailed the unknown Brahms as the awaited master of the future. His own mind, however, was failing; after attempting to drown himself in the Rhine in 1854 he was confined to an asylum, where he died two years later.

The road continues to Felix Mendelssohn.
