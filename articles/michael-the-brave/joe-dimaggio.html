Joe DiMaggio

American baseball player, 1914–1999.

Joseph Paul DiMaggio was born in Martinez, California, the eighth of nine children of Sicilian fishermen who had emigrated from Isola delle Femmine to the wharves of San Francisco. He played his first professional baseball with the San Francisco Seals of the Pacific Coast League, where in 1933 he hit safely in sixty-one consecutive games. The New York Yankees bought his contract, and from his major-league debut in 1936 he played centre field in pinstripes for thirteen seasons, broken only by three years in the army during the Second World War.

His swing was famously economical, his outfield work effortless, and his demeanour stoic to the point of remoteness. He won three American League Most Valuable Player awards, two batting titles, two home-run titles, and a place on the All-Star team in every season he played. In the summer of 1941 he hit safely in fifty-six consecutive games, a streak whose statistical improbability has kept it in place ever since as one of the records most often nominated as unbreakable. The Yankees won nine World Series during his tenure, and he was elected to the Hall of Fame three years after his 1951 retirement.

His personal life enlarged him into a national icon beyond the sport. In 1939 he married the starlet Dorothy Arnold, with whom he had a son; in January 1954 he married Marilyn Monroe at San Francisco City Hall, an event covered as it had been a coronation. The marriage lasted nine months — broken by his jealousy of her career and the photographers' delight in her skirt-blowing-up scene from The Seven Year Itch — but they remained close, and after her death in 1962 he had roses delivered to her crypt at Westwood three times a week for twenty years. Hemingway named him in The Old Man and the Sea, Simon and Garfunkel asked where he had gone, and his image as the modest American hero outlived almost everyone who had played against him.
