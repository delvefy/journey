Galba

Roman emperor, 3 BCE–69 CE

Servius Sulpicius Galba was the first of the four men to claim the throne in the chaotic year that followed Nero's fall, and the first Roman emperor drawn from outside the Julio-Claudian house. Born in 3 BCE to an ancient and wealthy noble family, he enjoyed a long and respected public career, governing in turn Aquitania, Upper Germany, Africa, and finally Hispania Tarraconensis under a succession of emperors from Tiberius to Nero.

In 68 CE, as revolts flared against Nero, Galba threw in his lot with the rebellion and let his soldiers hail him emperor. When Nero's nerve broke and he took his own life, the Senate recognised Galba, and the aged governor marched on Rome as master of the empire, ending the dynasty of Augustus.

His brief reign was a failure. Now nearly seventy, he proved harsh, miserly, and easily led by grasping favourites. He alienated the soldiers above all by refusing to pay the bounty that had been promised in his name, declaring that he chose his troops and did not buy them—a principle admirable in the abstract and fatal in practice.

The end came swiftly. When Galba passed over the ambitious Otho and adopted the young Piso as his heir, Otho bought the loyalty of the Praetorian Guard, who cut Galba down in the Forum in January 69 after barely seven months of rule. The historian Tacitus delivered the lasting epitaph: that everyone would have judged him fit to rule, had he only never ruled.

The road continues to Otho.
