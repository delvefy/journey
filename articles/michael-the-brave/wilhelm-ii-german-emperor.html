Kaiser Wilhelm II

Last German Emperor and King of Prussia, 1859–1941

Grandson of Queen Victoria through his mother and of the first German Emperor through his father, Wilhelm came to the throne in 1888. Born with a withered left arm, vain, impulsive, and craving admiration, he chafed at once under the tutelage of the great chancellor Otto von Bismarck, and in 1890 he “dropped the pilot,” dismissing the architect of German unity and taking the direction of policy into his own restless hands.

His “personal rule” proved disastrous for the peace of Europe. He pursued Weltpolitik—a place in the sun, colonies, and above all a great battle fleet that alarmed and antagonised Britain—and his bombastic speeches and clumsy diplomacy isolated Germany among suspicious neighbours. Bound to Austria-Hungary, he gave it the “blank cheque” of support in the crisis of 1914 that helped tip Europe into the First World War, a catastrophe he had blustered toward without ever quite intending.

During the war real power passed to his generals, and the Kaiser became a figurehead. As defeat closed in and revolution broke out in 1918, he was forced to abdicate and fled to the Netherlands, ending the German monarchy and the Hohenzollern line. He lived out more than two decades in comfortable exile at Doorn, embittered and unrepentant, dying there in 1941 as the armies of a new German dictator overran Europe.
