Maximian

Roman emperor, c. 250–310 AD

Maximian was born around 250 near Sirmium in Pannonia, a rough soldier of peasant stock who became the trusted comrade-in-arms of Diocletian. In 286 Diocletian raised him to co-emperor as Augustus of the West, and the two ruled as partners—Diocletian placing himself under the patronage of Jupiter, Maximian under that of Hercules.

From his capitals at Trier and Milan he defended the western provinces: he crushed the great peasant revolt of the Bagaudae in Gaul, beat back Germanic raids across the Rhine, and secured North Africa against Berber incursions. When Diocletian formalised the Tetrarchy in 293, Maximian accepted Constantius as his junior Caesar.

In 305 Diocletian obliged him to abdicate at the same moment, but Maximian chafed in retirement. When his son Maxentius seized power in Rome in 306, the old emperor resumed the purple to back him, and he gave his daughter Fausta in marriage to the rising Constantine, binding the two men in alliance.

Restless and grasping, Maximian soon quarrelled with both his son and his son-in-law, tried to reclaim sole power, and at last turned openly against Constantine. Defeated and cornered at Massilia in 310, he was forced to take his own life. Though his memory was officially condemned, he had been one of the architects of the empire's recovery, and through his children he stood as father-in-law and grandfather to the dynasty that came after.
