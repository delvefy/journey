Martin Luther King Jr.

American civil rights leader, 1929–1968

The son of an Atlanta Baptist minister, King was a gifted preacher and scholar who took his doctorate in theology and embraced the nonviolent philosophy of Gandhi. Thrust into leadership at twenty-six by the Montgomery bus boycott, sparked when Rosa Parks refused to give up her seat, he became the voice of the movement to end racial segregation in the American South. Through the Southern Christian Leadership Conference he led campaigns of disciplined civil disobedience that met police dogs and fire hoses with dignity.

Jailed in Birmingham, he wrote a celebrated letter defending the moral duty to disobey unjust laws. In August 1963, before a quarter of a million people gathered at the Lincoln Memorial—a century after the Emancipation Proclamation—he delivered the “I Have a Dream” speech that became the conscience of a nation. The marches he led helped secure the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965, and that year he became the youngest man then to receive the Nobel Peace Prize.

In his last years he turned to the poverty that bound black and white alike and spoke out against the war in Vietnam, losing allies as he did so. On 4 April 1968, in Memphis, he was killed by an assassin’s bullet at the age of thirty-nine. A national holiday now bears his name. He had consciously placed his struggle in the lineage of the president who freed the slaves, Abraham Lincoln.
