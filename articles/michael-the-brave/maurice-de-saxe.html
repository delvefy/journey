Maurice de Saxe

Marshal General of France, 1696–1750

Maurice, Count of Saxony, was the foremost soldier in the service of France in the first half of the eighteenth century. Born in 1696, the illegitimate son of Augustus the Strong and the Swedish countess Aurora von Königsmarck, he took to war as a boy, campaigning in his early teens, and after a restless youth across the armies of Europe he settled into French service and made his name there.

His genius shone in the War of the Austrian Succession. At Fontenoy in 1745, carried to the field in a wicker litter because he was dying of dropsy, he directed the French army to a famous victory over the British and their allies under the Duke of Cumberland, with Louis XV himself looking on. He followed it by overrunning the Austrian Netherlands, taking Brussels and Antwerp, and winning again at Lawfeld, until a grateful king raised him to the rare supreme dignity of Marshal General of France.

He was also a reflective soldier. His treatise, the Reveries on the Art of War, written in a fever during a single fortnight, brimmed with original and eccentric ideas on tactics, morale, and the organisation of armies, and was pored over by later commanders, Napoleon among them.

His private life was no less vivid: he was the devoted lover of the celebrated tragic actress Adrienne Lecouvreur, and through an illegitimate line he became the great-great-grandfather of the novelist George Sand. Rewarded with the great château of Chambord, he died there in 1750.
