Pertinax

Roman emperor, r. 193

Publius Helvius Pertinax was born in 126, the son of a freedman timber-merchant, and rose from the humblest beginnings in the Roman world. He started life as a teacher of grammar before entering the army, where his talent carried him upward through the reign of Marcus Aurelius and the Marcomannic Wars to the consulship, the governorship of provinces and finally the prefecture of the city of Rome — a career of extraordinary social ascent.

When the tyrannical Commodus was strangled on the last night of 192, the conspirators turned to the respected old soldier Pertinax and proclaimed him emperor. He set at once to repair the ruin Commodus had left, restoring the public finances and discipline, but in doing so he denied the Praetorian Guard the lavish rewards they expected. After only eighty-six days the soldiers he had tried to govern mutinied, broke into the palace, and cut him down.

His death opened one of the most squalid episodes in Roman history, as the Praetorians put the empire up for sale and knocked it down to the highest bidder, Didius Julianus. But Pertinax was not forgotten: from the provinces Septimius Severus marched on Rome proclaiming himself the avenger of the murdered emperor, deified him, and took the name Pertinax into his own. He stands as the first of the five emperors who contended for power in the chaos of the year 193.
