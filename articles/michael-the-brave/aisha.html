Aisha

Wife of the Prophet and early Muslim authority, c.614–678

Aisha, daughter of Abu Bakr, was born in Mecca around 614 and married the Prophet Muhammad in Medina, becoming, after the death of Khadija, the youngest and most beloved of his wives. Quick-minded and strong-willed, she lived at the very centre of the early community. When malicious tongues accused her of misconduct after she was accidentally left behind on a journey, the resulting crisis — the “affair of the slander” — was resolved, the tradition holds, by a revelation that cleared her name and rebuked her accusers.

Honoured as a Mother of the Believers, she became after Muhammad’s death one of the great authorities of the young religion. She had absorbed the Prophet’s words and ways at first hand, and she transmitted them in more than two thousand traditions, becoming a respected teacher of law, scripture and the life of Muhammad to whom companions and scholars turned for guidance.

She did not stay aloof from the bitter politics that followed the murder of the caliph Uthman. Opposing the new caliph Ali, she joined Talha and Zubayr in raising a force against him, and in 656 she was present, seated in a litter upon a camel, at the battle that took its name from her mount — the first great clash of Muslim against Muslim. Ali’s side prevailed; Aisha was treated with honour and escorted home to Medina, where she withdrew from public strife. She died there in 678, remembered as one of the most learned and influential women in the history of Islam.
