Antonio Salieri

Italian composer and Viennese court conductor, 1750–1825

Born in the Italian town of Legnago, Salieri was brought as a youth to Vienna, where the composer Gluck took him up and guided his career. He rose to become imperial court composer and conductor under the Habsburg emperors, a position of great influence that he held for decades, and he was among the most successful opera composers of his day, winning triumphs not only in Vienna but in Italy and at Paris.

At the centre of Viennese musical life, he was a contemporary and, in matters of court appointments and opera commissions, a rival of Mozart. A persistent later legend, dramatised in poetry, opera, and film, held that he had poisoned the younger man out of jealousy, but there is no historical foundation for the story, and the two appear to have respected each other’s gifts. The myth has unfairly overshadowed his real achievements.

Salieri’s most enduring importance may lie in his teaching. As the leading musical authority in Vienna he instructed an astonishing roster of pupils, among them the young Beethoven, Franz Schubert, and the future virtuoso Franz Liszt, as well as Czerny and Hummel. Generous with his students, many of whom he taught without fee, he helped pass the Viennese tradition from the classical masters to the rising romantic generation. Honoured in his lifetime and laden with official dignities, he died in Vienna in 1825.
