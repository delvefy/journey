Francis II, Holy Roman Emperor

Last Holy Roman Emperor; first Emperor of Austria, 1768–1835

Francis II was the last man to wear the crown of the Holy Roman Empire and the first to bear the new title of Emperor of Austria. Born in Florence in 1768, son of Leopold II, he came to the throne in 1792 just as the French Revolution turned to war, and almost his whole reign was consumed by the long contest with revolutionary and Napoleonic France.

Again and again his armies were beaten. The worst blow fell at Austerlitz in 1805, the “Battle of the Three Emperors,” where Napoleon shattered the joint forces of Francis and the Russian tsar. Reading the times, Francis had already proclaimed himself hereditary Emperor of Austria in 1804, and in 1806, with Napoleon dominant in Germany, he laid down the ancient imperial crown and declared the thousand-year-old Holy Roman Empire dissolved.

To gain a respite and bind the conqueror to his house, he married his daughter Marie Louise to Napoleon in 1810. When the tide finally turned, his chancellor Metternich guided the diplomacy that brought Napoleon down and, at the Congress of Vienna, restored Austria to the first rank and set it at the centre of a conservative new order.

His later years were cautious and repressive, haunted by the fear of revolution. He died in 1835, an emperor of a different kind than his ancestors had been. The sovereign who had shared his defeat at Austerlitz and his triumph at Vienna was the Russian emperor Alexander I.
