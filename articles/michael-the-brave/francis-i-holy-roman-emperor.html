Francis I, Holy Roman Emperor

Francis Stephen of Lorraine, founder of Habsburg-Lorraine, 1708–1765

Francis I was the husband of Maria Theresa and the founder of the line of Habsburg-Lorraine that would reign in Vienna until 1918. Born at Nancy in 1708, heir to the Duchy of Lorraine, he was brought as a boy to the court of Charles VI, where he grew up alongside the emperor's daughter and won her lasting love.

That love cost him his patrimony. To settle the War of the Polish Succession and remove the objections to his marriage, Francis agreed to give up ancestral Lorraine, which passed toward France, accepting in its place the Grand Duchy of Tuscany. In 1736 he married Maria Theresa, and the union proved both affectionate and astonishingly fruitful, producing sixteen children.

When Charles VI died in 1740, his daughter inherited the Habsburg dominions and made Francis her co-regent, and in 1745, after a brief Bavarian interruption, he was elected Holy Roman Emperor. Yet everyone understood that the real ruler was his wife. Francis, content with the dignity, devoted himself to what he did brilliantly—managing money and enterprise, until he had made his family enormously rich—and to his private enthusiasms for science and Freemasonry.

His children carried the dynasty into the heart of European history; among them were the emperor Joseph II and the ill-fated Marie Antoinette of France. Francis died suddenly at Innsbruck in 1765. The crown of the Empire passed first to his eldest son and, in time, to a second son who reigned as Leopold II.

The road continues to Leopold II, Holy Roman Emperor.
