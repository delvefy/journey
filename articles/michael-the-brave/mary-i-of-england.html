Mary I of England

Queen of England and Ireland, “Bloody Mary”, 1516–1558

Mary was born at the Palace of Placentia in Greenwich on 18 February 1516, the only surviving child of Henry VIII and Catherine of Aragon. Doted on through her early girlhood as the king's heir, she was given her own household at Ludlow as Princess of Wales in 1525 and a humanist education arranged by her mother and by the Spanish-born Juan Luis Vives. Her world collapsed with the king's pursuit of Anne Boleyn: the marriage of her parents was annulled in 1533, she was declared illegitimate by act of parliament in 1534, separated from her mother, and pressed to take the Oath of Supremacy. She submitted in 1536 only after Catherine's death and the king's threats had broken her.

Restored to the succession by the Third Succession Act of 1544, behind her half-brother Edward, she stood out under his reign of 1547–53 as the most prominent Catholic of the realm and continued to hear the Latin Mass in private against statute. On Edward's death she defeated the bid of John Dudley to set Lady Jane Grey on the throne, riding into London on 3 August 1553 amid genuine popular acclaim, and the next year married her cousin Philip of Spain by proxy. Her parliament restored papal authority in 1554 under the legation of Cardinal Pole, and from 1555 her bishops began the burnings, nearly three hundred in three years, that fixed her name as "Bloody Mary" in Foxe's Book of Martyrs — Cranmer, Ridley, and Latimer among them.

Foreign policy followed her marriage. England joined Spain's war against France and lost Calais, the last continental possession, on 7 January 1558. She had hoped to bear an heir who would entrench the Catholic restoration; two phantom pregnancies in 1554–55 and 1557–58 destroyed the project. She named her half-sister Elizabeth her successor on her deathbed at St James's Palace and died of an influenza epidemic on 17 November 1558. Within two years her religious settlement had been undone.
