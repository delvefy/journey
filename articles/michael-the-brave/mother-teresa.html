Mother Teresa

1910–1997, Albanian-Indian Catholic nun and missionary, founder of the Missionaries of Charity

Anjezë Gonxhe Bojaxhiu was born in Skopje, then in the Ottoman vilayet of Kosovo and now in North Macedonia, to a family of ethnic Albanian Catholics whose father, a builder and grocer, died when she was eight. She received her schooling in the local Sacred Heart parish, was drawn early to missionary stories from Bengal, and at eighteen entered the Sisters of Loreto in Rathfarnham near Dublin to learn English. She took the name Teresa after Thérèse of Lisieux and the following year sailed for India.

For seventeen years she taught and from 1944 served as principal of St Mary's School for girls at Entally in Calcutta, run by Loreto for the daughters of the city's professional families. On a railway journey to Darjeeling in September 1946 she received what she later described as “the call within the call,” an inner instruction to leave the convent and live and work among the destitute of the city's streets. After three years of negotiation she received permission in 1948, took out Indian citizenship, did a short nursing course at the Holy Family Hospital in Patna, and put on for the first time the white sari with three blue stripes.

She founded the Missionaries of Charity in October 1950 in two small rooms on the Lower Circular Road. The home for the dying at Kalighat opened in 1952; the leprosy ambulatories from 1957; the children's home Shishu Bhavan from 1955. Indian officials including Jawaharlal Nehru and his daughter Indira Gandhi furnished public support, and the BBC documentary by Malcolm Muggeridge in 1969 made her internationally known. By her death the order had grown to 4,000 sisters in 123 countries.

She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979 and asked that the banquet money go to her work. Her friendships extended unusually widely — with Pope John Paul II, who beatified her in 2003 and called her his “icon of mercy”; with Princess Diana of Wales, with whom she met in New York and Rome in the months before both their deaths; with critics such as Christopher Hitchens. She died of heart failure in Calcutta on 5 September 1997, five days after Diana, and was canonized by Pope Francis in St Peter's Square in 2016.

The road continues to Pope Francis.
