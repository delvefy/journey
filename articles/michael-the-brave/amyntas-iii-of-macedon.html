Amyntas III of Macedon

King of Macedon, r. c. 393–370 BCE

Amyntas III was king of Macedon for some two decades in the early fourth century BCE, and is remembered above all as the founder of the dynasty's final greatness: he was the father of Philip II and the grandfather of Alexander the Great. He came to the throne around 393 BCE amid the chronic instability that plagued the Macedonian monarchy.

His reign was a long struggle for survival. Early on he was driven from his kingdom by an Illyrian invasion and recovered it only with difficulty and outside help. He faced lasting pressure from the powerful Greek city of Olynthus and its Chalcidian League to the south, against which he appealed to Sparta, and he guarded his frontiers through a shifting web of alliances, including one with the ambitious Jason of Pherae in Thessaly.

By patience and diplomacy rather than conquest he contrived to pass on an intact kingdom, a rare achievement for a Macedonian king of his age. His wife Eurydice bore him three sons, each of whom would reign in turn.

He died around 370 BCE and was succeeded first by his elder sons, Alexander II and Perdiccas III, whose short and violent reigns prepared the way for the youngest, Philip, to remake Macedon entirely.
