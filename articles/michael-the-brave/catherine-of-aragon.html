Catherine of Aragon

First queen consort of Henry VIII, 1485–1536

Catherine was born on 16 December 1485 in the archbishop’s palace at Alcalá de Henares, the youngest surviving daughter of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile. Her childhood was spent in the wandering court of the Catholic Monarchs, beside the campaigns that took Granada in 1492 and dispatched Columbus the same year. Educated by the Spanish humanist Antonio de Nebrija and a circle of women tutors, she could read and write in Latin from a young age; Erasmus would later praise her scholarship. At three she was betrothed to Arthur, Prince of Wales, and at fifteen she landed at Plymouth to marry him. He died five months later at Ludlow.

For seven years she was a widow in England, kept short of money by both fathers, dignified in poverty. The death of Henry VII and her father-in-law’s diplomatic shift made her marriageable again to her brother-in-law. Pope Julius II issued the dispensation that allowed her to marry Henry VIII in June 1509, six weeks after his accession. The first half of their marriage was a partnership: she was made regent during his French campaign of 1513, and the army that destroyed the Scots at Flodden moved in her name. Of perhaps six pregnancies only one child survived past infancy, the future Mary I, born in 1516.

From 1525 Henry began to argue that the marriage had violated Leviticus and was the cause of his lack of a son. Catherine refused every demand to retire to a nunnery or to concede the validity of her first marriage to Arthur. The legate’s court at Blackfriars in 1529 came to no decision; Pope Clement VII, prisoner of her nephew Charles V, would not annul the marriage. After Cranmer’s annulment in May 1533 she was demoted to “Princess Dowager,” confined to Kimbolton, and separated from her daughter. She refused the title to the last. She died at Kimbolton on 7 January 1536, having written a final letter to the king that signed itself “Katherine the Queen.”
