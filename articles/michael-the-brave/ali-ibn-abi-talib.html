Ali ibn Abi Talib

Fourth caliph and first Shia imam, c.600–661

Ali was born about 600 in Mecca, the son of Abu Talib, the uncle who had sheltered the orphaned Muhammad. Raised in the Prophet’s own household, he became, as a youth, among the first to embrace Islam. He married Muhammad’s daughter Fatima, and their sons Hasan and Husayn were cherished as the Prophet’s grandchildren. A warrior of legendary courage, wielding the sword called Zulfiqar, he fought in the great battles of the early community and once lay down in Muhammad’s bed on the night of the flight to Medina to draw off would-be assassins.

When Muhammad died in 632 the leadership passed first to Abu Bakr, then to Umar and Uthman, while Ali waited; the dispute over whether he should have succeeded at once lies at the root of the division between Sunni and Shia Islam, the latter holding that the Prophet had marked him out as heir. At last, after the murder of Uthman in 656, Ali was acclaimed the fourth of the Rightly-Guided Caliphs.

His reign was swallowed by civil war. He faced a revolt led by the Prophet’s widow Aisha at the Battle of the Camel, fought the Syrian governor Muawiya to a bloody stalemate at Siffin, and turned against the Kharijites who broke from his cause. Governing from Kufa in Iraq, renowned for his justice, eloquence and piety, he was struck down by a Kharijite assassin’s poisoned blade while at prayer in 661. Revered by Sunnis as the last of the Rashidun and by Shia as the first Imam, Ali stands among the most venerated figures in all of Islam.
