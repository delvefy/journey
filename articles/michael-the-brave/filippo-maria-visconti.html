Filippo Maria Visconti

Last Visconti Duke of Milan, 1392–1447

Filippo Maria Visconti was born in 1392, the younger son of Gian Galeazzo Visconti, the first duke of Milan. He inherited a state in collapse: after his father’s sudden death in 1402 the Visconti dominions had splintered, and his elder brother Giovanni Maria, a byword for cruelty, was murdered in 1412. Filippo Maria then claimed the duchy and set about reconquering it city by city, leaning on hired captains — among them the rising Francesco Sforza — to rebuild the patrimony his father had assembled.

He was one of the strangest rulers of the age. Physically ungainly and morbidly suspicious, he shut himself inside the castle of Porta Giovia, screened by guards, trusting almost no one, governing through fear and a web of informers. Yet he was a shrewd and tenacious prince. For three decades he fought the long Wars in Lombardy against Venice and against the Florence of the Medici, hiring and discarding the great condottieri of the day and playing them against one another so that none grew strong enough to threaten him.

His most consequential act was a marriage. In 1441, to bind his most dangerous captain to his house, he gave his only child, the illegitimate Bianca Maria, to Francesco Sforza. When Filippo Maria died in 1447 with no legitimate son, the male line of the Visconti died with him. Milan proclaimed a republic, but within three years his son-in-law had taken the ducal crown, and the Sforza succeeded the dynasty that had made the duchy.
