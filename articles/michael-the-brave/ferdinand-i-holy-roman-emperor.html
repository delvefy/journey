Ferdinand I, Holy Roman Emperor

Holy Roman Emperor, 1503–1564

Ferdinand was born in Spain in 1503, the younger brother of the Emperor Charles V. When Charles came into the immense Habsburg inheritance, he entrusted the Austrian hereditary lands to Ferdinand, who from the early 1520s governed central Europe as archduke and as his brother’s deputy in the Holy Roman Empire.

His fortunes were transformed by marriage and disaster. Wed to Anna of Bohemia and Hungary, he claimed both crowns when her brother, King Louis II, was killed in the catastrophic defeat by the Ottomans at the Battle of Mohács in 1526. The acquisition founded the Habsburg dominion over Bohemia and Hungary, but it placed Ferdinand on the front line against the advancing Turks, and he was forced to defend Vienna itself against the great army of Suleiman the Magnificent in 1529.

Within the Empire his task was to hold together a realm torn by the Reformation. Patient and conciliatory where his brother was rigid, he was the chief architect of the Peace of Augsburg in 1555, which allowed each German prince to choose between the Lutheran and Catholic faiths and brought a generation of uneasy calm. When Charles V abdicated, Ferdinand succeeded him as emperor in 1558 and ruled until his death in 1564, the founder of the Austrian line of the House of Habsburg.
