Cyril of Alexandria

Patriarch of Alexandria and Church Father, c.376–444

Cyril was born about 376 in Egypt and raised in the orbit of his uncle Theophilus, the powerful patriarch of Alexandria, whom he succeeded in that office in 412. He took up the see with a combative energy that he wielded against rivals within the Church and against the city’s Jewish and pagan communities alike. His tenure was scarred by violent strife with the imperial prefect Orestes, and it was in this poisonous atmosphere that a mob of his partisans murdered the philosopher Hypatia, a deed whose shadow has clung to his name ever since, however much his own part in it is debated.

Yet Cyril’s lasting fame rests on doctrine. When Nestorius, patriarch of Constantinople, objected to calling the Virgin Mary Theotokos, “God-bearer,” and seemed to divide Christ into two separate persons, Cyril rose as the champion of Christ’s undivided unity. Modelling himself on his great predecessor Athanasius, the earlier defender of Alexandrian orthodoxy, he marshalled scripture, tradition and the support of Rome against his opponent.

At the Council of Ephesus in 431 he presided over the condemnation and deposition of Nestorius and the triumphant affirmation of the Theotokos. His formulations of the person of Christ became a touchstone for later Christianity, honoured at Chalcedon and claimed as well by the churches that rejected it. Revered as a saint and Doctor of the Church across many traditions, Cyril died in 444, remembered both as a ruthless ecclesiastical politician and as one of the most influential theologians of the early Church.
