Margaret Thatcher

British Prime Minister, 1925–2013

Margaret Hilda Roberts was born in 1925 above her father's grocer's shop in Grantham, Lincolnshire. Alfred Roberts was a Methodist lay preacher and town alderman, and the household's Wesleyan thrift, self-help and civic seriousness stayed with her for life. She read chemistry at Somerville College, Oxford, worked briefly as a research chemist while reading for the Bar, married the businessman Denis Thatcher in 1951, and was elected Conservative MP for Finchley in 1959.

She served as Education Secretary in Edward Heath's government and in 1975 deposed him as Conservative leader. In May 1979 she became Britain's first woman Prime Minister, on a programme of monetarism, deregulation, lower direct taxation, council-house sales, and confrontation with the trade unions. The 1981 budget defended a tight monetary policy through the deepest recession since the war; the 1982 victory over Argentina in the Falklands restored her authority; the 1984–85 defeat of the National Union of Mineworkers under Arthur Scargill broke the post-war industrial settlement. She survived an IRA bomb at the Brighton Grand Hotel in 1984.

She privatised British Telecom, British Gas, British Steel, British Airways, and the regional water and electricity boards, and rewrote the British economy around financial services, ownership shares, and a deregulated City after the 1986 Big Bang. On the world stage she stood beside Ronald Reagan in pressing the Soviet Union toward the end of the Cold War, calling Mikhail Gorbachev "a man one can do business with" before her own government had reached the same view. The Single European Act of 1986 was largely her work; so was her growing hostility to the deeper political union that followed.

She won three general elections, governed for eleven years—the longest tenure of any twentieth-century British Prime Minister—and was forced out by her own Cabinet in November 1990 after a leadership challenge over Europe and the poll tax. She was made Baroness Thatcher of Kesteven in 1992 and died in London in 2013. Throughout all eleven years of her premiership the head of state with whom she met each week, the sovereign of an entirely different temper, was Queen Elizabeth II.
