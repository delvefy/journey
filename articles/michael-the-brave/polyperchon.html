Polyperchon

Macedonian general and regent, c. 394–303 BCE

Polyperchon was a veteran Macedonian commander who, late in a long life, was unexpectedly thrust into the highest office in the empire and then steadily lost it. He had served with distinction under Alexander the Great, fighting in the great Asian battles and the Indian campaign, and was already an old man when Alexander died.

His moment came in 319 BCE, when the dying regent Antipater, distrusting his own ambitious son Cassander, named Polyperchon to succeed him as regent of Macedon. Cassander refused to accept it, and a destructive civil war followed. To win allies, Polyperchon issued a ringing proclamation restoring freedom and democracy to the Greek cities—a stroke that, among its consequences, brought down the conservative Athenian statesman Phocion, condemned and executed by his own restored democracy in 318.

Polyperchon allied himself with Alexander's mother Olympias and with the brilliant Eumenes, but Cassander gradually got the upper hand, driving him from Macedon into the Peloponnese.

Reduced to a regional warlord, Polyperchon made his peace where he could; about 309 BCE, at Cassander's prompting, he murdered the young Heracles, Alexander's son, extinguishing a last claimant. He fades from the record soon afterward, an old soldier who had briefly held an empire and could not keep it.

The road continues to Phocion.
