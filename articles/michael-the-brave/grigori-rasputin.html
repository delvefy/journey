Grigori Rasputin

1869–1916, Russian Orthodox mystic, intimate of the imperial family

Grigori Yefimovich Rasputin was born in Pokrovskoye, a Siberian village on the Tura River in Tobolsk province, the son of a peasant. He worked as a carter and farmer, married young, and fathered seven children before, in his late twenties, undergoing what later admirers and detractors called a religious conversion. He spent the next several years on pilgrimage through the monasteries of European Russia and Mount Athos, returning home as a recognized starets — a holy elder — whose prayers and untrained but charismatic preaching attracted local followers.

In 1903 he reached St Petersburg, recommended to the city's church hierarchy by provincial bishops. His combination of unwashed peasant manners with piercing eyes and seeming clairvoyance made him a curiosity in the salons of devout aristocratic women. In November 1905 the Grand Duchess Militza presented him to Tsar Nicholas II and Tsaritsa Alexandra Feodorovna. From 1906 he was occasionally summoned to attend their haemophiliac son, the Tsarevich Alexei, and the abrupt relief of his attacks — whether by hypnotic suggestion, by removing the children's aspirin then in fashion, or by other means — convinced Alexandra that her son's life depended on the Siberian peasant.

Through the next decade Rasputin's influence at court was disputed by the press, by ministers, and by the Holy Synod, the more so during the First World War when Nicholas was at the front and Alexandra ran appointments by Rasputin's advice. Drunken nights, allegations of debauchery with society women, and the dismissal or appointment of senior ministers at his urging undermined what remained of dynastic prestige.

On the night of 30 December 1916 a small conspiracy of Prince Felix Yusupov, the right-wing deputy Vladimir Purishkevich and Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich invited him to the Moika Palace, fed him poisoned wine and cakes, shot him repeatedly, and finally pushed him under the ice of the Malaya Nevka. He died at forty-seven, and within ten weeks the dynasty he had shadowed had fallen.
