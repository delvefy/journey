Nero

Last Julio-Claudian emperor of Rome, AD 37–68

Nero was born Lucius Domitius Ahenobarbus in AD 37, son of the ambitious Agrippina the Younger, a sister of the emperor Caligula and great-granddaughter of Augustus. When Agrippina married her uncle, the emperor Claudius, she pressed him to adopt her son and advance him ahead of his own boy Britannicus. On Claudius's death in 54—widely rumoured to be by poison—the sixteen-year-old Nero became master of the Roman world.

His early years were guided by his tutor Seneca and the praetorian prefect Burrus, and the government was remembered as comparatively orderly. But Nero soon turned against those around him: Britannicus died at a banquet, and in 59 he had his own mother murdered. He scandalised Rome by performing publicly as a singer and charioteer, treating the empire as a stage for his artistic ambitions. During his reign Boudica's revolt convulsed Britain and was crushed with great slaughter.

In 64 the Great Fire devastated Rome; to deflect suspicion Nero blamed the city's Christians and launched the first imperial persecution of the faith, the traditional setting for the martyrdoms in Rome. He raised the vast Golden House on the ruins. A conspiracy in 65 led him to order the deaths of many, including Seneca. As provincial governors revolted and the Senate declared him a public enemy, Nero fled and took his own life in 68, reportedly lamenting "what an artist dies in me." His death ended the Julio-Claudian line and plunged Rome into the Year of the Four Emperors.
