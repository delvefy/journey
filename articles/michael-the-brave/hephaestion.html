Hephaestion

Macedonian general, c. 356–324 BCE

Hephaestion was the closest companion of Alexander the Great, his friend from boyhood and, in the eyes of antiquity, the Patroclus to Alexander's Achilles. A Macedonian nobleman of about the king's own age, he grew up at the court of Philip II and remained at Alexander's side through every stage of the conquest of the Persian Empire.

He held the highest honors the king could bestow. As commander of the elite Companion cavalry and later as chiliarch, the empire's second-ranking officer, he combined personal intimacy with real authority, and he supported Alexander's controversial policy of fusing the Macedonian and Persian elites. At the great mass wedding at Susa in 324 BCE, while Alexander married a daughter of Darius III, Hephaestion married her sister Drypetis, so that their children would be cousins.

His rivalry with the general Craterus was notorious, a tension Alexander managed by prizing each man in his own sphere. Educated, by tradition, alongside Alexander under Aristotle, he shared the king's tastes as well as his campaigns.

When Hephaestion died suddenly of a fever at Ecbatana in 324 BCE, Alexander's grief was overwhelming and theatrical: he is said to have cut his hair in mourning and demanded extraordinary honors for his friend. Within a year Alexander himself was dead, and the bond between them passed into legend as one of history's great friendships.
