Joanna of Castile

Queen of Castile and of Aragon, “Juana la Loca”, 1479–1555

Joanna was born at Toledo on 6 November 1479, the third child and second daughter of Ferdinand of Aragon and Isabella of Castile. She was educated as an infanta of the Catholic Monarchs alongside her brother Juan and her sisters Isabella, Maria, and Catherine, learning Latin from the humanist Beatriz Galindo, embroidering with her mother, and following the court through the long war against Granada. In 1496, at sixteen, she was sent by sea to the Low Countries to marry the eighteen-year-old Philip the Handsome, son of the Emperor Maximilian I and Mary of Burgundy, and Archduke of Austria, in the great double marriage that would bind Habsburg and Trastámara together.

The deaths of her elder siblings made her, by 1500, the heiress of Castile, Aragon, Naples, Sicily, and the Indies. Sworn in as Princess of Asturias in 1502, she returned to the Netherlands and bore Philip six surviving children, among them the future Charles V and Ferdinand I. Her mother's death in November 1504 made her Queen of Castile. Her father refused to surrender the regency, her husband refused to share power, and the marriage that had begun in passion soured into surveillance and confinement; contemporaries spoke of fits of jealous rage and prolonged refusals to eat or sleep. Philip died suddenly at Burgos on 25 September 1506, and she was reported to have travelled with his coffin by night across Castile, opening it to look at him.

From 1509 her father, and after 1517 her son Charles, kept her shut up in the royal palace of Tordesillas under the guard of a household that grew steadily more austere. Briefly proclaimed by the rebel Comuneros in 1520 as their lawful queen, she refused to sign their decrees, and the rising collapsed at Villalar in 1521. She remained at Tordesillas for the next thirty-four years, mostly silent, attended at the end by Francisco Borja and the Jesuits, and died there on Good Friday, 12 April 1555, fifty-one years after her accession to the Castilian throne.
