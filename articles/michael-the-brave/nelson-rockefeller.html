Nelson Rockefeller

American politician and Vice-President, 1908–1979

Nelson Aldrich Rockefeller was born on 8 July 1908 at Bar Harbor, Maine, the third child of John D. Rockefeller Jr and Abby Aldrich Rockefeller, and grandson of the Standard Oil founder John D. Rockefeller. He grew up between the family's vast estate at Pocantico Hills in the Hudson Valley, the great New York house on West 54th Street, and Bar Harbor. He was dyslexic, struggled at school, took a degree at Dartmouth, and then in his early twenties helped his mother plan the new Museum of Modern Art, becoming its president by his early thirties.

From 1933 he managed his family's interest in Rockefeller Center, the great Manhattan complex then under construction. It was in that capacity that he commissioned Diego Rivera to paint the lobby mural Man at the Crossroads; he had it chiselled from the wall when Rivera refused to remove a portrait of Lenin. He worked under President Franklin Roosevelt as Coordinator of Inter-American Affairs, building the wartime alliance with Latin America, and afterwards under Eisenhower as Under Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare.

In 1958 he was elected Governor of New York, an office he held through four terms and fifteen years. As Governor he tripled the state university system, expanded the state's highways and parks, and pushed through both the harsh Rockefeller Drug Laws of 1973 and a major liberalisation of abortion in 1970. He ran three times for the Republican presidential nomination—1960, 1964, and 1968—and was each time defeated by the conservative wing of the party, which never trusted him.

After Vice-President Spiro Agnew's resignation and President Richard Nixon's own resignation in Watergate, Gerald Ford appointed Rockefeller Vice-President in 1974, the only person other than Ford himself to fill the office through the 25th Amendment. He served two years, was dropped from the 1976 Republican ticket under conservative pressure, and retired to private business and his art collection. He died of a heart attack on 26 January 1979 at his Manhattan townhouse, in circumstances his staff worked hard to cover up. The grandfather whose oil monopoly had created the fortune that made every Rockefeller life possible, and who in his lifetime had become the richest person in modern history, was John D. Rockefeller.
