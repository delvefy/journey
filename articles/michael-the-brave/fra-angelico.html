Fra Angelico

Dominican friar and painter, c.1395–1455

Guido di Pietro was born about 1395 in the village of Vicchio in the Mugello, the same valley as Giotto. He was already a competent illuminator when, about 1420, he entered the reformed Dominican convent of San Domenico at Fiesole as Fra Giovanni; the byname Angelico, the angelic, came later, the writers of the next century insisting that nothing but a soul next to God could have painted what he painted. He took the prior’s vows under the strict observant reform of the Order, which would shape his subjects and the unworldly tenor of his colour. The early panels for San Domenico — the Annunciation now in the Prado, the Coronation of the Virgin in the Louvre — wedded a late-Gothic delicacy of gold and lapis to the new firm modelling and one-point perspective being worked out a few miles down the road in Florence.

In 1436 Cosimo de’ Medici bought the half-ruined Sylvestrine convent of San Marco in Florence, gave it to the observant Dominicans of San Domenico, and commissioned the architect Michelozzo to rebuild it and Fra Angelico to fresco it. Between 1438 and 1445 he and his workshop covered the cloister, chapter house, and forty-three friars’ cells with frescoes designed not for the public eye but for the daily contemplation of the community: an Annunciation at the head of the stairs, a Mocking of Christ on which Pilate’s hands and the soldiers’ spitting mouths float free in space, the Noli me tangere in cell 1, a Crucifixion with Saint Dominic embracing the cross in the chapter house. The library that Niccolò Niccoli’s books filled after his death is the room above.

In 1445 Eugenius IV called him to Rome to fresco the private chapel of his successor Nicholas V in the Vatican; the Cappella Niccolina, painted between 1447 and 1451 with the deacon-stories of Stephen and Lawrence, is the most accomplished of his later work. The same Pope is said to have offered him the archbishopric of Florence in 1446 and to have accepted his refusal. Fra Angelico spent his last years between Rome and Fiesole, prior of San Domenico in 1450–52, and died at the Dominican convent of Santa Maria sopra Minerva in Rome on 18 February 1455. He was buried there, the floor of his cell marked with a verse epitaph he had written for himself, and was beatified by John Paul II on 3 October 1982.
