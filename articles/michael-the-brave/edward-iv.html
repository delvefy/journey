Edward IV

King of England, first Yorkist king, 1442–1483

Edward of York was born at Rouen on 28 April 1442, the eldest surviving son of Richard, Duke of York, and Cecily Neville. He was Earl of March and a teenager when his father claimed the throne against Henry VI in 1460; the duke's death at Wakefield in December left Edward, at eighteen, the Yorkist heir. He won the bloody field of Mortimer's Cross in February 1461, entered London, was acclaimed king on 4 March, and on 29 March crushed the Lancastrian army at Towton in a snowstorm — by some counts the largest battle ever fought on English soil.

The first reign rested on his cousin and ally Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick. Edward broke with him in 1464 by secretly marrying Elizabeth Woodville, the widow of a Lancastrian knight, and afterwards lavishing patronage on her large family. Warwick allied with Edward's brother George of Clarence, drove the king out of England in October 1470, and restored the broken Henry VI. Edward returned in March 1471, landed at Ravenspur, defeated and killed Warwick at Barnet on 14 April, and the remaining Lancastrian army with its heir Prince Edward at Tewkesbury on 4 May. Henry VI died in the Tower within weeks, almost certainly murdered.

The second reign was peaceful, prosperous, and acquisitive. Edward financed himself through customs, forfeited estates, and "benevolences"; he invaded France in 1475 and exchanged his army for the pension promised at Picquigny by Louis XI; he patronised William Caxton, who set up England's first printing press at Westminster in 1476. The death of his brother Clarence — drowned, by tradition, in a butt of malmsey at the Tower in 1478 — closed the breach of 1470. Edward died unexpectedly at Westminster on 9 April 1483, aged forty, leaving the throne to his twelve-year-old son and the protectorate to his last surviving brother, Richard.

The road continues to Henry VI.
