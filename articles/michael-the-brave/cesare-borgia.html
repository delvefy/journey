Cesare Borgia

Duke of Valentinois and of the Romagna, 1475–1507

Cesare was born in September 1475, the natural son of the Spanish cardinal Rodrigo Borgia, vice-chancellor of the Roman church, by his Italian mistress Vannozza dei Cattanei. He was acknowledged from infancy, given clerical benefices in Spain as a child, and educated in canon law at Perugia and Pisa. When his father was elected pope in 1492 as Alexander VI, the eighteen-year-old Cesare became Archbishop of Valencia and the following year a cardinal. His elder brother Juan, made Duke of Gandia and captain-general of the church, was murdered in Rome in 1497, his throat cut and his body thrown into the Tiber. Whether or not Cesare ordered the killing — a rumour that pursued him for life — the path opened.

In August 1498 he became the first man ever to resign the cardinalate. Louis XII of France gave him the duchy of Valentinois and the hand of the king's cousin Charlotte d'Albret, and in return Cesare brokered the annulment that freed the king to marry Anne of Brittany. With French troops and papal money he embarked in 1499 on the conquest of the Romagna. In three campaigns he reduced Imola, Forlì, Pesaro, Rimini, Faenza, Urbino, and Camerino, and in 1501 was proclaimed Duke of the Romagna. At Senigallia in December 1502 he lured his rebellious condottieri — Vitellozzo Vitelli, Oliverotto da Fermo, the Orsini brothers — into a single room and had them strangled. Machiavelli, watching from the Florentine embassy, took notes that would later furnish Il Principe.

His state depended wholly on his father's life. When Alexander VI died in August 1503, Cesare lay sick of the same fever; the conclave that followed elected first Pius III and then, after twenty-six days, his enemy Della Rovere as Julius II. Stripped of his lands, betrayed in Naples by Gonzalo de Córdoba, he was sent in chains to Spain and locked up in the castle of La Mota. He escaped in 1506 and took refuge with his brother-in-law the King of Navarre, and on 12 March 1507, at the siege of Viana, he charged alone into an ambush and was killed by Castilian lances at thirty-one.

The road continues to Pope Alexander VI.
