Lucilla

Roman empress, c.149–182

Lucilla was a daughter of the emperor Marcus Aurelius and Faustina the Younger, born about 149 into the ruling house of Rome. While still in her teens she was married to her father’s co-emperor Lucius Verus and crowned Augusta, sharing the highest rank a Roman woman could hold. When Lucius died in 169 she was left a young widow, and her father, to bind a loyal general to the dynasty, gave her in a second marriage to Tiberius Claudius Pompeianus — a match she is said to have resented as beneath her station, though she kept her imperial title.

Her fall came with the reign of her brother. When Commodus succeeded their father as sole emperor in 180, Lucilla found herself eclipsed at court, forced to yield precedence to the new emperor’s wife. Bitterness hardened into conspiracy. In 182 she was drawn into a plot to murder Commodus; the assassin, lunging at the emperor with a drawn dagger, paused to announce that the Senate had sent it — and was seized before he could strike.

The plot collapsed, and Commodus took a terrible revenge on those involved. Lucilla was banished to the island of Capri and soon afterward put to death. A princess raised to the summit of the Roman world, she ended as one more victim of the tyranny her brother’s reign became.
