Licinius

Roman emperor of the East, c. 265–325 AD

Licinius was born of peasant stock in the Balkans around 265 and rose as a soldier and close companion of Galerius. At the Conference of Carnuntum in 308, summoned to repair the fracturing Tetrarchy, Galerius had him proclaimed Augustus, though for some years his real power lay in the Danube lands.

After Galerius died in 311, Licinius contended with Maximinus Daia for the eastern half of the empire. In 313 he sealed an alliance with Constantine by marrying Constantine's half-sister Constantia, and the two emperors jointly issued the Edict of Milan, granting Christians full toleration and the return of their confiscated property. That same year he crushed Maximinus Daia in battle and became master of the entire East.

For a decade Constantine and Licinius divided the Roman world between them, but the partnership was uneasy. A first war in 316 stripped Licinius of his European provinces, and tensions over religion and supreme authority festered until open war resumed in 324.

Constantine defeated him at Adrianople and again at Chrysopolis, and Licinius surrendered. Spared at first through the pleading of his wife Constantia, he was held under guard at Thessalonica but executed the following year, and his young son soon after. His fall left Constantine sole ruler of the Roman world, an event often taken to mark the true beginning of the Christian empire.

The road continues to Maximinus Daia.
