Richard Nixon

37th President of the United States, 1913–1994

Richard Milhous Nixon was born in 1913 in Yorba Linda, California, the second of five sons of a Quaker grocer. He worked his way through Whittier College and Duke Law School, served as a Navy supply officer in the South Pacific in the Second World War, and was elected to Congress from California's 12th district in 1946. His prosecution of the State Department official Alger Hiss before the House Un-American Activities Committee made him a national figure at thirty-five, and in 1952 Dwight Eisenhower took him on as running mate. He served as Vice-President for eight years.

He lost the 1960 presidential election narrowly to John F. Kennedy and the 1962 California gubernatorial race even more crushingly. Pronounced finished, he rebuilt himself from a Wall Street law firm and in 1968, running on law-and-order in a divided country, defeated Hubert Humphrey for the presidency. As President he opened the long Cold War isolation of the People's Republic of China with his February 1972 visit to Beijing and a celebrated week with Mao Zedong; he pursued détente with the Soviet Union in the SALT I arms-control treaty; he ended the American draft, ended the gold standard, founded the Environmental Protection Agency, and oversaw the Apollo moon landings inherited from his predecessors.

His foreign policy was largely the joint work of his national security adviser and then Secretary of State Henry Kissinger. The 1973 Paris Peace Accords brought American ground combat in Vietnam to a close. In the Bangladesh crisis of 1971 he tilted the United States sharply toward Pakistan against India, and quarrelled bitterly with Indira Gandhi.

The break-in at the Democratic National Committee offices in the Watergate complex in June 1972, ordered by his campaign and then covered up from the White House, unravelled across two years of Senate hearings, journalist reporting, and Supreme Court rulings. On 9 August 1974, facing certain impeachment, he became the only American President to resign the office. He retired to California, slowly rehabilitated himself as a foreign-policy elder, and died in 1994. The Chinese leader whose handshake in the Great Hall of the People closed twenty-three years of mutual non-recognition, and whose own pictured face on banknotes still hangs over Tiananmen Square, was Mao Zedong.
