Pedro Calderón de la Barca

Spanish dramatist, 1600–1681

Calderón was the last great master of the Spanish Golden Age theatre, the philosophical heir to Lope de Vega. Educated by the Jesuits, he refined and intellectualised the popular drama Lope had created, building tighter, more tightly reasoned plays freighted with theological and metaphysical weight. By the 1630s he was the leading playwright of the court, writing spectacular mythological dramas for the new palace theatre of his patron Philip IV, who knighted him into the Order of Santiago.

His masterpiece, Life Is a Dream (La vida es sueño), in which a prince raised in secret captivity wrestles with the unreality of human existence and the freedom of the will, is one of the supreme works of European drama. He excelled too in tense honour-tragedies such as The Mayor of Zalamea and The Surgeon of His Honour, and he perfected the auto sacramental, the allegorical religious play performed at Corpus Christi, of which he became the unrivalled author.

Ordained a priest in 1651, he withdrew somewhat from the public stage but continued to supply the court and the Church with plays until his death. When he died in 1681 the Spanish theatre’s golden century effectively died with him. His sombre brilliance was rediscovered by the German Romantics, who ranked him with Shakespeare, and Life Is a Dream has never left the world’s stage.
