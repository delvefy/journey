Perdiccas III of Macedon

King of Macedon, r. c. 365–359 BCE

Perdiccas III was king of Macedon for about six years, from 365 to 359 BCE, in the troubled generation before his youngest brother, Philip II, transformed the kingdom. A son of Amyntas III, he came to the throne by violence, killing the regent Ptolemy of Aloros, who had dominated Macedon and murdered Perdiccas's elder brother, Alexander II.

Once in power he showed a taste for Greek learning unusual in a Macedonian king. He drew scholars connected with Plato's Academy to his court, among them the Platonist Euphraeus, and is said to have taken a serious interest in philosophy and geometry even as he governed.

His reign was dominated, as his father's had been, by the threat from the Illyrians on the western frontier. In 359 BCE he led a large Macedonian army against them under their formidable king Bardylis. The battle was a catastrophe: Perdiccas was killed along with some four thousand of his men, and the kingdom lay open to invasion.

Out of this disaster his brother Philip seized first the regency and then the crown—and from the ruin of Perdiccas's defeat built the army that would conquer Greece and, under his son Alexander, the East.

The road continues to Bardylis.
