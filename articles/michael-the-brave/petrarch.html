Petrarch

Italian poet and scholar, "Father of Humanism", 1304–1374

Francesco Petrarca was born on 20 July 1304 at Arezzo, the elder son of Ser Petracco, a Florentine notary expelled from his city in the same White Guelph proscription that had banished Dante two years earlier. The family followed the papal court north and in 1311 settled at Avignon. Petrarch studied grammar and rhetoric at Carpentras under Convenevole da Prato and then law, against his inclination, at Montpellier and Bologna from 1316 to 1326. After his father's death he returned to Avignon, took minor orders, and entered the service of Cardinal Giovanni Colonna, which kept him in stipend and travel for two decades. On 6 April 1327, in the church of Sainte-Claire, he first saw the woman he called Laura — possibly Laure de Noves — who became the addressee of the Italian sonnets and canzoni he wrote and rewrote for the rest of his life.

From his thirties Petrarch reinvented what it meant to be a scholar. He scoured monastic libraries for the lost Latin classics, rediscovering Cicero's Pro Archia at Liège in 1333 and the great cache of Cicero's letters at Verona in 1345. He composed in elegant classical Latin his epic Africa on Scipio Africanus, the biographies De viris illustribus, the autobiographical Secretum (a dialogue with Saint Augustine), and the immense correspondence of the Rerum familiarium libri and Seniles which made the personal Latin letter a literary form again. On 8 April 1341 he was crowned poet laureate on the Capitoline in Rome, the first since antiquity. His climb of Mont Ventoux in 1336, in which the view from the summit dissolved into Augustinian self-reproach, has been read ever since as a charter for the Renaissance gaze.

Petrarch supported Cola di Rienzo's revival of the Roman Republic in 1347 and wrote to emperors and popes urging Italy's reunification and a crusade. From the 1350s he lived under the Visconti of Milan, then at Venice and Padua, and finally retired to Arquà in the Euganean Hills, often visited by Boccaccio. He died at Arquà on the night of 18–19 July 1374, found slumped over his Vergil.
