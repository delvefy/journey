Pope Urban VIII

Maffeo Barberini, pope 1623–1644

Maffeo Barberini was born in Florence in 1568 into a wealthy Tuscan family. Educated by the Jesuits in Rome and trained in law at Pisa, he rose through the curia as a protégé of his uncle Francesco, served as papal nuncio to the French court under Henri IV, and in 1606 received the red hat from Paul V. On the death of Gregory XV in 1623 the conclave elected him pope after a long August stalemate. He took the name Urban and would wear the tiara for almost twenty-one years, longer than any pope of the seventeenth century.

Urban’s Rome was the capital of the Baroque. He drained the swamp around the Vatican, fortified Castel Sant’Angelo, ordered new bastions for Civitavecchia, and stripped the bronze from the Pantheon’s portico to cast cannon and the great twisted baldachin that Gian Lorenzo Bernini raised over the tomb of St Peter. Bernini, working almost continuously under his patronage, also gave the city the Triton Fountain and the long northern wing of the Palazzo Barberini. The pope wrote and published his own Latin verse and revised the breviary’s hymns to classical metre.

His political reign was less brilliant. Determined to preserve the Papal States as a third force in Europe, he tilted quietly toward Bourbon France against the Habsburgs even at the height of the Thirty Years’ War, alienating Catholic Vienna and Madrid. He elevated his brother and nephews to wealth and red hats on a scale that gave nepotism its modern infamy. The disastrous War of Castro against the Farnese, fought from 1641, drained the treasury and was lost. And in 1633, after the publication of the Dialogue, he turned against his old friend Galileo Galilei and pressed for the trial that condemned him. Urban died in July 1644, mourned by few Romans.
