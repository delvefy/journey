Henry IV of France

First Bourbon king of France, 1553–1610

Henry was born in 1553 in the Béarnese castle of Pau, son of Antoine de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme, and Jeanne d’Albret, queen of Navarre. His mother had him raised a strict Calvinist; on her death in 1572 he became king of the small Pyrenean kingdom at the age of eighteen. Three months later, in August, he was married in Paris to Margaret of Valois, sister of Charles IX. Within the week the St Bartholomew’s Day Massacre cut down the Huguenot leadership around him and forced his temporary conversion to Catholicism.

Escaping the French court in 1576, he renounced that conversion and emerged as the political and military head of the Huguenot cause. The long French Wars of Religion turned, after the assassination of Henri III in August 1589, into a struggle for the throne itself, since by Salic right Henri of Navarre was now Henri IV of France. The Catholic League and Philip II of Spain opposed him for four years; victories at Arques and Ivry could not give him Paris. In July 1593, judging that "Paris is well worth a Mass," he abjured Calvinism a second time. Within a year the capital opened its gates.

His reign was short and consequential. The Edict of Nantes in 1598 granted the Huguenots civic equality and walled places of safety, ending nearly forty years of religious war. With his minister the duke of Sully he rebuilt royal finances, drained the Paris marais, planted the Place Royale and the Pont Neuf, encouraged silk in Lyon and grain across the south. His annulled marriage to Margaret was replaced in 1600 by a Florentine match with Marie de’ Medici. In May 1610, as he was preparing to intervene against the Habsburgs in the Jülich succession, the Catholic fanatic François Ravaillac stabbed him to death in his coach in the rue de la Ferronnerie.
