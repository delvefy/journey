Henrietta Maria

Queen consort of England, Scotland, and Ireland, 1609–1669

The youngest child of Henry IV of France and Marie de’ Medici, and sister of Louis XIII, Henrietta Maria was married at fifteen to Charles I of England. The match of a devout Catholic to a Protestant king was unpopular from the start, and the early years were cold; but after the assassination of the royal favourite Buckingham the couple grew devoted, and she bore Charles a large family, including the future kings Charles II and James II.

Her open Catholicism, her chapels and priests, and her influence over her husband fed the suspicions of his Puritan opponents and helped poison relations between crown and Parliament. When civil war came she threw herself into the royalist cause, pawning the crown jewels in the Low Countries to buy arms and munitions and braving a sea bombardment to return to England, earning Parliament’s charge of treason.

As the king’s fortunes collapsed she fled to her native France, where she lived on the charity of the French court and her nephew Louis XIV. There she received the shattering news of her husband’s execution in 1649. In poverty and grief she devoted herself to her children and her faith, founding a convent at Chaillot. The Restoration of 1660 brought her son to the throne and her a brief return to England before she retired to France, where she died in 1669. The American colony of Maryland preserves her name.
