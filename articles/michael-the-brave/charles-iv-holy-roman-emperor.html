Charles IV, Holy Roman Emperor

Holy Roman Emperor, King of Bohemia, 1316–1378

Charles was born at Prague on 14 May 1316, christened Wenceslaus, the elder son of John of Luxembourg, the soldierly and almost permanently absent king of Bohemia, and Elizabeth of the native Přemyslid line. From the age of seven he was raised at the French court of his uncle Charles IV of France, was confirmed under that name, and married his cousin Blanche of Valois at thirteen. The years at the Louvre gave him fluent French, a thorough humanist education under the future Pope Clement VI, and the political habits of a Capetian crown. He returned to Bohemia in 1333 as his father's regent, restoring order to a kingdom whose magnates had grown accustomed to John's absences.

His father's blindness and the Crécy charge that killed him in 1346 made Charles King of Bohemia in his own right. Clement VI had already engineered his election as antiking against the deposed Louis IV of Bavaria; Louis's death in 1347 left him uncontested in Germany. He was crowned king of the Romans at Aachen, king of Bohemia at Prague, king of Italy at Pavia, and emperor at Rome on Easter Day 1355 by a papal legate. He turned away from Italy almost at once. Bohemia became the heart of an empire run from a new capital. He founded the Universitas Carolina in 1348 — the first university east of Paris and the Rhine — laid out the New Town of Prague in the same year on a plan still legible, raised the Charles Bridge over the Vltava, and built Karlštejn Castle to house the imperial regalia.

The Golden Bull of 1356 fixed the constitution of the Empire for four centuries, naming the seven electors and the procedures of election. By inheritance, purchase, and marriage he added Silesia, Lusatia, and finally in 1373 Brandenburg to the lands of his house. He married four queens — Blanche of Valois, Anne of the Palatinate, Anne of Schweidnitz, and Elizabeth of Pomerania — and outlived three. His Vita Caroli is the first medieval royal autobiography. He died at Prague on 29 November 1378, leaving Bohemia to his elder son Wenceslaus IV and Brandenburg and Hungary to Sigismund.
