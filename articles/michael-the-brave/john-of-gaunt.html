John of Gaunt

Duke of Lancaster, son of Edward III, 1340–1399

John was born at the Abbey of Saint Bavo in Ghent on 6 March 1340 — the place gave him his name — the third surviving son of Edward III and Philippa of Hainault. Earl of Richmond from infancy, he was married in May 1359 to his cousin Blanche, the eventual heiress of Henry of Grosmont, Duke of Lancaster; her father's death in 1361 and her elder sister's in 1362 brought to John the duchy of Lancaster, the greatest private estate in England, and made him the wealthiest peer in the kingdom. Blanche died of plague in 1368 and her elegy was the first work of Geoffrey Chaucer, who would marry Philippa Roet, sister of John's later mistress.

From the early 1370s, with the Black Prince ill and Edward III aged, Gaunt led the war in France and the English government at home. His second marriage in 1371 to Constance, daughter of the murdered Pedro I of Castile, gave him a claim to that crown which he prosecuted with mixed results: a chevauchée from Calais to Bordeaux in 1373 was a winter horror, and the abortive Castilian expedition of 1386–87 finally ended in the Treaty of Bayonne by which his daughter Catherine married the future Henry III of Castile. At home he protected John Wycliffe from Archbishop Sudbury and the bishops at Lambeth in February 1377 and from the bishops again at Blackfriars in 1382. The Peasants' Revolt of 1381 made him its principal target — his Savoy Palace on the Thames was sacked and burned.

Throughout the difficult middle years of Richard II's reign he was the only magnate strong enough to keep his nephew on the throne and the Appellants in check; his return from Castile in 1389 restored a fragile peace. He married Katherine Swynford in 1396 and obtained the legitimation of their Beaufort children. He died at Leicester Castle on 3 February 1399. Richard II's confiscation of the duchy of Lancaster within weeks of his funeral pushed his exiled son Henry Bolingbroke to return, depose the king, and found the Lancastrian dynasty.
