William Shakespeare

English playwright and poet, 1564–1616

Shakespeare was baptised at Stratford-upon-Avon on 26 April 1564, the eldest surviving son of John Shakespeare, a glover and alderman, and Mary Arden of a farming family in nearby Wilmcote. He attended the town’s grammar school, where the syllabus was Latin grammar, Ovid, Plautus and Terence—the working tools of his later art. In 1582, aged eighteen, he married the older Anne Hathaway; three children followed in five years. By 1592 he was in London, mocked in a pamphlet by Robert Greene as an upstart “Shake-scene” with feathers borrowed from his betters.

From 1594 he was a sharer in the Lord Chamberlain’s Men, the most successful playing company of the decade. The partnership built the Globe on Bankside in 1599 and, after the accession of James VI and I, was renamed the King’s Men and made grooms of the royal chamber. In this company Shakespeare wrote, on a steady professional schedule, the body of about thirty-nine plays that defines him: the early histories from Henry VI to Henry V, the great comedies (A Midsummer Night’s Dream, Twelfth Night), the four major tragedies (Hamlet, Othello, King Lear, Macbeth), and the late romances (The Winter’s Tale, The Tempest). His 154 sonnets and the narrative poems Venus and Adonis and The Rape of Lucrece, dedicated to Henry Wriothesley, Earl of Southampton, established his reputation in print well before his plays did.

His acting partner Richard Burbage played most of the great roles; his younger collaborator John Fletcher succeeded him as the company’s chief dramatist. Around 1613 Shakespeare withdrew to New Place in Stratford, where he had bought property steadily for years. He died on 23 April 1616 and was buried in Holy Trinity Church. Seven years later his fellow actors Heminges and Condell published the First Folio, preserving thirty-six plays that would otherwise largely have been lost.
