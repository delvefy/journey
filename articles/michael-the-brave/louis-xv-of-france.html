Louis XV of France

King of France, 1710–1774

Louis XV reigned over France for nearly sixty years, from glittering promise to weary decline. Born in 1710, he came to the throne in 1715 as a boy of five, succeeding his great-grandfather Louis XIV, and ruled at first through a regency and then through the steady hand of his old tutor, Cardinal Fleury, who gave the kingdom two decades of peace and recovery.

For a time he was genuinely loved—"Louis the Beloved"—and his armies prospered, with Marshal Maurice de Saxe winning glory at Fontenoy in the War of the Austrian Succession. But the middle of his reign brought a great reversal of alliances, the Diplomatic Revolution of 1756, which bound France to its ancient Habsburg enemy under Maria Theresa against Prussia and Britain. The Seven Years' War that followed was a catastrophe: France lost Canada and its foothold in India to the British, and the king's prestige never recovered.

His court was ruled by famous mistresses—above all the Marquise de Pompadour, who for twenty years shaped patronage, the arts, and even policy, and in his last years Madame du Barry. Meanwhile his quarrels with the law-courts, his mounting debts, and his private scandals drained away the affection of his people.

To seal the new Austrian alliance, he married his grandson and heir to Maria Theresa's daughter, Marie Antoinette, in 1770. Four years later Louis XV died of smallpox, leaving to that grandson, Louis XVI, a weakened throne and an exhausted treasury on the eve of revolution.
