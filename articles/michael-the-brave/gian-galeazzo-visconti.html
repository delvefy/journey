Gian Galeazzo Visconti

First Duke of Milan, 1351–1402

Gian Galeazzo Visconti was born in 1351, son of Galeazzo II of the family that had ruled Milan for generations as lords. As a boy of nine he was married to Isabelle of Valois, a daughter of John II of France, in a match that brought a French princess to Lombardy and money to a captive French crown. In 1385 he made himself master of the whole Visconti inheritance by a single bold stroke, luring his uncle and co-ruler Bernabò into a trap, arresting him, and taking sole power over Milan.

Over the next fifteen years he built the most formidable state in Italy. By war, purchase and diplomacy he gathered in Verona, Vicenza, Padua, Pisa, Siena, Perugia and Bologna, until his armies stood at the gates of a frightened Florence, which cast itself as the last defender of republican liberty against Milanese tyranny. In 1395 he crowned his ascent by buying from the king of the Romans the hereditary title of Duke of Milan, the first man to hold it.

He was a builder as well as a conqueror, founding the great Cathedral of Milan and the Certosa di Pavia. His dynastic reach proved as long as his armies: his daughter Valentina married Louis of Orléans, and from that union later French kings, Louis XII among them, would derive their claim to Milan and their pretext for invasion. In 1402, with Florence seemingly at his mercy, Gian Galeazzo died suddenly of plague. His empire fragmented at once, and the duchy passed in time to his sons Giovanni Maria and Filippo Maria.

The road continues to Bernabò Visconti.
