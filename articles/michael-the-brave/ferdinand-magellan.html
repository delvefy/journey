Fernão de Magalhães, Portuguese navigator in Spanish service, c. 1480–1521

Fernão de Magalhães was born around 1480, probably at Sabrosa in the Trás-os-Montes of northern Portugal, into a family of the lesser nobility. From boyhood he served as a page at the court of Queen Eleanor and then of King John II and Manuel I, where he received the bookish education of a young fidalgo and learned the rudiments of navigation. In 1505, at twenty-five, he sailed in the great fleet of Francisco de Almeida, the first viceroy of Portuguese India. Over the next seven years he fought in the battle of Cannanore, at Diu in 1509 under Almeida, and at the storming of Malacca in 1511 under Afonso de Albuquerque. He returned to Lisbon in 1513 with a slave from the Spice Islands named Enrique, who would later interpret for him on the other side of the world.

A campaign in Morocco soured his relations with Manuel I. Accused of trading with the enemy, denied a small increase in his royal stipend, refused permission to seek service elsewhere, he renounced his Portuguese allegiance and crossed into Spain in October 1517. Charles V’s minister Cardinal Adrian and the merchant Cristóbal de Haro backed his proposal to reach the Moluccas by sailing west, through what he and the cosmographer Ruy Faleiro believed must be a strait at the bottom of the New World. On 20 September 1519 five small ships, the Trinidad, San Antonio, Concepción, Victoria and Santiago, with about 270 men, sailed from Sanlúcar de Barrameda.

Mutiny at Port San Julián, the wreck of the Santiago, the desertion of the San Antonio, and the bitter five-week passage through the strait that bears his name reduced the fleet to three ships. The Pacific crossing took ninety-eight days; many died of scurvy before they made landfall at Guam. In March 1521 they reached the Philippines. There Magellan was drawn into a local quarrel on the side of the converted Rajah Humabon, and on 27 April 1521 was killed on the beach of Mactan by warriors of Lapu-Lapu. Juan Sebastián Elcano brought the Victoria home alone to Sanlúcar in September 1522, the first ship to sail around the world.

The road continues to Afonso de Albuquerque.
