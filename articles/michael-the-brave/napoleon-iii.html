Napoleon III

Emperor of the French, 1808–1873

Charles-Louis Napoléon Bonaparte was a nephew of the great Napoleon, and the family legend shaped his whole life. After two farcical attempted coups and a spell in prison, from which he escaped, his moment came with the revolution of 1848. Trading on his magic name, he was elected president of the new French Republic by a landslide, and in 1851, barred from re-election, he seized power in a coup and a year later proclaimed himself Emperor of the French as Napoleon III.

His Second Empire was a paradox of authoritarian rule and dazzling modernisation. He presided over rapid industrial growth, promoted railways and banking, and commissioned Baron Haussmann’s wholesale rebuilding of Paris into the city of grand boulevards still admired today. Abroad he fought in the Crimea, aided the unification of Italy, and pursued costly ventures, including a disastrous attempt to install a puppet emperor in Mexico. The poet Victor Hugo, exiled and implacable, lashed him in verse as “Napoléon le Petit.”

His undoing came from Prussia. Manoeuvred by Bismarck into the war of 1870, the French armies were crushed and the ailing emperor himself taken prisoner at Sedan. The Empire collapsed, and Napoleon III went into exile in England, where he died in 1873. He was the last monarch to rule France.
