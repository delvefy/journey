Waraqa ibn Nawfal

Meccan hanif and Christian, d. c. 610

Waraqa ibn Nawfal was a Meccan of the Quraysh, a cousin of Khadija, who lived in the generation before Islam. He belonged to the hanifs, those few who had turned away from the idols of their city in search of the older monotheism of Abraham. According to the traditions of the Prophet’s life, Waraqa went further and embraced Christianity, becoming learned in the Hebrew and Christian scriptures and even transcribing the Gospel in his own hand; by the end of his life he was old and had lost his sight.

His place in memory rests on a single famous scene. When Muhammad returned shaken from the cave of Hira, having received his first revelation, Khadija brought him to her aged cousin. Waraqa listened and declared that the being who had come to Muhammad was none other than the Law — the same that God had once sent down to Moses — and that Muhammad was the prophet of his people. He added that he wished he might live to stand by him when his own folk would drive him out, for, he said, no man had ever brought such a message without being opposed.

Waraqa died soon afterward, around 610, before the persecution he had foreseen began in earnest. Brief as his role was, the tradition honours him as a man of the older faiths who recognized, at the very threshold of Islam, the prophet whose mission was just beginning.
