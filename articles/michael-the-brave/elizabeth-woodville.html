Elizabeth Woodville

Queen of England, c.1437–1492

Elizabeth Woodville was born around 1437 into a family of the minor nobility and first married a Lancastrian knight, Sir John Grey, who was killed fighting for his cause. Left a young widow with two small sons and famed for her beauty, she caught the eye of the Yorkist king Edward IV, who in 1464 married her in secret, an astonishing match between a reigning king and a commoner of the defeated party.

The marriage and the rapid advancement of her numerous relatives outraged the old nobility, above all the mighty Earl of Warwick, who had been seeking a grand French bride for the king. The slight helped push Warwick into open rebellion and reignited the Wars of the Roses, plunging the realm back into years of bloodshed even as Elizabeth’s family climbed to wealth and influence.

After Edward IV’s death in 1483, catastrophe overtook her. Her young sons, the elder briefly king as Edward V, were lodged in the Tower by their uncle Richard III and never seen again, while Elizabeth fled into sanctuary. From there she joined the conspiracy that backed Henry Tudor and sealed it with the promise of her daughter, Elizabeth of York, in marriage. She lived to see the Tudor triumph but withdrew to a convent, where she died in 1492, the ancestress of the dynasty that would rule England for over a century.
