Pope Leo X

Medici pope of the High Renaissance, 1475–1521

Giovanni di Lorenzo de' Medici was born in Florence on 11 December 1475, the second son of Lorenzo the Magnificent and Clarice Orsini. Destined for the church from boyhood, he received the tonsure at seven, was made an abbot at eight, and was created cardinal-deacon in pectore by Innocent VIII at the age of thirteen, the youngest in the history of the office; he took his hat publicly in 1492. The expulsion of the Medici from Florence later that year drove him into exile across Germany, the Low Countries, and France, but he returned to Rome and to the household of his cousin Giulio, the future Clement VII. In 1512, Spanish arms and a peasant rising restored the Medici in Florence; the next year, on 11 March 1513, he was elected pope and took the name Leo X.

His pontificate combined a passion for the arts and letters with a careless, ruinous expenditure. He confirmed Raphael as overseer of the building of St Peter's after Bramante's death, kept Michelangelo at work on the Medici tombs and the façade of San Lorenzo, and patronised humanists, antiquarians, and musicians on a scale Rome had not seen. The Fifth Lateran Council closed without reform under his presidency in 1517. In the same year the Wittenberg friar Martin Luther posted theses against the indulgence preached in Germany to fund the new basilica; Leo treated the affair at first as a monkish squabble, formally condemned forty-one propositions in Exsurge Domine in 1520, and excommunicated Luther in January 1521.

In foreign affairs he negotiated the Concordat of Bologna with Francis I in 1516, granting the French crown the nomination of bishops in exchange for the burial of Gallican conciliarism, and swung between France and the empire in the rivalry that culminated in the election of Charles V in 1519. He survived the Petrucci conspiracy of 1517 among the cardinals and died of fever in Rome on 1 December 1521, leaving the papal treasury empty and the Reformation already irreversibly under way.
