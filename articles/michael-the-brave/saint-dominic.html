Saint Dominic

Founder of the Order of Preachers, c. 1170–1221

Dominic de Guzmán was born about 1170 at Caleruega in Castile and became a canon of the cathedral of Osma, a man marked early by austerity and learning. The turn of his life came on a journey through southern France with his bishop, when he saw at first hand the strength of the Cathar heresy in Languedoc and judged that it could not be answered by the wealth and pomp of the established clergy. The reply, he concluded, must be poverty matched with knowledge: preachers who lived as simply as those they hoped to convert, but who could out-argue them in doctrine.

Out of that conviction grew the Order of Preachers. Amid the violence of the Albigensian Crusade Dominic kept to the word rather than the sword, gathering a small band of educated, mendicant friars whose vocation was study and preaching. The order won the approval of Pope Honorius III in 1216, in the reforming climate left by Innocent III and the Fourth Lateran Council, and spread with astonishing speed across the universities and cities of Europe.

Dominic himself, restless and self-denying, governed the young order through constant travel and the first general chapters before his death at Bologna in 1221; he was canonised in 1234. He left behind no great writings, but an institution that shaped the intellectual life of the Middle Ages. The black-and-white friars he founded—punningly called the Domini canes, the hounds of the Lord—would fill the chairs of the new universities and produce the supreme theologian of the age, the man who more than any other gave the Dominican vocation of faith joined to reason its enduring monument.
