The Duke of Marlborough

John Churchill, English general, 1650–1722

John Churchill, first Duke of Marlborough, was the greatest English soldier of his age and one of the supreme commanders in European history. Born in 1650 to a Devon gentry family ruined by the Civil War, he made his way at court as a page to the Duke of York, the future James II, and learned his trade in early campaigns and in the suppression of Monmouth's rebellion.

His career turned on the crisis of 1688, when he abandoned the Catholic James for William of Orange in the Glorious Revolution and was rewarded with an earldom. His full greatness emerged only under Queen Anne, who made him Captain-General of her armies. In the long War of the Spanish Succession he led the Grand Alliance against the France of Louis XIV.

There he proved himself a master of every part of war—the secret march, the supply train, the timing of the decisive charge. At Blenheim in 1704, joining forces with Prince Eugene of Savoy, he destroyed a Franco-Bavarian army and saved Vienna; further triumphs followed at Ramillies, Oudenarde, and the terrible slaughter of Malplaquet. He is said never to have lost a battle nor failed to take a town he besieged.

A grateful nation built him the vast palace of Blenheim. Yet his fortunes depended on his wife Sarah's friendship with the Queen, and when that friendship soured and his political enemies gained the upper hand, he was dismissed in disgrace in 1711. Restored to honour under the next reign, he died in 1722. Two centuries later his descendant Winston Churchill would write his life. The king he had served in the Revolution was William III of England.
