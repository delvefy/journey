Miltiades

Athenian general, c. 550–489 BCE

Miltiades the Younger was the Athenian general whose victory at Marathon in 490 BCE saved his city in the first Persian invasion of Greece. Born around 550 BCE into the wealthy and powerful Philaid family, he spent years as a semi-independent ruler of the Thracian Chersonese under Persian overlordship, and even took part, however reluctantly, in Darius's Scythian campaign before breaking with Persia and returning home to Athens.

When Darius sent a punitive expedition against Athens in 490, the Athenians and their Plataean allies marched out to meet it on the plain of Marathon. Miltiades, one of the ten elected generals, is credited by tradition with urging the decisive attack and with the tactics that won the day: a deliberately thinned center and reinforced wings that enveloped the Persian line. The Athenians routed the invaders and drove them back to their ships, inflicting heavy losses while suffering remarkably few.

The triumph made Miltiades the foremost man in Athens, but his glory was brief. The following year he led a fleet against the island of Paros, which failed; he was gravely wounded in the thigh, and on his return he was prosecuted and heavily fined by his political enemies. He died soon afterward of the festering wound.

His son Cimon would inherit his prominence in the next generation, and Marathon itself passed into legend as the battle in which a free citizen army turned back the might of Persia.
