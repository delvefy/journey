Frida Kahlo

Mexican painter, 1907–1954

Magdalena Carmen Frida Kahlo y Calderón was born in 1907 at the Casa Azul, her family's Coyoacán house in Mexico City, the daughter of a German-Hungarian Jewish photographer and a Mexican mother of mostly indigenous descent. She survived polio at six, which left her with a withered right leg, and at eighteen, in September 1925, the trolley-bus she was riding to school collided with a tram. An iron handrail entered her hip and exited through her pelvis; her spine, collarbone, ribs, and the leg already crippled by polio were all broken. She spent months immobilised in a body cast, with a mirror set above her bed and oils and a brush within reach, and so began to paint.

In 1928 she carried a portfolio to the painter Diego Rivera, twenty-one years her senior, for his honest opinion of her work; he praised it, courted her, and married her the following year. The marriage was tempestuous, with infidelities on both sides—Diego's with her younger sister, Frida's with men and women including Leon Trotsky, whom the couple sheltered in the Casa Azul from 1937. They divorced in 1939 and remarried in 1940.

She painted herself almost obsessively—some 55 of her roughly 150 surviving paintings are self-portraits—in compositions that combined Mexican retablo iconography, indigenous costume, and graphic depictions of her own miscarriages, surgeries, and physical pain. The Two Fridas, The Broken Column, Henry Ford Hospital, Without Hope, and the late Self-Portrait with Thorn Necklace and Hummingbird made her, by midcentury, the most recognisable Mexican painter after Rivera. André Breton claimed her for Surrealism; she dryly replied that she did not paint dreams, only her own reality.

Her right leg was finally amputated in 1953 for gangrene. She died at the Casa Azul on 13 July 1954, aged forty-seven. From the late twentieth century she became a global feminist icon, her unibrow and her colours reproduced on every conceivable surface. The Mexican muralist husband with whom her life and art were so completely intertwined, and who himself transformed twentieth-century public painting, was Diego Rivera.
