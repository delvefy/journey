Indira Gandhi

Prime Minister of India, 1917–1984

Indira Priyadarshini Nehru was born in 1917 at Allahabad, the only child of Jawaharlal Nehru and Kamala Nehru. Her childhood was spent in the cross-currents of the Indian independence movement; her mother died of tuberculosis when she was eighteen. She was educated at Visva-Bharati under Rabindranath Tagore and later at Somerville College, Oxford, where she met the Parsi journalist Feroze Gandhi (no relation to the Mahatma), whom she married in 1942 over her father's reservations.

She served as her father's official hostess and political secretary through his seventeen years as Prime Minister, and entered Cabinet herself as Minister of Information under Lal Bahadur Shastri. On Shastri's sudden death in 1966 she was chosen by the Congress old guard as a compromise candidate, on the assumption that she would be malleable. She was not. She nationalised the major Indian banks in 1969, abolished the privy purses of the former princes in 1971, and in December of that year ordered the Indian intervention in the East Pakistan crisis that produced, in fourteen days, the independent state of Bangladesh.

She tilted India toward the Soviet Union, signed the 1971 Treaty of Peace, Friendship and Cooperation with Moscow, and authorised India's first nuclear test, "Smiling Buddha," in 1974. A 1975 Allahabad High Court ruling on a campaign offence threatened to bar her from office; she persuaded the President to declare a state of national Emergency, suspended civil liberties, and ruled by decree for twenty-one months. She lost the 1977 election on her record, was briefly imprisoned, and was returned to office in a landslide in 1980.

Her decision in 1984 to send the Indian army into the Golden Temple at Amritsar to dislodge Sikh militants—Operation Blue Star—ended in heavy casualties and the desecration of Sikhism's holiest shrine. On the morning of 31 October 1984 she was shot dead by two of her own Sikh bodyguards in the garden of her New Delhi residence. The American President with whom she had clashed bitterly during the Bangladesh war of 1971, and whose Oval Office tapes preserved her in a stream of personal abuse, was Richard Nixon.
