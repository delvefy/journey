Artaxerxes II

King of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, r. 404–358 BCE

Artaxerxes II was the longest-reigning king of the Achaemenid Persian Empire, holding the throne for nearly half a century, from 404 to 358 BCE. The elder son of Darius II and Queen Parysatis, he came to power only to face at once a deadly challenge from his younger brother, Cyrus the Younger.

In 401 BCE Cyrus marched against him with a Greek mercenary army, and the brothers met at Cunaxa near Babylon. Though the Greeks routed the troops opposite them, Cyrus was killed attacking the king in person, and Artaxerxes kept his throne. Yet his long reign was marked more by slow erosion than by triumph: Egypt broke away and stayed independent for decades, and his western frontier was harried by the Spartans, whose king Agesilaus II campaigned deep into Asia Minor.

Artaxerxes answered the Spartan threat with Persian gold rather than Persian armies, subsidizing Sparta's Greek enemies and helping to stir up the Corinthian War in order to draw Agesilaus home. In 387 BCE he imposed the settlement known as the King's Peace, which made the Great King the arbiter of Greek affairs and confirmed Persian control of the Asian Greek cities—a striking diplomatic recovery.

Late in his reign he weathered a sprawling revolt of his western satraps. He is the subject of a biography by Plutarch, which preserves vivid and often grim scenes of his court. He died in 358 BCE, very old, and was succeeded by his son Artaxerxes III.
