Madonna

American singer and entertainer, born 1958.

Born Madonna Louise Ciccone in Bay City, Michigan, the third of six children in an Italian-American Catholic family, she lost her mother to breast cancer at five — an absence she would credit with much of her later ambition. A scholarship dancer at the University of Michigan, she dropped out and moved to New York in 1978 with thirty-five dollars and the conviction that she would become famous. She studied with Pearl Lang, briefly joined Patrick Hernandez's disco backing band in Paris, returned to play drums and guitar in punk bands on the Lower East Side, and in 1982 was signed by Sire Records.

The 1984 album Like a Virgin, the 1985 Material Girl single (whose video staged her, in Marilyn Monroe's pink dress, as the "Diamonds Are a Girl's Best Friend" gold-digger), and her 1985 marriage to Sean Penn made her the most discussed performer in pop. Through the next decade she reinvented her image with each cycle — the Boy Toy of Like a Virgin, the New Wave child-woman of True Blue, the religious provocateur of Like a Prayer, the bondage and queer-cabaret poseur of Erotica and the Blond Ambition tour, the elegant Eva Perón of the 1996 Evita film, the electronic mother of Ray of Light. The strategy of constant reinvention became the template every subsequent pop star — Lady Gaga, Beyoncé, Britney Spears — was forced to negotiate.

She lived in London during her second marriage to the director Guy Ritchie, adopted children from Malawi where she founded the charity Raising Malawi, and continued to tour internationally into her sixties, breaking ticket-sales records that she had previously set herself. As the most successful female recording artist in history and the maker of the modern pop video, she trained generations of younger performers to treat persona as a serial work of art — an idea she had taken in her youth from Marilyn Monroe and the Warholian downtown New York around her.

The road continues to Marilyn Monroe.
