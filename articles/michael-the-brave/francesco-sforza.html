Francesco Sforza

Condottiero and Duke of Milan, 1401–1466

Francesco Sforza was born in 1401, the son of the celebrated mercenary captain Muzio Attendolo Sforza, and learned war in his father’s company. He became the most sought-after condottiero of his generation, selling his sword to Milan, Venice, Florence and the papacy in turn, and using each contract to enlarge his own power. His decisive step was personal as well as military: in 1441 he married Bianca Maria Visconti, the illegitimate but cherished daughter of Filippo Maria Visconti, the last Visconti duke of Milan, which gave a soldier of fortune a claim on one of the richest states in Italy.

When Filippo Maria died without a legitimate male heir in 1447, the Milanese proclaimed the short-lived Ambrosian Republic. Francesco served it, fought it, and finally besieged the starving city until it opened its gates; in 1450 he entered Milan as duke, founding the Sforza dynasty on the ruins of the Visconti. He governed with unexpected steadiness, befriending Cosimo de’ Medici of Florence and turning their alliance into the axis of a new order. The Peace of Lodi of 1454 and the Italic League that followed it held the five great Italian powers in a guarded balance for some forty years.

He rebuilt the great Castello of Milan as a ducal fortress and began the vast Ospedale Maggiore for the city’s poor. To later eyes his career became exemplary: Machiavelli held him up as the man who, with his own arms and ability, climbed from private citizen to prince. He died in 1466, leaving the duchy to his sons, among them the future Ludovico il Moro.
