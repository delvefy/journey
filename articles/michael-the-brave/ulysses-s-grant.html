Ulysses S. Grant

Union general and 18th President of the United States, 1822–1885

An Ohio tanner’s son and West Point graduate, Grant fought in the Mexican War, then failed at one civilian venture after another and was nearly forgotten when the Civil War called him back to the army. In the western theatre he revealed a relentless will to win, capturing Fort Donelson with a demand for “unconditional surrender” and, after the carnage of Shiloh, splitting the Confederacy in two with the masterful siege of Vicksburg in 1863.

Lincoln, who had at last found a general who would fight, made him commander of all the Union armies. Grant ground down Robert E. Lee through a brutal Virginia campaign and the long siege of Petersburg until, in April 1865, he received Lee’s surrender at Appomattox Court House, offering generous terms that began the healing of the nation. Elected president in 1868, he worked to enforce Reconstruction and crush the Ku Klux Klan, though his two terms were stained by corruption among his associates.

Impoverished and swindled in retirement, and dying of throat cancer, Grant raced to write his memoirs to provide for his family. Published by his friend Mark Twain, the Personal Memoirs proved both a financial triumph and a literary masterpiece, finished only days before his death in 1885. His great opponent, whom he had defeated yet treated with magnanimity, was Robert E. Lee.
