George III of the United Kingdom

King of Great Britain and Ireland, then of the United Kingdom, 1738–1820

George III succeeded his grandfather in 1760 as the first of the Hanoverian kings to be born in Britain and to speak English as his native tongue. Earnest, dutiful, and devoted to his family and his farms, which earned him the affectionate nickname Farmer George, he took a more active part in government than his predecessors, determined to assert the proper influence of the Crown through his chosen ministers.

His reign was dominated by great upheavals. The attempt to tax the American colonies provoked resistance that hardened into revolution, and despite a long and costly war the thirteen colonies won their independence, with George Washington leading the rebellion against the king’s authority. The loss was a bitter blow, though George reconciled himself to it. At home he presided over the union with Ireland that created the United Kingdom in 1801, and he supported William Pitt the Younger through the long struggle against revolutionary and Napoleonic France.

From middle age the king suffered recurrent bouts of a debilitating mental illness, during which he raved and lost his reason, alarming the nation and raising the question of a regency. After a final relapse he was permanently incapacitated, and from 1811 his son ruled in his place as Prince Regent. Blind, deaf, and secluded at Windsor, George lived out his last years apart from the world. For all his troubles he was, by the end, a respected symbol of national endurance through an age of revolution and war.
