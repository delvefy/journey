Harpalus

Macedonian treasurer of Alexander the Great, d. 323 BCE

Harpalus was a Macedonian nobleman, a boyhood friend of Alexander the Great, who rose to become treasurer of the vast wealth the conquest of Persia poured into Macedonian hands—and who is remembered for spending and stealing it on a spectacular scale. Lame and unfit for war, he was given charge of the imperial treasury, first at Babylon and then across the conquered lands.

Left to administer fabulous riches while Alexander campaigned in India, Harpalus lived like a king, importing luxuries and famous courtesans and squandering public funds. As Alexander's return drew near and the accounts came due, his nerve failed. In 324 BCE he fled west with five thousand talents and a force of mercenaries, making for Athens, where he hoped his money might purchase a refuge.

His arrival threw the city into turmoil. Much of his treasure mysteriously vanished, and a great corruption scandal followed, in which leading politicians—Demosthenes among them—were accused of taking his bribes. The Athenians, unwilling to defy Alexander openly, confined Harpalus and then allowed him to slip away.

He sailed to Crete, where, in 323 BCE, he was murdered by one of his own officers—a sordid end to a career that had touched both the summit of empire and the depths of greed.
