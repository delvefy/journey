Arcangelo Corelli

Roman violinist and composer, 1653–1713

Arcangelo Corelli was the violinist and composer who, more than any other single figure, founded the classical school of violin playing and gave the concerto grosso its enduring form. Born in 1653 at Fusignano, near Ravenna, he trained at Bologna, joining its distinguished Accademia Filarmonica while still in his teens, and by the mid-1670s had settled in Rome, where he would spend the rest of his life.

In Rome his gifts as a player and as a disciplined leader of orchestras won him the greatest patrons of the city. The exiled Queen Christina of Sweden, then presiding over a brilliant artistic court, accepted the dedication of his first published sonatas, and he later lived in the palace of Cardinal Pietro Ottoboni, directing the famous concerts held there.

Corelli published with extreme care, releasing only six sets of works in his lifetime. The trio sonatas of his early opuses, the solo violin sonatas of Opus 5—closing with celebrated variations on La Follia—and above all the twelve Concerti Grossi of Opus 6, issued just after his death and including the beloved Christmas Concerto, became models studied across Europe.

His clarity of harmony, his balanced phrasing, and his sequences became the common grammar of late Baroque music, shaping Handel, Bach, and Vivaldi alike, while his pupils carried his manner of playing through the next generation. Honoured in his last years, he was buried in the Pantheon among the great men of Rome. In the cosmopolitan musical world of that city he stood beside the leading composer of Italian opera of the age, Alessandro Scarlatti.
