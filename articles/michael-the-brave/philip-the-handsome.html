Philip the Handsome

Duke of Burgundy and King of Castile, 1478–1506

Philip was born in 1478, the son of Maximilian of Habsburg and Mary of Burgundy. When his mother died in a riding accident in 1482, the boy inherited the rich Burgundian lands of the Low Countries, which were governed in his name until he came of age. Charming and good-looking, qualities that earned him his epithet, he grew into a self-willed prince eager to rule in his own right.

In 1496 he married Joanna, a daughter of Ferdinand of Aragon and Isabella of Castile, as part of a web of marriage alliances binding the Habsburgs to the Spanish monarchs. The early deaths of Joanna’s brother and elder sister unexpectedly made her heiress to Castile, and the couple’s stormy marriage, clouded by his infidelities and her consuming jealousy, suddenly carried the highest political stakes.

On Isabella’s death in 1504 Joanna became queen of Castile, and Philip pressed his own claim to the throne, quarrelling bitterly with his father-in-law Ferdinand for control of the kingdom. Recognised as King Philip I of Castile in 1506, he died only months later, struck down by a sudden fever at the age of twenty-eight amid rumours of poison. His distraught widow, remembered as Joanna the Mad, was said to keep his coffin near her, while their son passed into history as the Emperor Charles V.

The road continues to Ferdinand I, Holy Roman Emperor.
