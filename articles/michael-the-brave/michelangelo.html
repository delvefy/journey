Michelangelo

Florentine sculptor, painter, and architect, 1475–1564

Michelangelo Buonarroti was born on 6 March 1475 at Caprese in the Casentino, where his father Lodovico was for a year the Florentine podestà; the family returned almost at once to Florence and to the village of Settignano, among the stonecutters of its quarries. Apprenticed against his father's wishes to Domenico Ghirlandaio at thirteen, he was soon taken into the household of Lorenzo de' Medici and trained in the sculpture garden at San Marco under Bertoldo di Giovanni, eating at the Medici table beside the future Leo X and Clement VII. Two early reliefs and the wooden Crucifix of Santo Spirito date from these years.

After the Medici fell in 1494 he spent five years between Bologna and Rome, where in 1499 he finished the Pietà of St Peter's at twenty-four. Recalled to Florence, he carved the colossal David from a long-abandoned block of Carrara marble and set it before the Palazzo Vecchio in 1504. Julius II summoned him back to Rome in 1505 for a vast funerary monument that consumed him, intermittently, for forty years; in 1508 the same pope put him on scaffolding under the vault of the Sistine Chapel, and in four years he frescoed the Genesis cycle, the prophets and sibyls, and the ignudi alone, supine. Three decades later, under Paul III, he returned to the chapel to paint the Last Judgment on its altar wall.

His later life was given to architecture and to poetry. From 1547 he was chief architect of St Peter's, redesigning Bramante's plan and raising the great drum on which his dome would rest, and he laid out the Piazza del Campidoglio on the Capitoline. He carved the Medici tombs at San Lorenzo, the Moses for Julius's tomb, and the unfinished late Pietàs. His sonnets and madrigals to Tommaso Cavalieri and to Vittoria Colonna survive as one of the finest bodies of Italian verse of the century. He died in Rome on 18 February 1564 and was carried home for burial at Santa Croce.
