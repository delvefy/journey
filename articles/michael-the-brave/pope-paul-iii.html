Pope Paul III

Alessandro Farnese, pope 1534–1549

Alessandro Farnese was born in 1468 into a Roman baronial family and owed his early rise to the favour shown his house by Pope Alexander VI, who made him a cardinal in 1493. Worldly, learned and patient, he passed decades as a Renaissance prince of the Church, fathering children and gathering wealth, until in 1534 the conclave chose him as a safe elder statesman. As Pope Paul III he proved to be the hinge between two ages: the last great patron-nepotist of the old Rome and the first pope of the Catholic reformation.

The old habits never left him. He raised teenage grandsons to the cardinalate and detached the duchy of Parma and Piacenza from the papal lands to bestow it on his son. Yet the same pope grasped that the Church had to answer the Protestant revolt with more than arms. In 1540 he gave his approval to the Society of Jesus, the disciplined new order of Ignatius of Loyola; in 1542 he set up the Roman Inquisition; and in 1545, after years of delay, he at last convened the Council of Trent, the great assembly that would define Catholic doctrine and discipline for centuries. He also pronounced the excommunication of Henry VIII of England.

Paul III remained a man of magnificent taste throughout. He drove forward the new Saint Peter’s, naming Michelangelo its chief architect, and set the same artist to paint the Last Judgment across the altar wall of the Sistine Chapel. He built the vast Palazzo Farnese and sat for Titian, whose searching portraits show a shrewd, stooped old man with watchful eyes. He died in 1549, having set in motion the forces that would remake the Roman Church.

The road continues to Ignatius of Loyola.
