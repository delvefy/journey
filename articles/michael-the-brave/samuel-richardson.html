Samuel Richardson

English novelist and printer, 1689–1761

Samuel Richardson was born in Mackworth in Derbyshire in 1689, son of a joiner who hoped his boy would become a clergyman but could not afford the education. He was apprenticed to a London printer instead, worked his way up to journeyman and then to the ownership of a substantial printing house, and by his forties was one of the most prosperous tradesmen in the city, printer to the House of Commons. He had no thought of authorship until two booksellers in 1739 asked him to compose a model letter-writing manual for the country reader.

The project grew, almost by accident, into Pamela, or Virtue Rewarded, published in 1740. Told entirely in the letters of a servant girl resisting the seductions of her master, it became the literary sensation of the century, was preached on from pulpits, parodied (by Henry Fielding's Shamela), and pirated everywhere. Richardson followed it in 1748 with Clarissa, a million-word tragedy in letters of a young woman trapped by her family and destroyed by the rake Lovelace—the longest novel in the English language and, for many of his contemporaries, the greatest. His last book, Sir Charles Grandison of 1753, attempted the harder feat of a virtuous male protagonist.

His epistolary form transformed European fiction. Rousseau modeled Julie, ou la Nouvelle Héloïse directly on Clarissa; Goethe drew on him for the inwardness of Werther. Samuel Johnson, who otherwise disdained novel-reading, set Richardson apart as a great moralist of the heart. Jane Austen, half-mockingly, called Sir Charles Grandison her favourite novel.

He lived quietly in his Salisbury Court house and his country retreat at North End, surrounded by a circle of admiring young female correspondents whose letters he kept and edited. He died in 1761. Across the Channel his Clarissa was received not as a curiosity but as the supreme modern novel, and the great French philosophe who placed him among the immortals, in an enraptured "Éloge" composed within weeks of Richardson's death, was Denis Diderot.
