Ophellas

Macedonian governor of Cyrene, d. 309 BCE

Ophellas was a Macedonian commander who served under Alexander the Great and afterward became governor of Cyrene for Ptolemy, the founder of the dynasty that would rule Egypt. He secured the rich coastal region of Cyrenaica for Ptolemy around 322 BCE, defeating the freebooter Thibron, and governed it for more than a decade with growing independence.

His ambition outgrew his post. In 309 BCE the Syracusan tyrant Agathocles, who had boldly carried his war against Carthage onto African soil, sought an ally and appealed to Ophellas, dangling before him the prospect of a great new dominion in the west. Ophellas raised a large army, including many Athenian adventurers, and made the long and difficult march along the desert coast to join him.

The alliance proved a trap. Once the two forces had united, Agathocles turned on his guest, had Ophellas killed, and absorbed his soldiers into his own army. So ended a career that had begun in the service of Alexander and foundered on the treachery of a fellow adventurer.
