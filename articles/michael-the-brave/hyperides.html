Hyperides

Athenian orator, 390–322 BCE

Hyperides was one of the ten great orators of classical Athens, a contemporary of Demosthenes and, like him, a champion of Athenian independence against the rising power of Macedon. Born in 390 BCE, he studied under both Plato and the rhetorician Isocrates, and made his living as a speechwriter and prosecutor in the law courts before entering politics.

In the assembly he stood with Demosthenes in urging resistance to Philip II and then to Alexander, and he attacked the advocates of submission with skill and biting wit. Yet their alliance was not unbroken: in the scandal of 324 BCE, when Alexander's fugitive treasurer Harpalus arrived in Athens with vast sums and many leading men were accused of taking his money, Hyperides turned against Demosthenes and helped secure his condemnation.

After Alexander's death in 323, Hyperides became the foremost advocate of the revolt known as the Lamian War, and it was he who delivered the public funeral oration over the Athenians who fell in it—a speech partly recovered in modern times from an Egyptian papyrus.

The revolt failed. When the victorious Macedonian regent Antipater demanded the surrender of the anti-Macedonian leaders, Hyperides fled but was hunted down and put to death in 322 BCE. With him and Demosthenes perished the last great generation of Athenian political oratory.
