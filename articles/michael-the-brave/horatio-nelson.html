Horatio Nelson

British admiral, 1758–1805

Nelson was born in Norfolk, the son of a country parson, and went to sea in the Royal Navy at the age of twelve. He rose swiftly through ability and reckless courage, losing the sight of one eye and then his right arm in battle. A commander of dash and intuition, he forged with his captains a bond of trust and aggression he called the "Nelson touch," and he repeatedly gambled on decisive action where others counselled caution.

His victories transformed the naval war against Revolutionary and Napoleonic France. At the Battle of the Nile in 1798 he annihilated the French fleet and stranded Napoleon’s army in Egypt; at Copenhagen in 1801 he pressed home the attack, famously raising his telescope to his blind eye to ignore a signal to withdraw. His passionate and very public affair with Emma, Lady Hamilton, scandalised society even as his fame grew.

His crowning triumph came on 21 October 1805 off Cape Trafalgar. Flying from his flagship Victory the signal "England expects that every man will do his duty," he split the combined French and Spanish fleet in two and won an overwhelming victory that secured British command of the seas for a century. In the hour of triumph he was struck down by a sniper’s bullet and died below decks as the guns fell silent, passing at once into legend as Britain’s greatest naval hero.
