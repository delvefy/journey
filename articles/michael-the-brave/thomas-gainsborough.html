Thomas Gainsborough

English portrait and landscape painter, 1727–1788

Born in the Suffolk town of Sudbury, Gainsborough showed a precocious gift for drawing the countryside he loved. Though landscape remained his deepest passion, it was portraiture that paid, and he built a fashionable practice first in the spa city of Bath and then in London. His handling was unmistakable: light, feathery, rapid brushwork that gave his sitters an air of effortless grace, as in the celebrated Blue Boy and the cool double portrait of Mr and Mrs Andrews set in their own fields.

A founding member of the Royal Academy, Gainsborough became the favourite painter of George III and Queen Charlotte, even as Joshua Reynolds presided over the Academy as its first president. The two were opposites—Reynolds the learned theorist, Gainsborough the instinctive natural talent—and their friendly rivalry defined the era of British portraiture, though they admired one another and were reconciled at Gainsborough’s deathbed.

Above every living painter he revered the long-dead Flemish master Anthony van Dyck, whose elegance he sought to revive; his reported last words declared that they were all going to heaven, “and Van Dyck is of the company.” A lover of music who befriended the composer Johann Christian Bach, Gainsborough also pioneered the “fancy picture” of rustic children. He died of cancer at sixty-one, his reputation secure as one of England’s greatest painters.
