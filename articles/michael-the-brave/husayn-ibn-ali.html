Husayn ibn Ali

Grandson of the Prophet and martyr of Karbala, 626–680

Husayn was born in Medina in 626, the second son of Ali ibn Abi Talib and of Fatima, the daughter of Muhammad, and so a grandson of the Prophet himself, who is remembered as cherishing him dearly. With his elder brother Hasan he stood at the heart of the Prophet’s household, the Ahl al-Bayt, and after Hasan’s death he became its head and, to the Shia, the third of the Imams who alone could rightly lead the community.

For as long as Muawiya lived, Husayn kept an uneasy peace. But when Muawiya died in 680 and his son Yazid demanded the oath of allegiance, Husayn refused to lend his sanction to what he saw as a corrupt and tyrannical seizure of the caliphate. Summoned by the people of Kufa, who promised to rise for him, he set out across the desert with his family and a small band of followers.

They never reached Kufa. Yazid’s troops intercepted them on the plain of Karbala, cut them off from the waters of the Euphrates, and on the tenth day of Muharram — the day of Ashura — overwhelmed them. Husayn and nearly all his companions were killed, and the women and children carried off as captives. The martyrdom at Karbala became the defining tragedy of Shia Islam, mourned every year in passion and procession, and a timeless emblem of the stand of the righteous against injustice. His shrine at Karbala draws pilgrims by the millions still.
