Antoninus Pius

Roman emperor, r. 138–161

Antoninus Pius was born in 86 into a wealthy senatorial family of Gallic descent and rose through the offices of state with a reputation for fairness and integrity. In 138, as his health declined, the emperor Hadrian adopted the childless Antoninus as his heir, on the condition that Antoninus in turn adopt two younger men — Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus — so securing the succession for a generation to come. On Hadrian’s death that year, Antoninus took the throne.

He won his surname Pius, “the Dutiful,” early, most likely for the loyalty with which he insisted, against a reluctant Senate, on the deification of his adoptive father. His reign of twenty-three years was the calmest and most prosperous of the imperial age — so free of war, crisis and drama that it left historians little to record. He governed the empire from Italy, which he never once left, with frugality and care, reforming the law and tending the provinces, and in Britain his armies pushed the frontier north to raise the turf rampart known as the Antonine Wall.

Counted among the Five Good Emperors, he presided over the Roman world at the very height of its peace and confidence. When he died in 161, the succession he had guarded passed smoothly to Marcus Aurelius, who ruled alongside Lucius Verus — and almost at once the long tranquillity of Antoninus’s reign gave way to war and plague.
