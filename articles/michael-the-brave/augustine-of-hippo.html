Augustine of Hippo

Church Father and theologian, 354–430 AD

Augustine was born at Thagaste in Roman North Africa to a pagan father and a devout Christian mother, Monica, whose prayers for her brilliant, wayward son he would later immortalise. Trained in rhetoric, he taught the art at Carthage, Rome, and Milan, while his restless mind passed through the philosophy kindled in him by reading Cicero and through years as an adherent of the Manichaean sect. His youth of ambition and pleasure he summed up in the famous prayer, "Lord, make me chaste—but not yet."

At Milan the preaching of Bishop Ambrose and the writings of the Neoplatonist Plotinus opened a path back to the faith of his mother. In a garden, hearing a child's voice chant "take up and read," he opened the letters of Paul and was converted; Ambrose baptised him in 387. He returned to Africa and in 395 became bishop of the coastal town of Hippo, which he served for the rest of his life.

From that modest see he reshaped the mind of the Western Church. His Confessions created the form of spiritual autobiography, and his vast City of God, written as the Roman world reeled from the sack of Rome, set the earthly city against the eternal one and founded the Christian philosophy of history. In long combat with the Donatists and with Pelagius he hammered out the doctrines of original sin and saving grace that would govern Western theology for a thousand years and feed the Reformation of Luther and Calvin. He died in 430 as the Vandals besieged his city, the greatest of the Latin Fathers and a bridge from the ancient world to the age to come.

The road continues to Ambrose.
