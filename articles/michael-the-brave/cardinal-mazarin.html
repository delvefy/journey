Cardinal Mazarin

Italian-born chief minister of France, 1602–1661

Born Giulio Mazzarino in the Abruzzi and educated by the Jesuits, he began as a papal diplomat and soldier before the dazzled Cardinal Richelieu drew him into French service. Naturalised as a Frenchman and made a cardinal—though never a priest—he succeeded Richelieu as chief minister on the old cardinal’s death in 1642, and after Louis XIII died the following year he governed France as the indispensable partner of the regent, Anne of Austria, to whom he was bound by a devotion many believed a secret marriage.

His ascendancy provoked the Fronde, a series of revolts by parlement and high nobility, including the great general Condé, that twice drove him into exile. Supple, patient, and endlessly cunning, Mazarin outlasted his enemies, returned in triumph, and restored the authority of the crown stronger than before. Abroad he crowned Richelieu’s work: the Peace of Westphalia in 1648 ended the Thirty Years’ War to France’s advantage, and an alliance with Oliver Cromwell’s England helped force Spain to the Peace of the Pyrenees in 1659, sealed by Louis XIV’s marriage to a Spanish princess.

He amassed an enormous fortune and a famous library, and groomed able servants such as the young Colbert. Above all he educated the boy-king in the arts of rule, so that when Mazarin died in 1661 Louis XIV chose to govern alone. The cardinal left France the dominant power in Europe.
