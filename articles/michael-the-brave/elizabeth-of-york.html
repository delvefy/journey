Elizabeth of York

Queen of England, 1466–1503

Elizabeth was born in 1466, the eldest child of King Edward IV and his commoner queen, Elizabeth Woodville, and her early life was shadowed by the long dynastic struggle of the Wars of the Roses. On her father’s death in 1483 her two young brothers, the elder briefly proclaimed Edward V, vanished into the Tower in the keeping of their uncle, who seized the crown as Richard III and had Elizabeth and her siblings declared illegitimate.

This made the surviving princess the most coveted heiress in the kingdom. Henry Tudor, the exiled Lancastrian claimant, publicly vowed to marry her and so unite the warring houses of York and Lancaster. After he overthrew Richard III at Bosworth in 1485 and took the throne as Henry VII, he kept his word, and their marriage the following year fused the red and white roses into the emblem of the new Tudor dynasty.

As queen, Elizabeth was admired for her gentleness and piety and bore the king several children, among them the future Henry VIII. Her union with Henry VII gave the fragile Tudor regime the legitimacy of York blood and helped close the era of civil war. She died in 1503, on her thirty-seventh birthday, after the birth of a short-lived daughter, mourned deeply by her husband and people.
