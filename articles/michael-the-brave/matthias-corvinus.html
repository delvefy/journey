Matthias Corvinus

King of Hungary, 1443–1490

Matthias Hunyadi was born on 23 February 1443 in Kolozsvár in Transylvania, the second son of the great soldier John Hunyadi. After his father’s death and the execution of his elder brother by a jealous court, the teenaged Matthias was himself held captive in Prague; yet in 1458 the Hungarian nobles, weary of foreign kings, elected him to the throne at the age of fifteen. He took the surname Corvinus, from the raven of his family arms, and set about making himself one of the most formidable monarchs of the age.

To free himself from dependence on the fractious barons he built the Black Army, a salaried professional force that became one of the first standing armies in Europe, and with it he campaigned on every frontier. He drove back the Ottomans in Bosnia, contested the crown of Bohemia, and waged a long war against the Emperor Frederick III, capturing Vienna in 1485 and ruling from the conquered city. At home he centralized justice and taxation so firmly that folk memory remembered him as “Matthias the Just.”

He was also a true Renaissance prince. Married to Beatrice of Naples, he drew Italian humanists, artists and scholars to Buda and assembled the Bibliotheca Corviniana, among the richest libraries in Europe outside Italy, its volumes famed for their illumination. When he died suddenly in Vienna on 6 April 1490, leaving no legitimate heir, his standing army was disbanded, his conquests slipped away, and his great library was scattered — but the memory of Hungary’s golden age under Matthias endured.
