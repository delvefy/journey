Isabella I of Castile

Catholic Monarch of Spain, 1451–1504

Isabella was born at Madrigal de las Altas Torres on 22 April 1451, the elder child of King John II of Castile by his second wife Isabella of Portugal. Her father died when she was three; her mother withdrew into religious melancholy; she and her younger brother Alfonso were raised at the court of their elder half-brother Henry IV, whose weak reign and disputed succession made them political pieces from infancy. In 1469, at eighteen, she eluded the king’s plans for her and married, in haste and secrecy at Valladolid, her second cousin Ferdinand of Aragon. The marriage was the foundation of modern Spain.

On Henry IV’s death in 1474 she claimed Castile against his ostensible daughter Juana la Beltraneja. The five-year War of the Castilian Succession, settled by the Treaty of Alcáçovas in 1479, confirmed her on her throne the same year that Ferdinand inherited Aragon. The two ruled as equal partners—tanto monta, monta tanto—reforming Castilian finances, breaking the urban hermandades that had defied the crown, founding the Spanish Inquisition under Tomás de Torquemada in 1478, and pressing the long war against the emirate of Granada. The fall of Granada on 2 January 1492 ended seven centuries of Muslim rule in the peninsula, and Pope Alexander VI rewarded the couple that same year with the title Catholic Monarchs.

The other events of 1492 were of comparable weight. The Edict of Expulsion in March drove the Jews from Spain. In August Christopher Columbus, whose proposal Isabella had finally accepted at Santa Fe in April, sailed from Palos under her flag and her promise of revenues, returning the next spring with news of land across the Ocean Sea. The Indies brought her crown of Castile its American empire. Her later years were shadowed by grief: her son Juan died in 1497, her daughter Isabel and the infant Miguel soon after, leaving the unstable Joanna, married into the Habsburgs, as heir. She died at Medina del Campo on 26 November 1504.
