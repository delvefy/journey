Origen

Christian theologian of Alexandria, c. 184–c. 253 AD

Origen was born into a Christian family in Alexandria, and his faith was forged in fire: his father Leonides was martyred in a persecution while Origen was still a youth, and the boy, by tradition, was restrained from rushing to share his fate. Precociously learned, he took charge of the great catechetical school of Alexandria while still young and studied philosophy in the city—reportedly under Ammonius Saccas, the teacher also of the Neoplatonist Plotinus.

He became the most prodigious scholar of the early Church. He compiled the Hexapla, a monumental edition setting the Hebrew scriptures and their Greek versions side by side in parallel columns, pioneering the textual study of the Bible. In On First Principles he attempted the first systematic theology of the Christian faith, and in Against Celsus he mounted its most formidable defence against pagan philosophy. To these he added a vast tide of sermons and commentaries, reading Scripture, after the manner of his beloved Plato, for its hidden spiritual meanings.

His fusion of the Gospel and Greek philosophy made him profoundly influential, but some of his speculations—the pre-existence of souls and the final restoration of all things—would later be condemned as heresy. He quarrelled with his bishop and removed to Caesarea, and in the persecution under Decius he was imprisoned and tortured, dying a few years later of his sufferings. Revered and disputed in equal measure, never formally a saint, Origen shaped Christian thought for centuries; the Latin Father Jerome would first champion and then turn against his memory.
