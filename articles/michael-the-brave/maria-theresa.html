Maria Theresa

Archduchess of Austria and Holy Roman Empress, 1717–1780

Maria Theresa was the only woman ever to rule the Habsburg lands, and one of the ablest sovereigns of the eighteenth century. Born in 1717, she inherited Austria, Hungary, and Bohemia in 1740 under the Pragmatic Sanction her father, Charles VI, had laboured to secure. Her accession was the signal for attack: Frederick the Great of Prussia seized her wealthy province of Silesia and plunged Europe into the War of the Austrian Succession.

The young queen fought back with extraordinary resolve, rallying the Hungarian nobles with a famous personal appeal and holding her sprawling inheritance together. She kept her crowns, but never recovered Silesia, even after her chancellor Kaunitz engineered the startling Diplomatic Revolution of 1756, allying Austria with its old foe France under Louis XV, and even after the long Seven Years' War against Frederick.

At home she was a tireless reformer, centralising the administration, overhauling the army and the treasury, and introducing compulsory schooling, all while remaining a devout and conservative Catholic. With her husband, the Emperor Francis I, she had sixteen children, and married them across the courts of Europe—among them Joseph II, who became her co-ruler and heir, the future Leopold II, and Marie Antoinette, queen of France.

In her later years she shared power uneasily with the impatient, reforming Joseph, opposed but ultimately joined the First Partition of Poland alongside Catherine the Great and Prussia, and presided as matriarch over the Habsburg-Lorraine dynasty until her death in 1780.
