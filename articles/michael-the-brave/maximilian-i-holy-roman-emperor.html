Maximilian I, Holy Roman Emperor

Habsburg King of the Romans and Emperor, 1459–1519

Maximilian was born at Wiener Neustadt on 22 March 1459, the only surviving son of the Emperor Frederick III and Eleanor of Portugal. His father's caution and parsimony left him as a young man with a precocious appetite for the chase, for arms, and for the chivalric romance he would later cultivate as a public language. In August 1477, at eighteen, he married Mary of Burgundy at Ghent, the heiress of Charles the Bold, who had been killed seven months earlier at Nancy. The marriage brought the Burgundian Netherlands and Franche-Comté into the Habsburg lands and made him, in his own and the empire's eyes, the heir of the dukes who had nearly built a middle kingdom between France and Germany.

Mary's death in a riding accident in 1482 left him to defend her inheritance against Louis XI of France and the cities of Flanders, a struggle that ended only with the Peace of Senlis in 1493. The same year he succeeded his father as King of the Romans, head of the empire; in 1508, with the consent of Julius II, he took the title Emperor-elect, the first to do so without coronation at Rome. His Italian wars against Venice and France, fought through the League of Cambrai and the Holy League, were costly and inconclusive. Inside the empire he reformed the Imperial Diet, founded the Reichskammergericht, divided Germany into ten Circles, and pressed the levies that founded the imperial postal service.

His diplomacy was dynastic. His daughter Margaret was betrothed in turn to Charles VIII and to the Spanish heir Juan; his son Philip the Handsome was married to Joanna of Castile, and the double marriage of his grandchildren Ferdinand and Mary to the Jagiellons brought Bohemia and Hungary, after Mohács, into the family. He patronised Conrad Celtis and the German humanists, employed Albrecht Dürer on the woodcuts of the Triumphal Arch and the Triumphal Procession, and oversaw the half-allegorical autobiographies Theuerdank and Weisskunig. He died at Wels in Upper Austria on 12 January 1519 and was succeeded as emperor, after a contested election, by his grandson Charles V.
