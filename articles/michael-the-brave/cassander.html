Cassander

King of Macedon, c. 358–297 BCE

Cassander was one of the successors of Alexander the Great and, for the last decade of his life, king of Macedon. The eldest son of Alexander's powerful regent Antipater, he was passed over for the succession when his father died, the regency going instead to the aged general Polyperchon. Resentful and ambitious, Cassander allied with the other commanders and fought his way to mastery of Macedon and Greece.

His rise was marked by a ruthless resolve to extinguish Alexander's own line. In 316 BCE he captured and put to death Alexander's fierce mother, Olympias. A few years later he had Alexander's widow, the princess Roxana, and their young son and heir, Alexander IV, secretly murdered, and later destroyed Heracles, Alexander's illegitimate son as well. With them ended the royal house of Macedon.

To bind himself to that house he married Thessalonike, a half-sister of Alexander, and gave her name to a new city he founded—Thessalonica, which survives as a great city to this day. He also rebuilt Thebes, which Alexander had razed.

In the long wars of the successors he joined the coalitions that checked the overreaching Antigonus and his son Demetrius, sharing in the victory at Ipsus in 301 that broke Antigonus's bid to reunite the empire. Cassander took the royal title around 305 and ruled Macedon until his death from disease in 297 BCE, after which his own sons quickly lost the throne his crimes had won.

The road continues to Olympias.
