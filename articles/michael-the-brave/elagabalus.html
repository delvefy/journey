Elagabalus

Roman emperor, c. 204–222 AD

Born at Emesa in Syria, Elagabalus was the hereditary high priest of the local sun-god El-Gabal, from whom he takes his name. He owed his throne to his formidable grandmother Julia Maesa, sister of the empress Julia Domna, who put about the claim that the boy was an illegitimate son of Caracalla and won the eastern legions to his cause. In 218 their forces overthrew the emperor Macrinus, and the fourteen-year-old priest became Augustus under the dynastic name Marcus Aurelius Antoninus.

His brief reign was dominated by religious revolution. He carried the great black stone of his sun-god to Rome, raised a splendid temple to house it, and sought to set the Syrian deity above Jupiter himself as the supreme god of the empire—an affront to Roman tradition that scandalised the capital. Hostile historians heaped upon him further tales of extravagance and sexual outrage, much of which may be exaggeration, but which fixed his name forever as a byword for decadence.

Real power lay with the women of his family, and when his behaviour turned the soldiers against him, his grandmother coolly transferred her support to his young cousin, whom she compelled Elagabalus to adopt. In 222 the Praetorian Guard rose against him; Elagabalus and his mother were cut down, their bodies dragged through the streets and thrown into the Tiber, and his memory was officially condemned. The throne passed to his adopted heir, Alexander Severus.
