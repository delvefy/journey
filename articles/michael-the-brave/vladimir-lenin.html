Vladimir Lenin

Russian revolutionary and founder of the Soviet Union, 1870–1924

Vladimir Ilyich Ulyanov was born in 1870 at Simbirsk on the middle Volga, the son of a provincial school inspector ennobled for his services. The hanging of his older brother Alexander in 1887 for conspiring to assassinate Tsar Alexander III turned the teenage Vladimir against the autocracy. He read Marx in his late teens, took a brilliant external law degree from St Petersburg in absentia, and entered the Marxist underground in the early 1890s under the alias of Lenin. Arrest in 1895 and three years of Siberian exile followed.

Abroad after 1900 he edited the émigré paper Iskra and wrote What Is to Be Done? (1902), arguing that a successful socialist revolution required not a mass democratic party but a tight, disciplined vanguard of professional revolutionaries. The 1903 split of Russian Social Democracy into Bolsheviks (the majority faction Lenin led) and Mensheviks crystallised the doctrine. He spent the next fifteen years in Geneva, Paris, Cracow, and Zurich, writing and waiting.

The February Revolution of 1917 took him by surprise in Switzerland. The German General Staff, hoping he would knock Russia out of the war, sent him east in a sealed train through Germany and Scandinavia. He arrived at the Finland Station in April with the April Theses, an uncompromising call to seize power; in October the Bolsheviks did. He pulled Russia out of the First World War at Brest-Litovsk, defeated the Whites and the Western interventions in a civil war that ravaged the country, and in 1922 founded the Soviet Union, the world's first state explicitly built on Marxist principles.

His New Economic Policy of 1921 admitted limited markets to repair the economy he had helped destroy. He suffered a series of strokes from 1922 and died at Gorki on 22 January 1924 at fifty-three, his Testament warning the Party against Stalin already drafted and ignored. His embalmed body was placed in a mausoleum on Red Square that has held him ever since. The rival co-revolutionary, his comrade in 1917 and his most brilliant lieutenant, who would after Lenin's death lose the succession struggle to Stalin and end with an ice-axe in his Mexican exile, was Leon Trotsky.
