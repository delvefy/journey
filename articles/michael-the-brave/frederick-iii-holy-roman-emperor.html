Frederick III, Holy Roman Emperor

Holy Roman Emperor, 1415–1493

Frederick III was born on 21 September 1415 at Innsbruck, a prince of the house of Habsburg. Elected King of the Romans in 1440, he travelled to Rome in 1452 to be crowned emperor by Pope Nicholas V — the last Holy Roman Emperor ever to receive the imperial crown in the Eternal City from the pope’s own hands. His reign of fifty-three years was the longest of any emperor, and for much of it he seemed the least effective: cautious to the point of paralysis, he was driven from Vienna by Matthias Corvinus of Hungary and wandered his own realm while his authority drained away.

Yet beneath the torpor lay a stubborn dynastic patience. Frederick adopted the cryptic device A.E.I.O.U., taken to mean that the whole world was destined to be subject to Austria, and he laboured above all to advance his family. His most fateful stroke was to secure for his son Maximilian the hand of Mary of Burgundy, heiress of Charles the Bold, a marriage that brought the wealthy Burgundian lands into Habsburg hands and laid the foundation of the empire on which his great-grandson Charles V would one day say the sun never set.

His court drew the brilliant humanist Enea Silvio Piccolomini, who served as imperial secretary and was crowned poet laureate by Frederick before rising to the papacy as Pius II. Outliving nearly all his rivals, Frederick died at Linz on 19 August 1493, having achieved through sheer endurance and a well-judged wedding what abler men had failed to win by the sword: the future greatness of his dynasty.
