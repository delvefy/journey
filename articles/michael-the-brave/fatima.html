Fatima

Daughter of the Prophet Muhammad, c.605–632

Fatima was the youngest daughter of Muhammad and his first wife Khadija, born in Mecca in the years before her father’s prophetic call. Of all his children she was the dearest to him and the one whose line endured: from her descend the sayyids and sharifs who trace their blood to the Prophet. Tradition honours her as al-Zahra, the Radiant, and as the foremost of the women of paradise.

She married her father’s cousin Ali ibn Abi Talib, and their home became the model of pious simplicity at the centre of the early community. Their children — Hasan and Husayn, and the daughters Zaynab and Umm Kulthum — carried the Prophet’s household into the next generation, and with Ali and her two sons she was gathered with Muhammad beneath his cloak as the People of the House, the Ahl al-Bayt whom the Shia revere above all.

Her life ended in grief. Within months of her father’s death in 632 she too died, still young, after a bitter dispute with the caliph Abu Bakr over the inheritance of the Prophet’s property at Fadak — a quarrel whose memory still divides Sunni and Shia accounts of those days. Mourned as the purest of women, she became one of the most venerated figures in Islam; the Fatimid caliphs who later ruled Egypt took their very name from their claim to descend from her. Through Fatima alone the family of the Prophet continued.
