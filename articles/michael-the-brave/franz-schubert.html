Franz Schubert

Austrian composer, 1797–1828

Schubert was born on the outskirts of Vienna, the son of a schoolmaster, and received early musical training that included lessons from the court composer Salieri. Though he worked for a time as a teacher, he soon devoted himself entirely to composition, living modestly among a devoted circle of friends who gathered for evenings of his music that came to be known as Schubertiads. In a short life he produced an enormous quantity of work of extraordinary beauty.

He was, above all, the supreme master of the German art song, or lied. Setting the poetry of Goethe, Schiller, Heine, and others, he wrote some six hundred songs, from the dramatic Erlkönig, composed as a teenager, to the great cycles Die schöne Müllerin and Winterreise, in which voice and piano together convey worlds of feeling. He fused melody and harmony to give simple verses profound emotional depth.

Schubert also composed symphonies, including the haunting “Unfinished” and the expansive “Great” C major, along with chamber works such as the Trout Quintet, piano sonatas, and Masses. He revered Beethoven, who lived nearby in Vienna, and was among the torchbearers at the older master’s funeral. Little more than a year later Schubert himself was dead at only thirty-one, his genius scarcely recognised beyond his friends. At his own request he was buried near Beethoven, and posterity came to rank him among the greatest of all composers.
