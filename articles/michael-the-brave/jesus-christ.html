Jesus Christ

Jewish teacher of Roman Judea, founder figure of Christianity, c. 4 BC–c. AD 30

Jesus of Nazareth was a Jewish preacher and healer who lived in Roman-ruled Galilee and Judea in the first century. The Gospels relate that he was born in Bethlehem to Mary and raised in Nazareth, and that his public ministry began when he was baptised by John the Baptist in the Jordan. Most historians accept the broad outline of a Galilean teacher who gathered followers, was crucified by the Roman authorities, and became the object of a movement that outlived him.

According to the Gospel accounts, Jesus travelled through Galilee proclaiming the coming of the kingdom of God, teaching in vivid parables, and—as his followers believed—working healings and other signs. He drew around him twelve close disciples, chief among them Peter, and his teaching of love, forgiveness, and the reversal of worldly fortune, preserved in passages such as the Sermon on the Mount, became the moral core of the new faith.

His growing reputation brought conflict with religious authorities in Jerusalem. After a final supper with his disciples he was, by tradition, betrayed by Judas Iscariot, tried, and crucified under the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate. Christians hold that he rose from the dead, and this conviction became the foundation of their faith. Spread by apostles such as Peter and Paul, the movement grew from a Jewish sect into Christianity, today the world's largest religion. Revered by Christians as the Son of God and the Messiah and honoured in Islam as a prophet, Jesus is among the most influential figures in all of human history.
