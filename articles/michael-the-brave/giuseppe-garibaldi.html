Giuseppe Garibaldi

Italian revolutionary and general, 1807–1882

Garibaldi was born in Nice and went to sea as a young man before being drawn into Giuseppe Mazzini’s movement Young Italy. Condemned to death for his part in a failed rising, he escaped to South America, where for more than a decade he fought in the wars of Brazil and Uruguay, forged the band of red-shirted followers that became his trademark, and won a reputation as a guerrilla commander of genius, the "Hero of Two Worlds."

Returning to Italy, he fought in the revolutions of 1848 and helped defend the short-lived Roman Republic against French arms. His supreme exploit came in 1860, when he sailed for Sicily with roughly a thousand volunteers, the famous Mille. Against all odds his red shirts overran Sicily and then the mainland, toppling the Bourbon Kingdom of the Two Sicilies and conquering Naples in a campaign that astonished Europe.

At the climax he met King Victor Emmanuel II and, declining personal power, handed over his conquests with the single word "I obey," making possible the proclamation of a united Kingdom of Italy in 1861. Twice more he marched on Rome and twice was turned back. He had earlier refused an offer, conveyed on behalf of Abraham Lincoln, of a command in the American Civil War. A republican by conviction who served a king for the sake of unity, he became a worldwide symbol of liberty and died on his island of Caprera in 1882.
