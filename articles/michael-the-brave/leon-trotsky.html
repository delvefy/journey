Leon Trotsky

Russian revolutionary and theorist, 1879–1940

Lev Davidovich Bronstein was born in 1879 to a Jewish farming family in the steppe of southern Ukraine. He turned to Marxism as a teenager in Odessa, was first arrested at eighteen, and adopted the pseudonym Trotsky—the name of one of his Siberian jailers—during his first escape from exile in 1902. He moved through the émigré circles of London and Vienna, sided sometimes with the Mensheviks and sometimes with the Bolsheviks, and was the elected leader of the St Petersburg Soviet during the failed 1905 revolution.

He returned to Russia in May 1917, joined the Bolsheviks decisively, and organised the October seizure of power in Petrograd. As People's Commissar for Foreign Affairs he negotiated the brutal Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with Imperial Germany; as People's Commissar for War from 1918 he created the Red Army out of nothing, built its officer corps largely from former Tsarist officers under political commissars, and led it to victory in the Russian Civil War. His armoured train carried him back and forth across thousands of miles of front for three years.

He was at the peak of his power when Lenin fell ill in 1922. Lenin's Testament warned the Party against Stalin's growing administrative grip and recommended Trotsky as the most able successor, but the Testament was suppressed, Trotsky was politically outmanoeuvred, and by 1927 he had been expelled from the Party, by 1929 from the Soviet Union. He passed through Turkey, France, and Norway and in 1937 reached Mexico, where the painters Diego Rivera and Frida Kahlo gave him refuge in the Casa Azul at Coyoacán.

From Mexico he wrote his History of the Russian Revolution, his biography of Stalin, and the founding documents of the Fourth International, attempting to keep alive an authentic revolutionary Marxism against Stalinism. On 20 August 1940 the Spanish Stalinist agent Ramón Mercader, posing as a sympathetic friend, drove an ice-axe into his skull in his Coyoacán study; he died the next day. The Mexican painter who had sheltered him, whose own short, violent life had already made her her country's most recognisable artist, was Frida Kahlo.
