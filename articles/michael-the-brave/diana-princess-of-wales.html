Diana, Princess of Wales

British royal, 1961–1997

Diana Frances Spencer was born in 1961 at Park House on the Sandringham estate, the fourth child of the future 8th Earl Spencer. The family had served the Royal Household for generations, and the Spencer girls grew up among the corridors of an English landed gentry already in slow decline. After her parents' bitter divorce in 1969 she was raised between Park House, Althorp, and Norfolk boarding schools, and worked briefly as a kindergarten assistant in Pimlico in the months before her engagement.

In July 1981, at twenty, she married the Prince of Wales in St Paul's Cathedral before a global television audience estimated at 750 million, becoming the Princess of Wales. The first photographs of her with her infant son William in 1982, and the birth of Prince Harry in 1984, made her the most photographed woman in the world. Her marriage, almost from the start, was unhappy: by 1992 the couple was formally separated, by 1996 divorced.

What rescued her from royal seclusion was a public ministry of her own. Her embrace of HIV patients in 1987, at a time when the disease was still met with public terror, and her walks through cleared minefields with the Halo Trust in Angola in 1997 reshaped what British royals could do in the world. She worked with hundreds of charities, was a steady advocate for landmine victims and the homeless, and lent her presence to causes which had not previously had royal protection.

She died in the early hours of 31 August 1997 in a car crash in the Pont de l'Alma tunnel in Paris, with her partner Dodi Fayed and the driver Henri Paul. The public mourning that followed—the mile-long crowds at Kensington Palace, the funeral in Westminster Abbey, her brother's pointed eulogy, Elton John's reworked "Candle in the Wind"—forced changes in the conduct of the British monarchy that the institution had not envisaged. The husband from whom she had been divorced and who would later, in 2022, succeed his mother as the British sovereign was Charles III.

The road continues to Charles III of the United Kingdom.
