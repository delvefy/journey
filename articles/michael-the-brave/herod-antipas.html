Herod Antipas

Tetrarch of Galilee and Perea, ruled c. 4 BC–AD 39

Herod Antipas was a son of Herod the Great, and on his father's death in 4 BC the emperor Augustus divided the kingdom among the heirs, granting Antipas the tetrarchy of Galilee and Perea. He governed these districts for more than forty years as a client of Rome. His most lasting public work was the founding of a new capital on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, which he named Tiberias in honour of the emperor Tiberius, under whom he held his power.

Antipas set aside his first wife, a daughter of the Nabataean king Aretas IV, in order to marry Herodias, the former wife of his half-brother. The match drew the public rebuke of John the Baptist, whom Antipas imprisoned and, according to the Gospels, beheaded after the dance of Herodias's daughter. The discarded marriage also provoked a war with Aretas, in which Antipas's army was destroyed—a defeat some contemporaries read as retribution for the prophet's death.

He appears again in the Gospel of Luke, which relates that Pontius Pilate, learning Jesus was a Galilean, sent the prisoner to Antipas during his trial; the tetrarch questioned and mocked Jesus before returning him to the governor, and the two rulers were said to have become friends that day. Jesus is reported to have called him "that fox." Late in life, prompted by Herodias to seek the title of king, Antipas instead fell to the accusations of his nephew Agrippa and was deposed and exiled by the emperor Caligula.
