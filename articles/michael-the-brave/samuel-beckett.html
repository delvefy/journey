Samuel Beckett

1906–1989, Irish novelist and playwright

Samuel Barclay Beckett was born at Foxrock, on the southern outskirts of Dublin, into a comfortable Anglo-Irish Protestant family. He read modern languages at Trinity College Dublin, took a first in French and Italian, and in 1928 went to Paris as lecteur d'anglais at the École Normale Supérieure. There he was introduced to James Joyce, then composing Finnegans Wake, and for two years served as a sometime amanuensis and walking companion. His first published prose, a 1929 essay called “Dante... Bruno. Vico.. Joyce,” defended Joyce's method, and his 1931 monograph Proust read Marcel Proust as a metaphysical novelist of memory and habit. Dante, learned at Trinity, supplied him with characters and cosmology for the rest of his life.

He drifted between Dublin, London and Paris through the 1930s, publishing the stories of More Pricks Than Kicks and the novel Murphy, was stabbed in a Paris street in 1938, and during the war served in the French Resistance cell Gloria SMH. The Gestapo broke the cell in 1942, and he hid out the war in the Vaucluse village of Roussillon, where he wrote Watt. He returned to Paris in 1945 and began to write directly in French.

The years 1946 to 1953 produced the trilogy of novels Molloy, Malone Dies and The Unnamable and the play Waiting for Godot. Godot, first staged in Paris in 1953 and in London in 1955, made him world-famous and recast post-war theatre. Endgame, Krapp's Last Tape, Happy Days, and the radio plays All That Fall and Embers followed, his work growing steadily shorter and more austere. In 1965 he directed Buster Keaton in his only film, the silent twenty-two-minute Film.

He received the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1969 and refused to attend, calling the award a catastrophe. He lived modestly in Paris with his wife Suzanne, attended rehearsals, and corresponded warmly with disciples among them Harold Pinter. He died in a Paris nursing home in December 1989, six months after Suzanne, and is buried beside her in Montparnasse.
