Giovanni Bellini

Venetian painter, founder of the Venetian school, c. 1430–1516

Giovanni was born about 1430 in Venice, the younger son — possibly natural, possibly legitimate — of the painter Jacopo Bellini, whose two great drawing-books in London and Paris remain the chief witness to the geometry he taught his sons. He grew up in the family workshop alongside his elder brother Gentile and his sister Nicolosia, who in 1453 was married to Andrea Mantegna of Padua; the dry, sculptural manner of Mantegna marks Giovanni's earliest signed work, the Crucifixion of about 1455, and the Agony in the Garden of about 1460 still answers Mantegna almost composition for composition.

The decisive technical change of his career was the slow adoption, from the 1470s, of oil painting on canvas in place of egg tempera on panel, learned in part from the visit of Antonello da Messina to Venice in 1475–76. The shift gave his colour a depth and atmosphere that became the hallmark of the Venetian school. Through the great altarpieces — the San Giobbe sacra conversazione of about 1487, the San Zaccaria altarpiece of 1505, the San Giovanni Crisostomo of 1513 — and through the Doge Leonardo Loredan of 1501, with its quiet enamelled stare, he reshaped Venetian devotional and civic painting. In 1483 he was named pittore ufficiale of the Republic and charged with the historical canvases of the Sala del Maggior Consiglio in the Doge's Palace, lost in the fire of 1577.

He outlived his brother Gentile, took over his unfinished St Mark Preaching in Alexandria, and trained or absorbed almost every painter of the next Venetian generation: Cima, Carpaccio, Sebastiano del Piombo, and above all Giorgione and Titian, who came out of his workshop. In late life he turned to mythological subjects he had earlier refused — the Feast of the Gods of 1514 for Alfonso d'Este, completed after his death by Titian — and to small private pictures of remarkable freedom. Albrecht Dürer, visiting Venice in 1506, judged him "very old, but still the best in painting". He died in Venice on 29 November 1516.

The road continues to Titian.
