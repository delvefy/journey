Queen Victoria

Queen of the United Kingdom and Empress of India, 1819–1901

A granddaughter of George III, Victoria came unexpectedly to the throne in 1837, a girl of eighteen, after the deaths of her uncles left no closer heir. Her reign of nearly sixty-four years, the longest in British history until the present age, gave its name to a whole era. In 1840 she married her cousin Prince Albert of Saxe-Coburg, and their devoted partnership—he as her closest adviser, she bearing nine children—set a new model of domestic royalty. Albert’s death in 1861 plunged her into decades of secluded mourning.

During her reign Britain reached the zenith of its industrial and imperial power. She presided, as a constitutional monarch, over a parade of great ministers, warming to the flattering Conservative Benjamin Disraeli, who had her proclaimed Empress of India in 1876, and chafing under the earnest Liberal William Gladstone. Coaxed from her seclusion in later years, she became the beloved symbol of empire, her Golden and Diamond Jubilees vast imperial celebrations.

Through the marriages of her many children and grandchildren across the courts of Europe she earned the title “grandmother of Europe”; her descendants included the German Kaiser Wilhelm II and the Russian empress. When she died in 1901, having outlived her century, an age seemed to end with her, and the throne passed to her son Edward VII.

The road continues to Kaiser Wilhelm II.
