Adolf Hitler

Dictator of Nazi Germany, 1889–1945

Born at Braunau am Inn in Austria, Hitler drifted through poverty in Vienna, where his hopes of becoming an artist failed, absorbing the antisemitism and German nationalism that would define him. He served as a soldier in the First World War, was decorated and wounded, and emerged embittered by Germany’s defeat. In Munich he joined a tiny nationalist party, transformed it into the National Socialist movement, and after a failed putsch in 1923 set down his ideology in Mein Kampf while imprisoned.

The Great Depression carried him to power. Appointed chancellor in January 1933, he swiftly dismantled the Weimar Republic, banned opposition, and made himself absolute Führer of a one-party state built on terror, propaganda, and a cult of the leader. He rearmed Germany in defiance of the Versailles Treaty, absorbed Austria and Czechoslovakia, and in September 1939 invaded Poland, unleashing the Second World War.

His armies overran most of Europe before the tide turned at Stalingrad and in the skies over Britain. Central to his regime was the Holocaust, the systematic murder of some six million Jews along with Roma, the disabled, and other victims—the gravest crime of the modern age. As Soviet troops fought into Berlin, Hitler killed himself in his bunker in April 1945. He left behind a ruined continent, tens of millions dead, and a name that has become a byword for evil.
