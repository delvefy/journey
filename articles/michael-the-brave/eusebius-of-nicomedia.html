Eusebius of Nicomedia

Arian bishop and leader of the Eusebian party, died 341 AD

Eusebius came from a noble Syrian family and in his youth studied under the martyr-theologian Lucian of Antioch alongside Arius, forming a bond that would shape his whole career. Through court connections he rose to become bishop of Berytus, then of the imperial city of Nicomedia, which placed him close to the seat of power.

He became the foremost political champion of Arius and the leader of the bishops who resisted the Nicene definition. Though he subscribed reluctantly at the Council of Nicaea in 325 and was briefly exiled soon after, his skill and connections quickly restored him to influence, and he came to dominate Constantine's later religious policy.

It was Eusebius who baptised the dying Constantine in 337, and under Constantine's son Constantius II he stood at the height of his power, transferring to the see of Constantinople in 338. From there he orchestrated the repeated depositions of Athanasius and other Nicene leaders, labouring tirelessly to overturn the verdict of Nicaea.

His reach extended beyond the empire's frontiers: he consecrated Ulfilas as bishop to the Goths, helping to make Arian Christianity the faith of the Germanic peoples for generations. He died in 341, still bishop of Constantinople, the most effective organiser the Arian cause ever had, and the man whose followers, the “Eusebians,” carried the struggle on long after Arius himself was gone.
