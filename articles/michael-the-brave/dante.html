Dante

Florentine poet, author of the Commedia, 1265–1321

Dante Alighieri was born in Florence between mid-May and mid-June 1265, the son of Alighiero di Bellincione, a small landholder and money-lender, and Bella, who died when Dante was very young. He was educated in grammar and rhetoric at one of the city schools, possibly under Brunetto Latini, and read philosophy in the Dominican and Franciscan studia. Tradition says he first saw Beatrice — almost certainly Beatrice Portinari, who later married the banker Simone de' Bardi — at a May feast in 1274, when he was nine; her death in June 1290 became the centre of the autobiographical prose-and-verse Vita Nuova which he composed in the early 1290s. He married Gemma Donati about 1285; they had four children.

Dante took the usual cursus honorum of a Florentine commoner. He fought as a cavalryman at Campaldino in 1289 against Arezzo, enrolled in the guild of physicians and apothecaries in 1295, served on minor councils, and in June 1300 was one of the six elected priors of the republic. The factional crisis between the Black and White Guelphs broke during his priorate. In 1301 Boniface VIII summoned the leading Florentine Whites to Rome; while Dante was at the curia, Charles of Valois entered Florence at the pope's invitation and a Black coup overthrew the regime. Dante was sentenced to perpetual exile in January 1302 and never returned.

The remaining twenty years he spent moving among the courts of northern Italy — Verona, Lunigiana, and finally Ravenna under Guido Novello da Polenta. He composed the Latin treatises De vulgari eloquentia and De monarchia, the unfinished philosophical Convivio in Italian, and, above all, the Commedia — Inferno from about 1308, Purgatorio in the 1310s, Paradiso finished only just before his death. His political hopes rested on the imperial expedition of Henry VII of Luxembourg in 1310–13, and their collapse fed the Commedia's polemics against Boniface VIII and Florence. He died at Ravenna on the night of 13–14 September 1321, of a fever caught on an embassy through the marshes, and was buried there.
