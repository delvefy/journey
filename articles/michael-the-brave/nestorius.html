Nestorius

Patriarch of Constantinople, c.386–450

Nestorius was born about 386 in Roman Syria and trained at Antioch, whose theological school stressed the full and distinct humanity of Christ. A monk celebrated for his eloquence in the pulpit, he was raised in 428 by the Emperor Theodosius II to the patriarchate of Constantinople, the most prominent see of the Eastern empire. There his preaching soon ignited a storm.

Nestorius and his circle objected to the popular title Theotokos, “God-bearer,” given to the Virgin Mary, urging instead “Christ-bearer,” lest the divine and human in Christ be confused. To his enemies this seemed to split the Saviour into two persons, a man merely joined to God. Cyril of Alexandria seized upon the words and made Nestorius the great heresiarch of the age, rallying Rome and the Egyptian church against him.

At the Council of Ephesus in 431 Cyril opened the proceedings before Nestorius’s Antiochene defenders could arrive, and the assembled bishops condemned and deposed him. Stripped of his office, Nestorius was sent first to a monastery and at last into exile in the Egyptian desert, where he died around 450, composing a long defence of his orthodoxy. The doctrine that bears his name — whether truly his or a caricature — lived on in the Church of the East, which carried a dyophysite Christianity across Persia and along the Silk Road as far as India and China, long after his own church had cast him out.
