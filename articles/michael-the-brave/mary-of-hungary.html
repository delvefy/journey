Mary of Hungary

Governor of the Habsburg Netherlands, 1505–1558

Mary was born in 1505, a daughter of Philip the Handsome and Joanna of Castile and a sister of the Emperor Charles V and of Ferdinand I. Married young to Louis II of Hungary, she was left a childless widow at twenty-one when her husband perished at the Battle of Mohács in 1526, and she never married again, devoting herself instead to the service of her family and its empire.

Recognising her ability and iron will, Charles V appointed her Governor of the Habsburg Netherlands in 1531, and for nearly a quarter of a century she ruled the rich, populous, and restless Low Countries in his name. She defended the provinces against French invasion, grappled with the spread of the Reformation, and managed the finances on which her brother’s wars so heavily depended, all with a firmness that won the respect even of her opponents.

A cultivated patron, she sat for Titian and gathered a celebrated collection of paintings, and she was famed for her passion for the hunt. When Charles V laid down his crowns in the mid-1550s, Mary resigned her own office and surrendered the government of the Netherlands to his son, Philip II, before retiring with her brother to Spain. She died in 1558, only weeks after the emperor, remembered as one of the most capable of all the Habsburg regents.
