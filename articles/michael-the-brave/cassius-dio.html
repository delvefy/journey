Cassius Dio

Roman senator and historian, c. 155–235 AD

Cassius Dio was born at Nicaea in Bithynia, a Greek-speaking province of Asia Minor, the son of a Roman senator and governor. Following his father into public life, he pursued a long and distinguished career at the heart of the empire, entering the Senate under Commodus and rising through the high offices of state during the Severan age. He governed provinces, served as proconsul of Africa, and crowned his career with a second consulship in 229, holding it jointly with the emperor Alexander Severus himself.

From this vantage Dio witnessed at first hand the turbulent reigns of Commodus, Septimius Severus, Caracalla, and their successors, and he set down what he saw in a vast Roman History, written in Greek across eighty books. The labour of more than twenty years, it traced Rome from the legendary arrival of Aeneas all the way to his own day, a span of a thousand years.

Though much of the work survives only in fragments and later Byzantine summaries, large stretches endure intact, and it remains one of the indispensable sources for the Roman past. His detailed narrative of the reign of Augustus is a foundation of all later study of that period, and his eyewitness account of the cruelty and folly of Commodus, who fought as a gladiator before the people, gives a vivid picture of an empire sliding from its golden age into disorder. With Tacitus, Cassius Dio stands among the chief historians of imperial Rome.
