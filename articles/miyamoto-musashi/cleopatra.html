Cleopatra

Last pharaoh of Ptolemaic Egypt (69–30 BC)

Cleopatra VII was the last pharaoh of Egypt and one of the most famous women in all of history, a ruler whose intelligence, charm, and ambition entangled her with the greatest men of Rome. She belonged to the Greek dynasty of the Ptolemies, descended from a general of Alexander the Great, and came to the throne amid the intrigues of a fading kingdom.

Locked in a struggle with her brother for the crown, she allied herself with Julius Caesar when he came to Egypt, winning his support and his affection; she bore him a son and followed him to Rome. After Caesar's assassination she returned home, and soon bound her fortunes to another Roman master of the East, Mark Antony, whose love for her and lavish grants of territory scandalized and alarmed Rome.

Their union led to war with Caesar's heir, Octavian. At the sea battle of Actium in 31 BC the fleets of Antony and Cleopatra were broken, and the cause was lost. Antony took his own life, and Cleopatra, refusing to be led as a captive through the streets of Rome, followed him in death, by the famous tradition pressing a venomous asp to her body.

With her died the dynasty of the Ptolemies, and Egypt passed into the hands of Rome. Her story, retold by Plutarch and dramatized ever after by poets and playwrights, made her the very emblem of regal allure and tragic grandeur.
