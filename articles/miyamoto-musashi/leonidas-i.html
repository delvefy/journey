Leonidas I

King of Sparta, hero of Thermopylae (d. 480 BC)

Leonidas I was a king of Sparta whose death in battle made him one of the most celebrated soldiers in history. When the Persian king Xerxes led his immense army into Greece in 480 BC, the Greek states chose Leonidas to command the force sent to hold the narrow coastal pass of Thermopylae, the gateway into central Greece.

With a small army built around his personal guard of three hundred Spartans, together with allied contingents, Leonidas blocked the pass against overwhelming numbers. For two days the Greeks held, their discipline and the narrowness of the ground neutralizing the Persian host, until a local traitor revealed a mountain track that let the enemy circle behind them.

Knowing the position was lost, Leonidas sent most of the allies away but remained himself with his Spartans and a few others to cover the retreat and fight to the end. They were annihilated, Leonidas among them, selling their lives to buy time for the Greek cause.

The sacrifice at Thermopylae became, almost at once, a legend, immortalized in the epitaph bidding the passer-by to tell the Spartans that their men had died obedient to the laws. Though a defeat, it steeled Greek resolve for the victories at Salamis and Plataea that followed, and it has stood ever since as the supreme emblem of courage and devotion to duty against hopeless odds.
