Sasaki Kojiro

Japanese swordsman, rival of Musashi (d. 1612)

Sasaki Kojiro, also known by the art-name Ganryu, was a Japanese master swordsman remembered above all as the great rival defeated by Miyamoto Musashi. Much of his early life is obscured by legend, but tradition holds that he trained within the sword lineage descending from Toda Seigen before founding his own school, the Ganryu.

Kojiro fought with an unusually long sword, a weapon nicknamed the "drying pole," and was famed for a swift, sweeping cut called the swallow's-turn, said to have been inspired by watching swallows wheel through the air. His skill and reputation won him a position as official sword instructor to Hosokawa Tadaoki, the powerful lord of the Kokura domain in northern Kyushu.

His fame today rests almost entirely on his final fight. In 1612, under the sanction of the Hosokawa house, he agreed to meet the wandering Musashi in single combat on a small island in the strait between Honshu and Kyushu. By the celebrated account, Musashi arrived deliberately late to unsettle his opponent and struck Kojiro down with a wooden sword reportedly carved from a boat's oar.

Kojiro died on that shore, and the island was thereafter named Ganryujima after his school, a memorial to the loser rather than the victor. Though the documentary record of his life is thin and much that is told of him belongs to later storytelling, his duel with Musashi became one of the most retold episodes in Japanese history, dramatized endlessly in novels, theatre, and film.
