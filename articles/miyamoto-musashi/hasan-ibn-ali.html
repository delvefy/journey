Hasan ibn Ali

Grandson of Muhammad, second Shia Imam (c. 625–670)

Hasan ibn Ali was the elder grandson of the Prophet Muhammad, the first son of Ali and Fatimah, and a figure revered by Shia Muslims as the second of their Imams. Born in Medina, he and his younger brother Husayn were cherished by their grandfather, who is remembered in tradition as carrying them on his shoulders and calling them the leaders of the youth of paradise.

When his father Ali was assassinated in 661, the people of Kufa acclaimed Hasan as caliph. But he faced in Mu'awiya, the powerful governor of Syria, an opponent with a stronger army and a longer reach. Rather than plunge the community into further bloodshed, Hasan chose to negotiate.

He concluded a treaty with Mu'awiya, yielding the caliphate on agreed terms and withdrawing to a quiet life in Medina. The settlement ended the first civil war and gave the year its name as the Year of Unification, though it left the claims of Ali's house unresolved.

Hasan lived out his remaining years in retirement and died around 670; Shia tradition holds that he was poisoned at the instigation of his enemies, to clear the path of the succession. Honoured as an Imam descended directly from the Prophet, he is remembered for choosing peace over power, and from him descends one of the two great branches of the Prophet's family.
