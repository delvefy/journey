Raphael

Italian painter and architect of the High Renaissance (1483–1520)

Raphael was, with Leonardo and Michelangelo, one of the three supreme masters of the Italian High Renaissance, celebrated above all for the grace, harmony, and serene clarity of his art. Born in 1483 in the cultured court city of Urbino, the son of a painter, he trained under Pietro Perugino and quickly surpassed him.

Moving to Florence around 1504, the young Raphael studied the works of the older Leonardo da Vinci and the formidable Michelangelo, absorbing their lessons in composition and the human figure while painting a famous series of tender Madonnas that made his reputation.

His genius reached full flower in Rome. Summoned by Pope Julius II, he was given the papal apartments of the Vatican to decorate, and there he painted The School of Athens, a luminous gathering of the philosophers of antiquity that remains the very emblem of Renaissance humanism. Under Julius and his successor he became the most sought-after artist in the city, running a great workshop, painting incomparable portraits, and eventually taking charge of the building of Saint Peter's and the care of Rome's antiquities.

Affable and admired where his rivals were solitary or fierce, Raphael won the devotion of patrons and pupils alike. He died suddenly in 1520, at only thirty-seven, and was buried with honour in the Pantheon. His balanced, idealized beauty would be taken as the model of perfection by academies of art for the next three hundred years.
