Hypatia

Alexandrian philosopher and mathematician (c. 360–415)

Hypatia of Alexandria was the most renowned woman scholar of the ancient world, a philosopher, mathematician, and astronomer who led the Neoplatonic school of her city. The daughter of Theon, himself a noted mathematician, she was schooled by him in the sciences and surpassed him in fame, drawing students from across the Mediterranean to hear her teach the philosophy of Plato and the mysteries of the heavens.

She was a working scientist as well as a teacher. She produced commentaries on the great mathematical works of antiquity, on the algebra of Diophantus and the conics of Apollonius, and she helped revise the astronomical tables of Ptolemy, whose system of the heavens she expounded. She is said to have designed instruments such as the astrolabe for charting the stars.

Admired for her wisdom and her virtue, she moved freely in the public life of Alexandria and was the friend and counsellor of the Roman governor Orestes. But Orestes was locked in a bitter struggle with the patriarch Cyril, and Hypatia, the pagan philosopher close to the governor, became a target of the city's religious passions.

In 415 a mob seized her in the street and killed her with savage cruelty. Her death was felt even then as the snuffing out of a great light, and in later ages she became an enduring symbol of learning and reason destroyed by fanaticism.
