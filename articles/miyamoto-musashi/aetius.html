Aetius

Roman general, "the last of the Romans" (c. 391–454)

Aetius was the soldier and statesman who, in the twilight of the Western Roman Empire, held it together by force of will and arms, earning the title "the last of the Romans." As a youth he had been given as a hostage first to the Goths and then to the Huns, and from that experience he gained both a deep knowledge of the barbarian peoples and friendships among the Huns that he would later turn to Rome's advantage.

Rising to supreme command under the emperor Valentinian III and his mother Galla Placidia, Aetius became the true ruler of the West. For decades he campaigned ceaselessly, playing the barbarian nations against one another, hiring Hunnic warriors to crush Goths, Burgundians, and Franks, and so preserving a semblance of Roman Gaul.

His supreme hour came in 451, when his old allies the Huns, now led by Attila, turned on the West and poured into Gaul. Aetius forged a desperate coalition of Romans and Visigoths and met Attila on the Catalaunian Plains, where in a tremendous battle he checked the Hunnic advance and turned Attila back, in one of the decisive engagements of history.

Yet his very greatness doomed him. In 454 the jealous and fearful emperor Valentinian struck him down with his own hand. With Aetius died the last effective defence of the West, and the empire he had shielded soon dissolved.
