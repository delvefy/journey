Cesare Borgia

Italian condottiero and prince (1475–1507)

Cesare Borgia was the ruthless and brilliant son of Pope Alexander VI, a soldier-prince whose career of conquest and treachery in Renaissance Italy became the very model of unscrupulous statecraft. Born in 1475, he was raised to the cardinalate by his father while still a teenager, but he had no taste for the Church and became the first man ever to resign the office of cardinal.

Backed by his father's authority and allied to the king of France, who made him Duke of Valentinois, Cesare set out to carve a state of his own from the lawless lordships of the Romagna. He took city after city by a mixture of force, cunning, and outright betrayal, most notoriously luring his treacherous captains to Senigallia and having them strangled. To survey and fortify his conquests he employed, for a time, the engineer Leonardo da Vinci.

His daring and decisiveness made a deep impression on a Florentine envoy who watched him closely, Niccolo Machiavelli, who would later hold him up in The Prince as the example of the new ruler who seizes and secures power by sheer ability and will.

Yet Cesare's strength rested wholly on his father's life. When Alexander VI died in 1503 and the family's bitter enemy ascended the papal throne as Julius II, Cesare's dominion crumbled with astonishing speed. Stripped of his lands and imprisoned, he eventually escaped to Spain, where he was killed in a minor skirmish in 1507, his great ambitions undone.
