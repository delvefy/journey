Furuta Oribe

Japanese samurai and tea master (1544–1615)

Furuta Oribe was a samurai lord and tea master who, after the death of Sen no Rikyu, became the leading authority on the tea ceremony in Japan and the great arbiter of taste in his generation. Born in 1544, he served as a warrior under Oda Nobunaga, then under Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and finally under the Tokugawa, fighting on the winning side at Sekigahara.

As Rikyu's foremost disciple he inherited the master's prestige but not his temperament. Where Rikyu had prized quiet austerity, Oribe delighted in boldness, asymmetry, and surprise. He favored deliberately warped and irregular forms, and his name endures in Oribe ware, the striking green-glazed, distorted pottery made to his taste, one of the most recognizable styles in Japanese ceramics.

His eminence carried him to the heart of the new regime, where he served as tea instructor to the second shogun, Tokugawa Hidetada, shaping the tea culture of the warrior elite. For a time he was the most celebrated man of taste in the land, consulted by lords and craftsmen alike.

His end mirrored his master's. In 1615, amid the final destruction of the Toyotomi at Osaka, Oribe was suspected of secret disloyalty to the Tokugawa. Offered no real chance to clear himself, he was commanded to take his own life, and he did so in silence along with his son. His pupils, above all Kobori Enshu, carried his vision of tea into the long peace that followed.
