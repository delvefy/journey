Elijah

Hebrew prophet of the northern kingdom (9th century BC, by tradition)

Elijah is one of the great prophets of the Hebrew Bible, remembered as the fierce champion of the worship of the God of Israel in an age of apostasy. According to the scriptural account he arose in the northern kingdom during the reign of King Ahab, whose Phoenician queen Jezebel promoted the cult of the foreign god Baal and persecuted the prophets of the Lord.

His most famous deed was the contest on Mount Carmel, where he challenged the prophets of Baal to call down fire upon their sacrifice. When their god remained silent, Elijah's offering was consumed by fire from heaven, and the people acclaimed the Lord as God. Pursued afterward by the vengeful Jezebel, he fled into the wilderness to the holy mountain of Horeb, where, the story tells, he found God not in the wind or the earthquake or the fire, but in a still, small voice.

The narratives credit him with many wonders, from sustaining a widow through famine to raising her dead son, and with anointing his disciple Elisha to carry on his work. By tradition he did not die at all but was carried up into heaven in a whirlwind, in a chariot of fire.

So towering was his memory that later Jewish hope made him the herald who would announce the Messiah, and the Gospels present him appearing beside Moses at the Transfiguration of Jesus and identify John the Baptist as Elijah returned. His figure thus links the law and the prophets of old to the faith of later ages.
