Xerxes I

King of the Achaemenid Persian Empire (c. 519–465 BC)

Xerxes I was the king of Persia who launched the most famous invasion in ancient history, the great assault on Greece that ended in legendary disaster. The son of Darius the Great and of Atossa, daughter of Cyrus the Great, he inherited both the throne and his father's unfinished quarrel with the Greeks, first crushing revolts in Egypt and Babylon to secure his vast empire.

In 480 BC he gathered an army and fleet of unprecedented size and marched on Greece, bridging the Hellespont with boats lashed together and cutting a canal through a mountainous peninsula to spare his ships. At the narrow pass of Thermopylae the Spartan king Leonidas and his small band held the immense host at bay for days before being overwhelmed, a stand that became the very symbol of courage against the odds.

Xerxes pressed on, captured and burned an abandoned Athens, and seemed on the verge of total victory. But in the straits of Salamis the outnumbered Greek fleet lured his ships into close water and shattered them, and the king watched the ruin of his navy from a throne on the shore.

He withdrew to Asia, leaving an army that was destroyed the next year at Plataea, and the great enterprise collapsed. Xerxes turned to building at his capital of Persepolis until, in 465 BC, he was murdered in a palace conspiracy. To the Greeks he became the very image of overweening pride brought low.
