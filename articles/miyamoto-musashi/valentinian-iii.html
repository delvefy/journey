Valentinian III

Western Roman emperor (419–455)

Valentinian III was the Western Roman emperor under whom the empire slid toward final ruin. The son of the general Constantius and of Galla Placidia, and a grandson of Theodosius the Great, he was placed on the throne as a child in 425 with the support of his cousin, the Eastern emperor Theodosius II, while his mother ruled for years as regent.

Throughout his long reign the true defence of the West rested on his great general Aetius, who beat back the Huns and held Gaul together. But the empire bled away regardless: the Vandals of Geiseric seized Africa, and when Attila invaded in 451, the pretext was the scandalous appeal of Valentinian's own sister Honoria, who had sent her ring to the Hun.

Resentful and fearful of the general who overshadowed him, Valentinian committed in 454 an act of supreme folly: he murdered Aetius with his own hand. A bystander is said to have remarked that he had cut off his right hand with his left.

The vengeance was swift. The next year followers of the dead Aetius struck the emperor down, ending the line of Theodosius in the West. His death threw the West into anarchy and brought Geiseric's Vandals down upon Rome, and the empire he had failed to defend now entered its last agony.
