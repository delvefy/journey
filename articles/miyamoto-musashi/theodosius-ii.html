Theodosius II

Eastern Roman emperor (401–450)

Theodosius II was the Eastern Roman emperor whose long reign, though personally unremarkable, left monuments that outlasted the empire itself. Grandson of Theodosius the Great, he came to the throne as a child in 408, and for much of his life the real governing was done by able officials and above all by his formidable elder sister Pulcheria.

Two great works secured his fame. He completed the mighty land walls of Constantinople, the triple line of fortifications that would shield the city against besiegers for a thousand years. And he ordered the compilation of the Theodosian Code, the first comprehensive collection of imperial law in generations, a foundation on which later legislators would build.

His reign was dominated by fierce disputes over the nature of Christ. He summoned the Council of Ephesus in 431, where the patriarch Cyril of Alexandria triumphed over his rival Nestorius and won for the Virgin Mary the title of God-bearer, a turning point in Christian doctrine.

In the wider world he faced the rising terror of the Huns, and rather than fight Attila he bought him off with ever-larger payments of gold, trusting to his walls to keep the enemy from the capital. He died in 450 after a fall from his horse, and was succeeded by Marcian, who married Pulcheria.
