Sandro Botticelli

Florentine painter of the early Renaissance (c. 1445–1510)

Sandro Botticelli was a Florentine painter whose graceful, dreamlike figures came to embody the spirit of the early Renaissance at the court of the Medici. Born around 1445 and trained under Fra Filippo Lippi, he developed a delicate, flowing line and a wistful beauty unlike anything else of his time.

Under the patronage of the Medici and their circle he painted the mythological masterpieces that made him immortal, The Birth of Venus and Primavera, luminous allegories steeped in the Neoplatonic philosophy then fashionable in Lorenzo de' Medici's Florence. He was a contemporary of the young Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo in that crowded city of genius, and his fame stood high.

His reputation carried him to Rome, where in the early 1480s he was among the masters summoned to adorn the walls of the newly built Sistine Chapel with frescoes for the pope. Few painters of his generation were more sought after.

In his later years Botticelli fell under the influence of the fiery preacher Girolamo Savonarola, whose calls for repentance shook Florence. His art grew darker and more austerely religious, and he is said to have lost heart for the sensuous beauty of his youth. He died in 1510, half-forgotten, and his name lay in shadow for three centuries before a later age rediscovered him as one of the supreme poets of the brush.
