Mary, mother of Jesus

Mother of Jesus, venerated in Christianity and Islam (1st century)

Mary, the mother of Jesus, is the most venerated woman in Christianity and a figure honoured also in Islam. What is told of her comes chiefly from the Gospels and from later tradition, which present her as a young woman of Nazareth in Galilee, betrothed to a carpenter named Joseph.

According to the Gospel accounts, an angel announced to her that she would conceive and bear a son, Jesus, by the power of God, an event Christians call the Annunciation. Soon afterward she travelled to visit her kinswoman Elizabeth, who was herself expecting the child who would become John the Baptist, a meeting remembered as the Visitation and beloved by painters ever since.

The Gospels show her at the great moments of her son's life, from his birth, traditionally at Bethlehem, to the wedding at Cana, and finally standing in grief at the foot of the cross, where the dying Jesus is said to have entrusted her to the care of his disciple John.

Around this slender record grew an immense devotion. Christians came to honour her as the Virgin, the Mother of God, and the gentle intercessor for sinners, and her image as the Madonna became the most frequently painted subject in all of Western art. In the Quran she is revered as the pure mother of the prophet Jesus, the only woman named in its pages. Few human beings have been so universally loved or depicted.
