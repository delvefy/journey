Galla Placidia

Roman empress and regent of the West (c. 388–450)

Galla Placidia was a Roman empress whose extraordinary life carried her from captivity among the barbarians to the regency of the Western Empire. The daughter of Theodosius the Great and half-sister of the emperor Honorius, she was in Rome when Alaric's Goths sacked the city in 410, and they carried her off as a hostage.

Among the Goths her fortunes took a remarkable turn: she married Alaric's successor Athaulf, becoming for a time a queen of the very people who had taken her, in a union that seemed to promise reconciliation between Roman and Goth. When her husband was murdered, she was returned to the Romans and married the general Constantius, who was raised briefly to the purple; their son was the future emperor Valentinian III.

On the death of Honorius and the suppression of a usurper, Galla Placidia secured the throne for her young son and governed the West as regent for more than a decade, navigating the rivalries of ambitious generals, foremost among them Aetius.

A devout Christian and a great patron of building, she left behind in Ravenna the small jewel-like mausoleum that bears her name, glowing with some of the finest mosaics of the age. She died in 450, on the eve of the storm that Attila would soon bring upon the West.
