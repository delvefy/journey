Zenobia

Queen of the Palmyrene Empire (c. 240–after 274)

Zenobia was the warrior-queen of Palmyra, the desert caravan city of Syria, who for a few astonishing years carved out an empire of her own at the expense of Rome. The wife of Odaenathus, the Palmyrene lord who had held the eastern frontier against Persia, she took power after his murder around 267, ruling in the name of her young son.

Ambitious, capable, and famously beautiful, Zenobia did not long content herself with governing a client state. Her armies pushed outward until they had seized Egypt and much of Asia Minor, and she declared an independent empire stretching across the Roman East, minting coins in her son's name and then with the imperial title.

She cultivated a brilliant and learned court, gathering scholars and philosophers about her, and she proudly claimed descent from the Ptolemies, styling herself a successor to the great Cleopatra whose Egypt she now ruled. For a moment she seemed to have torn the richest provinces from the empire.

It could not last. The emperor Aurelian marched east, defeated her forces, and captured Palmyra in 272, taking Zenobia herself prisoner. By the best-known tradition she was carried to Rome to walk in golden chains through his triumph, and then permitted to live out her days quietly in Italy. Whatever her true end, she has never been forgotten, remembered ever after among the boldest queens of the ancient world.
