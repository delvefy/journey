Sen no Rikyu

Japanese master of the tea ceremony (1522–1591)

Sen no Rikyu was the most revered master of the Japanese tea ceremony, the man who raised a social pastime into a profound spiritual and artistic discipline. Born in 1522 into a merchant family of the prosperous port of Sakai, he studied tea under Takeno Joo and devoted his life to perfecting the way of tea, or chanoyu.

Rikyu brought to fruition the aesthetic known as wabi, a love of austerity, rusticity, and the quiet beauty of imperfect and humble things. He preferred tiny thatched tea rooms to gilded halls, plain native utensils to costly imports, and he had a low crawling entrance built so that every guest, however exalted, must bow to enter as an equal. Working with humble potters, he helped create the simple raku tea bowls prized ever since.

His mastery made him indispensable to the rulers of Japan. He served as tea master and trusted adviser first to Oda Nobunaga and then to Toyotomi Hideyoshi, standing at the very center of power and counting great lords such as Hosokawa Tadaoki among his disciples.

That closeness proved fatal. For reasons still debated, Hideyoshi's favor turned to anger, and in 1591 the dictator ordered the old master to commit ritual suicide. Rikyu obeyed, reportedly performing one last tea gathering before taking his life. His descendants founded the schools of tea that still govern the art today, and his vision of beauty in simplicity remains one of the deepest currents in Japanese culture.
