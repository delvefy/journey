Lorenzo de' Medici

Ruler of Florence, patron of the Renaissance (1449–1492)

Lorenzo de' Medici, called the Magnificent, was the uncrowned ruler of Florence at the height of the Italian Renaissance and the most celebrated patron of art and learning of his age. Born in 1449 into the great banking family founded by his grandfather Cosimo, he took the reins of the city as a young man and held them, without any formal title, for more than two decades.

His rule was nearly cut short in 1478, when rivals backed by the pope struck during Mass in the cathedral, murdering his brother Giuliano at the altar; Lorenzo, wounded, fought his way clear and crushed the conspirators. Thereafter he governed with consummate diplomatic skill, holding the balance among the quarrelsome Italian states so deftly that he was called the needle of the Italian compass.

It is as a patron, above all, that he is remembered. He supported the painter Sandro Botticelli, took the boy Michelangelo into his own household to study sculpture, and gathered around him a brilliant company of humanists and poets who revived the study of Plato and the classical past. He was himself a gifted poet in the Tuscan tongue.

For all his magnificence, the family bank declined under his hand, and after his death in 1492 Florence soon fell under the sway of the preacher Savonarola. Yet his court had nurtured a flowering of genius rarely equalled, and later writers, among them Machiavelli, would look back on his rule as a golden age.
