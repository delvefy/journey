Muhammad

Prophet and founder of Islam (c. 570–632)

Muhammad was the prophet and statesman who founded Islam, today one of the world's great religions. Born around 570 in the trading city of Mecca, he was orphaned in childhood and raised by relatives, growing up to work as a merchant with such a reputation for honesty that his people called him the trustworthy one. He married Khadija, a widow whose support sustained him in the years to come.

According to Islamic belief, when he was about forty, while meditating in a cave outside Mecca, he began to receive revelations from God through the angel Gabriel. These messages, calling men to submit to the one God and to live justly before the coming Judgment, would be gathered into the Quran. He preached this monotheism in a city devoted to many idols, and he and his followers met growing hostility.

In 622 he led his community in an emigration to the city of Medina, an event so pivotal that it marks the beginning of the Muslim calendar. There he became the leader of a new society bound by faith, guiding it through years of conflict with Mecca until, in 630, he entered his birthplace in triumph and cleansed its ancient shrine, the Kaaba, of its idols.

Presenting his message as the restoration of the pure monotheism of Abraham, Muhammad united much of Arabia under the new faith before his death in 632. His closest companions, among them Ali, would carry that faith outward to build one of history's great civilizations.
