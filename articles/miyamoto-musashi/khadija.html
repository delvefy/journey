Khadija

First wife of Muhammad, first Muslim (c. 555–619)

Khadija bint Khuwaylid was the first wife of the Prophet Muhammad and, by Islamic tradition, the very first person to believe in his message. A wealthy and respected merchant widow of Mecca, she ran a prosperous caravan trade, and it was as her trusted agent that the young Muhammad first entered her service. Impressed by his honesty and character, she proposed marriage, though she was the elder of the two.

Their union was close and devoted, and so long as she lived Muhammad took no other wife. She bore his children, among them his daughter Fatimah, through whom alone his line would continue.

Her greatest role came at the very birth of Islam. When Muhammad returned from the mountain trembling after his first encounter with the angel, it was Khadija who wrapped him, calmed him, and assured him that God would not forsake him, becoming the first to accept his prophethood. Her wealth and her unwavering loyalty shielded the little community of believers through the bitter years of persecution in Mecca.

She died in 619, in a year of such loss that Muslims remember it as the Year of Sorrow, before the faithful made their famous emigration. Honoured ever after as a Mother of the Believers and one of the greatest women of Islam, she was the ancestress of the Prophet's family, grandmother of the beloved Hasan and Husayn.
