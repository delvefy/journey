Themistocles

Athenian statesman and naval strategist (c. 524–459 BC)

Themistocles was the far-sighted Athenian statesman whose vision and cunning saved Greece from Persian conquest. Rising in the young democracy, he grasped earlier than his rivals that Athens's future and safety lay upon the sea. When a rich vein of silver was struck in the state mines, he persuaded his fellow citizens to spend the windfall not on themselves but on building a great fleet of warships.

That fleet proved the salvation of Greece. When Xerxes invaded in 480 BC, Themistocles maneuvered the combined Greek navy into the narrow strait of Salamis, where the cramped waters robbed the Persians of their advantage in numbers, and there the invader's fleet was shattered, turning the tide of the whole war.

He further secured Athens by rebuilding its walls and fortifying its harbour, laying the foundations of the maritime empire to come. Yet his brilliance bred envy and distrust. His great rival Aristides and others turned against him, and in time he was ostracized and then condemned in absence as a traitor.

In an extraordinary turn, the man who had broken Persia's fleet fled for refuge to Persia itself, where the king received him with honour and granted him cities for his support. There Themistocles lived out his days, dying around 459 BC far from the city he had saved, his memory clouded by exile but his achievement at Salamis secure.
