Michelangelo

Italian sculptor, painter, and architect (1475–1564)

Michelangelo Buonarroti was the towering genius of the Italian High Renaissance, a sculptor, painter, architect, and poet whose works set a standard against which Western art has measured itself ever since. Born in 1475 near Florence, he trained in the workshop of the painter Ghirlandaio and studied antique sculpture in the garden of the Medici, absorbing both classical form and the humanist learning of his city.

While still young he carved two works that announced an extraordinary talent: the tender Pieta in Rome and the colossal David in Florence, a marble giant that became the very emblem of the Renaissance. Though he thought of himself above all as a sculptor, his patrons demanded more.

Pope Julius II summoned him to Rome and set him to paint the vast ceiling of the Sistine Chapel, a labour of four years that produced the unforgettable scenes of Creation, and to design a grandiose papal tomb, for which he carved his brooding Moses. Decades later, under Pope Paul III, he covered the chapel's altar wall with the terrifying Last Judgment and took charge of rebuilding Saint Peter's, crowning it with the dome that still defines Rome's skyline.

Fiercely independent and famously difficult, he worked in rivalry with the other masters of his age, among them the older Leonardo da Vinci, with whom he was once set to compete in painting battle scenes in Florence. He wrote searching poetry and laboured almost to the end, dying in 1564 at the age of eighty-eight, revered in his own lifetime as "the divine one."
