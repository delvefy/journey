Augustus

First Roman emperor (63 BC–AD 14)

Augustus was the founder of the Roman Empire and its first emperor, the man who ended a century of civil war and gave Rome the long peace and the imperial form it would keep for centuries. Born Gaius Octavius in 63 BC, he was the great-nephew of Julius Caesar, who adopted him as son and heir; when Caesar was murdered, the unproven youth of nineteen stepped forward to claim his name and his legions.

Allying with Mark Antony and Lepidus, he hunted down Caesar's assassins and divided the Roman world among the three. The partnership could not hold. Antony, bound to Cleopatra in the East, became his rival, and at the sea battle of Actium in 31 BC Octavian destroyed their fleets; the annexation of Egypt left him master of the whole Roman world.

In 27 BC the Senate granted him the honoured name Augustus. With consummate political art he disguised his supremacy beneath the forms of the old Republic, calling himself merely the first citizen while holding the reality of power. Under him began the Pax Romana, the long Roman peace.

He reorganized the army and the provinces, reformed Rome's finances and administration, and adorned the city so lavishly that he boasted he had found it brick and left it marble. He fostered a brilliant age of literature, patronizing poets such as Virgil. Reigning more than forty years, he died in AD 14, was declared a god, and left his name as a title for the emperors who followed.
