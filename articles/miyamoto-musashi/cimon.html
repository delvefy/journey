Cimon

Athenian statesman and general (c. 510–450 BC)

Cimon was a leading Athenian statesman and soldier of the generation between the Persian Wars and the age of Pericles, the son of Miltiades who had won Marathon. After Athens helped repel the Persian invasion, Cimon emerged as the city's foremost general, carrying the war into the eastern Aegean at the head of the new league of Greek states.

His masterpiece was the double victory at the river Eurymedon, where in a single day he is said to have destroyed both the Persian army and its fleet, driving Persian power from the Aegean coast and confirming Athenian supremacy at sea. The plunder of his campaigns he spent lavishly on his city, and his open-handed generosity made him beloved of the people.

In politics he was a conservative aristocrat who admired Sparta and urged friendship between the two great Greek powers. But when he led Athenian troops to aid the Spartans in a crisis and was coldly sent home, his policy was discredited.

The democratic reformers, among them the young Pericles, seized the moment, and Cimon, the rival of Themistocles before him and of Pericles after, was ostracized as Athens turned toward a more radical democracy. Recalled in time, he died around 450 BC while campaigning once more against the Persians in Cyprus, the last great champion of the older Athens.
