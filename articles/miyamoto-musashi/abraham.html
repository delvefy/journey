Abraham

Patriarch of the Abrahamic faiths (c. 2nd millennium BC, by tradition)

Abraham is honoured as the first of the patriarchs and the common father of three great religions, Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. The Book of Genesis tells how God called him to leave his native city in Mesopotamia and journey to the land of Canaan, promising that his offspring would become a great nation and that through him all the families of the earth would find blessing.

Though he and his wife Sarah had grown old without children, the promise was kept. Sarah's Egyptian servant Hagar bore him a son, Ishmael, and in time the aged Sarah herself bore Isaac. The most searching episode of his story is the binding of Isaac, in which God commanded Abraham to offer up the son of the promise, and Abraham, in unflinching obedience, prepared to do so until a divine voice stayed his hand.

For this readiness to trust God against all reason he became, for the traditions that look back to him, the very model of faith. The Jews revered him as the ancestor of Israel through Isaac and Jacob, and Christians, following Paul, called him the father of all who believe.

In Islam he is Ibrahim, the upright monotheist who turned from idols to the one God, and who, with his son Ishmael, is said to have raised the Kaaba at Mecca, the shrine toward which Muslims pray. It was to the pure faith of Abraham that the Prophet Muhammad would summon his people many centuries later.
