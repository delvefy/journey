Girolamo Savonarola

Dominican friar and reformer of Florence (1452–1498)

Girolamo Savonarola was the Dominican friar whose fiery preaching briefly made him the master of Florence and whose violent end made him a martyr to some and a fanatic to others. Born in Ferrara in 1452, he entered the order as a young man and came to the convent of San Marco in Florence, where his apocalyptic sermons denouncing vice, tyranny, and the corruption of the Church drew vast and fervent crowds.

When the Medici were driven out in 1494 amid a French invasion, Savonarola stepped into the void as the spiritual guide of a new Florentine republic, preaching that the city might become a new Jerusalem if it repented. His followers gathered books, paintings, mirrors, and finery into great heaps and set them ablaze in the famous Bonfire of the Vanities.

His attacks on the worldliness of Rome brought him into open conflict with Pope Alexander VI, who excommunicated him and demanded his silence. As the mood of fickle Florence turned, his enemies seized their chance. In 1498 the friar was arrested, tortured, condemned, and then hanged and burned in the city's great square.

Savonarola left a divided memory. To the Church he was a dangerous rebel; yet a generation later the reformer Martin Luther would look back on him as a godly man martyred by a corrupt papacy, reprinting his prison meditations and claiming him as a forerunner of the Reformation.
