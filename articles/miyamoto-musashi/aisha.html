Aisha

Wife of Muhammad, Mother of the Believers (c. 614–678)

Aisha bint Abi Bakr was a wife of the Prophet Muhammad and one of the most influential women in the history of Islam. The daughter of Muhammad's closest friend, Abu Bakr, she became his wife in Medina and, after the death of Khadija, the most beloved of his household. Muhammad died in her arms and was buried in her chamber, beside which her father and Umar would also be laid.

Quick-witted, learned, and forceful, Aisha emerged after the Prophet's death as a major religious authority in her own right. She transmitted many hundreds of accounts of his words and deeds, becoming one of the foremost narrators of hadith and a vital source for Islamic law, ritual, and the memory of the Prophet's life. Scholars and jurists sought her judgment for decades.

She also played a dramatic part in the political storms of the age. After the murder of the caliph Uthman, she opposed the new caliph Ali and rode out at the head of an army; the clash that followed, the Battle of the Camel, was the first time Muslims met Muslims in open war. Defeated, she withdrew from public strife but kept her great prestige.

Honoured by Sunnis as a Mother of the Believers, Aisha left a vast legacy of remembered tradition. Her countless reports would form a major part of the great canonical collections of hadith, above all the renowned compilation of Muhammad al-Bukhari.
