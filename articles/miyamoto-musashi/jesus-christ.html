Jesus Christ

Jewish preacher, central figure of Christianity (c. 4 BC–c. AD 30)

Jesus of Nazareth was a Jewish preacher and teacher of first-century Roman Judaea who became the central figure of Christianity, worshipped by his followers as the Son of God and the promised Messiah. Almost everything recorded of his life comes from the Gospels written by the early Christian community; he himself left no writings.

According to those accounts he grew up in Galilee and, after being baptized by John the Baptist, began a short public ministry of preaching and healing. He proclaimed the coming of the Kingdom of God, called people to repentance and love of neighbour, and taught in vivid parables, gathering around him twelve close disciples and many other followers drawn by his words and the wonders attributed to him.

His message and growing fame brought him into conflict with religious authorities and with the Roman order. Going up to Jerusalem, he was arrested, tried, and condemned, and was crucified under the Roman governor Pontius Pilate. To his followers this was not the end: they proclaimed that he had risen from the dead, and that his death and resurrection brought salvation to humankind.

From that conviction his disciples, above all Peter and Paul, carried his teaching across the Roman world, and within a few centuries it had become the dominant faith of the West and, in time, the largest religion on earth. Honoured also as a prophet in Islam, Jesus has shaped the beliefs, art, morals, and calendars of much of humanity. By Christian belief he was born of his mother, Mary, in a manner held to be miraculous.
