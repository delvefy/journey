Moses

Hebrew lawgiver and prophet (c. 13th century BC, by tradition)

Moses is the towering lawgiver and prophet of the Hebrew Bible, the man through whom, by tradition, the people of Israel were freed from slavery and given their Law. According to the scriptural account he was born to Hebrew slaves in Egypt at a time when the Pharaoh had ordered their infant sons killed; hidden and then set adrift on the Nile, he was found and raised within Pharaoh's own household.

Grown to manhood, he fled into the desert after killing an Egyptian overseer, and there, at a bush that burned without being consumed, he encountered God, who named himself the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and commanded Moses to deliver his people. Returning to Egypt, Moses confronted Pharaoh, and through a series of devastating plagues and the parting of the sea he led the Israelites out of bondage in the Exodus.

In the wilderness of Sinai he received the Ten Commandments and the Law, sealing the covenant between God and Israel and shaping its worship, justice, and identity for all time. For forty years he led the often rebellious people toward the land they had been promised.

He brought them at last to its borders but, by the account, was not permitted to enter, dying on a mountaintop within sight of it and passing his leadership to Joshua. Honoured as the founder of their faith by Jews, revered by Christians, and named again and again in the Quran, Moses stands beside Elijah at the Transfiguration as the very embodiment of the Law.
