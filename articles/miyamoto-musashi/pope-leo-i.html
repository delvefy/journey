Pope Leo I

Pope and Doctor of the Church, "Leo the Great" (c. 400–461)

Pope Leo I, remembered as Leo the Great, was one of the most commanding figures ever to hold the office of bishop of Rome, a pope who asserted the authority of his see and steadied the Church amid the wreck of the Western Empire. Reigning from 440, he taught with unmatched force that the pope, as successor of Saint Peter, held a primacy over the whole Church.

His most lasting contribution was doctrinal. In a famous letter known as the Tome of Leo he set out the orthodox understanding of Christ as one person in two natures, divine and human, and when it was read at the great Council of Chalcedon in 451 the assembled bishops are said to have cried that Peter had spoken through Leo.

He is best remembered, however, for two dramatic encounters with the barbarians who threatened Rome. In 452 he went out to meet Attila the Hun as he advanced on the city, and Attila turned back; tradition gave the credit to the pope's holy presence. Three years later, when the Vandals under Geiseric seized Rome, Leo could not prevent the plunder but won a pledge that the people would be spared slaughter and the city from burning.

Through such acts, and through his writings and administration, Leo raised the prestige of the papacy to new heights as the secular power of Rome decayed. He died in 461, the first pope to be called Great.
