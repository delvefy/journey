al-Ma'mun

Abbasid caliph, patron of learning (786–833)

Al-Ma'mun was the seventh Abbasid caliph and one of the great patrons of science and philosophy in the history of Islam. The son of the famous Harun al-Rashid, he came to power in 813 only after a ruinous civil war against his half-brother al-Amin, which saw Baghdad itself besieged. Once secure, he turned the resources of the caliphate toward the life of the mind.

Under his patronage the institution remembered as the House of Wisdom in Baghdad flourished as never before. He gathered scholars, paid handsomely for manuscripts, and sponsored the translation into Arabic of the great works of Greek, Persian, and Indian learning, the philosophy of Aristotle, the geometry of Euclid, the astronomy of Ptolemy, and the medicine of Galen. Mathematicians and astronomers in his service advanced their sciences and even measured the size of the earth.

A man of restless intellect, al-Ma'mun embraced a rationalist school of theology and tried to impose its doctrine that the Quran was created upon the scholars of the realm, launching the inquisition that broke against the stubborn faith of men like Ahmad ibn Hanbal.

He died in 833 while campaigning on the Byzantine frontier. Though his religious policy failed and was later reversed, the movement of translation and inquiry he had fostered helped preserve and transform the heritage of antiquity, feeding a golden age of learning whose fruits would one day pass to medieval Europe.
