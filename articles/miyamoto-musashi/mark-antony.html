Mark Antony

Roman general and triumvir (83–30 BC)

Mark Antony was a Roman general and statesman whose career and ruin were bound up with the fall of the Republic and the rise of the empire. A bold and popular soldier, he became one of Julius Caesar's most trusted lieutenants, and it was Antony who, at Caesar's funeral, turned the crowd against the assassins with his eloquence.

After Caesar's death he joined the young Octavian and Lepidus in the Second Triumvirate, and together they hunted down their enemies in bloody proscriptions, the orator Cicero among the victims, and crushed Brutus and Cassius at Philippi. Antony then took the rich eastern half of the Roman world as his own.

There he met Cleopatra, queen of Egypt, and their alliance grew into one of history's most famous love affairs. He divided his time between her court at Alexandria and his Roman duties, and his lavish grants of territory to Cleopatra and her children gave Octavian the pretext to turn Rome against him.

The rivalry ended at the sea battle of Actium in 31 BC, where Antony's fleet was broken and he and Cleopatra fled to Egypt. The following year, as Octavian's forces closed in and a false report reached him that the queen was dead, Antony took his own life; Cleopatra soon followed. Remembered through Plutarch and Shakespeare, he endures as the image of the gallant soldier undone by love and ambition.
