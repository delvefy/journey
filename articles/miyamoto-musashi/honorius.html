Honorius

Western Roman emperor (384–423)

Honorius was the Western Roman emperor during the catastrophe that saw the eternal city fall to a barbarian army, and he is remembered as one of the feeblest rulers ever to hold the office. The younger son of Theodosius the Great, he was made emperor of the West as a boy when his father died in 395, and for years the real governing was done by his formidable guardian, the general Stilicho.

Honorius repaid that service with suspicion. In 408, swayed by court intrigue, he ordered Stilicho's execution, stripping the empire of its ablest defender at the worst possible moment. With the way now open, the Goths of Alaric closed in, and in 410 they entered and sacked Rome.

Through these disasters the emperor sheltered behind the marshes of Ravenna, which he had made his capital, seemingly more concerned with his own safety than with the empire crumbling around him. A famous, if doubtful, story tells that on hearing that Rome had perished he was relieved to learn it meant the city, not his pet hen of that name.

His long reign saw Britain abandoned and barbarian peoples pour across the Rhine into Gaul and Spain. When he died in 423, the Western Empire was a shrunken and shaken thing, and the fortunes of his dynasty would pass largely through his resourceful half-sister, Galla Placidia.
