Theodosius I

Roman emperor, last to rule a united empire (347–395)

Theodosius the Great was the last emperor to rule over both halves of the Roman world united under one hand, and the ruler who made Christianity the official religion of the empire. A capable Spanish-born general, he was raised to the throne in 379 to confront the catastrophe of the Gothic invasions, after a Roman army and emperor had been destroyed at Adrianople.

Unable to drive the Goths out, he made terms, settling them within the empire as armed allies bound to serve in its wars, a fateful expedient that planted a foreign nation inside Roman borders. In matters of faith he was decisive: by the Edict of Thessalonica he established Nicene Christianity as the state religion, summoned a great council at Constantinople, and moved to suppress both heresy and the old pagan cults.

His authority did not place him above the Church. When his soldiers slaughtered the people of Thessalonica in reprisal for a riot, the bishop Ambrose barred him from communion and compelled him to do public penance, a humbling that became legendary.

Theodosius crushed a series of usurpers to keep the empire whole, but at his death in 395 he divided it between his two young sons, East and West, a partition that proved permanent. The Gothic warriors he had settled, led soon by Alaric, would within a generation march on Rome itself.
