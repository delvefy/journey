Husayn ibn Ali

Grandson of Muhammad, martyr of Karbala (626–680)

Husayn ibn Ali was a grandson of the Prophet Muhammad and the supreme martyr of Islam, whose death at Karbala became the defining tragedy of the Shia faith. He was the younger son of Ali and of Fatimah, the Prophet's daughter, and was cherished in childhood by Muhammad himself. After the death of his elder brother Hasan, he became the head of the Prophet's household and the focus of those who believed the leadership of Islam belonged to Ali's line.

When Mu'awiya died in 680 and his son Yazid succeeded him as Umayyad caliph, Husayn refused to recognize a ruler he regarded as unjust and impious. Summoned by supporters in the Iraqi city of Kufa, he set out across the desert with his family and a small company of followers.

They were intercepted by the caliph's far larger army and surrounded on the plain of Karbala, cut off from the river under a burning sun. On the tenth day of the month of Muharram, the day remembered as Ashura, Husayn and his companions were overwhelmed and slain, and his women and children carried off as captives.

The killing of the Prophet's grandson horrified the Muslim world and became, for Shia Islam, the emblem of righteous suffering against tyranny. Husayn is mourned each year in passionate commemorations, his shrine at Karbala is among the holiest of pilgrimage sites, and his memory endures as a call to stand for justice whatever the cost.
