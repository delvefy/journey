Fatimah

Daughter of Muhammad, mother of the Imams (c. 605–632)

Fatimah was the daughter of the Prophet Muhammad and his first wife Khadija, and she is among the most beloved and venerated women in all of Islam. Honoured with the title al-Zahra, the Radiant, she was the child closest to the Prophet's heart, and the traditions tell of the special tenderness between father and daughter through the years of struggle in Mecca and Medina.

She married Ali ibn Abi Talib, the Prophet's cousin, and their household became, for Muslims, the very model of a holy family. From their union came the Prophet's only surviving line of descent, through their sons Hasan and Husayn and their daughters, and this family, the People of the House, stands at the spiritual centre of Shia Islam.

After Muhammad's death in 632, Fatimah was caught up in the painful disputes over his succession and his estate, pressing a claim against the new caliph that tradition long remembered. Worn by grief at the loss of her father, she herself died only a few months later, still a young woman.

Revered by Sunnis and Shias alike, she is counted among the greatest women who ever lived, the mother of the Imams and the ancestress from whom the Fatimid caliphs would one day take their name. Her purity and devotion made her, for countless generations, an enduring ideal of womanhood in the Muslim world.
