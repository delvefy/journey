Alaric

King of the Visigoths, sacker of Rome (c. 370–410)

Alaric was the Visigothic king who in the year 410 captured and sacked the city of Rome, an event that shook the ancient world to its foundations. He began his career in the service of Rome itself, leading Gothic warriors as allies in the army of the emperor Theodosius, and fighting in his campaigns.

After Theodosius died and the empire was divided between his feeble sons, Alaric found his people slighted and unpaid. He rose in revolt, ravaging the Balkans and Greece and then leading his nation into Italy in search of land and tribute. For years the able Roman general Stilicho held him in check, defeating and outmaneuvering him.

But when the suspicious western emperor Honorius had Stilicho executed in 408, the last barrier fell. Alaric marched on Rome and, after negotiations with the emperor hiding behind the marshes of Ravenna broke down, his army entered the city in August 410 and plundered it for three days.

The walls that had not been breached by a foreign foe for eight centuries had fallen, and the news echoed across the empire, moving Augustine to write his great defence of the faith. Alaric did not long enjoy his triumph; he died within months, while planning to carry his people to Africa, and was buried, legend says, beneath the bed of a river.
