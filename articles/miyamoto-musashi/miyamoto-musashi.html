Miyamoto Musashi

Japanese swordsman, strategist, and artist (c. 1584–1645)

Miyamoto Musashi, born around 1584 in the Harima region of Japan, was a swordsman, strategist, painter, and author who became the most celebrated warrior in his country's history. The son of a martial artist, he recorded that he fought his first lethal duel at the age of thirteen, defeating a wandering adept named Arima Kihei, and from then on lived the wandering life of a masterless samurai, or ronin.

Over the following decades he claimed to have fought more than sixty duels without a single defeat. In a series of encounters around 1604 he humbled the renowned Yoshioka school of Kyoto, and through ceaseless practice he developed the distinctive two-sword fighting method he called Niten Ichi-ryu, wielding the long and short blades together. He sought not merely victory in single combats but a deeper mastery he termed the Way of strategy.

His most famous fight came in 1612 on the islet later named Ganryujima, where he defeated and killed the formidable Sasaki Kojiro, reputedly striking him down with a wooden sword carved from an oar. By tradition Musashi also took part in the great battles of his age, including the campaigns around Sekigahara and the sieges of Osaka.

In his last years the lord Hosokawa Tadatoshi gave him patronage at Kumamoto, where Musashi withdrew to a cave called Reigando and composed The Book of Five Rings, a profound treatise on swordsmanship and strategy still read worldwide, along with the austere precepts of the Dokkodo. He was also a gifted ink painter and calligrapher. He died in 1645, leaving a legend that has only grown.
