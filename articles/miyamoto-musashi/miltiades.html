Miltiades

Athenian general, victor of Marathon (c. 550–489 BC)

Miltiades was the Athenian general whose victory at the Battle of Marathon turned back the first Persian invasion of Greece and entered the realm of legend. Born into a powerful Athenian family, he spent years ruling a domain in the Thracian Chersonese and serving, then defying, the Persian king Darius, before returning to Athens with an intimate knowledge of how the Persians fought.

When Darius sent a punitive expedition across the Aegean in 490 BC, it landed on the plain of Marathon, within striking distance of Athens. Miltiades, one of the elected generals, urged an immediate attack rather than a wait behind the walls. Drawing up the heavy Greek infantry with strengthened wings, he loosed them at a run across the plain; the Persian centre was held while the wings enveloped and routed the enemy.

The triumph was overwhelming and, for the Greeks, almost miraculous: a single city had defeated the army of the great king. The feat would echo down the ages, and the long run carrying news of the victory gave its name to the modern race.

Glory proved fleeting. The next year Miltiades led an expedition against the island of Paros that failed; wounded in the attempt and fined by the Athenians for the disappointment, he died soon after of his injury. His son Cimon would inherit his name and carry Athenian arms to new victories against Persia.
