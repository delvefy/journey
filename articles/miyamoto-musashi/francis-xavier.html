Francis Xavier

Spanish Jesuit missionary, apostle of the Indies and Japan (1506–1552)

Francis Xavier was one of the greatest Christian missionaries in history, the man who carried Catholicism across Asia and first planted it in Japan. Born in 1506 to a noble family in the Kingdom of Navarre, he went as a young man to study at the University of Paris, where a fellow student named Ignatius of Loyola gradually won him to a life of religious devotion.

In 1534 Xavier was among the small band who, with Ignatius, took vows that would grow into the Society of Jesus. When the king of Portugal asked for missionaries to serve his eastern empire, Xavier was sent. Reaching Goa in 1542, he threw himself into preaching among the peoples of the Indian coast and the islands of the East Indies, baptizing many thousands.

His boldest venture came in 1549, when, guided by a Japanese convert he had met in Asia, he landed at Kagoshima and became the first Christian missionary to Japan. There he laboured to win converts and to understand a sophisticated and unfamiliar civilization, founding a mission whose later followers would include the Christians of Shimabara.

Restless for new fields, Xavier set his sights on the vast Ming empire of China. He died in 1552 on a small island off the Chinese coast, within sight of the land he could not enter. Canonized as a saint, remembered as the apostle of the Indies and of Japan, he became the model of the missionary vocation for centuries to come.
