Geiseric

King of the Vandals, conqueror of Roman Africa (c. 389–477)

Geiseric was the king of the Vandals who tore the richest provinces from the Western Roman Empire and made himself a master of the Mediterranean. In 429 he led his whole people, perhaps eighty thousand strong, across the strait from Spain into Africa, and in a relentless campaign overran the Roman provinces there. As his armies advanced they besieged the city of Hippo, within whose walls the aged bishop Augustine lay dying.

By 439 Geiseric had taken Carthage, the second city of the West and the source of Rome's grain, and made it the capital of an independent Vandal kingdom. He built a fleet and turned pirate-king of the western seas, raiding coasts at will and beating off the empire's attempts to dislodge him.

In 455 he descended upon Rome itself. The murder of the emperor Valentinian III had thrown the West into chaos, and Geiseric sailed up the Tiber and plundered the city for two weeks; Pope Leo I could not prevent the sack but won a promise that the people would not be massacred. He carried off the imperial women as captives and bound the dynasties by marrying his son to Valentinian's daughter.

A shrewd and ruthless ruler and a zealous Arian who persecuted the Catholic clergy, Geiseric reigned nearly fifty years and died in his bed in 477. The kingdom he founded endured until the armies of Justinian destroyed it half a century later.
