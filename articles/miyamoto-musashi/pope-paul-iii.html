Pope Paul III

Roman pope, patron and reformer (1468–1549)

Pope Paul III, born Alessandro Farnese in 1468, was the pontiff who stood at the hinge between the worldly splendour of the Renaissance papacy and the stern renewal of the Catholic Reformation. He rose to prominence through family favour in an age of nepotism and was made a cardinal while still young, but as pope from 1534 he proved an able and far-sighted ruler of the Church.

He was a magnificent patron of art. It was Paul III who commissioned Michelangelo to paint the towering Last Judgment on the altar wall of the Sistine Chapel and who appointed the aging master chief architect of the new Saint Peter's Basilica. He also raised the great Farnese Palace and sat for memorable portraits by Titian.

Yet he was equally a reformer. Recognizing the gravity of the Protestant challenge, he approved new religious orders, above all the Society of Jesus in 1540, established the Roman Inquisition, and in 1545 convened the Council of Trent, the assembly that would define Catholic doctrine and discipline for centuries.

His reign was crowded with the politics of princes. He manoeuvred between the emperor and the king of France, sought to organize resistance to the advancing Ottomans, and in 1538 pronounced the excommunication of Henry VIII of England. He did not abandon the habits of his class, carving out a duchy for his own son, but he died in 1549 having set the Roman Church firmly on the path of reform.
