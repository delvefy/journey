Stilicho

Roman general and regent of the West (c. 359–408)

Stilicho was the great soldier who, in the last age of the Western Roman Empire, held its enemies at bay almost single-handed. The son of a Vandal cavalry officer and a Roman mother, he rose to the highest command under the emperor Theodosius, who trusted him so far as to give him his niece Serena in marriage and, on his deathbed, to entrust to him the guardianship of his young son Honorius.

For more than a decade Stilicho was the true master of the West. He beat back wave after wave of invaders, defeating the Goths of Alaric in hard-fought battles and annihilating another vast barbarian host that poured into Italy. He bound himself ever closer to the throne, marrying his daughters to the emperor Honorius.

But his very power, and his foreign blood, bred suspicion at court. His policy of negotiating with Alaric rather than destroying him was cast as treachery, and his rivals poisoned the weak emperor's mind against him.

In 408 Honorius ordered his arrest and execution, and Stilicho, refusing to let his loyal troops resist, went calmly to his death. With him fell the empire's last competent defender; in the slaughter of barbarian families that followed, many of his soldiers went over to Alaric, and within two years Rome was sacked.
