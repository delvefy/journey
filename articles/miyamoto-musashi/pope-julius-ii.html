Pope Julius II

Roman pope and warrior, patron of the arts (1443–1513)

Pope Julius II, born Giuliano della Rovere in 1443, was the formidable "warrior pope" whose decade on the throne of Saint Peter combined ruthless statecraft, military campaigning, and the most magnificent patronage of art in the history of the papacy. Raised to the cardinalate by his uncle Pope Sixtus IV, he spent years as the bitter enemy of the Borgia family, even fleeing into exile during their ascendancy.

Elected pope in 1503, he set out to restore the authority and territory of the Papal States. He stripped the fallen Cesare Borgia of his power, recovered lost cities, and then, donning armour himself, led armies against Venice and afterward against the French, vowing to drive the foreign "barbarians" out of Italy.

Yet it is as a patron that Julius is best remembered. He compelled a reluctant Michelangelo to paint the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel and to begin a grandiose tomb crowned by the statue of Moses. He gave the young Raphael the papal apartments to adorn, and he charged Bramante with tearing down the ancient basilica of Saint Peter to raise the vast new church that still stands.

Imperious, impatient, and grand in all his ambitions, Julius poured the resources of the Church into war and art alike. He died in 1513, having reshaped both the political map of Italy and the artistic heritage of Rome, leaving a legacy of stone and paint unmatched by any pope before or since.
