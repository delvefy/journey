Aurelian

Roman emperor, restorer of the empire (c. 214–275)

Aurelian was the soldier-emperor who, in a reign of only five years, pulled the shattered Roman world back together and earned the proud title Restorer of the World. Risen from humble Balkan origins through the army, he took the throne in 270 to find the empire broken into three pieces, with a rebel state in Gaul to the west and, in the east, a powerful breakaway realm centred on the desert city of Palmyra.

In a whirlwind of campaigns he reunited them all. Marching east, he defeated the forces of the warrior-queen Zenobia and captured her capital, ending the Palmyrene empire; then, turning west, he reclaimed the Gallic provinces. Within a few years the Roman world was whole again under a single ruler.

He was as much a builder and reformer as a conqueror. To guard the capital against the barbarian raids that now reached deep into Italy, he ringed Rome with the massive defensive walls that still bear his name. He reformed the debased coinage and promoted the worship of the Unconquered Sun as a unifying faith for the whole empire.

His vigour made enemies, and in 275 he was struck down by a conspiracy of officers deceived by a forged list of names. Yet the recovery he achieved was real and lasting, and the work of restoration he began would be carried to completion a decade later by Diocletian.
