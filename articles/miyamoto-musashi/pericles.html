Pericles

Athenian statesman of the golden age (c. 495–429 BC)

Pericles was the statesman who guided Athens through its golden age, so dominating its public life for some thirty years that the era is simply called the Age of Pericles. A gifted orator and a shrewd politician, he led the radical democracy, broadening the people's share in government, paying citizens to serve on juries, and making the common Athenian the master of the city.

Under his leadership Athens turned the alliance it headed into an empire, and he did not scruple to use its tribute to glorify his city. He launched the magnificent building programme on the Acropolis, crowned by the Parthenon, raised under the direction of the sculptor Phidias, and gathered around him philosophers, artists, and thinkers, including his brilliant companion Aspasia.

His vision of Athens as the school of Greece found immortal expression in the funeral oration he delivered for the war dead, preserved by the historian Thucydides, a ringing celebration of democracy, freedom, and civic devotion.

Yet his policies also led Athens into the long and ruinous Peloponnesian War with Sparta. He adopted a cautious strategy of withdrawing behind the city's walls, but when plague broke out among the crowded population it carried off a great part of the people, and in 429 BC Pericles himself. He left behind the memory of Athens at the very summit of its power and brilliance.
