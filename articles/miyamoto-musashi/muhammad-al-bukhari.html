Muhammad al-Bukhari

Compiler of the most authoritative hadith collection (810–870)

Muhammad al-Bukhari was the greatest of the collectors of hadith, the reports of the words and deeds of the Prophet Muhammad, and the author of a book that Sunni Muslims revere as second in authority only to the Quran. Born in 810 in the Central Asian city of Bukhara, he displayed a prodigious memory in childhood and devoted his life to the sacred traditions.

For many years he travelled the breadth of the Islamic world, from Khurasan to the Hijaz, Egypt, and Iraq, gathering hundreds of thousands of reports from the scholars who preserved them. What set him apart was the rigour of his method: he examined the chain of transmitters behind every saying, weighing the honesty, memory, and life of each narrator, and admitted only those that met his exacting standards.

From this vast mass he distilled a few thousand sound traditions into his great work, the Sahih, organizing them by subject as a guide to belief, worship, law, and conduct. The reports he preserved reached back through the Companions, among them Aisha, to the Prophet himself, and his science of authentication shaped Islamic scholarship ever after.

Pious, austere, and uncompromising, al-Bukhari fell into conflict with the authorities of his day over a fine point of doctrine and spent his last years away from the great cities, dying near Samarkand in 870. His collection remains, twelve centuries later, among the most studied and trusted books in the Muslim world.
