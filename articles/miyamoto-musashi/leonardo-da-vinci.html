Leonardo da Vinci

Italian polymath of the Renaissance (1452–1519)

Leonardo da Vinci was the supreme embodiment of the Renaissance ideal, a painter, scientist, engineer, and inventor whose restless curiosity ranged across nearly every field of knowledge. Born in 1452 near the Tuscan town of Vinci, he was apprenticed in Florence to the master Andrea del Verrocchio, in whose workshop his precocious gifts soon outshone his teacher.

For nearly twenty years he served Ludovico Sforza, duke of Milan, as artist and engineer. There he painted The Last Supper, a mural of unprecedented psychological drama, and laboured over designs for war machines, waterworks, and a great bronze horse never cast. Returning to Florence, he painted the Mona Lisa, whose enigmatic smile would become the most famous image in all of art.

Leonardo filled thousands of notebook pages, written in his peculiar mirror hand, with studies of anatomy, the flight of birds, the flow of water, geology, optics, and machines that anticipated inventions of distant centuries. He dissected corpses to understand the body and sketched flying contraptions long before flight was possible, though he completed few of his grand projects.

He worked for a time for Cesare Borgia as a military engineer and competed in Florence with the younger Michelangelo. In his last years King Francis I of France brought him to the chateau of Amboise as an honoured guest, and there he died in 1519. Leaving behind only a handful of finished paintings but a universe of ideas, Leonardo remains the very symbol of human genius.
