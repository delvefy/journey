John the Baptist

Jewish prophet and forerunner of Jesus (early 1st century)

John the Baptist was the ascetic preacher and prophet whom Christians honour as the forerunner of Jesus. According to the Gospels he was the son of an aging priest, Zechariah, and Elizabeth, a kinswoman of Mary, and his birth was foretold as miraculous. He grew up to take the life of a desert hermit, clothed in camel's hair and living on locusts and wild honey.

Preaching in the wilderness along the river Jordan, John called the people of Judaea to repent and be baptized in water for the forgiveness of their sins. Crowds streamed out to hear him, and among those he baptized was Jesus himself, an event Christians regard as the opening of Jesus's public ministry; John, recognizing him, declared himself unworthy to loose his sandals.

The Gospels present John as the promised return of the prophet Elijah, the herald who would prepare the way of the Lord. Bold and uncompromising, he publicly condemned the ruler Herod Antipas for taking his brother's wife, and for this he was thrown into prison.

His end became one of the most retold of all biblical stories: at a banquet the daughter of Herodias so pleased the tetrarch with her dancing that he swore to grant her any wish, and, prompted by her mother, she demanded John's head on a platter. So the prophet was beheaded. He is venerated as a saint in Christianity, as a prophet in Islam, and as the chief teacher of the Mandaeans.
