Vlad’s Journey is a personal encyclopædia — a place where articles accumulate slowly, one at a time, on whatever subject proved worth writing down. It borrows the manners of a traditional encyclopedia: titled entries, ruled sections, tags in place of categories, and a search desk at the front. It makes no claim to completeness, only to care; the journey of the title is the author’s own, and the shelf grows in whatever direction curiosity happens to be facing.

How it is organised

Every article is a single, self-contained page shelved in the articles folder. A small ledger of titles, summaries, tags and dates is kept in the db folder; the front page consults it to offer search and to list what was added most recently. There is no machinery behind any of this — no server, no build step, no framework — merely pages, in the conviction that an encyclopedia ought to outlive its tooling.

Tags do the work that categories do elsewhere. Clicking a tag — here or on the front page — searches the archive for everything filed under it. Tags are kept lowercase, short, and few; a new one is invented only when no existing tag will serve, which is the same policy a careful librarian applies to new shelves.

The shape of an article

Articles here come in exactly two shapes, and the choice between them is made by the subject, not the author’s stamina. Every entry begins with an overview of five hundred to a thousand words: the whole subject in miniature, opening with a lead paragraph that defines it and continuing through a few short sections covering the essentials. A reader who stops at the end of the overview should still walk away with the complete picture, the way a traveller who only visits the capital may still fairly say what country they have seen.

Where the material is abundant, the overview is followed — below a ruled break — by a deeper delve: five to ten thousand words in which the full history, mechanics, figures and controversies are given room to breathe. Where the material is thin, the article ends honestly at the overview. Padding is forbidden in both directions: a slight subject is never inflated to look learned, and a rich one is never compressed to look tidy.

House style

Entries are written in the encyclopedia voice: third person, measured, slightly old-fashioned, with wit welcome and hype turned away at the door. They open with an illuminated initial, divide into ruled sections, and may carry an infobox in the right margin for facts best given briefly. Quotations are set apart in the old manner:

“Not all those who wander are lost — some are merely taking notes.”

The typography keeps the old courtesies — proper dashes, curled quotation marks, dates written out in full — and the design holds to a single theme: a leather-bound encyclopedia read by lamplight, parchment ink on deep umber, with amber reserved for what glitters — links, tags, and the occasional drop cap. References, where an entry has them, are listed last of all, in the manner of a bibliography rather than a footnote apparatus.

This very page follows the house style and may be read as a specimen of it — an overview without a delve, its subject being too small to sustain one, which is precisely the point.
