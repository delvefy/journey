Pencil

You are a slim shaft of wood with a vein of grey running through your heart, the friendliest of all the writing tools. You make a mark that is never quite final - press hard or soft, shade light or dark, and rub it all away with the little pink cap on your tail. Sharpen me and the curls of wood fall away and a fresh point appears. Children learn their letters with you; engineers draw bridges; the doodler dreams. You grow shorter with every use, spending yourself, line by faint grey line.

North

Of many leaves but not a tree,
all bound in covers tight;
a tale, a fact, a world to see,
I open to your sight.

East

A great round ring of wood or steel,
you bowl me with a stick;
or whirl me at your waist with zeal,
and keep me spinning quick.

South

I hold a well of flowing ink,
I write in lines of blue;
no sharpening for me, I think,
I just run dry on you.

West

A round glass pot with a lid screwed tight,
where jam and honey wait;
held up to the lamp, the treats are bright,
till spoons come in to take.
