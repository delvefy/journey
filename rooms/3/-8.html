The Kindling

South of the rising and east of the doubling, the seed of the other kindles toward a self. A step north the many began to rise, the wild waking warm on every side; a step west the one first doubled, the seed of an other beginning, barely, to be. Here, at the primal pole, that seed quickens further: the wild wakes it toward a self, the other's faint beginning warming into a first warmth that is starting to be a someone — not yet present, not yet met, but kindling, the seed of the companion catching the first heat of a self in the deep before the many.

It is the tender middle of all beginning: the other no longer a mere germ, not yet a woken presence, but a seed kindling toward selfhood, a warmth gathering toward a someone in the floor-dark beside you. You draw the spiral and the second motion is surer now, a kindling hand learning the gesture, the doubled whorl warming toward whole. The coil in your skin turns, and beside it the seed of another coil kindles toward its own first turn, the company warming from a seed toward a self, the wild waking the other you were always going toward.

Your heart keeps the deep slow count, and beside it a second beat kindles — no longer a mere seed of a beat but a faint warm stroke beginning to find itself, the other's heart catching the first heat of its own rhythm, the two beginning, in the primal deep, to warm toward the one time they will keep.

To the north the kindling rises toward the many, the wild waking warm on every side. To the east the seed kindles fuller toward a self, the other warming toward presence, the deep teeming. To the south it settles back toward the seed and the first faint warmth, the deep before the kindling. To the west the doubling, the seed of the other just beginning to be. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here that someone kindles toward a self beside you in the floor-dark, the wild warming them toward you — almost present, almost named — and still the warm coil turns, kindling its twin, and still one beat keeps the time a second is warming, in the deep, to keep.
