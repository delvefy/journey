The Gathered Dark

East of the one answer, the dark is full. Where a single warmth turned to you in the black a step west and answered when you called, here there are many — the warm dead, the lost, gathered close in a dark that has gone past the herd's hides and the beasts' breathing into something older and yet not colder: a deep the beasts have thinned out of, and the dead have filled. They are all turned toward you. They have been waiting, the way people wait for one who is late, and there is no reproach in it, only the warmth of a long-kept welcome.

Each of them, as you come near, says your name — not the herd's lowing shaped almost to sense, but words now, low and warm and nearly plain, the same name the breath beside you let go before the saying, here said and said again by many throats, passed around the circle of them like a coal kept alive through a long night. You almost have it. You reach, and this time many warmths take your hands, and one of them — you cannot see, only feel — is the one you were going with, the one whose pace your heart fell into in the dark to the west. You draw the spiral in the warm dust and it does not blow away; a dozen hands draw it with you, the old shared sign, the one thing the living and the dead still write the same.

The doubled heart of the answering dark has become a chorus, many bloods keeping one warm time, the herd-beat gone gentle and human — all of them beating with you and for you, the oldest count made at last into a kind of singing.

To the north the dark gathers back its beasts, the warm herd of living and long-dead, the horned one speaking in the old sound. To the east the company comes fully plain and present, the lost speaking your name whole, the high Wyld where the dead are simply here and the name is freely given. To the south the warm dead thin too into the older dark, before even the lost, the deep that kept no one. To the west the company narrows back to one warmth answering in the black. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here they are, all of them, holding your name warm among them, and one of them is the one — and they part to let you go on, still saying it, almost, almost plainly.
