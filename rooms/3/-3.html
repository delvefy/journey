Knife

A handle and a blade, and an edge thin enough to part what looks unpartable. The knife is the sharpest thing on the table and the most ancient tool in the drawer - older than the fork, older than the wheel. It slices the bread, halves the apple, spreads the butter, and asks only that you mind which end you hold. Cold, bright, and serious, it does its one keen job perfectly, and waits, edge down, for the next.

North

I am deep and round and wide,
I keep your cereal inside;
I curve up high so naught will spill -
of soup or grapes I take my fill.

East

I am clear and hold your drink,
juice or milk or water pink;
I have no handle, smooth and tall,
and shatter if I take a fall.

South

I have a long and narrow neck,
a cap on top to keep in check
the milk, the pop, the sauce inside -
tip me up and pour my tide.

West

I am deep with handles two,
I bubble up your hearty stew;
upon the stove I boil and steam,
the biggest cooker of the team.
