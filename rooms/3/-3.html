Rake

A long handle and a row of teeth, dragged across the ground to gather what has fallen. The rake is a made thing, plain and solid, the friend of autumn and the gardener's hand. It keeps the made, touchable south-east, among tools and worked iron. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

Link on link of welded steel,
I bind, I hold, I haul, I lock;
the dog is on me, the gate is shut,
the anchor hangs me down the dock.

East

A flat blade set on a long straight pole,
I chop the weeds and break the row;
between the beans I scrape the earth —
down the long garden I slowly go.

South

Four iron prongs upon a haft,
I lift the hay, I turn the ground;
not at the table — out in the field —
I pitch and toss the heap around.

West

A flat steel blade you push with your foot,
I bite the soil and lift it clear;
I dig the trench, I turn the bed —
the gardener leans on me all year.
