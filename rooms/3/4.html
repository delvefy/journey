The City of Names

East of the page and north of the one figure, the names have built a city. What stood alone a step south as a single shape of cold fire is here a populace: ranks of them, lit figures each woven of a name, filling streets and tiers and towers made of the same cold light they are — a city that is nothing but names stood up and living in it. The written charms of the room to the west have stepped wholly off their shelves and become the things they spelled, and walk here, and the walking is the city: a place where to be named is to be, and every citizen a word made flesh of light.

They move on errands of pure meaning, and as you pass among them each turns the spiral of its lit spine and says itself and is answered, a city all pronouncing its own roster at once. Somewhere in it is your name — walking, embodied, a citizen like the rest — and you go through the bright crowd hunting the one figure that is yours, the way you would hunt a face in a station, certain you would know it, never quite finding it; every name you near says itself plainly and none of them is the one, or all of them are, and the crowd closes and carries the searching on. The whole city is laid in a turning plan, street winding into street, a great slow spiral of cold fire, and you walk its coil inward and never reach a centre.

The two-throated voice of the single glyph is a city's clamour here, ten thousand names saying and answering, call and reply braided into one vast bright speech that never falls silent — the bell grown into the sound of a whole people pronouncing themselves, and your own name somewhere in the roar, said and answered by everyone but you.

To the north the city refines past flesh-of-light into pure circuit and number, the names thinking without bodies, the lit pole where a word remakes matter at a switch. To the east the city's names come fully sovereign, gods of light walking, reality grown consensual, a name reshaping the world at the saying. To the south the city narrows to a single standing figure, one name embodied, turning to you. To the west the citizens step back onto their shelves and become standing charms, written and working but not yet walking. You were going somewhere, under a name. It lives here, a citizen among a city of names, walking the bright coil with all the rest, and you search the crowd for it and cannot find the one face that would be your own.
