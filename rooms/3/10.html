Alarm

You are the loud sharp warning, the cry that breaks the quiet to say wake, or beware, or run. No object - a made sound and the fright it brings, both at once. The clock shrills you at dawn; the smoke and the burglar set you screaming; the whole town once rang you from the church when danger came. You raise the heart and quicken the feet. People dread you and depend on you. A wrought blast of caution, you are the world's loud voice for danger, the noise that will not let you sleep through trouble.

North

The musical ring of tuneful bells,
the clock that strikes the hour;
a soft sweet peal of falling notes
from the old church tower.

East

The loud flat parp a car horn makes
to warn or to complain;
or the cry of a flying goose -
a blaring, brassy strain.

South

The soldier's snap of hand to brow,
a sign of stiff respect;
given to the flag and chief -
a greeting men expect.

West

The reply you give to a question asked,
the right word that they seek;
the sum worked out, the riddle solved -
the thing you're asked to speak.
