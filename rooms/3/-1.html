Lamp

A lamp burns low on the shelf, a vessel of glass and brass with a wick at its heart, fashioned by a maker's hand. Its body you can lift and carry; its light you cannot, though it fills the room and pushes the dark back into the corners. Solid and wholly made, it stands in the south-east of the world. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

A wheel whose rim is cut to teeth,
I turn and turn the mill;
set my edge against my kin
and run the whole works still.

East

A heavy iron block I stand
where the smith comes to pound;
hot metal on my back is shaped
and rings with every sound.

South

I am a rope of iron rings,
each one within the next;
I hold the anchor to the ship
and leave the captive vexed.

West

Turn the key and hear me click,
I keep the door obeyed;
no thief slips past my little bolt
unless a pick is made.
