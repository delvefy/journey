Spoon

Further east into the made and held, the gentlest tool waits by your plate. A spoon is wrought — beaten or cast into one smooth curve — and entirely tangible: cool silver or bright steel that warms in the soup it carries. It has no edge to cut, no point to stab; it only cradles and lifts, the first tool a child is trusted with. It stirs, it measures, it feeds. Speak the names, and let the next way open.

North

Round I spin upon my hub,
I roll the bike, the cart;
the mill, the car, the spinning top —
I set them all to start.

East

I have three or four sharp prongs
to spear your meat and peas;
I sit beside the knife at supper —
lift your food with ease.

South

Round and deep, I hold the soup,
the cereal and the rice;
no flat plate could keep them in —
my hollow does it nice.

West

A handle on my rounded side,
I'm brimming, warm, and small;
I bring the tea up to your lips
and rest beside them all.
