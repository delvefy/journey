The Loosed Circle

South of the circle the wards let go. The double ring scored deep into the stone above is here only a track worn in bare earth, paced smooth by something that has gone round it longer than there has been stone to cut. The quarter-marks are not iron and coal and feather but what the dark grew in their places: a knuckle of pale fungus, a curl of root, a chalk-white bone, a feather no bird you can name let fall. No hand laid them; they came up where the working would have wanted them, as though the ground had dreamed the ring before anyone thought to cut it.

Inside the worn ring stands a shape you have met twice and not once — the smoke-and-held-breath that waited in the circle to the north, the two low lights and the curling horn that watched from the dark to the west, here the one standing thing. Nothing called it; it was not summoned and cannot be dismissed. The ring grew up around its standing. Where the spiral lay graven in the floor above and curled in horn in the burrow behind, here it is worn into the earth itself by the going-round, the same coil pressed into the ground by patient feet.

The bell's answer comes from no glass and no skull now but from underfoot — a low second beat in the packed earth, felt through the soles before it is heard, the slow heartbeat from the burrow keeping a time older than counting.

To the north the track sharpens back into the graven circle, the wards laid by craft, the shade held and called. To the west the ring loosens into the tended dark, only instinct and root, the watcher content to stay hidden. To the east the standing thing grows surer, and the air fills with others like it, the dead no longer strangers but neighbours, one of them keeping a name for you. To the south the ring goes back to before any ring, the dark older than feet. You were going somewhere, under a name. The shape stands in the circle that grew up around it, patient as the ground that dreamed it.
