Chime

You are the clear sweet ring of tuned bells - the doorbell's ting, the clock that strikes the hour, the little glass bells that sing in the wind. A hand casts you and hangs you; a tap, a breeze, the turning hour sets you ringing. Ting, ting, ting, you spill your bright round notes into the air. You are the singing of bells.

North

A short bright blast of a horn or whistle,
'toot-toot!' the little train;
the trumpet's peep, the tugboat's call -
a cheerful blowing strain.

East

The loud harsh ring of iron struck -
a hammer on a gong,
a heavy gate slammed shut at night -
a metal CLANG, headlong.

South

The shrill loud bell that wakes you up
or warns of fire or thief;
it shrieks until you stop it dead -
it brings the heart to grief.

West

It starts with who or what or why
and ends in a curling mark;
you ask it when you need to know -
it shoots a thought-filled spark.
