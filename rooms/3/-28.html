calendar

A calendar is the map of the year, hung on the wall in a grid of little squares - one box for every day, a fresh page for every month, usually a cheery picture up top. You cross off the days as they pass, ring the birthdays and the holidays in red, and count the sleeps until the big day comes. It tells you what is today and how far off is Christmas. Turn the last page in December, and the whole year starts again.

North

A stiff little card with a picture in front,
'wish you were here!' scrawled on the back;
a stamp, an address, then into the box -
from the seaside, in the postman's sack.

East

Tall and wooden, full of shelves,
I hold your books in a neat long row;
fat ones, thin ones, side by side -
pick one out and off you go.

South

A thin bright booklet of cartoon strips,
superheroes, 'POW!' and 'BANG!';
read the pictures box by box -
a funny tale, the whole gang.

West

A ring of gold for a royal head,
set with jewels that gleam and shine;
a king or queen will wear me proud -
'this whole land,' they say, 'is mine.'
