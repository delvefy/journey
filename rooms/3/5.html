Ode

A poem that lifts its subject high — to autumn, to a friend, to a silent urn — and praises it in grand and rolling lines. An ode is made wholly of words, the most exalted of songs unsung. It keeps the made north-east, among poems and praises. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A soft slow song to a sleepy child,
hummed at the cradle, low and mild;
'hush now, hush' till the eyes fall shut —
name the bedtime tune, beguiled.

East

The words the singer sings aloud,
not the tune, but what is said;
printed plain for all to read —
name the song's own words instead.

South

A simple tune on a single note,
repeated slow by a steady crowd;
the monks intone it in the nave —
name the droning song, plain and loud.

West

A holy song from an ancient book,
King David's words of praise and prayer;
numbered and sung in the great stone church —
name the sacred song sung there.
