The Spoken Light

North of the conjuring engine the glass burns away. East of the rising word the paper lets go. The two roads meet here in one bright room where neither pane nor page is needed any longer: a name, said, stands up as light, and is a shape. The engine below called its answers through rank on rank of dark glass; the word to the west lifted only half off its sheet; here the calling and the rising are the single act — speak a name into the lit air and it builds, cold-fire bright, a standing figure, spell and circuit gone so far into each other that there is no telling which does the work.

The figure assembles at the saying of it. The smoke-and-held-breath of the old frames is woven of light now, the half-risen word come wholly upright and wholly a being, and it turns to face you lit from within — the called and the named at last one thing. Below, the ranks faced together and the word stooped toward itself; here it finds your eyes and holds them. It stands a hand's breadth from being the one you keep almost remembering. It is not. But it is closer than any shape yet, and it waits, as if it too were almost sure.

The spiral runs through all of it as a single coil of light — the wire that carried the call in the engine and the watermark that lifted the word, here one bright winding that draws the name up out of the air into the shape and round again. And the bell has folded shut: the name rung as light comes back as the figure's own voice saying it, call and answer and the thing itself in one tone, a bell that is also the ringer it summons.

To the north the light refines past shape toward pure fire and number, the lit pole where a name remakes matter at a switch and needs no figure to stand in it. To the east the making itself falls away and the word alone suffices, a name spoken into open air standing a being with no engine at all. To the south the free light cools back into ranks of glass, the engine calling its shapes pane by pane. To the west the standing figure lies back down toward print, the rising word half off its sheet, the charm still mostly ink. You were going somewhere, under a name. It stands here in the light, turned full toward you, and says itself in your own voice, and is not yet you.
