The Living Glyph

North of the engine and east of the rising page, the word and the calling become one thing, and the one thing stands up. What the ranks of glass below could only call through, and what the lit page to the west could only half-raise, has come together here and needs neither glass nor paper: a figure woven of cold fire and living glyph stands in the open room, upright, made of nothing but a name said in light. The salt-line, the wards, the very letters of the name have lifted off their mountings and their pulp and braided themselves into a standing shape, lit from within, turning slowly on the spot.

It is your name, given a body — the same name pied in lead, fogged in glass, pulled bright, called down the ranks, half-risen off the page, here stood wholly up and wearing a form of light. The spiral is its spine, a coil of cold fire winding foot to crown, the watermark and the ward and the maker's mark all become the one line that holds it whole. It turns to face you. For the length of a held breath it nearly finds your eyes — and then, like the rising word, it faces itself instead, says itself, answers its own saying, a charm still speaking toward its own glyph before it speaks to you.

The chord of the engine and the call-and-answer of the page have braided into one voice with two throats: the figure speaks, and the same figure replies a beat later, the bell that learned to ring and then to answer now ringing and answering in a single lit body — a time that is almost, not yet, a conversation with you.

To the north the figure refines past light into pure circuit and number, ranks of standing names filling a city that thinks without flesh, the lit pole where a word remakes matter at a switch. To the east the lit name comes fully alive and free, a guest who turns and speaks first and gives you the name plainly, the high Lumen where the made and the called are one people. To the south it lies back into the ranks of calling-glass, the shapes stepping through, not yet free of the engine. To the west it sinks to a printed name half-risen off a lit page, charm and print not yet one. You were going somewhere, under that name. It stands before you in cold fire, nearer than it has ever been, and turns the last degree toward you — and you, who have no voice this bright, cannot yet say it back.
