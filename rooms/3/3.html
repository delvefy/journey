Carol

A song made for the cold months, sung at doors by lantern-light while breath turns white in the air. A carol is wrought of words and tune, a thing made by voices, yet you cannot hold it — it lives only while it is sung. It keeps the bright, made north-east, where things of the mouth and mind belong. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

One line of words, said over and over,
by monks in stone, by crowds at play;
half-song, half-speech, in steady time —
the same few words, sung all the day.

East

A grand and solemn song of praise,
a nation stands and sings of me;
before the game, beneath the flag,
they raise me up for all to see.

South

Hum me without a single word
and still you carry me along;
the melody, the air, the part
you whistle from a favourite song.

West

A song or poem comes in these,
each one a stanza, neatly penned;
the singer takes another breath
and starts the next from end to end.
