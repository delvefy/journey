Fork

Iron prongs on a wooden haft, pitched into the hay to lift and toss it. Out in the field, not at the table — this fork is a made tool of the harvest. It keeps the worked south-east, among the tools of the land. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

Teeth in a row on a long light pole,
I comb the grass and clear the ground;
the autumn leaves, the new-mown hay —
I drag them all into a mound.

East

Whirring round at a furious rate,
I bore a hole both neat and round;
through plank or wall I screw my bit —
press, and out the shavings pound.

South

A broad curved blade upon a shaft,
I scoop the coal, I clear the snow;
I do not dig as neat as a spade —
I lift great heaps and away they go.

West

Flat and baked and laid in rows,
I clad the roof against the rain;
or square on the floor, all glazed and smooth —
step across me, clean and plain.
