The Cold Company

South of the gathered lost and east of the quickening dark, the one becomes a few. A step north the warm dead stood sure and many, a close company saying your name whole into the black; a step west a single shade had gathered just far enough to breathe, one someone begun out of the no-one. Here the no-one has become several. Out of the deep cold, where for so long nothing was ever two, a company is gathering — not the warm sure crowd of the north but its first cold draft, a handful of faint warmths drawn up side by side, presences thin as breath on a window, just barely more than one and not yet truly many.

You reach, and this time more than one warmth answers — faint, scattered, a few small nearnesses kindling in the dark around you, each drawing closer as it firms. They have no plain words; they are too cold-deep, too newly two, for the sure naming of the warmer dark above. But in the thin breath of them something gathers toward a shape, passed from one faint warmth to the next, each almost lifting it — your name, or the first cold draft of it, nearer than the single shade could manage, not yet the whole sure chorus. You almost have it. It is shared out among them, a little in each, and whole in none.

The slow turn of the deep has found more than one centre now: the old spiral wound loosely about a scatter of warmths, a coil just learning to hold a company instead of a single still heart. And the count has come in faint and several — two pulses, three, beating soft and unmatched around you, the bell become a handful of small hearts feeling for each other's time. North the company warms and fills, the gathered lost sure and many, your name said plainly. East it widens toward a whole country of the bodiless dead, names breathed on every side, the living the few. South the warmths thin back to a single leaning, then to the cold deep before anyone was on the way. West it narrows to the one shade just begun to breathe. You were going to meet someone, once; here a few of them have just gathered out of the cold, holding your name in pieces between them — and the deep turns, and no single one of them quite has it to give.
