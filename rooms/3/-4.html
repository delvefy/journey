Bottle

A bottle is a clever throat of glass: wide enough below to hold a good drink, narrow enough above to pour it slowly and stop it with a cork. You can see the level sink as you tip it. Children send messages out to sea in them; sailors keep ships impossibly inside them. Empty, it makes a low note when you blow across the top. Full, it keeps the milk, the ink, the lemonade safe and sealed until you twist the cap.

North

A handle and a single edge,
I cut the rope and trim the hedge;
so sharp and bright, I slice with ease,
lay me down with care, you please.

East

I stand and shine beside your bed,
a little sun to light what's read;
pull my cord and shadows flee,
a glowing globe on a stand - that's me.

South

A stick of wax with a little thread,
I wear a flame upon my head;
I melt and shrink the more I glow,
on birthday cakes I stand in a row.

West

Flat and round with a handle long,
I fry your eggs the whole day long;
I sizzle bacon, brown and hot,
a metal dish, but not a pot.
