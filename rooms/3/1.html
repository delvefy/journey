Rhyme

East of the tune, the made world makes its words chime. A rhyme is wrought — chosen, fitted, two sounds set ringing together — and there is nothing to hold: it lives only in the ear, in the small click of cat against hat, moon against June. It is the oldest trick for making words stick, the spine of every nursery song and skipping chant. Children find it before they can read. Speak the names, and let the chiming way open.

North

In 'truth or me,' I'm half the game,
I push you to be bold;
I make you do the risky thing
you'd never do if told.

East

I'm built of lines that often rhyme,
set down in tidy rows;
I sing of love or war or spring
in words a poet chose.

South

Perfectly round, I love to roll,
I carry cart and car;
with spokes or solid, rim and hub,
I'll take you near or far.

West

Whistle me upon the road,
I have no words at all;
I'm a song stripped to its melody
that you can't help recall.
