Flute

A flute lies cool and slender on the sill, a silver pipe pierced with little stops, made by a careful hand. Empty and silent, it is only metal and air-holes; set your breath to it and it gives up a high clear voice you can neither catch nor keep. Wrought through and through, yet its whole gift is the untouchable note. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A little melody you can hum,
with neither words nor rhyme;
the music-box plays one of me
again and again in time.

East

Strike three strings at once and hear
a single, fuller sound;
I am not one note but a few
together, richly bound.

South

A toothed iron wheel that bites
another like a friend;
turn one alone and the machine
will turn from end to end.

West

Lay me on your shoulder, lean,
and rake me with your thumb;
my golden strings, from short to long,
will set the great hall hum.
