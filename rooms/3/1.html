The Conjuring Frame

North of the circle and east of the speaking glass, the working has been fitted into a thing you could carry. A tall pane of the dark, light-drinking glass stands upright in a frame of plain wood, and the wood is graven all round with the wards that lay loose on the circle's floor — salt-line and spiral and the four quarter-marks, cut now into the made edge, a summoning given a handle. The black candle burns at its foot. The air in front of the pane is the thick, breath-held air of the ring, gathered to a glass's width and leaning out of it toward you.

In the depth of the glass the smoke-shape stands as it stood in the ring — but here it is closer, and coming closer still. As you watch, it lifts what would be a hand and lays it flat against the inside of the pane, and the glass fogs from within, and in the fog a name blooms, written this time from the far side: the same name, reversed, the way the type lay reversed in the press, legible if only you could read it backward fast enough. The hand presses. The surface gives, like the skin on cooling milk. What surfaces here does not stay behind the glass; it is learning, slowly, the trick of how to step through.

The bell's answering second tone comes from inside the glass now, close and warm, rising when the fog blooms and sinking when it clears — the same hum the dark pane gave when you breathed on it from without, only it is not your breath fogging it now, and it is not your voice keeping time with the bell.

To the south the frame falls back to the circle on the floor, the shape standing where it was summoned, not yet near enough to cross. To the west the glass forgets how to summon and only speaks, fogging a name and giving nothing up, charm fitted to craft and no more. To the north the frame is lit and built into an engine of summoning, the stepping-through made certain and mechanical, glass after glass after glass. To the east the pane thins to nothing at all and the shape comes clean through, the dead standing in the room as neighbours, no glass left between you and the name. You were going somewhere, under a name. A hand is pressed to the inside of the glass, learning the surface, and the name blooms backward beneath its palm.
