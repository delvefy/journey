hobble

To hobble is to walk badly, all stiff and lopsided, the way you do when one foot is sore or one leg is hurt. You bob up and down, lean on whatever is near, and shuffle along far slower than usual - ouch, ouch, ouch with every other step. A pirate with a wooden leg hobbles; so do you on the morning after a long, long walk. It is the wobbly limping walk of someone who would much rather sit down.

North

Lift one foot and set it down,
forward, just a little way;
one of these and then another -
that's how you walk all day.

East

Drag something rough along the ground,
a chair across the floor - screeech!;
skin your knee on the gritty path -
that harsh rough rub is what I teach.

South

A long bold step, you march along,
legs swinging wide and proud and fast;
cover the ground with great big steps -
you walk with purpose, first not last.

West

Press my button and out comes BZZT,
a flat rude buzz, no tune at all;
the quiz, the door, the 'time is up!' -
one angry rattle, that is all.
