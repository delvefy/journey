Chain

A run of iron links, each one closed through the last, lies heavy and cold across your hand. A chain is wholly made — forged ring by ring at the anvil — and wholly solid, weighing in the palm. It binds, it hauls, it holds the gate fast shut. Wrought and plainly touchable, it keeps the south-east. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

I hold a little flame or bulb
behind my glass so bright;
set on the table or the post,
I push away the night.

East

Bent like a finger of hard steel,
I dangle from beam or wall;
I hold your coat, I catch the fish —
one curve that grips them all.

South

With many thin teeth in a row
I gather the leaves in a heap;
I never bite, I only comb
the lawn the gardeners keep.

West

Stretched skin on a hollow round,
I'm beaten with two sticks;
I keep the marching soldiers' time
with my rat-a-tat-tat ticks.
