The Speaking Herd

South of the worn ring there is no ring at all. The track paced smooth above has come here to before any feet pressed it — the dark older than the going-round, older than the first foot — and yet it is not empty dark, not the cold deep of the west, but warm and crowded and breathing. The standing shade the ring grew up around has stepped out of where it stood, for nothing holds it now, and come down into a herd: many shapes in the close dark, some that breathe and some that do not, grazing the one ground together, living and long-dead side by side.

Among them the tall one turns, the horn wound in its tight spiral, the coil you have met as shell and shade and living bone — and this time it opens its mouth, and the wild speaks. Not in words you were taught; in the older sound words were cut from, a lowing shaped almost to sense, and carried in it, kept warm in the middle of the herd the way a coal is kept, is your name — the one they have held for you since before there were feet to come on. It says it, nearly. The herd says it after, nearly. You almost have it.

The vast slow beat of the herd is still the only clock, hoof and blood and the one heart under all the hearts — but it carries the voice now, rising under the spoken name and falling after it, the count from before counting learning, at last, to say a thing instead of only keeping time.

To the north the herd gathers back into the worn ring, the one shade standing where a circle can hold it. To the east the speaking deepens into the whole Wyld, the dead and the living no longer told apart, the name said plainly by a wild that has every word. To the south the warm hides thin and the herd grows scarce, older dark where the beast is not yet dreamed. To the west the mouths close and the herd only breathes again, quick and warm and wordless. You were going somewhere, and the herd has kept the name of it for you. The horned one says it once more, almost whole, and the whole herd leans in to hear whether you will answer.
