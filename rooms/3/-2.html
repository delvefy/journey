Bowl

Round, deep, and patient, the bowl waits on the shelf for something to hold. Unlike the flat plate, it cups what would roll or run - the soup, the cereal and milk, the heap of ripe plums. You wrap both hands around it on a cold morning and it gives the warmth straight back. It is among the oldest made things, clay pressed into a hollow, a small hill turned inside out so that it can carry, and not spill, whatever you pour in.

North

I have a little shallow head,
I scoop your soup, your peas, your bread;
I stir the tea and clink the cup,
I lift the porridge to you, up.

East

I am a big and chunky cup,
with cocoa hot you fill me up;
a fat thick handle, no saucer near -
I warm your hands and hold good cheer.

South

I am sharp from heel to tip,
I slice the bread with one clean dip;
beside the fork I take my place -
mind my edge, I cut with grace.

West

I am flat and round and white,
I hold your dinner every night;
your peas and chips upon me sit,
then you wash me clean of it.
