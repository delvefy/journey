Chalk

You are a soft white stick of powdered stone, made to write upon the blackboard and the pavement. A hand presses you, and you squeak and leave your dusty line; press too hard and you snap in two. You draw the hopscotch squares, the sums, the teacher's lesson. You wear away as you write, growing shorter, leaving white on every finger.

North

A flat straight stick marked off in lines,
in inches and in centimetres;
you draw your straight lines down its edge
and measure all your metres.

East

A little wooden spinning toy,
you wind it, let it go;
it dances on its pointed tip
till it tires and falls below.

South

A little stick of coloured wax
to draw the sun bright yellow;
a box of thirty, every hue -
each one a waxy fellow.

West

A round shallow thing of clay or glass
you eat your dinner from;
it holds the stew, it holds the peas -
washed up when the meal is done.
