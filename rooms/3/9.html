Salute

You are a sharp gesture of respect - the flat hand snapped to the brow, the soldier's greeting to the flag and the officer. No object, no sound, only a movement people agreed long ago would mean honour. You are given on parade, returned with precision, dropped only when the senior drops his first. A wrought thing, a rule of the body, untouchable but unmistakable: in one crisp motion you say, without a word, I see you, and I stand for you.

North

A loud and ringing warning sound
that wakes you from your bed,
or screams when fire or thieves are near -
a fright, a sudden dread.

East

A sudden sharp and breaking sound,
a twig or biscuit cracked;
I'm what your fingers do for a beat,
a quick clean snap, in fact.

South

A gentle tap with a flat soft hand
upon the head or back;
I praise the dog, I soothe the child,
a kindly little smack.

West

I keep the past inside your head,
the things you used to know;
a face, a tune, a long-gone day -
I hold them, even so.
