Crayon

You are a little stick of coloured wax, wrapped in a paper sleeve, made for a child's hand to draw with. A hand rolls and dyes you - red, blue, gold, and thirty more in the box. You smooth bright colour onto the page, wear down to a stub, snap if pressed too hard. You melt to a blob on a sunny windowsill.

North

A soft white stick that writes on slate
and squeaks across the board;
it crumbles into dust and snaps -
the teacher's writing sword.

East

A long plank balanced in the middle,
a child on either end;
one goes up, the other down -
up and down, my friend.

South

Coloured wet stuff you brush on a page,
or roll on a wall in a coat;
it drips, it dries, it brightens all -
in pots and tubes it floats.

West

A soft light plug for a bottle's neck,
pushed in to seal it tight;
'POP!' it leaves the wine at last -
it bobs and floats, all right.
