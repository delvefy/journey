The Answering Circle

East of the threshold the working stops waiting. The salt the hedge-witch laid loose is graven here, scored deep into the stone floor in a double ring, and set around it at the four quarters are iron nails, a dish of still water, a coal, a feather — the wards, each in its place, holding the circle the way a frame holds a pane. At the centre the black candle that stood unlit below is burning, a small steady flame with no draught to trouble it, and the air inside the ring is thicker than the air where you stand, as though the room were holding its breath there.

Within the ring something stands that is not quite there — a gathering of smoke and held breath in the shape of a person, turned toward you, patient. You did not call it. Perhaps the room did; perhaps it has stood here the whole while, waiting for someone to come near enough to see. When you find the place where its eyes would be, a word forms in the thick air between you, almost spoken, almost your name — the same name pied in lead a world to the north, fogged in glass, half-yours everywhere — and it inclines its head, as though to ask whether it has the name right.

The bell that waited between its strokes is answered now. After each stroke in your skull there comes a softer second tone, lower, from inside the ring — not an echo, for it differs; a voice keeping time with the bell, replying to it, the way one bell across a valley takes up another.

To the west the circle loosens back to the witch's salt and her unlit candle, the working only promised, the name unspent. To the north the ring is fitted to craft and mechanism, summoning and made thing fused, the called name set running in light. To the east the one presence becomes many, the dead no longer summoned but simply neighbours, a whole air of standing shapes. To the south the wards loosen into the wild, the shade less called than always-here, grown out of root and dark like the horned thing in the wood. You were going somewhere, under a name. The smoke-shape waits inside the ring, patient as a held breath, for you to say whether it is yours.
