Gear

A single gear lies on the bench, a wheel of iron ringed with teeth, cut and filed by a patient hand. Alone it does nothing at all; set it to bite against its fellows and the whole machine wakes and turns. Hard, cogged, and wholly made, it sits due east on the wrought axis, where nothing is left to chance. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A slim long pipe of silver bright,
I sing when you breathe through;
your fingers dance to stop my holes
and pipe a tune for you.

East

Round I run and round I turn,
I never tire of the road;
four of me beneath a cart
will bear the heavy load.

South

Set me on the table's edge
when evening shadows crawl;
my little flame inside the glass
throws gold across the wall.

West

I have a face and two thin hands
but never wave goodbye;
I count the day from noon to noon
and never blink an eye.
