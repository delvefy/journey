Wheel

Three rooms east into the made world, the great invention turns. A wheel is wrought — no such perfect circle grows in nature — and it sits on the axis between things you grip and things you only use: an idea given an iron rim. It carries the cart, drives the mill, spins the potter's clay, and rolls the whole world's work along. Set it turning and labour grows light. Speak the names, and let the way roll open.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

Moon and June, and cat and hat,
I match the sounds on the end;
two lines I tie with the same tail —
verse leans on me, its friend.

East

On two great wheels I bear the load
the patient donkey hauls;
I bring the hay, I bring the goods,
and rattle past the stalls.

South

A small round scoop upon a stem,
I dip into your dish;
I carry soup up to your mouth,
I'm silver, if you wish.

West

I wear a face but cannot smile,
two hands sweep round my rim;
I tick away the steady hours
from morning's light to dim.
