Candle

You are a stick of wax with a thread up the middle, and a small flame is your whole purpose. Lit, you give a soft and trembling light and a thin curl of smoke; you lean a little, you drip and pool, and all the while you spend yourself to shine. Hour by hour you grow shorter. A single breath can put you out. When the wick burns down to nothing, the dark slips quietly back in.

North

I have a narrow neck and cap,
but never any head;
I hold your milk or fizzy pop,
till every drop is shed.

East

I fly the highest on a string,
when March winds blow strong;
of paper light and a long, long tail,
I dip and dance along.

South

Round and bouncy, thrown and caught,
I roll across the floor;
you kick me high or toss me up,
then fetch me back for more.

West

Flat and shallow, with a rim,
I carry cups and plates;
you hold me out with both your hands,
piled high with food that waits.
