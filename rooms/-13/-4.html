Newt

A small slick lizard of the pond's edge, no longer than your finger - a newt slips between water and land like it cannot quite choose. Smooth-skinned and damp, often a secret orange beneath, it hides under stones and stirs the still water with a flick of its tail. You might lift one, briefly, cool and squirming in your palm, before it wants the wet again. Made by no hand, it is one of the quiet, easily missed little lives of the bank.

North

A smiling grey swimmer
that leaps from the sea;
clever and friendly,
it clicks and squeaks with glee.

East

A little black wriggler
with a long thin tail;
I live in the pond
and grow legs without fail.

South

Once a crawling caterpillar,
now I float on painted wings;
from flower to flower
I drift in the spring.

West

A tiny horse
that lives in the sea;
it curls its tail
round the weeds, you see.
