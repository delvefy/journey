choke

To choke is to have something stuck in your throat so the air cannot get past - a crumb gone down the wrong way, a gulp of water that turns the wrong corner. You cough and splutter, your eyes go wide and watery, and for a frightening moment you cannot breathe at all. A thump on the back usually sorts it out. It is the body's alarm that something is where it should not be, and it wants it gone now.

North

Lean in close and look real hard,
screw up your eyes to see;
squint at the tiny far-off thing -
look long and carefully.

East

Cough and spit and spray it out,
'p-p-please!' you splutter and choke;
water down the wrong way, words all wet -
a spraying, sputtering, gasping joke.

South

The smallest drink, just touch your lips,
a tiny taste of something hot;
careful, careful, just a drop -
not a gulp, just a little spot.

West

Draw the air in through your nose,
to smell a rose or stop a drip;
a quick short snort to catch a scent -
or after crying, a snuffly sniff.
