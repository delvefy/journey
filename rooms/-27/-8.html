wheat

Wheat is the golden grain that fills the summer fields, tall thin stalks all leaning the same way when the wind runs over them like a wave. Each head is packed with little seeds, and those seeds, ground to flour, become nearly all our bread and cake and pasta. The farmer cuts it in late summer, and the stubble is left behind. It is the quiet hero of the dinner table - the most important plant most of us never notice.

North

A great soft bag beneath a cow,
four teats hang down all pink and warm;
the farmer pulls and milk hisses out -
it fills the pail on the morning farm.

East

Cut grass dried golden in the sun,
piled in bales in the barn so high;
the horse and cow munch me all winter -
a sweet dry stack beneath the sky.

South

Fine soft powder of cut-up wood,
it puffs up gold when the saw bites through;
sprinkled on floors, it soaks up spills -
a woody dust on the joiner's shoe.

West

Little grains cooked soft for breakfast,
porridge steaming in your bowl;
horses love a nosebag of me -
a warm and filling morning roll.
