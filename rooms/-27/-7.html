udder

An udder is the great soft bag that swings beneath a cow, pink and warm and heavy with milk. Four teats hang from it, and at milking time the farmer pulls them gently and the milk hisses into the pail. The calf butts at it to feed; the cow stands chewing, patient. It is the start of every glass of milk, every wedge of cheese, every scoop of ice cream - all of it begins here, under the cow.

North

A fish breathes through me, not a nose,
red slits along the side of its head;
I take the air from the water it swims -
without me a fish would be dead.

East

Hard and pointy upon a head,
a bull, a goat, a rhino wears me;
I curve up sharp to butt and poke -
a fearsome point for all to see.

South

Golden in the summer field I sway,
tall and thin, all in a row;
grind my grain to make your bread -
the staff of life, as farmers know.

West

At the back end of a dog or cat,
I wag when glad and droop when sad;
a fox's brush, a monkey's rope -
the wiggly end that beasts all had.
