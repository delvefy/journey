gill

A gill is how a fish drinks the water for its air. Behind its head the slits open and close, red and feathery underneath, combing the breath it needs out of the cold river. You will never use one - you have lungs - and yet here it is, soft and frilled and wholly of the body, a thing you can lift and look at. Natural, touchable, and built for a whole life spent underwater.

North

A small soft fruit with golden skin,
fuzzy like a peach but small;
a stone sits hidden in my middle,
sweet and orange, loved by all.

East

A fish wears hundreds, head to tail,
each a tiny shining plate;
slippery, silver, overlapped -
I am its armour-coat.

South

A cow swings me beneath her belly,
pink and full and round;
squeeze me gently in the morning
and warm white milk comes down.

West

Down a lion's neck I tumble,
shaggy, golden, grand;
a horse tosses me when it gallops -
long hair, no comb, no hand.
