sip

A sip is the smallest drink there is - just a touch of the cup to your lips and the tiniest bit of warmth or sweetness over your tongue. You sip the cocoa that is still too hot, the medicine you don't quite trust, the tea a grown-up gave you. It is slow and careful, the opposite of a great thirsty gulp. One little sip, then another, and the long drink lasts and lasts.

North

When food slips down the wrong way
and blocks your throat so you can't breathe;
you cough and splutter, eyes gone wide -
thump my back to bring relief.

East

A great big swallow, glug-glug-glug,
you drink it down in one quick go;
when you're scared your throat does it too -
one loud nervous swallow, slow.

South

The feeling when you see the stars
and your mouth falls open, eyes go wide;
'how? and why?' your busy mind asks -
amazement mixed with questions inside.

West

My tongue's own job, to tell me sweet
from sour and salty and bitter too;
lick the spoon, the lime, the jam -
I'm what the flavour tells to you.
