octopus

An octopus is all arms and no bones - eight long rubbery arms lined with little suckers, joined to a soft round head. It pours itself through gaps no bigger than its eye, squirts black ink when it is scared, and changes colour to match a rock until you cannot find it at all. It tastes with its arms and thinks with all of them at once. Soft, clever, and strange, it is the cleverest cold thing in the sea.

North

Sleek and grey, I bark and clap,
I flop on rocks and slip in the sea;
I balance a ball upon my nose -
a swimming star, both quick and free.

East

A flat brown bug that loves the dark,
I scuttle fast when the light clicks on;
I hide in cracks and behind the fridge -
by the time you look, I'm gone.

South

I walk sideways on the sand,
two snapping pincers, a hard round shell;
I scuttle through rock pools by the sea -
pinch your toe and run pell-mell.

West

I slice through the sea like a silver knife,
a long sharp blade sticks out of my face;
fast as an arrow I hunt and dart -
the pointy-nosed fish of the deep blue place.
