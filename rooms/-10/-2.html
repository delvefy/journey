Stream

You are a little run of moving water, smaller than a river, hurrying over its bed of pebbles down the hillside. No hand dug you - the rain and the spring made you, and you find your own way down, chattering and clear and cold. Trout hang in your quiet bends; children dam you with stones and float twigs on your back. You join others, and grow, and become a river at last. Quick, bright, and never still, you are the water always going home to the sea.

North

The hard horned foot of horse and cow,
it clip-clops on the road;
the smith shoes it with curving iron
to bear the heavy load.

East

Water frozen hard and clear,
so cold it bites the hand;
it caps the pond, it spears the eave,
and melts at spring's command.

South

A whiskered swimmer of the cold grey sea,
it flops upon the shore;
it barks, it claps its flippers wet,
and dives down for one more.

West

A small still patch of water, calm,
where you might swim or wade;
the rain leaves one, the rocks hold one
with crabs in cool dark shade.
