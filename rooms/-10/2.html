Flame

You are the bright dancing tongue of fire, gold at the heart and blue at the root, leaping up from the burning wood or wick. No hand made you, though a match can call you; you feed on the fuel and the air, and the moment they fail, you die. You give light and heat and the oldest comfort there is. You flicker, you lean in the draught, you cannot be held or kept. Beautiful and hungry, you are fire made visible, a living shape of light.

North

The droning hum a bee gives off,
or a fly against the glass;
a phone that shivers on the desk -
a sound that won't quite pass.

East

A tiny fleck of leaping fire,
struck off the flint or wheel;
it lands in straw and starts a blaze -
the smallest fiery zeal.

South

The dry and longing need to drink
when your throat is parched and tight;
a tall cold glass will set it right
on a hot and dusty night.

West

A great big roaring fire that leaps
and lights the whole night sky;
the bonfire's wild and crackling heart,
with sparks that float on high.
