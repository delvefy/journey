Slug

You are a soft slow shell-less creature, a snail without its house, gliding on a ribbon of slime. No hand makes you; you ooze out after rain to eat the garden's tender leaves, leaving a silver trail behind you. The gardener groans to find you. You carry no armour, only damp grey softness, and you never hurry.

North

It sleeps all day hung upside down,
then flits out when it's dark;
no feathers - leathery wings instead -
it squeaks above the park.

East

A little buzzing speck with wings
that lands upon your food;
it rubs its legs, it loves the bin -
a pest both quick and rude.

South

A small quick reptile, scaled and dry,
that basks upon a wall;
it scurries off on four short legs
and sheds its tail in a fall.

West

A tiny speck that bites and jumps,
it hides in the dog's warm hair;
it leaps a hundred times its height -
a circus master's care.
