Mountain

You are the great stone giant of the land, rising mile on mile until your head is lost in cloud and crowned with snow. No hand built you - the earth itself heaved you up over ages beyond counting, and the wind and ice have carved you ever since. Rivers are born on your flanks; eagles ride the air above you. You are climbed, and feared, and worshipped, and you do not notice. Vast, patient, and ancient, you are the world's own raised and rocky shoulder.

North

The dry-throat longing for a drink,
when your mouth is parched like sand;
a tall cold glass of water answers me
on a hot day in the land.

East

The low green ground between two hills,
where a river often runs;
sheltered, deep, and full of farms,
out of the wind and sun.

South

The hard horned foot of horse or cow
that clatters on the road;
shod with iron by the smith,
it bears a heavy load.

West

A great wide wood of crowding trees,
dark and deep and tall;
the wolves and deer and owls live there,
where shadows softly fall.
