Bat

You are the only flying beast that is no bird, leather-winged and small, hanging upside down by day in cave and belfry and hollow tree. No hand made you - you were born to the dark and learned to see with sound, flitting after moths through the dusk on wings of stretched skin. You are no blind ugly thing, whatever the tales say, but a clever night-hunter, soft-furred and quick. Strange, silent, and useful, you are the evening's own small leathery flier, sweeping the sky of insects.

North

A grey sleek swimmer of the cold sea,
it suns upon the rock;
it barks and slaps its flippers wet
and dives down with the flock.

East

A long-eared, long-legged, racing beast,
wilder than any rabbit;
it sprints across the open field -
to run away's its habit.

South

A shell-less, slimy, crawling thing
that slithers through the dew;
it leaves a silver trail behind
and chews the cabbage through.

West

A warty, hopping, bug-eyed beast,
a cousin to the frog;
it crouches in the damp at night
beneath a stone or log.
