Glint

A quick wink of light off something bright - a glint is the smallest flash the world can give. Sun on a knife's edge, a coin at the bottom of a well, the sly shine in an eye that knows a secret. It is there and gone before you can be sure you saw it, never lasting, never held. You cannot make a glint stay; you can only catch it, once, at the corner of your eye, and wonder.

North

The colour of the sky
on a clear summer day,
and the deep wide sea
where the dolphins play.

East

A thin grey veil
on a hot still day;
it blurs the far hills
and the sun's soft ray.

South

A fizzing, bubbling gladness
that makes you clap and crow;
too merry to sit still,
it sets your face aglow.

West

The colour of the grass
and the leaves on the tree,
of a frog and a lime
and the peas at your tea.
