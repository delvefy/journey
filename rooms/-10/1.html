Thirst

You are the dry-throated longing for a drink, the body's plain demand for water. No hand makes you; the salt and the heat and the running about wring you out until your tongue is sticky and your throat is sand. A long cold glass answers you in a moment, and the relief is sweeter than any feast. You drive the camel and the marathon runner and the child in from play. Wordless and ancient, you are the body's plainest cry made known: drink, or wither.

North

The bright hot tongue upon the wick
that flickers gold and red;
it gives off light, it gives off heat,
and dies when it's not fed.

East

The sense you use to read these words,
that works by light and eye;
shut your lids and it is gone -
the seeing of the sky.

South

The highest thing in all the land,
of rock and snow and scree;
it takes a week to climb the top
and look out on the sea.

West

The crawling tickly need to scratch
upon your back or knee;
a bite, a rash, a woolly shirt -
it begs your nails, you see.
