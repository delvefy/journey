fleece

A fleece is the thick woolly coat a sheep grows to keep out the winter, sheared off in one great curly blanket come summer and spun into wool. We borrowed the name for the soft fuzzy jackets we wear on cold days - warm, light, and snug as a lamb. In the old story Jason sailed across the sea to win a golden one. Whether on the sheep's back or zipped up over yours, a fleece is warmth you can wrap around you.

North

A little tool with a sharp split blade,
run me down a carrot or spud;
off comes the skin in long thin strips -
I leave it bare and good.

East

A warm knit top you pull over your head,
arms in the sleeves, all woolly and snug;
Granny knits one every year -
cosy as a winter hug.

South

Two rows of little metal teeth,
pull the tab and they lock up tight;
zzzip! to close your coat or bag -
slide it down to open at night.

West

Made from the hide of a cow,
brown and tough, it bends but won't tear;
shoes and belts and jackets too -
a hard-wearing skin to wear.
