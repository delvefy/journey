Clam

You are the shut-up shellfish of the seabed, two hard halves clamped tight together, buried down in the wet grey sand. No hand made you; the cold sea did. Squirt a jet of water and dig yourself deeper when the gull comes near. Happy as a clam, they say - and who could be happier, shut safe in your own two doors?

North

A tiny pink curl of the sea,
with feelers, fine and thin;
smaller than your littlest finger -
a mouthful, tail and fin.

East

A tiny upright fish that stands,
with a horse's little head;
it grips the weed with a curling tail -
the dad bears young instead.

South

A long thin bug with shining wings
that hovers over the pond;
it darts and zips, all blue and green -
of summer days, it's fond.

West

A bigger cousin of the shrimp,
pink-grey, with a curling tail;
you peel its shell and eat it whole -
from net or bucket-pail.
