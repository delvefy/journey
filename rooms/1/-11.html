Shirt

You are the cloth worn on the top half of the body - chest and back and arms - buttoned up the front or pulled over the head. A hand cuts you and sews you, gives you a collar and two cuffs. Under a coat or out on its own, white for school, striped for play. You wrinkle, you tuck in, you come untucked. You are the everyday skin of cloth.

North

A tall strong shoe that guards the foot
through puddle, mud, and snow;
it laces up above the ankle -
on stamping feet you go.

East

A shallow round thing, china or glass,
you eat your dinner from;
it holds the stew, it holds the pie -
to the sink when the meal is done.

South

It hangs down from the waist alone,
no top half, and no legs in two;
a girl might twirl it when she spins -
it swishes side to side for you.

West

An upright stake driven in the ground,
of wood or iron, standing tall;
the gate hangs on it, the sign hangs from it -
it never moves at all.
