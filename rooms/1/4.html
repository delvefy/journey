The Kindling Word

East of the press and north of the proof, the word leaves the page for good. The drying sheets are gone; in their place a wall of small glass panes stands lit from within, a braid of fine wire running pane to pane, and the words no longer wait on paper to be read — they run as light along the wire and bloom in the glass, a made day with the night taken out of it that will not dim. And where below the lit letters only meant what they spelled, here, for the first time, they have begun to do it.

You watch a pane spell warm and a warmth comes off it; a pane spell bright and the room answers brighter — small printed charms that act on the saying, the spell and the circuit no longer two things. On one pane, over and over, faster than the rest, runs the name: the one pied in lead and fogged in dark glass and almost-said your whole way here. Here it does not merely hang and blur. It gathers. For the length of a held breath the letters firm and the pane leans toward you and something in the room turns to face the name as though it had been called, half-risen, about to stand and answer to itself — then the light pulses and scatters it, and the room forgets, and the name runs on round the wire to try again.

The wire is wound at the room's heart into a bright slow coil, the spiral kindled in glowing filament, and the lit word chases round and round it gathering speed, the old watermark turning now fast enough to see. The proof's low hum and the press's quick chatter have braided into one sound — a bright rising tone laced with a racing tick, swelling when the word firms toward doing and never, now, sinking all the way back to silence. The made day hums. It is the bell with the night taken out of it, ringing an hour that does not end.

To the north the lit word sheds the wire too and stands as pure light and number, the city of panes where spell and circuit are one thing, the made day of the pole. To the east the words leave off being printed at all and are simply spoken into being, the name no longer half-risen but standing, answering when called. To the south the panes dim back to drying sheets, the word glowing but inert, meaning and not yet doing. To the west the glow goes out of it and the word merely races, printed fast and warm and acting on nothing. You were going somewhere once, under a name, and here it has almost caught light enough to be yours. The coil turns; the name comes round again, brighter; the pane leans; and the pulse takes it before you can answer back.
