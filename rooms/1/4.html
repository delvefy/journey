The Sleepless Light

North of the drying sheets the word lets go of paper. The ranked lamps that warmed the proofs below have become the page itself: a wall of panes, each lit from within, ranged floor to ceiling and on past counting — a made day with no lamp, no sun, and no night ever let to fall. The word is everywhere on them, flung up the wire from the room to the west and caught here on glass faster than reading, a hundred panes saying a hundred things at once, none of them to you and all of them at once.

On every pane, between the weather and the prices and the hours, your name surfaces — set bright and true, the same name pied in lead and pulled glowing and fogged in dark glass, shown here on a hundred screens together. You turn to one and it has already gone to the next; you cannot read them all, and no single one will hold it long. And with the faint new magic of this place the shown words have begun, barely, to act on the showing: when rain crosses a pane the air goes briefly damp, and when your name crosses, something in the room turns, very slightly, toward you. The light is learning to mean what it displays. Held to the nearest pane, your hand throws a shadow shaped, for an instant, like a slow spiral.

The hum braided from wire and lamp has sharpened to a chime, a clean bright ping that runs pane to pane down the wall — the bell flung into glass, ringing the hour onto every screen at once and into no ear at all.

To the north the word sheds even the pane, light and number needing no surface, a made day that thinks without a head, the bright pole. To the east the shown words leave off showing and begin to do, displayed charms that act on the saying, your name half-risen off the glass to answer for itself. To the south the panes dim to drying sheets, the glow gone back into paper, the lucent proof. To the west the light narrows to a single wire and its tape of dots, the sending that is only signal and means nothing yet. You were going somewhere, under that name. It crosses the wall a hundred times in a breath, and the room turns toward you each time, and never long enough for you to keep it.
