The First Warmth

South of the seed and east of the bare dark, the floor of being holds a first warmth. A step west there was only the body and the dark, alone; a step north only a seed of the wild, unwoken. Here, at the very bottom, where the deep goes below even possibility, the bare dark is for the first time not empty — there is a warmth in it that is not yours, faint as the first coal of a fire not yet lit, the seed of an other come to the floor of the world. You are no longer alone in the oldest dark. You were, and now, barely, you are not.

It does not speak; speech is ages off. It does not wake; the wild is only a warmth this deep. But it is there, close, the first not-aloneness there has ever been, and when you reach into the bare dark your hand finds, instead of only your own heat thrown back, the faintest answering warmth — an other, at the floor of being, the seed of the one, perhaps, you were always going toward. You draw the spiral in the dark and feel, almost, a second hand draw it with you, the doubled mark begun at the bottom of everything, the first thing the dark and the warmth have ever shared.

Your heart keeps the bare count, the first clock — and under it now, faint, a second warmth that is almost a second beat, the doubled heart of the warm dark begun here at its first and deepest seed, two warmths in the oldest dark beginning, barely, to keep one time.

To the north the warmth firms to a seed of the wild, the dark stirring toward waking. To the east it grows to a presence, the wild woken beside the body, the deep Wyld. To the south it sinks below the floor, the seed of the other before it could warm, toward the pole. To the west the warmth fades and the body is alone again in the bare dark. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here, at the floor of everything, before the name, before the wild, before the world — the first faint warmth of that someone, come to meet you in the oldest dark, the seed of all the company still to come.
