Spade

A flat steel blade, a wooden shaft, a place for your boot to push - and the whole garden answers to you. A spade is made for one honest job: to cut into the earth, lift it, and turn it over. You break the new bed with it, dig the holes for the trees, bury the seed and, one sad day, the old dog too. Lean on it to rest, and the upturned soil smells of everything about to grow.

North

I have a heavy iron head,
upon a handle tall;
I drive the nails in good and tight,
bang-bang against the wall.

East

I have a row of pointed teeth,
upon a long thin pole;
I gather up the fallen leaves,
and scratch the grass and soil.

South

I have lots of steps but not one stair,
I lean against the wall;
you climb me up to reach the roof,
hold tight and do not fall.

West

I am a lump of shiny black,
dug deep from down below;
you burn me in the fire grate,
to make the red flames glow.
