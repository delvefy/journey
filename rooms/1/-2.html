Cup

A cup sits waiting on the ledge, thrown on a wheel and fired hard, the work of a potter's hands. Empty, it is only a shape; fill it, and it holds the drink against your lip. You can lift it, chip it, turn it in the light — a made and solid thing, here at the warm tangible edge of the world. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

Stamped in metal, round and thin,
I ring upon the ground;
drop me in a beggar's cap
and hear my cheerful sound.

East

Skin and a barrel, that is all,
I have no throat to sing;
yet tap my taut and shining face
and out the thunders ring.

South

Born of grain and water both,
I swell up in the heat;
golden-crusted, warm and soft,
the loaf you love to eat.

West

White and hard, I lie within,
a frame beneath the skin;
the dog will beg me from the meat
and bury what I've been.
