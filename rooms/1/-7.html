Ladder

You are the simplest way up - two long rails and a row of rungs between, and suddenly the roof, the loft, the high branch are all within reach. You lean against whatever needs climbing and ask only to be set at a safe angle. Step by step, you turn a wall into a staircase; a painter, a firefighter, a child after a stuck kite all trust their weight to your thin wooden bones. You go nowhere yourself, yet you take everyone higher.

North

With a flat steel blade and a wooden shaft,
I bite into the ground;
I dig the hole, I turn the earth,
and heap the soil around.

East

A square of card with a fold-down lid,
I hold whatever's put inside;
your toys, your shoes, a birthday gift -
with flaps shut tight, I hide.

South

A woven thing with a handle on top,
I carry your shopping home;
or eggs, or bread, or a picnic spread,
wherever you may roam.

West

When the fire has burned right down,
I'm all that's left behind;
a soft grey dust, still faintly warm,
the finest you will find.
