Bread

A warm loaf sits on the board, its crust still ticking faintly as it cools. It is the most made of plain foods — grain from the field, ground and wetted and worked by hand, then turned to gold by fire. Solid, sliceable, here to be eaten, with only a memory of the field left in it. It rests in the low south. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

A hollow little bowl with an ear,
I hold your drink up high;
fill me, sip me, set me down,
and leave me by and by.

East

A long-handled iron blade,
I dig the trench and grave;
press me with your heavy boot
and lift the earth I cave.

South

A baked clay block, red and square,
I stack to make a wall;
mortar binds me to my kin
to raise a house up tall.

West

When the bright fire dies to grey,
I am the dust that stays;
cold and soft upon the hearth,
the end of all the blaze.
