The Breathing Dark

Below the painted hollow the pictures give out. One last beast goes by on the stone as you come down — a long horned back the ochre has all but let go — and then the wall is only wall, and then there is no wall at all, just the close warm dark you have been in before, one step east of where the body stood alone. But you are not alone in it now. The marking thinned to nothing and left, at the very end of it, a thing that was never marked: another breath, somewhere ahead in the black, holding very still.

You reach, the way you learned to reach in the dark to the west, and this time the warmth does not slip away. It stays. It turns. Where your fingers once found only your own heat thrown back, here there is a give that breathes on its own count, slow, in and out — and, almost, a sound shaped at the end of a breath that is nearly a word, nearly the name you keep not-quite-hearing, let go before the throat could close on it. Not spoken. Only readied. The dark has woken just enough to hold someone who might, one day, speak.

Underneath, the old count goes on, but it has doubled: your own heartbeat, the first clock, and close against it another, near enough to feel through the warm air, the two not yet in time — falling together, drifting apart, the way two who once walked a long road fell in and out of step.

To the north the dark gives back its first pictures, the beasts and the red hand, the urge to keep a thing by marking it. To the west the breath beside you fades again to your own warmth thrown back, the body alone with its single count. To the east the breathing comes nearer and finds a voice, the warmth a true presence now, shades that are company and answer when called. To the south the breath goes out and is not drawn again, the dark older than any breath, the deep that lay here before anyone came to lie in it. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Something breathes in the black beside you, keeping a count almost like yours, and does not yet say your name.
