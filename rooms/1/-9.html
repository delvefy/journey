sack

You are a sack, rough cloth sewn into a mouth and a belly, and you exist to be filled. Grain, potatoes, coal, the post - you swallow whatever is poured in and slump heavy in the corner, tied at the neck with a twist of string. No one admires you; you are not a basket nor a box, just honest burlap that holds and carries and is mended when you tear. Empty, you fold to nothing. Full, you can bow a strong back.

North

I'm woven all of bending cane,
a handle arching high;
fill me up with fruit or grain,
and on your arm I'll ride.

East

A little can of shining metal,
I keep your dinner sealed;
beans and soup inside me settle
until my lid is peeled.

South

I swallow up your foot and shin,
through mud and rain and snow;
lace me tight and stamp right in -
where puddles are, we go.

West

A little square of baked hard clay,
in tidy rows I lie;
I floor the room, I roof the day,
and keep the wet sky dry.
