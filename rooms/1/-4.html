Axe

A wedge of iron on a stout wood handle, the axe is force gathered to an edge. Swing it and the whole weight of your arms lands on one bright line, and the log splits with a crack that echoes off the trees. It is among the oldest tools, and the simplest: heavy, sharp, and honest. It fells the tree, splits the wood, and feeds the winter fire - and it asks, in return, only respect, and a careful hand.

North

I am a blade with jagged grin,
I eat the wood and chew right in;
push and pull, I rasp and screech,
and cut the plank beyond your reach.

East

Round and flat with handle long,
on the hot stove I belong;
I fry the bacon, sizzling hot,
and toss the pancake like a shot.

South

I have a heavy iron head,
I knock the nail right into bed;
bang, bang, bang, I strike it true,
I build the shed and fence for you.

West

I am the beach beneath your feet,
a million grains in summer heat;
you build me into castles tall,
then watch the sea wash down them all.
