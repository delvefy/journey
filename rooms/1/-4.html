Brick

A block of baked red clay, squared at every corner, sits heavy and rough in your hand. A brick is made for one thing — to be stacked with its fellows into a wall that stands. Wholly wrought and wholly solid, it keeps the south-east. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

Of flour and water, yeast and heat,
I rise and bake up brown;
sliced and buttered, warm from the oven,
the loaf of every town.

East

Flat and baked and laid in rows
on the roof to shed the rain;
I lap my neighbour, row on row,
and keep the dry from stain.

South

A long flat board of sawn-down wood —
I bridge the stream or floor;
lay me out from bank to bank
and walk across, dry-shore.

West

White grains the sea leaves drying,
sharp and stinging on the tongue;
I keep the meat from going off
and season every one.
