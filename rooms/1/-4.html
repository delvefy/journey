The Root Dark

East of the empty deep the dark is not quite empty. A step to the west the great black had no inside and no second in it, only your own heart beating where nothing kept time; and to the north a breath lay close and went in and out beside you. Here the breath has gone out and is not drawn again — but where it left off, something older does not leave. The dark itself has the faintest grain of being awake. Not a body. Not a breath. Only the sense, the way you sense someone in a black room before they so much as stir, that the deep has noticed you are in it.

It is the root-dark beginning to wake — the always-here in its first and oldest form, before it ever stood up into a shape, or grew a horn, or learned a name. You reach and find nothing to hold, yet the reaching is met somehow, attended, the dark leaning the least bit toward your hand the way the warded stone would not. The spiral is whorled in your fingertip still, and you have the strange certainty that the dark knows that mark — has always known it — that it is the oldest thing the two of you share, older than the body that carries it.

Your heart keeps its count, the first clock; but under it now runs a second time, far slower, sap-slow, a pulse you feel in the dark itself and not in any breast — the deep keeping a measure of its own at last, ancient and unhurried, the oldest heartbeat there has ever been.

To the north the dark draws a breath again and holds a body, the breathing other, the doubled heart. To the east the root-dark wakes wholly and rises into a presence, the always-here grown solid and turned to you, the wild that needs no calling. To the south the stirring sinks back beneath itself, the seed-dark from before there was anything to wake, the deep the wild is dreamed out of. To the west the awareness goes out and the deep is empty again, bodiless, your heart the only count. You were going somewhere, to someone. The dark, very slowly, has begun to know that you are someone, and going — and this deep, that is nearly enough.
