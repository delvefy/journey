Lie

A lie is a thing built out of nothing - words shaped to look like the truth and sent out to fool. It costs nothing to make and can cost everything to keep, for one always needs another to prop it up, until you have built a whole crooked house of them. Children learn early how easily it slips out and how badly it sits afterwards. Unlike a secret, which is simply true and hidden, a lie is false and loud, and the longer it lives the bigger it grows.

North

I say what you may and may not do,
in games and school I guide you through;
'no running here', 'be home by eight' -
break me, and you'll be in a state.

East

On your marks, get set, and go!
The fastest wins, the slowest's slow;
we run or swim or drive to see
who first across the line will be.

South

I am a thing you must not tell,
a hidden word you guard so well;
whispered low behind a hand,
known by few in all the land.

West

When the sun goes down to bed,
and the sky turns dark instead;
the stars come out, the owls take flight,
and all is still from dusk to light.
