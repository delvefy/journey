Boot

You are the tall tough shoe for hard going, rising past the ankle, soled thick against the stones. A cobbler cut and stitched and nailed you from leather or rubber; you are wholly wrought, built to be stamped and trudged and kicked in. You tramp through mud and snow, you crush the puddle, you wait by the back door caked with the day's dirt. Wellington, walking, work, or riding - whatever your kind, you take the weather and the rough road so the foot inside stays whole.

North

A big rough bag of jute or hemp,
wide-mouthed, and strong, and deep;
it carries grain and coal and post
in a heavy slung-up heap.

East

I sit on top of pot and jar
and seal the good things tight;
lift me to let the steam escape,
then clamp me down at night.

South

Worn upon the chest and back,
with sleeves and buttons too;
it has a collar round the neck -
you iron it when new.

West

A long flat board of sawn-up wood,
nailed down to make a floor;
the pirates make you walk me out
above the sea's loud roar.
