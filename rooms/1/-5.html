The Seed-Dark

East of the groundless, the not-yet begins to dream. A step west you could not be sure there was a here, or a you; a step north the dark had woken enough to know you were in it. Here, between, is the seed of that waking — the deep still mostly the under-being, the great not-yet, but with the first faint stir in it, the earliest possibility of the wild, curled and waiting like something not yet planted that might still grow. Nothing is here. But for the first time the nothing leans, very slightly, toward becoming something.

It is the seed the root-dark grew from, met before it grew. You reach, and the deep does not yet know your hand — but it is on the very edge of knowing, a not-quite-recognition, the dark turning the first hair's-breadth toward you the way a seed turns toward a warmth it cannot yet feel. The spiral in your skin is the warmth it turns to: you draw the coil and the seed-dark stirs at it, faintly, the first event in a place that has barely agreed to have them, the mark not held and not lost but noticed, almost, by a wild that is itself only almost.

Under the uncertain count you carried from the groundless — your heart unsure it beat — there comes now the first faint stir of another, sap-slow, not yet a pulse but the promise of one, the wild's heart before its first beat, so that for the first time in the deep your uncertain count is almost, almost, answered.

To the north the seed wakes, the root-dark stirring into a presence that knows you are there. To the east the seed quickens toward the waking wild, the first presence beginning to rise and turn to you. To the south the seed sinks below even possibility, the deep from before the wild was dreamable, toward the pole. To the west the stir goes out and the deep is the groundless not-yet again, being itself unsure. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here there is almost no one, and almost no you — only a seed in the deep beginning to dream that one day it might know your name, and the first faint answering of a heart not yet beating, which is, this deep, very nearly enough.
