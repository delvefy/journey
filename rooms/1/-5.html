Plank

A long flat board sawn from a tree, laid down to walk on or build with. The plank is a made thing, plain and strong, the floor of the ship and the gangway over the gap. It keeps the worked south-east, among built and sawn things. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

Baked hard from clay, red-brown and square,
I stack in rows with mortar between;
wall after wall the builder lays —
house and chimney from me are seen.

East

Grey rock that splits in flat thin sheets,
nailed on the roof to shed the rain;
or framed for the schoolchild's chalk —
wiped clean, then written on again.

South

A great thick timber, squared and strong,
set up high to bear the load;
the roof and floor both rest on me —
the house's bone of seasoned wood.

West

Sweet white grains you spoon in tea,
from cane and beet they're ground and dried;
the cake, the jam, the candy bar —
the sweetener. Name what's inside.
