Basket

You are woven from supple withies, over and under, into a belly that carries and a handle that lets you be carried. The farmer fills you with eggs, the shopper with bread and apples, the picnic-maker with the whole bright spread for the grass. Light and strong and open to the air, you let what you hold breathe. The cat curls asleep in you; the washing rides in you to the line. Empty, you nest neatly in your fellows; full, you are the simplest way ever found to carry a great deal at once.

North

Two tall poles with rungs between,
I lean against the wall;
climb my steps to heights unseen,
take care you do not fall.

East

A round glass pot with a lid screwed tight,
where jam and pickle keep;
I show my treats through my clear side,
till spoons come in to reap.

South

A big rough bag of cloth or jute,
tied tight up at the neck;
I hold the spuds, the grain, the loot,
slung heavy on your back.

West

A block of clay baked hard and red,
I'm stacked to build a wall;
with many more a house is made,
strong and square and tall.
