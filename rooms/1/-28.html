chopstick

A chopstick is a slim smooth stick, and it never works alone - you need the pair, balanced in one hand, to pinch up your rice and noodles and dumplings. It takes some learning: the bottom one stays still, the top one taps like a beak. Across much of the world this is how dinner is lifted to the mouth, no fork in sight. Made of wood or smooth painted bamboo, two little sticks become the cleverest of tools.

North

A little bowl on the end of a stick,
you scoop your soup and pudding too;
round and smooth, it holds the food -
stir your tea and sip the stew.

East

A ring of gold upon a head,
set with jewels that gleam and shine;
a king or queen will wear me proud -
'I rule this land, and all is mine.'

South

Two arms that pinch and grab and squeeze,
pick up the toast or the lump of coal;
a squeeze of the hand and snap they shut -
a grabber that keeps your fingers whole.

West

A tiny curved hair on your eyelid's rim,
a row of them fence in your eye;
catch one that falls, then make a wish -
blow it off and watch it fly.
