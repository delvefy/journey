Song

North of the name, the made world learns to sing. A song is wrought — someone shaped these words to this tune — yet you cannot lay a finger on it; it lives only while it is sung, then folds back into silence. It rides out on the breath, gathers a crowd, lifts the roof of a church, and the saddest one can still be the one you love best. Speak the names, and let the doors carry the tune.

North

A line, a pause, a sudden twist,
and then the room will roar;
tell me well, and all will laugh —
tell me twice, a bore.

East

Blow the candles out and think me,
drop a coin into the well;
catch a falling star and hold me
quiet — never tell.

South

I was given the day you were born
and I cost you not a thing;
I am not your face or hand,
yet across the room I ring.

West

I am what is left when the music ends,
what snow lays on the town;
no ear has ever truly heard
the sound of me come down.
