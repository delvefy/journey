Song

A song hangs in this room, half made of words and half of air. No one can hold it, yet a voice can make it out of nothing and give it away without losing it. It is older than writing — the lark has it, the cradle has it, the marching road has it. Made by the throat and gone on the breath. Four riddles wall the room; sing the right word to each.

North

Told at the fireside, never sung,
the fairy kind, or tall, or grim;
“once upon a time” I begin,
and end when the candle's low and dim.

East

Moon calls to June, and cat to hat;
when the ends of two lines sound the same,
the poet has caught me in his net —
a ringing match is all my game.

South

Given to you on your first day,
yet others say me more than you;
called across the crowded square,
your head turns round — you know it's true.

West

Speak me, and at once I am gone;
the crowded hall has none of me;
I fill the night when all is still,
and the snow comes down most quietly.
