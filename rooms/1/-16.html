Label

You are a label - a little slip of paper or cloth, stuck or sewn or tied on tight. You tell what a thing is and where it goes: jam on the jar, a name in the coat, a price on the toy. Made by a hand and printed with words, you are small and flat and easy to miss, yet without you the cupboard is a guessing game. Read me, and the unknown becomes known.

North

A little pinch of bent steel wire,
I hold your papers tight;
squeeze my jaws and slip them in -
they will not take to flight.

East

A flat card wallet, bent in half,
to keep your papers neat;
slide your drawings safe inside
and tuck them, sheet by sheet.

South

Round and flat and often white,
I sit beneath your tea;
pile me up with peas and chips -
you eat your lunch off me.

West

A long straight bar of metal or wood,
the same all down my length;
I hold the curtains, catch the fish,
and bend but keep my strength.
