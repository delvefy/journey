Name

You have crossed to where the made meets the unmade. A name is the most wrought thing of all — hammered out of breath, not iron — and the least touchable: it weighs nothing, casts no shadow, yet it is the first gift the world hands you and the last it lets you keep. Here every door answers to a name. Speak the right one and the wall forgets it was ever a wall.

North

I am woven out of words and breath,
and rise when hearts are glad;
the lark pours me out at dawn —
sing the sweetest you have had.

East

I am a song with all words gone,
you whistle me, you hum;
stuck fast inside your head I stay
and rattle like a drum.

South

I hang above the village square,
an iron tongue inside;
strike me once, I ring for all —
for fire, for prayer, for bride.

West

Call out once across the glen
and I will call it too;
I own no voice but borrow yours
and hand it back to you.
