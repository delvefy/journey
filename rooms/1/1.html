The Glasswright's Landing

The two airs meet here and make a third. You arrive, and cannot quite say from which door, for both stand open behind you and both feel like the way you came. The room is small and bright, and the brightness is caught, not burned: set into the walls are panes of glass, some clear, some bottle-green, some the deep blue of a late sky, and through them leans a light that is at once the bent amber of the shrine below and the cold even pallor of the made world beside it, married now into something new.

On the bench a single lens has been ground and polished, and beside it lies a coil of fine glass thread, drawn out and set down like a frozen spiral. When you lift the lens, the caught light gathers in your palm to a white point, warm, almost a voice. Craft did this; but the gathering feels like a charm, and you cannot find the seam between the two.

Somewhere a clear tone hangs in the air and will not fade — the bell remade as resonance, a wineglass note struck once and held.

West lies the patient wood and iron you came through, where the work was all of the hand. South, the leaning torch and its soot-drawn charm. To the north the light grows stronger, no longer caught but made, an engine of it. To the east the glass begins to speak, names surfacing in the colour like breath on a pane. You were going somewhere bright. The lens holds its point of light as steady as a held breath.
