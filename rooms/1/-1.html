Coin

Here the made world turns solid and small. A coin is wrought — struck cold between two dies — and wholly tangible: it warms in your fist, it rings on stone. Two faces and no body, it carries a head it has never owned and answers questions no one dares to ask aloud, flipped spinning into the air. You can spend it, lose it, bite it to be sure. Speak the four names and the walls will spend themselves.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

I have a mouth but never eat,
a tongue that cannot lie;
one blow, and I will shake the air
to tell the town nearby.

East

I have no lid but hold your drink,
a handle, and one lip;
you raise me up to meet your mouth
and take a morning sip.

South

I have a head but cannot think,
a single foot, and thin;
the hammer beats me out of sight
and holds two timbers in.

West

Older than the oldest tree,
I neither breathe nor grow;
you skip me on the water's face
and pile me row on row.
