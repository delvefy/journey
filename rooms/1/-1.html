Coin

Here everything has a weight and a worth. A coin is a small flat sun of metal, struck by a hand and stamped with a face that never blinks. You can close your fist on it, spend it, lose it down a crack in the floor. It is the most touchable kind of promise — bought and sold, head and tail. Four riddles wall this room; pay each its one true word.

North

Of bronze I am cast, and hung up high,
a single tongue, no taste, no teeth;
swing me, and one long note will fly
to wake the town that sleeps beneath.

East

Key is my partner, I am his twin;
without his teeth I will not part;
I hold the door and keep all in,
and click to guard the treasure's heart.

South

I have a lip but cannot kiss,
one ear-shaped handle, yet no arm;
pour the hot tea down into me,
and held in both your hands, I'm warm.

West

I lie in the field and never grow,
the wall is built of me, and stays;
strike me on steel, a spark will show;
I keep my shape for all my days.
