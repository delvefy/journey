Elbow

You are the elbow - the bendy hinge halfway down the arm, where it folds in two. You lean on it at the table, you knock it on the door and yelp at the funny-bone tingle. Bend it to lift a cup, straighten it to reach. It is the joint that lets a hand find the mouth and a shoulder swing the bat. Hard and knobbly, and ticklish in the very strangest way.

North

A big round bear of black and white
from bamboo woods of the east;
I sit and munch green stalks all day -
a gentle, chubby beast.

East

A small grey bear who hugs a tree,
high in the gum tree's crown;
I sleep all day and chew the leaves
and rarely come back down.

South

Between your hand and arm I bend,
a joint so quick and small;
a watch is buckled round me tight,
I let your hand turn at all.

West

Soft and round on either side
of your nose, below the eye;
it goes bright red when you're ashamed
and puffs out when you're shy.
