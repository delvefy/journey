Red

You are the colour of blood and fire, of the rose, the ripe tomato, the warning light that says stop. No hand made you; you are the loudest colour there is, the one the eye leaps to first. Hot, alive, and urgent. Paint a thing red, and it shouts; the bull, they say, sees me and charges.

North

The colour of mud and bark and bears,
of chocolate and of toast;
of autumn leaves and a conker's shine -
the earthy one we like the most.

East

The bright glad leap of joy inside
when something lovely comes;
a treat, a friend, a sunny day -
it fizzes up and hums.

South

The jolt that stops your breath quite still,
the bad news, the cold splash;
you stand there frozen, eyes gone wide -
struck dumb by the sudden flash.

West

The colour of night with no moon at all,
of coal and a witch's cat;
the darkest shade, with no light caught -
of a chimney sweep's old hat.
