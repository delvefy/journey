Sting

You are the sharp quick prick of pain - the nettle's brush, the bee's hot jab, the slap of cold water, the cut rubbed with soap. No hand makes you; the hurt flares up all on its own, fierce and sudden and small. You make the eyes water and the breath hiss in. Sharp, hot, and gone in a minute.

North

Not the burn of fire, just gentle heat -
the sun upon your skin,
the glow beside the hearth at night -
a cosy peace within.

East

A tickle on the skin that begs
to be scratched again and again;
a midge's bite, a woolly vest -
it nags you, now and then.

South

A vast dry land of sand and stone,
where almost no rain falls;
the dunes roll on, the hot sun beats -
a camel crosses, that is all.

West

What the hot stove gives your hand,
what the bonfire's lick will do;
red and sore, it blisters up -
the fire's painful clue.
