ruffle

To ruffle is to disturb something smooth into a mess of little folds - the wind ruffles the surface of the pond, a fond hand ruffles your hair, a cross bird ruffles up its feathers till it looks twice the size. It is a gentle roughing-up, never quite damage, just a tousling. Smooth it down again and the ruffle is gone. It is the opposite of neat: the quick rumpling touch of a breeze or a hand.

North

Drop a stone in the still flat pond
and watch the little rings spread wide;
tiny waves that race outward -
a gentle wrinkle on the tide.

East

Get taller, bigger, day by day,
a seed becomes a towering tree;
a puppy into a great big dog -
measure me on the wall, you'll see.

South

A sail puffs out, a curtain swells,
smoke rolls up in a great fat cloud;
the wind fills me and out I bloom -
I puff and roll, all soft and proud.

West

Let the air all hiss right out,
the balloon goes wrinkly, small and sad;
the tyre goes flat, the lilo sinks -
all the puff is gone, too bad.
