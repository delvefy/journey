Robe

You are the long loose gown for indoors, soft and warm, tied at the waist with a cord - the dressing-gown after the bath, the wizard's sweeping garment, the king's heavy ceremonial folds. Made by patient hands. Pull me on, and the day is over or not yet begun. There is no hurry in a robe.

North

A single loop of thread sewn through
to join two edges tight;
the needle dips and rises up -
small even rows, drawn right.

East

A little cloth tied round the neck
to keep the baby clean;
it catches all the drips and crumbs -
at mealtimes it is seen.

South

A thin soft cloth across the face,
the bride wears one of white;
you see right through its misty net -
it hides her, soft and light.

West

A soft warm cloth around the shoulders,
knitted, with no sleeves;
Granny wears one by the fire -
it wraps and warms and weaves.
