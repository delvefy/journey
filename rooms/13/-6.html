Stitch

You are one small loop of thread pulled through the cloth, the smallest step in sewing - in, out, in, out, until the seam is whole. Made by the needle's careful dip, you join what was apart. A stitch in time, they say, saves nine. Pull me tight, row on row, and the torn thing is mended.

North

A tall shut box with doors that swing,
against the kitchen wall;
it hides the plates, the cups, the tins -
shut tight, it keeps them all.

East

A scrap of cloth sewn over a hole,
on the knee of your old jeans;
a square of new on the worn-out coat -
it mends the rips and seams.

South

A long loose gown you wear at home,
warm round you, soft and deep;
the wizard drags one, hem to floor;
you pull it on to sleep.

West

A little track of joining teeth,
you pull the tab and run;
it shuts your coat from waist to chin -
and 'zzzip,' the fastening's done.
