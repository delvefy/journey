Cuff

You are the turned-back end of the sleeve, the band of cloth that rings the wrist where the hand comes out. Made by the tailor's neat fold. Roll me up to wash the dishes; button me down for best. A pair of you, one on each arm - and sometimes a secret note, or an ace, is hidden up me.

North

A thin soft cloth across the face,
the bride wears one of white;
you see right through its misty net -
it hides her, soft and light.

East

You wear it up on top of your head,
to keep off sun or rain;
a woolly one, a sun one, a cap -
you doff it, then again.

South

A big gold cup you lift up high
when your team has won;
they pour the drinks in it sometimes -
a prize for what you've done.

West

The arm of a coat or a shirt,
you push your hand right through;
short for summer, long for cold -
two of them on you.
