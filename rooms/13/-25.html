tugboat

A tugboat is small and stubby and astonishingly strong - a little boat with a giant engine, whose whole job is to push and pull ships far bigger than itself. The great liner cannot turn in the narrow harbour, so the tug noses up, takes the strain on a thick rope, and heaves it round to the dock. Chug, chug, chug it goes, low in the water and busy. The smallest boat in the harbour does the heaviest work.

North

A big flat boat that crosses the water,
cars drive on and people too;
back and forth across the bay -
it carries the whole town through.

East

Two wheels and a board, a handle to hold,
push with one foot to zoom along;
fold me up and carry me home -
down the path you whizz, headstrong.

South

No engine at all, just a great white sail,
the wind pushes me over the sea;
I lean and tilt and skim the waves -
blow, wind, blow, and carry me.

West

A long thin boat for just one person,
a double paddle, dip left, dip right;
sit low in the water, skim the river -
tip me over, hold on tight.
