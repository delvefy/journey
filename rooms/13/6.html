Puff

You are a short quick blow of breath or smoke - the candle's end, the steam train's chuff, the dragon's little snort of grey. No still thing makes you; a sudden push of air does. One small burst, here and gone. Round your lips and blow once, sharp: that is me, a puff, and the feather lifts and falls.

North

You do it when you touch your toes,
the road does round the hill;
a wire once straight is now a curve -
it gives, and holds it still.

East

Push the air out through your lips,
to cool the soup or the tea;
you do it hard at birthday cakes -
the candles flicker free.

South

Grind it round and round with your teeth,
the food to mush, then down;
the cow does it the whole day long -
a slow jaw, up and down.

West

When you have run and lost your breath,
you puff both in and out;
the dog does this with tongue hung low -
fast breathing, there's no doubt.
