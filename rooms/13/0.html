Ship

You are a great vessel built to cross the sea - decks and cabins, a tall mast or a smoking funnel, an anchor to drop and a wheel to steer. A hand builds you in the yard and slides you down to the water. You carry hundreds of folk and a hold full of cargo over the deep, riding the waves through storm and calm.

North

How many blind mice in the rhyme,
how many sides has a triangle got,
how many bears found Goldilocks?
Count them - it's the lot.

East

A big slow farm machine with great fat wheels
that drags the plough through mud;
it chugs across the field all day -
slow, strong, and caked with crud.

South

A light kept safe inside a glass cage,
swung on a handle or hung on a hook;
the old night-watchman carried one -
a candle's caged-in look.

West

Bigger than a van, with mighty wheels,
it growls along the road;
it hauls the timber, stone, and steel -
a giant with its load.
