Trophy

You are a trophy - a shining gold cup or statue, won for coming first. You stand on the mantelpiece for all to admire, your name and the year engraved upon your base. The football team lifts you high; the spelling champion clutches you tight. Made by careful hands from gleaming metal, you are the proof of a win, the prize that says 'you were the best of all'. Polish me, and the victory shines again.

North

The folded end of a shirt's long sleeve,
that buttons round your wrist;
turn me up or button me down -
I'm easy to have missed.

East

A long straight bar of metal or wood,
the same from end to end;
I hold the curtain, catch the fish,
and I am stiff to bend.

South

I have a tongue but cannot taste,
a mouth that never feeds;
swing me and I sing out loud -
'ding-dong' the sound proceeds.

West

A round bright disc upon a ribbon,
pinned proud upon your chest;
won for being first or brave -
a prize for doing best.
