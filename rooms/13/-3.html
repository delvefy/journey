Bench

You are a long hard seat, just a plank on legs, made to sit several folk in a row - in the park, the church, the changing room, the workshop. A hand bolts you together to last. You weather grey in the rain and bear a hundred carved initials. Folk feed the ducks from you and rest their tired feet.

North

The little metal joint that lets
a door or a gate swing wide;
oil it well, or it will creak -
two flaps and a pin inside.

East

A tall wooden cupboard for hanging your clothes,
with a door and a rail inside;
push through the coats at the back, they say,
and a snow-world you may find.

South

A long soft seat with cushioned back
where a family can flop;
you watch the telly sunk in it -
two or three can plop.

West

A little seat with no back at all,
on three or four short legs;
you perch on it to reach up high,
or sit to scrub the steps.
