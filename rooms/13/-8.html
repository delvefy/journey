Veil

You are the thin soft cloth across the face, the misty net you can see through but not be seen behind - the bride's white fall, the widow's black, the mosquito net at night. Made to half-hide. Lift me, and the face beneath is revealed; let me drop, and the world goes soft and far away.

North

A long loose gown you wear at home,
warm round you, soft and deep;
the wizard drags one, hem to floor;
you pull it on to sleep.

East

A long fine dress for a grand event,
the bride's, the ball's, the queen's;
it sweeps the floor in silk and lace -
the finest dress there's been.

South

The end of the sleeve, turned back round
the wrist where the hand comes out;
you roll it up to wash your hands -
a button holds it, no doubt.

West

False hair you wear upon your head
when your own is thin or gone;
the judge, the clown, the dressing-up box -
you pop this borrowed mop on.
