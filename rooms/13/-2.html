Hinge

You are the little metal joint that lets a door or a gate or a lid swing open and shut - two leaves and a pin between them. A hand screws you on and oils you. Without you nothing opens; the door is just a wall. Oil me, or I creak; rust me, and I stick.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A light kept safe inside a glass cage,
swung on a handle or hung on a hook;
the old night-watchman carried one -
a candle's caged-in look.

East

A little bar that drops in a slot
to hold the gate or door;
you lift it up to let folk through -
clack, it falls once more.

South

A long hard seat for several folk,
of wood or iron slats;
you find one in the park or church -
sit in a row, like that.

West

A little round ball stuck on a door,
you grip it, give a turn;
it works the latch and lets you in -
a small round thing to learn.
