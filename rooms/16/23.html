squat

To squat is to fold yourself down low without sitting on anything - knees bent right up, bottom near your heels, balanced on the flats of your feet. You squat to look at a beetle, to dig in the sand, to peer under the bed. Your legs soon ache and beg you to stand, but for a minute you are small and low and close to the ground, looking at the world from a frog's-eye view.

North

Go down on your knees upon the floor,
bend your legs and rest your weight;
to pray, to weed, to tie a lace -
down low on your knees you wait.

East

Press it hard between your hands,
a hug too tight, an orange juiced;
push the toothpaste, grip it close -
a firm hard pinch is what's produced.

South

A bird sits high upon a twig,
or you on the edge of a wall or stool;
balanced up on a narrow spot,
settled, but ready to move, no fool.

West

A great loud cloud of buzzing bees,
or ants that pour across the ground;
thousands moving all as one -
a crowd that hums with a busy sound.
