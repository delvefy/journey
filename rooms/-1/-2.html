Seed

Under the world, the smallest promise waits. A seed is grown by no hand — wind or bird or fruit let it fall — and it is tangible, a hard speck you can roll between two fingers, yet folded inside it is a whole tree it has not become. Give it the dark, the damp, a little patience, and it splits and reaches up. Everything green begins this small. Speak the names, and let the ground open to let you down.

North

I work below where no light comes,
I grip the earth and clay;
I drink the rain and hold the tree
against the wind all day.

East

I am hard and white and buried deep,
the part of you that stays;
the dog has loved me down the years
and dug me up for days.

South

I have no legs, I have no eyes,
I wriggle through the ground;
after rain I'm on the path
and robins want me found.

West

I am the smallest piece of tree,
I snap beneath your tread;
the bird will carry me away
to build her nest a bed.
