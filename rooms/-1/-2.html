The Bone Hollow

The cold deepens, and it is older than any fire, because no fire has ever reached here. You leave the painted beasts behind — there are no pictures on these walls, no ochre, no hand; the impulse to mark a thing has not yet been born this far down. There is only rock and a fine drift of pale grit that is not ash, for nothing here has burned, and your breath stands in the air before you, the one warm thing in the room, and losing the argument.

Half out of the grit lies a long curve of bone, ancient and ungnawed, belonging to no animal you could name. You kneel and, from the old habit you cannot account for, draw the spiral beside it with one finger. The grit slumps softly back and fills the line as you make it, so the coil closes behind your fingertip and is gone — the mark refusing to hold here, the way a word refuses to hold in a tongue that no one has ever spoken.

There is no count at all. Not the heartbeat, not the drip, not the bell. Only your own pulse and your own breath, and the cold patiently subtracting both, and a silence that waits for nothing, because nothing here has ever expected an answer.

To the north the cinders and the dead charm, where the world at least remembers having ended. To the east the rock warms a degree and the dark begins, faintly, to listen. To the south there is older dark still, the body before the body, the deep where even bone gives out. To the west the grit pales toward the long Waste, ash without a fire, silence without a sound it ever lost. You were going somewhere, once. Here even that begins to feel like something you only dreamed.
