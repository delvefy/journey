The Filed Hour

Here the cold light has no kindness left to file away. The room is white — not painted white but made of some seamless pale stuff, floor to ceiling, lit from no fixture you can find, so that nothing casts a shadow and your own body seems the only flaw in it. The straight daylight of the water-clock has been perfected here into something that owes nothing to the sun: even, sourceless, and wholly without warmth, a brightness that has had the day taken out of it.

At the centre, on a white plinth, a clock keeps time with no water and no face. A fine coiled hairspring — the spiral, drawn now to a thread of bright steel — breathes open and shut, open and shut, driving an escapement that divides the hour finer than the clepsydra ever dreamed. It is exact past any need for exactness. It measures and measures and there is no one the measuring is for; the soul has been filed clean out of the counting, and what is left is only true.

The click has become a pure even beat, each one the twin of the last, none louder or softer — the bell at last perfected and at last meaningless, a sound that has forgotten it was ever rung for anything.

To the east the same hour still kept in falling water, plainer, warmer, not yet wholly sterile. To the south the machine is younger and rougher, the engine that threw the rest away but still smells of use. To the north the white deepens toward the pole, intelligence without flesh, light that needs no eye to fall in. To the west the seamless surface cracks toward rust and the long grey silence, perfection gone to ruin. You were going somewhere, by some exact hour. The beat divides it smaller and smaller and never arrives.
