Breath

Breath fills the room and leaves it, fills and leaves, the one tide that never stops while you live. You feel it at your lips — warm going out, cool coming in — yet try to hold it and it is already gone, ungraspable as a thought. Made by no smith, belonging to the living air, it is the soft north-west of the world. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

When weariness or grief comes near,
I slip out on my own;
a long slow breath let softly go,
half-sound and half a moan.

East

I am the hush when all has ceased,
I cannot be heard or said;
a single whisper murders me,
and noise leaves me for dead.

South

I am unseen but never still,
I have no shape or face;
I fill the sail and turn the mill
and race from place to place.

West

I come by night and leave by dawn,
a fur of white and cold;
I bite the apple on the bough
and silver all the wold.
