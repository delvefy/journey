Cloud

Higher than the fog, the water learns to float. A cloud is made by no one — only sun and river breathing upward — and you will never lay a hand on it: a mountain of white that weighs nothing you can feel, changing shape as you watch. It carries the rain across whole counties, throws its shadow on the hills, and at evening burns to copper and rose. Look up and read it. Speak the names, and let the high way open.

North

I am the blue above your head,
I hold the sun and moon;
the birds and clouds all sail through me
from morning until noon.

East

I am what fills the empty church
and what the snow lets fall;
break me with a single word —
I'm gone, no sound at all.

South

I chase the leaves across the yard,
I lift the kite to soar;
no eye has ever looked on me,
yet I rattle every door.

West

I tumble grey out of the cloud
and soak you to the skin;
I rap the roof, I swell the brook,
I make the gutters din.
