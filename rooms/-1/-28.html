daffodil

A daffodil is the loud yellow shout of spring - a ring of petals around a little trumpet, nodding on a long green stalk. They come up in crowds along the verges and under the bare trees just as winter gives up, hundreds of them bobbing in the cold bright wind. Pick a bunch for the kitchen table and the whole room turns sunny. Wherever you see them, you know the dark half of the year is over.

North

A smooth round cup on a tall green stem,
red or yellow, pink or white;
Holland grows me field on field -
I close at dusk and open at light.

East

A tiny curved hair on the rim of your eye,
a whole soft row to keep out grit;
one drops on your cheek - now make a wish! -
then blow it off, that's it.

South

A small bright yellow shiny flower,
hold me under your chin to see;
if the glow says yes, you like your butter -
in the meadow grass, that's me.

West

First through the cold late-winter ground,
a little cup of purple or gold;
I poke up brave through melting snow -
spring is coming, or so I'm told.
