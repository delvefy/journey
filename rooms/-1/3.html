The Stamping Room

West of the press the warmth goes out of the word. The oil and lampblack, the honest reek of damp paper waiting to be read — none of it reaches here. The room is the same long shape but lit with the cold sourceless white of the hour kept below, shadowless, and the machines in it do not compose so much as stamp. Ledgers stand open in racks; notices come off a roller in even sheets; a date-press cuts the same figures into form after form, the word reduced to record, printed not to be read but to be filed and believed by no one.

No compositor stands here, no printer; none is needed. The presses run themselves, and what they run off no eye will ever cross — the cold light deepens at the far end into a white that needs no one to read in it, the first hint of a making that will do its thinking without a head. A ribbon of paper feeds out and curls on the floor in a loose coil, the spiral again, printed over and over down its length with one entry: a name, perhaps your name, set perfectly and meaning nothing, a thousand true copies of a word no longer worth the saying.

The press's honest thump has gone clerkish too: only the flat thud of the stamp coming down, even as a heartbeat and as empty as the perfected clock, printing the hour onto paper instead of telling it to anyone — a bell that files its own ringing and moves on.

To the north the stamping quickens past paper onto wire and onto light, the word flung out for no reader at all, the cold made day of the pole. To the east the records warm back into the press, ink and oak and a name half-wanting to stand and be set right. To the south the stamping falls silent into the filed hour, the clock that keeps cold time with no one to keep it for. To the west the ledgers crack and yellow toward the long grey silence, the word stamped out and then the stamp forgotten, the Waste that records come to. You were going somewhere, and it has been entered, and filed, and never once read back. A name uncurls down the ribbon on the floor, perfectly printed a thousand times, and not one of them is read.
