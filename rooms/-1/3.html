Sigh

A long breath leaves you without a word, and the room seems to settle with it. A sigh is not quite speech and not quite silence — only air let go, a small weather of the chest. You cannot hold it back or take it in again once it slips out. Made by no hand and impossible to grasp, it drifts in the intangible north, a little to the wild west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

I creep up when you're tired and dull
and stretch your mouth up wide;
catch me from another's face —
you cannot keep me inside.

East

I am the softest way to speak,
no voice, just breath and word;
lean close and I will tell a secret
barely to be heard.

South

In through the nose and out the mouth,
I come and go for free;
you take me in each moment lived,
yet cannot capture me.

West

I settle on the grass at dawn
in beads of silver-clear;
the rising sun will drink me up
before the noon is here.
