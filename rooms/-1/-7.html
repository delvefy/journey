The Old Cold

South of the undoing and west of the unparting, the body sinks into the old cold. A step north the bare body had only begun to come undone, the warmth leaching slowly into the cold-after; a step east it sank below being into the warm undivided dark, merged but not unmade. Here the sinking and the undoing are one descent: the body goes down below the floor of being and the cold goes down with it, the warmth wholly leached now, the unmade body settling into a cold that was here before any warmth and will be here long after — the old cold, risen through the floor to take you the rest of the way.

It is not the warm un-parting of a step east; it is colder and older than that. The warmth does not spread into the dark, it is drawn out of you, beat by beat, into a cold that does not merge but takes. There is no other here, no answering heat, only the long cold and the bare fact of having been warm. You find the spiral in your skin and hold to it as the heat goes, the one mark the old cold comes for last, the final warm coil at the bottom of the undoing — and it stays, faint, the warmth in it nearly all leached but the turn of it untaken, the last thing the cold has not yet drawn off.

The heart keeps the sunk, leaching count, each beat cooler and wider than the last, the first clock sinking below the floor of being into the old cold — not stopped, not spent, only taken back, stroke by cold stroke, into the cold it briefly held off, a body's warmth returning to a dark that never had any.

To the north the old cold firms to the undoing, the body still warm enough to lose. To the east the sinking warms to the unparting, the body merged into the old dark rather than unmade. To the south it sinks below the old cold toward the pole, the body unmade past even the after, the floor beneath the floor. To the west the warmth is wholly gone to cold and Waste, the body undone absolute. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here you sink alone into a cold older than warmth, the heat leaching beat by beat into the dark — and still the warm coil turns, the last unleached thing, and still one cold stroke keeps the count the old cold is, slowly, taking back.
