Spider

You are eight legs and a long patience. From your own body you draw a silk finer than any thread and string it between twig and wall, a wheel of glass the morning hangs with dew. Then you wait, still as the corner you hide in, for one careless fly. You weave no cloth and turn no wheel, yet your work outshines the loom. Mend it each dawn, and dine on whatever the wind blows in.

North

A house upon a soft slow back,
I cross the leaf and stone;
one silver foot, one shining track,
I travel all alone.

East

When the fire has had its say,
I am the soft grey rest;
a warm light dust that drifts away,
the last of what burned best.

South

Smooth and small and round and grey,
the river rolled me here;
you skip me where the shallows play,
or pocket me, my dear.

West

Green and damp on legs that spring,
I leap from bank to log;
with one long tongue I catch the flies,
and croak across the bog.
