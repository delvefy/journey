Hunger

You are the empty, gnawing need to eat, the body's loud reminder that the fire must be fed. No hand makes you; the hours pass, the stomach hollows, and you growl and ache until a meal answers you. You sharpen the senses - bread smells like heaven when you are in full cry - and you drive every creature out to forage, to hunt, to graze. Ancient and honest, you are the engine under all the day's foraging: eat, you say, and keep on living.

North

Not a sharp and sudden hurt,
but a dull and dragging one;
your tooth, your head, your weary legs -
it nags till day is done.

East

The break you take when work is done,
to sit and ease your bones;
a Sunday off, a quiet hour,
no labour and no groans.

South

The black and airless deep beyond
the very highest sky;
where stars and planets, moon and sun,
go floating, far and high.

West

A soft low shine on metal or glass,
a glint of quiet light;
not a flash, but a steady glow
that catches at the sight.
