Pearl

You are a little globe of milky light, grown not in any mine but in the dark of an oyster shell. No hand made you - a grain of grit slipped in, and the creature wrapped it, layer on patient layer, into something round and lustrous. Divers go deep and risk their breath for you. Strung on a thread you circle a throat; on its own you are a single soft moon of the sea. Rare, smooth, and quietly shining, you are the grain of trouble turned to treasure.

North

A lump of hard grey stone I am,
too big to lift or throw;
I sit upon the hill for years
and watch the seasons go.

East

A long flat length of sawn-down tree,
smooth on the top and wide;
laid side by side they make a floor
for boots to tramp inside.

South

A small round fruit that grows in bunches
upon a climbing vine;
green or purple, sweet and juicy,
squeezed to make the wine.

West

A small white flower with a golden eye
that opens in the lawn;
make a chain of me to wear,
picked one bright spring dawn.
