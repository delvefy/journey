Ant

Down at the level of the grass roots there is a whole busy nation, and the ant is its tireless citizen. No bigger than a comma, it lifts crumbs and leaves many times its own weight and hauls them home along invisible roads, nose to tail in a marching line. Tap the hill and a thousand pour out at once. It has no time to waste on you - there is always a queen to feed, a tunnel to dig, a long sweet trail to follow.

North

In velvet black beneath the lawn,
I dig my halls from dusk to dawn;
with shovel-paws and tiny eyes,
I push up hills to your surprise.

East

White as snow but never cold,
in the sea and earth I'm found of old;
a single grain can spice your meal,
and on hot chips I'm the real deal.

South

I carry my house upon my back,
and leave a silver, slimy track;
no slower traveller is found,
I inch along the dewy ground.

West

I'm sticky earth, all soft and brown,
you squeeze and shape me, up and down;
the potter turns me into pots,
then fires me hard, the whole big lot.
