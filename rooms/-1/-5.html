The Aftermath

West of the not-yet, the deep tips toward after. A step east the groundless could still go either way, being itself undecided; here it has decided, and decided against — not the unbegun but the un-being, the deep of the already-ended, ash and cold and a quiet that feels, impossibly, like grief. Nothing ended here, because nothing was ever here to end. And yet the place has the whole weight of aftermath, the hush of a room after the mourners have gone, as though it had lost something it never had.

It is the cold deepened past the ash to the north, the spent place made a lasting state — and the strangeness of it is that you feel the loss as your own. You reach into the dark and find nothing, and the nothing aches, the way the place where a held hand was aches after it is gone. The spiral you draw in the cold ash does not hold; the grit closes over it slow and final, like earth over a thing put down for good. The whorl in your skin feels less a mark now than a relic — the last trace of someone, you almost think, already lost here, mourned in a deep that never met them.

Your heart still beats, but in the manner of the aftermath: not spending the count now but already spent, a heart going on past its own stopping, each beat the echo of a beat — the mourning-time of a clock that struck its last hour long ago and somehow tolls on, quietly, for no one.

To the north the after firms to the ash dark, the cold with at least the leavings of an absent body. To the east the deep tips back toward the not-yet, the groundless that could still begin. To the south the aftermath sinks toward the pole, the deep ended before it was, the absolute after. To the west the ash widens into the open Waste, the void of the ended, where nothing answers and nothing ever did. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here, where no one ever was, you grieve them anyway — the deep is all aftermath, and your heart tolls its spent count for a loss that never happened, which is, perhaps, the oldest loss of all.
