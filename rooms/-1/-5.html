Berry

A small round fruit of the bush and hedge — red, black, or blue — soft enough to crush between two fingers and stain them with its juice. The berry is wholly natural and wholly touchable, sweet food for bird and child alike. It keeps the rooted south-west, among growing things. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

I am the small brown nut of the oak,
a tiny cap upon my head;
bury me and wait long years —
a mighty tree climbs up instead.

East

Sweet white grains spooned into tea,
pressed from the cane and from the beet;
in cake and candy, jam and bun —
name the white that makes them sweet.

South

The dry rough wrapping round a seed,
the papery coat the corn-cob wears;
shuck it off to reach the grain —
name the corn's loose coat it bears.

West

A prickly weed with a purple crown,
too sharp to grasp, I guard the field;
Scotland's flower, all spikes and down —
name the thorn that will not yield.
