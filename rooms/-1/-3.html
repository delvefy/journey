The Cooling Dark

Below the bone the cold finishes its work. In the hollow above there was at least a bone to find, one long ungnawed curve to say a body had come this far; here even that is gone, ground to the pale grit it was always going to become, and the grit goes down past where you can feel a floor. This is the deep the hollow promised to its south — the body before the body, the place where even bone gives out. You are the last warm thing that has ever been here, and the cold is beginning to take that back.

A step to the east the dark was blood-warm and held a breath beside your own. Here the warmth leaches out of you into the patient cold, fingers first, then wrists, and the breath you push into the black does not come back as company — it disperses, a slow coil unwinding, the last shape your warmth will make before it is the room's and not yours. Whatever you reached for in the warm dark, there is nothing to reach for now. The dark is emptying, toward ash that never burned and bone already given up.

The heartbeat the warm dark called the first clock is still here, but slowing, and it is the only clock left — no drip, no bell, no breath but the one going out of you — and you understand, without being told, that the count is no longer keeping time so much as spending it.

To the north the grit firms back into rock and the long bone, the cold that at least remembers a body lay in it. To the east the dark warms again and holds another breath, the count doubled, the company you brushed and lost. To the west the cold pales the rest of the way into the Waste, ash without a fire, a dark that never had a warmth to lose. To the south there is older dark still, before even the cooling body, the deep that has never once been lain in. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Your heart spends the last of the count into the cold, and the cold takes it, and does not hurry.
