Worm

Back under the soil, the humblest digger does the world's quiet work. A worm is grown, not made — a soft pink rope with neither bones nor eyes — and wholly tangible, cool and squirming in the palm. It eats its way through the dark, turning dead leaves to good earth and loosening the ground for every root. The robin hunts it; the angler prizes it; the garden could not live without it. Speak the names, and let the soft way open.

North

I'm tiny, dry, and fit to hold,
yet I become a tree;
the bird will peck me, child will plant me,
rain will set me free.

East

Hard outside and hollow in,
I wash up on the beach;
the crab once wore me on its back —
now I'm yours to reach.

South

Velvet-coated, nearly blind,
I dig the dark all day;
I push up little hills of soil
and tunnel far away.

West

A chunk of trunk the axe brought down,
I'm heavy, round, and long;
stack me by the hearth to burn,
or sit and rest along.
