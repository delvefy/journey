The Ash Dark

South of the cooling, the cold was always here. A step to the north a body was still letting go of its warmth, fingers then wrists, the last coil of breath unwinding into the black; here there is no warmth to leach, for none ever reached this deep to begin losing it. The cold did not come. It is simply the oldest thing in the room, older than any warmth that might have cooled to make it, and over everything lies a fine pale leavings — ash without a fire, bone ground to grit before there was ever a bone.

It is the deep tilted westward, toward the Waste. Where the empty dark a step east keeps neither warm nor cold, here it has gone to ash, the long aftermath of a cold that had no before. You draw the spiral in the grit from the old habit, and it does not hold: the cold, patient as the draught in the unmade rooms far above, lets the line soften and the grey close over it, unwitnessed, as though no hand had ever passed. Perhaps the mark needs warmth to keep it. There is none here to spare.

Your heart still beats, the first clock, but the cold has slowed it to a spending — the same spending the cooling dark named, each beat costing more than the last. There is no second time beneath it, no sap-slow root-pulse such as woke the dark to the east; only your own, going out into a cold that will not keep it.

To the north the cold finds a body to take, the warmth still leaving, the spent heart. To the east the ash thins back to the empty deep, neither warm nor cold, the bodiless first dark. To the south the cold deepens past even ash, the dark from before there was warmth to lose. To the west it gives wholly over to grey silt and open Waste, leavings without end, where nothing answers and nothing recalls that it once did. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here the cold was before you and will be after, and your heart spends the last of the going into it, and it does not warm.
