Acorn

A small brown nut in a rough little cap lies in the leaf-mould under the oak. An acorn is grown, not made, and small enough to roll in your palm — yet folded inside it is a whole tree and a hundred years. Natural and touchable, it keeps the south-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

White and smooth in a nest of straw,
I cradle a yolk of gold;
sit on me warm and a chick will hatch,
or eat me fried when cold.

East

Dried from the sea in shining grains,
sharp white upon the tongue;
I save the meat, I lift the broth,
through every dish I'm flung.

South

Small and round and red or black,
I ripen in the summer hedge;
the birds and children pick me sweet
from the bramble at the edge.

West

A ragged green beside the lane
that looks so mild and meek;
but touch me with your naked skin
and burn for half a week.
