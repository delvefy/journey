The Undoing

West of the bare dark and south of the aftermath, the body begins to come undone. A step east there was the bare warm fact of being, the floor of it, a body whole in the oldest dark; here that warmth has begun to leach away — not into a companion, as it does a step further east, but into the cold, the Waste reaching up through the floor of being to take the body back. This is the undoing: the least of existence, the bare body itself, slowly unmade, the warmth that was the whole proof of being drawn off into a cold that was here before the warmth and will be here after.

You feel it go the old way, the way the cooling dark taught long ago — fingers first, then wrists, the heat unwinding in a slow coil into the dark. But here there is no other to lose it to, no breath beside you; the warmth simply leaves, into the cold-after, and you are left more and more the bare fact of having been a body. You find the spiral in your skin and hold to it as the warmth goes, the one mark the undoing comes for last, the final warm thing the cold is reaching toward.

Your heart keeps the bare count still, but it is leaching too, each beat a little cooler than the last, the floor of being undoing itself beat by beat — the first clock not stopped, not spent, but quietly taken back into the cold it briefly held off.

To the north the undoing firms to the aftermath, the grief of what never was. To the east the body is whole again on the bare floor of being. To the south the undoing sinks below the floor, the body unmade past the after, toward the pole. To the west the warmth is wholly leached, the body gone to cold and Waste, the after absolute. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here the body comes undone alone in the oldest dark, the warmth leaching into the cold-after, and you hold the one warm coil to the last, the undoing reaching for it, the going leaching away with the heat.
