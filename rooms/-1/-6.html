Snail

You go nowhere in a hurry. House on your back, you pour yourself along on one soft silver foot, leaving a glistening trail to mark how far you have come. When the world turns dangerous - a poking finger, a drying sun - you simply pull everything inside and shut the door. You ask for nothing but a damp leaf and a little time. Slow as you are, you carry your whole home with you, and are never once lost.

North

I am a tiny crawling thing,
that marches in a line;
I carry crumbs ten times my size,
and build a hill so fine.

East

I am a hard black shiny rock,
dug up from deep in the ground;
thrown on the fire I burn so hot,
and warm the house around.

South

I have eight legs and spin a web,
of silk so fine and round;
I wait so still to catch the flies
that in my net are found.

West

I buzz from flower to flower all day,
and gather honey sweet;
I wear a coat of black and gold,
with a sting for those I meet.
