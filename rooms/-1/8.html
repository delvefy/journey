Sun

You are the great gold heart of the sky, the fire that everything turns toward and nothing can touch. You climb each morning, blazing, and the whole world wakes to do its work in your light; you sink each evening and let the cold come in. Plants drink you, the day is measured by you, and not a creature alive would last a week without your warmth. You are too bright to look at and too far to reach, yet you fall freely on every upturned face. The oldest lamp, the first clock, the one star we call our own.

North

The great black nothing far up high,
where moon and planet float;
beyond the air, beyond the sky,
no breath, no sound, remote.

East

The rough loud rumble of deep sleep,
that saws out of the nose;
it shakes the bed, you cannot keep
the dog asleep - it goes.

South

A traveller trailing a tail of light,
once in an age I show;
across the dark I streak so bright,
then for years away I go.

West

Red and orange, I leap and crack,
I eat up wood and coal;
I warm the cold, I light the black,
but burn the careless soul.
