Root

Down here nothing was made; everything grew. A root is the tree's dark half, the part that never sees the sun, drinking from the soil and gripping the stone so the storm cannot take what stands above. You can dig it up and hold its cold, wet length in your hands. It is patient, blind, and stubborn. Find the word each wall keeps buried, and the way down opens.

North

I run downhill but never tire,
I have a mouth and have a bed;
no legs, yet I outpace the deer,
and to the sea I'm always led.

East

Hard and cold, I never grew;
the mason stacks me in his wall;
the brook wears me to pebbles smooth,
and I'll not rot, nor bend, nor fall.

South

Smaller than the nail you bite,
I hold a tree inside my skin;
bury me with rain and light,
and the green of me will begin.

West

Dug wet and grey from the river bank,
the potter spins me on his wheel;
shaped to a pot and baked by fire,
I harden into something real.
