Pebble

You are a small smooth stone, rolled and rounded by the river or the patient sea until every edge is gone. You fit the palm exactly, cool and grey, the kind of thing a walker pockets without quite knowing why. Skip across the water and you dance four times before you sink; heap us up and we make the beach that sings beneath the wave. Plain past all telling, you have nothing to say and nowhere to be - yet a child will choose just one of you from a million, and carry it home like treasure.

North

Eight thin legs and a silken thread,
I weave a wheel to snare;
I wait so still on a corner's head,
for the fly to blunder there.

East

A red baked block of hardened clay,
laid row on row up high;
I build the wall, I build the way,
a house that scrapes the sky.

South

A great hard lump of solid stone,
too heavy to be thrown;
I sit unmoved, all grey and lone,
the mountain's stubborn bone.

West

A white cup floating on the pool,
a flat green pad below;
the frog sits on me, calm and cool,
where the slow waters flow.
