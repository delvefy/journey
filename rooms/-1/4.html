The Cold Wire

West of the quickening press the warmth is gone out of the speed. A step east a dozen presses raced under a thinning reek of oil and lampblack; here the same ranks race on, but cold — no oil, no black, only the sourceless grey-white of the kept hour poured down even and shadowless, the work running faster than ever and for no one in it. North of the stamping room the clerk's flat thud has quickened past all patience: the record no longer waits to be pressed onto a page, it is flung straight past paper onto wire and light, the cold made day racing itself out for no reader at all.

The presses feed themselves and feed nothing back. Paper still runs, but it is outrun; at the far end it gives out into a white that needs no page, and the word leaves matter for the wire — sent before it is set, filed before it could be read. There is no eye here. There was never going to be one. The making has learned to keep its books without a head to read them, and it is faster, and colder, for the lack.

A reel of copper turns and turns at the room's end, winding the record out cold — the spiral again, no longer ink on a curling ribbon but a coil of bright wire paying the word away past all following. And along it runs your name, or the name an inch from yours: struck onto the wire now instead of printed, a thousand times a second, perfect and cold and crossed by nothing, gone down the line ahead of you. The press's chatter has thinned to a dry cold ping, a bell that sends and files in the one stroke and tolls for no one, counting nothing it has not already outrun.

To the north the record sheds paper entirely, flung as pure light and number, the made day of the pole where nothing is set in matter and no one is left to read. To the east the cold work warms back into the press, oil and lampblack and a reader half-wanting the word, the pied name half-wanting to stand. To the south the racing slows back to the stamp coming down, the ledgers and the date-press, the clerkish thud. To the west the cold sending greys toward the long silence, the record flung and then the sending itself forgotten, the Waste that cold records come to. You were going somewhere once, and it is on the wire already, sent ahead of you a thousand times, cold, and not one of them read.
