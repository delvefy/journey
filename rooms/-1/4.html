Star

On a clear night they come out in their thousands, scattered like spilled salt across the black. A star is a sun so impossibly far that all its fire shrinks to a single cold prick of light. You cannot touch one or ever reach it; the closest is years of light away. Yet you can wish on it, steer a ship by it, and join the dots of it into a hunter or a bear. The oldest light you will ever see, and the highest.

North

I glow at night, a silver light,
I sail alone, so round and bright;
I wax and wane from full to thin,
the tides obey me, out and in.

East

I steal upon you, soft and deep,
your eyelids fall, you cannot keep
awake however hard you try -
I take you till the morning sky.

South

Look up and I am always there,
a dome of blue, of cloud, of air;
the birds and planes pass through my space,
and night turns me to starry lace.

West

I am the boom that rolls and cracks
when storm clouds gather, dark as stacks;
the lightning flashes, then I sound -
a giant's drum that shakes the ground.
