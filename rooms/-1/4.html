Yawn

Sleep has not come yet, but it is close, and your jaw swings open of its own accord. A yawn is the body's slow signal, drawn out and wide, that no hand makes and no fist can hold. It is wholly natural and wholly untouchable — weariness made into one long breath. It keeps the high, dim north. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

Not deep asleep, not quite awake,
I am the half-nap warm chairs make;
an afternoon, a droning room —
your eyes fall shut, you slip into me. Whom?

East

I come to you with shut eyes only,
and show you things that are not there;
I fade the instant you awake —
a tale spun out of sleeping air.

South

One long sad breath let slowly out,
when you are weary, sad, or fond;
I am no word, and yet I speak
of all the tiredness beyond.

West

I break in the east as the night grows thin,
the first grey light, the cocks that crow;
before the sun has climbed at all
I open the day. What am I, so?
