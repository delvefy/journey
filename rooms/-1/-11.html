Grape

You are a small round fruit that grows in heavy bunches on a climbing vine - green or deep purple, smooth and cool to the touch. No hand makes you; the sun swells you sweet on the warm hillside. You are eaten fresh by the handful, dried into raisins, or pressed and pressed until your juice becomes wine. You are summer's little globe of sweetness on the vine.

North

A little ball of milky white
that grows inside a shell;
the oyster makes it round and smooth -
a sea-gem, polished well.

East

Sunk in the ground and standing straight,
a pole of iron or wood;
the gate hangs from it, signs nailed on -
it holds just where it should.

South

A soft round fruit with fuzzy skin,
gold-pink and sweet to bite;
a big hard stone sits in the middle -
its juice runs down, a delight.

West

A cup of colour on a stem
that opens up in spring;
red and yellow, smooth and tall -
from a bulb in the ground it springs.
