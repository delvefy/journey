Wind

Nothing here will hold still for you. Wind is natural — no maker, no off-switch — and the least graspable thing there is: you cannot see it, cannot catch it, cannot keep it in a box for later. Yet it bends the oak, fills the sail, slams the loose door, and carries the smell of rain a county off. You know it only by what it moves. Speak the names, and let the way blow open.

North

I float across the sky all day
and cast a roving shade;
I have no weight you'd ever feel,
yet of me rain is made.

East

I live where halls are tall and bare
and caves run deep and long;
I cannot speak a word of mine,
I only steal your song.

South

I am born in the mountain snow
and grow as I run down;
I carry boats upon my back
and split the field and town.

West

I creep in low across the moor
and swallow hill and tree;
the lamp turns weak, the road turns blind,
yet hands pass clean through me.
