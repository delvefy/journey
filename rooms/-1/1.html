Wind

Nothing here can be held. The wind has no body and no maker; it is only the air, gone restless, pushing at the door and moaning in the chimney. You feel it on your face and never see it. It bends the trees, drives the cloud, and is gone before you can name where it went. Untouchable and unmade — yet it opens doors. Catch the one word waiting at each wall.

North

You cannot see me, yet on a cold day
you watch me cloud before your face;
in and out of every chest I go,
and the diver holds me in one place.

East

Call out once across the gorge
and hear yourself called back;
I keep no word, I make no word,
I only hand your own word back.

South

Between two banks I wind and flow,
fed by the rain and melting snow;
the salmon climbs me, the bridge spans me,
and to the sea at last I go.

West

A low grey cloud that walks the ground,
I drink the hills and hide the road;
the ships all sound their horns in me,
and the morning sun will lift my load.
