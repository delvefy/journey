The Sleightroom

Here the cold has been made on purpose. The room is iron-floored and grey-lit, the light flat and shadowless and answering to no flame, and every surface has been worked to a trueness the hand finds unpleasant, with no mark of the hand left anywhere in it. The metal taste you caught at the edge of the workroom is the whole of the air here now, exact and joyless, and the grey of the scoured place below has hardened into edges that could cut you.

On a bracket turns a mechanism: gears in a case, and at its heart a spring wound into a tight steel spiral, ticking that spiral one notch tighter, then easing, then tighter again. It is the spiral once more — but cut in metal and set to labour, stripped of every meaning except its turning. And it counts: a small hard tick, the bell remade as a machine, keeping perfect time and caring nothing for the hour it keeps.

There is no charm here, no glass that sings, no green. Whatever wonder the world once had has been filed off and swept away, and what remains runs very well and means nothing at all.

To the east lies the half-built wheel, where the metal was still warm with use. To the south, the ash and the scoured stone you climbed from. To the north the machine grows colder and surer, light without fire, a future that kept the engine and threw the rest away. Westward the iron rusts back down toward the long grey silence. You were going somewhere. The mechanism does not care, and ticks, and ticks.
