Moon

Out it comes when the sun is gone: a great pale coin pinned to the dark, the only light the night will give you for free. The moon keeps no light of its own - it borrows the sun's and hands it down, silvering the rooftops and laying a cold path across the water. It swells and shrinks through the month, full to a thin curved nail and back. It pulls the whole sea to and fro, and shows the earth always the same quiet face.

North

A giant ball that rounds the sun,
a world of rock or gas - I'm one;
Earth and Mars and Jupiter too,
eight of us, and one is you.

East

The sun has set, the world grows black,
the stars peek out, the owls come back;
a blanket dark from west to east,
when most of the daylight things have ceased.

South

A pinprick light so far away,
I twinkle out when gone the day;
you draw me with five points, and then
you wish upon me now and again.

West

A thin grey breath upon the field,
the far-off hills I softly shield;
I hang at dawn, low, damp, and pale,
then lift away and leave the vale.
