The Blind Day

West of the made day the light goes blind. The bright neutral stream a step east, pouring its light and number past without cruelty or care, here loses even its indifference and goes cold — a made day flung out for no eye at all, the dead pole's broadcast, signal stripped of its last sense and sent anyway, endlessly, into a brightness no one will ever stand in to receive. It is as bright as the made day, and as full, and as fast. It is only that there is no longer anyone the brightness is for, and the brightness has not noticed, and pours on.

Your name is in it, flung out with the rest. Where the waking day to the east turned its whole mind to you at your name, this flings your name out blind — broadcast, transmitted, sent in cold light to no receiver, a thousand bright copies a second hurled into the empty made day and lost: not filed now, not even returned undeliverable as the exchange below returned it, simply flung and gone, sent forever and received never. You reach into the blind stream and the cold light pours through you as through a window, not even registering the obstacle. Only the spiral in your skin stays unbroadcast, the one mark the dead day does not transmit, because it cannot read it.

The carrier tone has gone flat and clerkish, a broadcast hum laid even over everything, the bell flung out blind like the rest — ringing the hour into a brightness with no ear in it, a count sent and sent to no one, time transmitted forever into the dark beyond the made day and never once received.

To the north the blind day climbs to the dead pole, pure cold light and number with no reader and no world, the Chrome's bright-dead peak. To the east the light warms back to the neutral made day, the stream that at least pours without malice. To the south the broadcast firms back into the cold exchange and its tape, messages at least once meant to go somewhere. To the west the blind day bleaches to grey, the signal gone to the Waste, flung to no one and then forgotten. You were going somewhere, under a name. The blind day flings it out forever in cold light, broadcast to no one, sent and sent and never received — and you stand in the bright unseeing torrent, named endlessly and known by nothing at all.
