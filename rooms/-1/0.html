The Scoured Room

West is colder, and quieter, in a way that has nothing to do with the air. The room is the same grey stone as the first, but stripped: no rushlight, no torch, only a dim even greyness with no source you can name and no warmth in it at all. A fine ash lies over everything, soft as felt and undisturbed but for the prints your own feet are leaving now.

On the door behind you — the one you came through — the spiral is cut deep into the wood at eye height, and then dragged across, again and again, half scratched out. From the far side it was a wound; from here you can see the labour of the unmaking, how someone set themselves to erase it and stopped partway, as if losing faith even in their own refusal. Your fingers fit the gouges exactly. You do not let yourself think about why.

You wait for the bell — the counted hour you have carried in your skull since waking — and it does not come. Here nothing counts. Here, it seems, nothing answers at all, and the silence has the texture of something that was once a sound.

To the north the grey hardens, edged and exact, a cold that is made rather than felt. To the south the ash deepens toward a colder dark, cinder and bone. To the west, beyond the far door, there is only more of this: the long quiet where even the asking has stopped. Eastward waits the rushlit room you came from, warm and credulous. You were going somewhere. You begin to wonder if you were leaving, instead.
