Whiff

You are the faintest passing smell, here and gone before you are sure - a breath of cut grass, a thread of someone's perfume, the ghost of last night's smoke. No hand makes you; a stray current of air carries you. Catch me, and the memory comes flooding; turn your head, and I am gone.

North

The smell a thing gives off on the air,
the rose's, the bread's, the pine's;
the dog can follow it for miles -
a trail of tell-tale signs.

East

Two score, four tens, the winks of a nap,
the thieves in the old tale;
the days and nights the rain came down -
how many, on this scale?

South

A faint and trembling little light,
the candle nearly gone;
the far-off star, the dying coal -
a weak and wavering one.

West

The quick soft flap of little wings,
the flag up in the breeze;
the moth around the lantern's glow,
the trembling of the leaves.
