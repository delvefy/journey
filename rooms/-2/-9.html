Rose

You are the flower everyone knows, red or white or yellow, opening petal over petal on a thorned green stalk. No hand made you - you grow from a root in the dark earth, you drink the rain, you turn your face to follow the sun. Bees climb into your heart for gold. Cut and given, you say what words cannot; left on the bush, you blow, and your petals fall one by one to the ground.

North

A tall white bloom with a trumpet's mouth,
I nod on a slender stalk;
at Easter held in folded hands,
and I am white as chalk.

East

Hard and grey, I never grow,
I sit upon the hill;
wind and rain may wear me slow,
but I am stubborn still.

South

A little flower in the grass,
white petals round a sun of gold;
children thread me in a chain
and count their love, I'm told.

West

Pink and round, I roll in mud,
with a curly little tail;
I grunt and snort inside my sty
and snuffle at the pail.
