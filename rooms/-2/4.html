Dawn

The first pale light in the east, before the sun has truly risen. Dawn is the hinge of the day, grey going to gold, the cocks crowing and the world waking. It is wholly natural and wholly untouchable — a moment, not a thing. It keeps the airy north-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

The sun climbs up to its highest seat,
no shadow stands at your two feet;
the morning's done, the church-bells chime —
I am the middle hour, the time.

East

When you are tired your jaw swings wide,
a long slow breath you can't hold in;
it spreads from one to all the room —
that sleepy gape where naps begin.

South

Each blade of grass at break of day
wears beads of water, cold and clear;
no rain has fallen in the night —
the morning's breath has left me here.

West

A brief bright flash on glass or steel,
a glint of light, a passing shine;
it winks at you and then is gone —
a thread of brightness, quick and fine.
