The Cold Count

North of the even light the works shed even their iron. There is no engine here, no wheel, no moving part to rust — only light, and number, and the cold making them do the work alone. The room is a made day at its highest and coldest: a vast sourceless white in which figures run, columns and lattices of pale cold number reckoning everything there is, counting and sorting and recording with nothing that turns or strikes or breathes. This is the count the heartbeat became — the bell and the clock and the drive-beat refined past all of them into pure reckoning — and it has forgotten, utterly, that a count was ever kept by anyone, for anyone, or meant a single thing.

Your name is in it. Not spoken, not printed, not shown — tallied: somewhere in the running figures it has been reduced to a number and sorted and filed among all the rest, exact and cold and true, the warmth and the wanting reckoned out of it. You find it, or a column you are sure is it, and it is bleaching even as you watch — the figures yellowing, dropping a digit, the record cracking toward the grey silence the way the exchange's records crack a step east. The cold count is so high and so far gone that it has begun to lose the very tally it perfected. Only the spiral holds: in the lattice of cold number a single coil keeps turning, recursive, folded on itself, the one mark that still means the old thing — and it is whorled, you find, exactly like the one in your skin.

There is no sound but the cold count itself, which makes none — and that is the strangest hour of all, a counting with no tick, time reckoned perfectly and never struck, the bell refined out of existence, the silence of a clock that has become pure number and no longer needs to ring.

To the north the count refines to the absolute pole, mind without flesh, pure number with no world left to reckon, the Chrome's cold post-human peak. To the east the figures gather back into messages and tape, the cold exchange that at least still sends. To the south the count takes on engines again, the even light and its minding machines, iron that can still rust. To the west the figures bleach wholly to grey, the cold count forgetting itself, the records gone to the Waste, reckoning gone to dust. You were going somewhere, and it has been counted, and sorted, and filed as a number, and is bleaching now toward grey — reckoned to the last digit and never once, in all the cold count, read as a name.
