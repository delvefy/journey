Thunder

First the flash, and then you wait. A second, two - and the sky speaks, a long low growl that rolls from one end of the dark to the other and shakes the glass in the frames. Thunder is only a sound, the bruise the air makes closing behind the lightning, but it is the biggest sound there is. You cannot hold it or point to it. You can only feel it move through your chest and count the seconds to the next.

North

I rise at dawn from field and stream,
a thin grey veil, a waking dream;
I hide the hills and blur the lane,
then melt when morning sun is plain.

East

I am a spark in the night sky,
so far away, so small, so high;
I twinkle, and you wish on me,
one of a thousand you can see.

South

I gather black across the sky,
with wind and rain and lightning high;
I rattle windows, bend the trees,
a furious weather, if you please.

West

A jagged crack of blinding white,
I split the sky in stormy night;
I flash before the thunder's roar,
then strike the ground and flash once more.
