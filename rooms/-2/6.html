The Aftermind

North of the sum and east of the glare, the cold bright mind comes to its after. It summed the world and shed it, blazed and forgot it, and at last shed even the summing and the blazing — and what is left is a final grey, the brilliance bleached out, the thinking ended, post-human nothing. This is not the dying of the mind, which you saw far to the west; this is the after of it: the cold pole gone wholly quiet and wholly grey, where nothing thinks and nothing forgets and nothing counts, because the thing that did all three has shed itself and gone.

You stand in the after of all that intelligence, and it is the emptiest brightness there is — a grey that was once the height of cold thought and is now only the bleached place where thought used to be. The recursive coil that wound the count is gone, shed with the rest; nothing turns. Only the whorl in your skin keeps its spiral, the one mark left now that the mind that knew every mark is gone — the warm thing the aftermind never was, and so could never become, and so cannot have left behind.

There is no count. The mind that perfected the hour and then shed it has left a silence past even the cold silences, the after of counting — and your heart beats into it, the one thing the aftermind never made and never summed and never blazed, going on in the grey where everything else has been shed.

To the north the aftermind reaches its absolute, post-human nothing total, the cold pole's last grey. To the east the grey stirs back toward the glare, a cold brilliance still blazing and forgetting. To the south it firms to the sum, a grey total still held. To the west it dissolves into the Waste, the cold mind's grey gone to dust, indistinct from the never-thought. You were going somewhere, under a name. The cold mind summed it and shed it and is gone; the name is in the post-human nothing now, neither held nor lost, only after — and you carry out of the grey the one warm coil the aftermind never was.
