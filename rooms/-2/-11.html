Tulip

You are a smooth bright cup of colour on a tall green stem - red, yellow, pink, or striped. No hand makes you; you sleep all winter as a fat brown bulb underground, then push up in spring and open to the sun. By the thousand you turn whole fields to colour. You close at night and open by day, nodding in the breeze.

North

A little flower in the grass,
white petals round a sun of gold;
you thread a chain of them for crowns -
the meadow's coin, I'm told.

East

Small and round, in heavy bunches,
green or purple on the vine;
eat them sweet or squash for juice -
or wait, and they make wine.

South

A bright red flower, papery-thin,
a black heart at its core;
it nods in fields of golden wheat
and scatters seed galore.

West

A big strong beast with a flowing mane,
four hooves that pound the ground;
you climb on its back and ride for miles -
it gallops with a neighing sound.
