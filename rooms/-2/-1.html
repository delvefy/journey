The Fallen Hearth

The dust thickens underfoot and stops pretending to be a floor. Somewhere above, things were kept; here they have fallen. A hearth has collapsed into the centre of the room, its stones tumbled and cold, the hollow where a fire once sat packed now with grey drift — not the cinder of any recent burning but dust so old it has forgotten it was ever anything, sifting down still from a roof that is half sky-coloured rock and half simply gone.

You crouch at the dead hearth and turn the drift with a hand. A few things surface and sink again: a sliver of black wax, a pinch of something that might once have been salt, a shard of fired clay no bigger than a nail — the last belongings of whoever kept the fire, ground almost back to ground. You draw the spiral in the dust out of the habit that will not leave you, and a slow cold draught, coming from the west like a long exhalation, smooths it flat before you finish.

No bell, no drip, no heartbeat, no tick. Only that draught, in and out, too slow to be breathing, the long after emptying the room into the dark a grain at a time.

To the north the dust pales back toward the kept room and its lapsed, lingering charm. To the east the drift warms by nothing at all into the cinder hollow, the dead end of two endings. To the south the ruin sinks past keeping into raw cold ground, the body and the older dark. To the west the draught comes from, and there is only the Waste it comes from: ash without ruin, because nothing was ever there to ruin. You were going somewhere. The draught has been going the other way far longer.
