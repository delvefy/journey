The Silt

The cold has finished here. A step to the east your warmth was still leaving you, fingers then wrists, the breath unwinding in a slow coil; here there is nothing left to take and nothing waiting to take it. The dark has cooled the whole way down to grey — the same weightless drift that banks in the unmade room above you, only older, the layer that drift comes to rest as when even the resting is long over. It is not ash; nothing burned. It is only what remains when the cold has had everything: a fine pale silt, floorless, that takes the weight of your knee without a sound and gives nothing back.

You draw the spiral in it from the old habit. There is no draught here to smooth the line — the silt simply forgets it, the walls of the groove sliding shut of their own slow weight, the coil filling from beneath until the grey is unbroken again, as if your hand had never moved. Not erased. Unwitnessed. The mark needs something to hold it, and here there is nothing that holds.

No count reaches this deep. Not the bell, not the drip, not the spent heart you felt cooling a step to the east. Only the faint sift of grey settling on grey, far below hearing, a sound you feel in the teeth more than catch in the ear.

To the north the silt firms back toward the unmade drift, ruin that at least once held the shape of a hearth. To the east the grey warms by a hair into the cooling dark, the last body that ever lay here giving up the last of itself. To the south the silt deepens past any floor into older dark, the deep that no warmth ever entered to begin cooling. To the west it pales out wholly into the open Waste, ash without even a body to have made it, a wind with nothing to carry. You were going somewhere, once. Here the going sifts down into the grey and is not even forgotten, for there was nothing to remember it.
