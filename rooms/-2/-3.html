Thorn

A sharp dark point juts from the briar-stem and catches at your sleeve. A thorn is grown, not made — the plant's own needle, woody and hard, daring you to grasp the rose. Natural and altogether touchable, as your pricked finger will tell, it keeps the south-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A thousand thousand tiny grains
the sea has ground from stone;
I slip and pour between your toes
on the beach where I am strown.

East

Smooth and white in a straw-lined nest,
I hide a yolk of gold;
keep me warm and a chick breaks out,
or fry me up when cold.

South

Lean your bare leg in the ditch
against my ragged green
and a hundred tiny fires will sting —
the meanest weed you've seen.

West

In the deep wood's shade I unroll
my feathers, frond by frond;
I grow no flower and drop no seed
by the damp and mossy pond.
