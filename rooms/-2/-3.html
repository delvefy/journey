The Long Before

Down here even the drift has stopped falling. A step north the grey was still sifting, still settling out of nothing; here it has finished. It has come all the way down and lain still, so fine now that it is less a floor than the memory of one — it gives under you without resistance and without bottom, the way water would if water had forgotten how to be wet.

You kneel, out of the old habit, and draw the spiral. There is no draught here to smooth it; the air is shut and finished. And still the mark will not hold: the grey is too fine to take a line, each grain sliding into the trough of your finger before you have lifted it, so the coil fills and closes of its own accord with nothing to blame for the losing. Even the erasing has given up its labour. The mark is not refused here. It simply never was.

A step east a heart was spending its last warmth into the cold, a breath dispersing; you can almost feel the place of it still, the way you feel a fire gone out in a room you have only now entered. Here there is no heat to lose, and so nothing leaches — no breath, no drip, no bell. Only your own pulse, small and surprised at itself, ticking into a dark that has never once heard a pulse and does not know to keep the count.

To the north the grey climbs back toward where it was at least still falling, ruin enough to settle into the shape of a body. To the east the dark warms by the faintest hair toward the breath that emptied there — a warmth this room has never had to mourn, because it never held one. To the south the grey goes down past even this, into the deep before anyone: before there was a someone to lie in any hollow, before the first lying-down. To the west it opens flat and pales out of even greyness — the Waste proper, ash without a fire and now without even the ash. You were going somewhere once, and were not alone in the going. Here the going has no floor to walk and no one left to walk beside; it only settles, the way you are settling, into the long before.
