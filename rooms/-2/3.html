The Unwatched Works

North of the single seized forge the machines multiply. One engine becomes a hall of them, rank on rank under the same cold sourceless white, and not one was built to be worked — they were built to work each other. A wheel here turns a belt there, which lifts a cam, which trips a wheel further down the line, the whole hall minding itself in a closed round that needs no hand and no fire and admits no wonder. Nothing is being made. The making has become the only thing made.

It is running, but it is also going. East of here the presses still stamped crisp and white; here the white has begun to yellow at the edges, the oil filming over, a belt frayed to a soft fringe and somehow still turning. The records the hall keeps of itself crack in their racks, paper gone the colour of old teeth, entered and filed and read by no one and now not even worth the filing. The wonder was banished long ago. Lately even the order it was banished for has started, very quietly, to let go.

A single wire feeds out of one engine and into the next in a slack coil on the floor — the spiral, drawn now in copper, carrying the hall's one thought round and round from machine to machine and never out to anyone. The beat of it is even and soft and total: ten thousand small taps fallen into one flat pulse, a clock the size of a room keeping time for an empty room. You wait for it to skip. It does not skip. It only thins, here and there, where a tooth has worn to nothing and the round closes over the gap and goes on.

To the north the works thin to wire and then to light, the engines shedding even their iron, thinking without a head under the cold made day of the pole. To the east the hall warms back toward the stamping room, paper and a printed name still crisp enough to almost read. To the south it narrows to the one seized forge, a single engine stopped where these still turn. To the west the belts crack and the copper greens and the whole closed round forgets it was ever turning, works gone to grit, the grey Waste that machinery comes to. You were going somewhere once, and somewhere in this hall a counter has been adding you up the while. It reads a number you cannot make sense of, and there is no one here to read it to.
