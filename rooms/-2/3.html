The Even Light

The works do not need you, and they do not pause for your not being needed. North of the seized forge the iron has been set running again — but by nothing, for no one. A long hall of machines stands minding machines: an arm that oils a wheel that drives a belt that turns the arm, a closed round of labour with no labour in it, every part attending every other and none of it attending you. The light gives it away. It falls dead even from no lamp, sourceless and shadowless and cold — the too-even light you once caught as a hairline under a far door, grown here into a whole flat day with the sun cut out of it.

Nothing in the round was ever asked of, or prayed to, or thanked. On the west wall the round has begun to fail: a bank of engines has run itself down to rust, its belt slack, its turning given over to the grey. A brass governor still spins above the dead works, its two weights flung out in a bright spiral, smoothing the speed of a thing that no longer moves — the coil kept perfectly, governing nothing. You set a thumb to it and it does not slow.

A pulse drives all of it, flat and even as the stamp thudding a room to the east, a single tick multiplied down a thousand shafts — the bell become a drive-beat, kept by no one, kept for no one.

To the north the works shed even their iron, the labour done by light and number with no moving part left to rust, the cold made day of the pole. To the east the engines gather into presses and the printed name, the record warming by a hair toward a word. To the south the round seizes and cools into the forge below. To the west the drive runs down to ore and the even day gutters toward grey, the Waste the machines come to. You were going somewhere, on some engine's account. A maker's plate is bolted to the near wall, a name stamped into it and polished blank by all the years no eye has read it.
