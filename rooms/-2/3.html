Storm

The sky goes green-grey and the air holds its breath, and then it breaks. A storm is not one thing but a gathering fury - wind that bends the trees, rain flung sideways, the white crack of lightning and the long roll after. You cannot touch it or hold it; it is weather at its loudest, a mood of the whole sky. You watch from the window as it throws itself at the world, and you feel small, and oddly glad of the glass.

North

I am the rumble in the sky,
the great loud growl from clouds on high;
I follow when the lightning's gone,
I shake the windows, deep and long.

East

I am the great blue overhead,
where sun and moon and clouds are spread;
I turn to black with stars at night,
the widest roof, and full of light.

South

Drip and drop, I wet the street,
I soak your hair and chill your feet;
I leave the pavements shining bright,
and bring a rainbow after light.

West

From a black cloud I come down hard,
like pebbles thrown across the yard;
not soft like snow but cold and round,
I bounce and rattle on the ground.
