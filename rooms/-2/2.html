Rain

The cloud at last lets go, and the world is given a drink. Rain is natural — wrung from the sky by no hand — and only half a thing you can hold: you feel it on your face, watch it bead the glass, yet try to grasp it and it runs away between your fingers. It greens the field, swells the brook, drums the roof through the night, and leaves the morning washed and smelling new. Speak the names, and let the wet way open.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

I bring the wind, I bring the rain,
the thunder and the flash;
the sky turns black, the windows shake,
the great oak hears my crash.

East

I drift in white across the blue
and change from whale to hill;
I hide the sun, I bring the rain,
I cast a shadow chill.

South

I am a cloud that fell to ground,
I creep and cannot stay;
I hush the hills, I dim the lamps,
till sunshine burns away.

West

In silent flakes I drift from sky
and cloak the world in white;
roll me into a giant man,
or sled me with delight.
