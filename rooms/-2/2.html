Frost

Overnight the frost has signed the window in white ferns, and the grass stands stiff and silvered. It is water again, kin to the fog and the dew, but caught and crystalled by the cold — a feathery rime you can see and almost touch, though it melts to nothing at a single breath. Natural, and all but ungraspable, it keeps the high north-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

I bead the grass at break of day,
a thousand tiny eyes;
the morning sun comes up and drinks
me till the meadow dries.

East

Out and in, the whole life long,
no living thing can hold me;
warm upon the lip I rise,
then the cold air folds me.

South

I crawl in low along the ground
and swallow hill and town;
the lighthouse moans, the ships creep slow
when my grey blanket's down.

West

I drift across the summer sky
in shapes of sheep and whale;
I darken, and the rain comes down,
I scatter in a gale.
