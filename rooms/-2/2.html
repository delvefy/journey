The Cold Forge

The grey hardens and squares itself, just as the door below promised, and becomes iron. This was a forge once, or a workshop with a forge in it: a squat brick furnace, a pitted anvil, a frame of cold machinery braced in the corner — all of it built, all of it square, none of it warmed for a very long time. The believing cold of the lapsed charm beneath has set here into something colder still, a cold that never believed at all, only measured and hammered and made.

Where the white room to the east keeps its hairspring breathing flawless and exact, the spring in this machine has rusted mid-coil and stopped — the spiral seized in iron, orange-scaled, holding its turn forever and never closing it. You set a thumb to it and rust comes away like pollen, and the wheel does not move. Everything built to run here has run down; the perfection cracked, as the door said it would, and bled into a long grey quiet. There is nothing to ask of it. There was never anything here to ask.

No bell, no even beat, no remembered ringing even — only, now and then, a single tick somewhere in the cold iron as it surrenders the last of a warmth it no longer recalls having: the sound of a made thing settling, counting nothing, expecting no one to keep the count.

To the east the iron gives way to the seamless white room and its flawless clock, the same cold but still running, exact and soulless and whole. To the south the forge softens back toward a hearth and a lapsed charm, a place that at least once believed. To the north the works multiply and need no hand and no fire, engines tending engines under a dead even light, the wonder banished for good. To the west the rust eats the last of the made thing and the grey opens out, machinery gone to ore again, a silence even the ticking leaves. You were going somewhere, by some made hour. The seized spring holds its half-turn and will not tell you how long you have stood here.
