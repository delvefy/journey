Sand

Sand pours through your fingers and will not be held in a fist for long — a thousand thousand grains, each one a worn-down crumb of older stone. No hand made it; the sea and the wind did, grinding the world fine over an age. Touchable by the heap, natural to the last grain, it lies in the far south-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

Wet and grey, I take a thumbprint,
soft until the kiln;
the potter spins me on his wheel
and bakes me to his will.

East

Small as a freckle, dry and still,
I hold a forest curled;
bury me and give me rain,
I push up through the world.

South

I guard the rose with a wicked point,
I snag the passing sleeve;
small and sharp upon the stem,
I draw the blood you grieve.

West

Soft green velvet on a stone,
I love the shade and wet;
no flower, no branch, no leaf to lose,
the softest cushion yet.
