The Unmade

Even the ruin gives out here. Behind you a hearth had at least fallen, and east of that a bone had at least lain; here there is neither hearth nor bone, because nothing was ever built to fall and nothing ever lived to leave a bone. The room is only drift — a grey, fine, weightless dust, paler than ash because no fire made it, banked in the corners and sifting through the still air, the leavings of nothing, settling out of nothing, with no end you can imagine to it.

Low in the drift lies a shallow hollow the length of a body, pressed there so long ago that the dust has nearly closed it over — the shape of someone who lay down and did not rise, or only the trick your eye plays, hunting for a shape in the featureless grey. You kneel beside it out of the old habit and draw the spiral with one finger. A slow cold draught comes from the west, steady as a long exhalation, and smooths the line flat as fast as you make it; the grit slumps softly back; the coil closes on nothing and is gone. The mark will not hold here. Nothing holds here.

There is no count. Not the bell, not the drip, not the heart you felt beating in the warm dark elsewhere. Only the draught, in and out, far too slow to be breathing, and your own pulse small inside it — and you have the sense, kneeling, that the silence is patient enough to outlast even that.

To the north the drift pales back toward the fallen hearth, ruin that at least remembers being kept, a cold that once held a fire. To the east the grit warms by a hair toward the bone in its hollow, the dark there just beginning, faintly, to listen. To the west the draught comes from, and gives onto more of the same and less: the open Waste, ash without even a ruin, a wind with nothing left to carry. To the south the drift deepens past the body's shape into the older dark, the deep before there was anyone to lie down in it. You were going somewhere, once. Here the going lies down in the grey and is smoothed over, like the spiral, like the shape in the dust.
