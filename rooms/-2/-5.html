Clay

Dig down past the loose topsoil and you may strike it: a dense, smooth, faintly cold earth that holds together in the hand. Clay is the most willing of materials - squeeze it and it keeps the print of every finger, roll it and it becomes a snake, pinch it and it becomes a bowl. The potter spins it into jugs and cups; the kiln then bakes it stone-hard for a thousand years. Soft enough for a child, lasting enough for the oldest pots we have ever found.

North

Rain and dirt together meet,
to make a brown and squelchy treat;
my puddles splash, my goo will cling,
to wellies, paws, and everything.

East

A speck with six small legs that runs,
and lugs a crumb that weighs a ton;
in long straight lines we troop and toil,
our city hidden in the soil.

South

I buzz from bloom to bloom all day,
and gather golden dust away;
I make sweet honey in my hive,
but sting you if you swat my drive.

West

Small and round and full of juice,
red or black for jam or use;
I hang in clusters, ripe and sweet,
a summer hedgerow's tasty treat.
