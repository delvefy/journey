The Lees

South of the bottomless, the falling finds its floor — and the floor is made of nothing. A step north the grey sifted down forever with no bottom to reach; here, at last, is the bottom, but it is not ground: it is the lees, the settled residue of all that endless drift, the sediment the Waste leaves when even the falling has finished. East of here a deep grieved for what never was; west, the open void; and here, between, all of it has come to rest, settled out into a floor of pure absence, the dregs of a world that was never poured.

You stand on it, or in it, the settled grey closing soft and final around your feet, and you understand this is where everything would have come to rest, had there been anything: the lost, the spent, the unanswered, the never-said — all of it would have settled here, and none of it did, and the floor is made of that, the sediment of things that never happened, packed deep and still. You draw the spiral and it sinks into the lees and is gone, not blown, not smoothed, but absorbed, taken down into the settled grey the way a stone is taken by still water. Only the whorl in your skin stays unsettled, the one thing in all the deep that has not yet come to rest.

The spent count of the aftermath has settled too, here, to almost nothing — your heart's toll grown so faint and so final that it is less a beat than the last grain of one, settling, the very dregs of the count coming to rest in a deep that will keep it the way it keeps everything: without knowing, without end.

To the north the lees lift back into the falling grey, the bottomless drift with no floor. To the east the residue stirs back into the grieving deep, the aftermath that mourns what never was. To the south the lees sink toward the pole, below even sediment, the absolute floor of the Waste-deep. To the west the settled grey widens into the open Waste, the void where even the residue is gone. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here is where it would have settled, with all the rest of what never was — and you stand in the lees of an unpoured world, your heart's last grain settling with it, the one unsettled thing the whorl in your own skin.
