Thistle

A tall weed armed with spines, crowned with a tuft of purple, that no bare hand will grip. The thistle is wholly natural and wholly touchable — Scotland's flower, all prickle and down, seeding the wind with silver. It keeps the rooted south-west, among weeds and wild things. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A green weed armed with stinging hairs,
brush me bare and your skin will burn;
boil me young, I make a soup —
name the smarting weed, in turn.

East

A small round fruit of the bush and hedge,
red or black or blue and sweet;
soft enough to stain your hand —
name the juicy summer treat.

South

A thorny tangle in the hedge,
that snags your sleeve and tears your skin;
yet come September, fruit hangs black —
name the prickly bush. Reach in.

West

A round green stalk by pond and bog,
once peeled and dipped to make a light;
woven into seats and mats —
name the marsh-stem, slim and bright.
