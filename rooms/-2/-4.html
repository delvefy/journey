Mud

Where the path dips and the rain has nowhere to go, the ground gives up being ground and becomes mud. You feel it grab at your boots, soft and brown and unwilling to let go. It is earth and water with the hard parts taken out, the most touchable thing in the wood and the messiest. Pigs love it. Children love it. Step in, and the world keeps a print of your foot.

North

I once stood tall with leaves on top,
then came the axe and made me drop;
now thick and round across the floor,
I'm bench and firewood - nothing more.

East

In a coat of velvet black,
with two great spades upon my back;
I push up hills across the lawn,
and tunnel on from dusk to dawn.

South

Dug from the ground, all soft and grey,
the potter spins me on a tray;
I take the shape of pot or cup,
then bake to stone when the kiln heats up.

West

I am a nut with a little cap,
I drop from oaks into your lap;
a squirrel hides me in the ground,
and one day a great tree is found.
