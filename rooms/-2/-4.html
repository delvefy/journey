Nettle

A weed with soft-looking leaves that bite. Brush the nettle and it stings; its fine hairs leave a rash that itches for an hour. It grows wild in the ditch and the wasteground, wholly natural and wholly there to be touched — though you'd rather not. It keeps the green south-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

I grow on the briar and the rose,
a needle-point on every stem;
reach for the bloom and I draw blood —
sharp little guard. Beware of them.

East

A little nut in a tidy cup,
I drop from the oak in the autumn chill;
the squirrel hides me, the pig roots up —
a mighty tree sleeps in me still.

South

A prickly weed with a purple crown,
all spines and spikes from root to head;
the badge of Scotland, fierce to grasp —
my fluff blows off when my flower is dead.

West

By the lake I stand in a whispering crowd,
tall, thin, and hollow, swaying slow;
the ducks all nest among my stems —
cut me, and a pipe I'll blow.
