The Unbegun Dark

South of the long before and west of the emptying dark, the deep goes down past even being a deep. A step north the grey still lay like the memory of a floor, fine enough to kneel on; a step east the cold had at least an emptying to do, a someone it had let go before it began. Here is neither. This is before the first lying-down — before there was a someone to fold into any hollow, before the dark had been entered even once — and the cold has paled, as it goes, the rest of the way toward the Waste: ash without a fire, then without the ash, a grey so emptied it has forgotten it was ever grey.

You kneel, out of the habit your hands keep when nothing else remains, and reach to draw the spiral. There is nothing to draw it in. No grit, no drift, not even the fine settled memory of a floor — your finger moves through a dark that takes no line because it has no surface, and the coil closes on nothing, in nothing, leaving not even the absence of a mark. The charm does not fail here. It was never a thing that could be tried. This is a silence that never had a warmth or a word to lose, and so has lost nothing, and so is whole in a way that has nothing to do with you.

You listen for your own pulse and are not sure you find it; so little keeps time here that the small tick of you seems a thing borrowed from somewhere with edges. The unwinding coil of the emptying dark has finished — not loose now, simply gone, a spiral with neither turn nor centre nor the ghost of one. North the grey firms back toward the memory of a floor, ruin enough to kneel in. East the void warms by a hair toward the dark that at least had a someone to let go. South the deep goes down before even itself, before there was a deep to be empty. West it opens flat and pale, the Waste proper, where nothing was lost because nothing was ever had. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here there is no here, and no one to have been going, and the name was never set down for the dark to forget.
