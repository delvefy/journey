The Bottomless

The silt gives up its floor. A step north there was at least a place to kneel, a near-solid grey that took the weight of a knee; here the grey goes down and keeps going, fine and weightless and endless, past any floor your foot hunts for — a drift with no bottom, and nothing at its bottom, because there is no bottom to have anything. You do not stand so much as hang in it, the cold leavings sifting slowly past you, down toward a deep that no warmth ever entered, that never cooled because nothing was ever warm here to cool.

It is the older dark the silt promised below itself, and the open Waste the ash gave onto, met and become one bottomless grey. You draw the spiral and your hand passes through drift that will not hold a line because it will not hold anything — the coil unmade in the sinking before it is even closed, the grey folding over your fingers the way it folds over everything, without resistance, without record. Only the whorl in your own skin keeps the mark now, and even that feels as though it is falling slowly away from you, down with the rest.

There is no count. Your heart beats, faint, and the grey does not catch it or echo it; each beat is a small warm thing dropped into a deep that swallows it without a sound, and there is no second time anywhere beneath — no root-pulse, no herd, no other — only your own, falling, and the slow endless sift of grey past grey.

To the north the drift firms to a near-floor, the silt that at least lets you kneel, the cold just finished. To the east the grey gathers into ash and given-up bone, the cold aftermath of bodies, leavings that were once at least something. To the south it deepens past even the older dark, the deep before the silt had anything to be the leavings of. To the west it opens wholly into the open Waste, ash to no horizon, the void where not even the grey settles. You were going somewhere, once. Here there is no floor to go on, only the slow falling of grey, and you in it, sinking at the pace of your own faint heart toward a deep that has never once held the bottom of anyone.
