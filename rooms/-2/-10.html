Daisy

You are the small white flower of the lawn, a ring of slim petals round a sunny yellow eye, low to the grass where the mower misses you. No hand made you - you open with the morning sun and shut again at dusk, scattered by the thousand across the field. Children pluck you, split your stem, and thread you into chains and crowns; they pull your petals one by one to ask if they are loved. Humble, cheerful, and everywhere, you are the meadow's own modest little star.

North

The queen of flowers, red and sweet,
upon a thorny stem;
picked for love on a special day,
a fragrant little gem.

East

A small round milky bead of white
grown deep in an oyster's keep;
no jeweller made me - I was formed
in the dark sea, soft and deep.

South

A cup-shaped flower on a stem,
of red or yellow, bright;
it springs up from a bulb in spring
and opens to the light.

West

A hill-climbing, bearded, horned beast
that eats most anything;
it butts the gate, it chews your coat,
and bleats in early spring.
