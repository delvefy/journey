Fire

You are the oldest companion and the oldest danger, the leaping red-gold creature that lives on wood and breathes out heat and light. Tamed in the hearth, you cook the meal and warm the winter night and gather the family close to your glow. Loosed in the forest or the roof, you are ruin, racing faster than a horse can run. You eat everything and leave grey ash; you give everything and ask only fuel and air. People first became people, the old tales say, the night they learned to keep you.

North

A sudden burst of blinding light,
gone quick as it appears;
the camera blinks, the sky goes white,
when the lightning nears.

East

The great gold ball that rules the day,
too bright to look upon;
it climbs, it warms, it slips away,
and leaves the dark when gone.

South

You cannot see me, cannot hold,
but feel me by the flame;
I melt the ice, I beat the cold,
in summer thick I came.

West

The far faint shine of the night-time sky,
from specks of distant white;
'star bright' you wish on me up high,
the palest, oldest light.
