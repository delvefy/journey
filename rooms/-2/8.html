The Husk

West of the blind brilliance and north of the running down, the machine future is wholly spent. A step east a cold brilliance built itself forever, blazing and blind; a step south the works and the mind wound down together toward their stop. Here they reach the cold pole and stop entire: the brilliance gone out, the works seized, the cold mind spent to a post-human nothing total — and what is left is a husk, the empty hollow shell of the machine future, nothing alive in it, nothing left inside, already rusting and dissolving westward toward the Waste. The dead future spent to its core, a cold husk forgetting it was ever a world.

It is the hollow aftermath of all that brilliance: a vast cold husk, the dead-bright gone dark, the endless self-building stopped mid-course and emptied, rust creeping over a structure with nothing left to hold up. You draw the spiral and find it stamped in a hollow plate, a maker's seal in an empty shell, flaking toward the Waste. Only the whorl in your skin keeps a warm and filled coil, the one thing not hollow, the single living mark in the spent husk of the cold future.

There is no count; the dead-bright clatter has spent itself to silence, the works stopped, the bell hollow and still. Your heart beats in the husk, the one warm filled stroke in an emptied shell, keeping a living time where the machine future has spent its last and gone hollow and cold.

To the north the husk reaches the cold pole, the machine-future wholly spent, the post-human nothing total. To the east the blind brilliance, the works still blazing and building. To the south the running-down, the works and mind still winding to their stop. To the west the husk rusts wholly into the Waste, the made forgotten, the corner. You were going somewhere, under a name, toward something built. Here the building is a spent hollow husk, your name a seal in an empty shell flaking toward the Waste — and still the warm coil turns, filled and living, and still one beat keeps on in the husk of the cold future, going to the grey.
