Sea

The river was leading here all along. The sea is natural beyond arguing — no hand could pour it, no box could hold it — and it lies on the axis between what you can touch and what you cannot: salt water you can wade into, and a horizon you never reach. It takes every river and asks for more. It is older than the shore it beats. Speak the names, and the waters will part a way.

North

I am a cloud that lost its sky
and settled on the bay;
the ships all sound their horns and creep
when I have come to stay.

East

I begin as a trickle high
and gather all the rain;
I wind between the hills and fields
to feed the sea again.

South

I bud in spring, all soft and green,
I shade you in the heat;
I blaze to red when autumn comes
and crackle round your feet.

West

I rise and curl and rush the shore
and fall in foam and spray;
I never rest, I come in rows,
the wind's own child at play.
