The Witch's Leavings

The quiet goes on, and thickens. This room was kept once — you can read it in the bones of the place: a low cold hearth, a shelf, hooks set in the beam where bundles hung. But the keeping has stopped. Sprigs of herb still dangle from the hooks, gone to grey dust that sifts down when you pass, and the air that ought to be sweet with them holds only the cold mineral nothing you have breathed since the ash began.

On the floor a spiral was laid once in white salt, the twin of some brighter one you feel you have stood inside. Here it is trodden and drifted to the faintest grey ring, a charm no one has renewed. A black candle stands at its centre, never lit, its wick stiff and clean. You hold a palm above it as you might have once, waiting for the small leaning-close, the ear in the quiet. Nothing leans. The asking has gone out of you here, and you feel its absence like a missing tooth.

No bell, no tone, no animal throat — only, very far off, the memory of a sound, the way a stripped tower keeps the shape of its ringing.

To the east the leavings warm a degree toward the rushlit room and its credulous flame. To the north the grey hardens and squares itself, a cold that never believed at all. To the south the dust darkens toward cinder and bone, the body and the older dark. To the west the last of the keeping falls away — no leavings there, only the long quiet that was once a sound, with nothing left in it to answer. You were going somewhere, under some name. The dead wick will not take a light you do not strike.
