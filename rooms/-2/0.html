Sea

No hand made this, and no foot will cross it. The sea is the great unmade water, salt to its depths, swallowing every river and never growing full. It heaves and shines and answers only to the moon. You can wade its edge but never hold it, never reach its far side. It is among the oldest things in the world. Four walls, four words — let the tide carry you on.

North

I am a cloud that lost its sky
and settled grey upon the shore;
the lighthouse blinks, the foghorns cry,
and ships creep slow till I'm no more.

East

I leave the mountain, thin and cold,
and broaden as I journey down;
I turn the mill, I bear the boat,
and run at last past every town.

South

Soft and wet, I take the print
of every thumb that presses me;
the kiln's hot breath will make me hard —
a brick, a bowl, a jug to be.

West

Twice a day I climb the sand,
and twice a day I slip away;
the moon, not wind, commands my pull —
I bare the shells, then hide the bay.
