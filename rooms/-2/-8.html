Lily

You are the cool white flower of still water, your broad green pads spread flat on the surface and your cupped bloom held just above. The frog rests on you, the dragonfly lands, the carp shadows beneath your roots that reach down into the dark mud. You open with the morning and close with the dusk, pale and waxen and faintly sweet. Where you grow, the pond is at its loveliest - a green floor laid with white stars, floating, going nowhere, asking nothing but the sun and the silence.

North

Green and damp, I leap and swim,
from the bank into the deep;
my throat puffs out, the light grows dim,
and croak my song I keep.

East

A small smooth stone the water rolled,
round and grey and worn;
you skip me where the stream runs cold,
or pocket me at morn.

South

A flower red with a sweet, sweet smell,
on a stem of thorns I grow;
of love and June I'm sent to tell,
the fairest bloom you know.

West

I scratch the yard, I peck the seed,
I cluck the whole day long;
I lay a smooth white egg to feed,
and mother all my throng.
