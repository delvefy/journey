Bee

You are a small gold engine of summer, and never once still. From the first light you comb the flowers, your legs growing heavy with their yellow dust, and carry it home to a city of wax you raised yourself. There you turn it, slow and patient, into honey - the one sweetness the world makes by the spoonful. You hum as you work. Sting in anger and you pay with your life; let be, and you feed half the meadow.

North

I am the smooth and willing earth,
that keeps the print of every hand;
the potter gives my shape its birth,
the kiln then bakes me where I stand.

East

My house is always on my back,
I cross the leaf so slow;
one silver foot, one shining track
to mark how far I go.

South

Green and slick, on legs so strong,
I leap and dive the pond along;
with sticky tongue I snap the flies,
and blink my two big bulging eyes.

West

I wear a shell both smooth and thin,
with white and golden yolk within;
a hen will lay me in her nest,
and breaking me will use me best.
