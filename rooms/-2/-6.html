The After

South of the lees and east of the undoing, the undoing is finished. A step east the body was still coming undone, the warmth still leaching into the cold; here the leaching is done, the warmth wholly gone, and what is left has settled — the body's last remains sifted down into the lees, below even the sediment, to the absolute floor of the Waste-deep. This is the after: not the dying, but the done, the cold remains of being come to rest in a place where nothing will ever disturb them, because there is nothing left anywhere to disturb.

And yet you are here to know it, which is the after's quiet riddle. You stand at the floor of your own undoing, the warmth gone, the body settled, and find that something has not settled with the rest: the whorl in your skin, gone nearly cold but not gone, and one slow beat still in you, the last of the count that the after has not quite finished taking. You draw the spiral in the settled cold and it stays, faint, the way the standstill's mark stayed — kept, unwitnessed, at the bottom of the after, a coil drawn by the last warmth in a place that has no more warmth to give.

The count is nearly done. One beat, and a long stillness, and another — the spent heart of the cold dark come to its last few strokes at the floor of the after, not stopping, never quite stopping, only settling, beat by far-spaced beat, into the done.

To the north the after firms to the lees, the settled residue of what never was. To the east the body comes undone again, the warmth still leaching. To the south it sinks below even the after, toward the pole, the floor beneath the floor. To the west it dissolves wholly into the Waste, no body, no remains, the void. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here the going has settled into the after with the rest of you — and still one cold coil and one far beat hold at the floor, the last unfinished things in a place that has finished everything else.
