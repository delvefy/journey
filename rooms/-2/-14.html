Buttercup

You are the small shiny flower of the field, gold as fresh butter, low in the grass where the cows graze. No hand made you; the meadow did. Children hold you under the chin to see who loves butter, and your gleam says yes. Bright, common, and glad, you turn the whole field yellow in June.

North

A tall spring flower, trumpet-gold,
that nods on the green bank;
first up after winter's snow -
for spring you have it to thank.

East

A big round fruit, green-skinned and cool,
pink-red and sweet inside;
full of black pips and dripping juice -
on a hot day, your pride.

South

A big round ball of green leaves
that grows down in the row;
leaf wrapped on leaf, all tight and crisp -
it's chopped for soup, you know.

West

A lumpy brown root from the ground,
you dig it from the dirt;
you mash it, chip it, bake it whole -
with salt, it's worth the work.
