The Seized Engine

The made cold goes on, but the making has failed in it. This room was iron once, true-cut and joyless like the room beside it, but the trueness has gone to rot: the floor is scabbed over, the walls weep long orange stains, and the flat grey light falls on a great machine that has stopped. Whatever it was built to do, it does no longer. It only stands, seized, in a drift of its own red flaking.

At its heart, behind a cracked case, is the spring — wound once into a tight steel spiral, the coil you have met as shell and salt and horn and ochre, set here to labour and tick and mean nothing. Now it is fused with rust mid-turn, locked halfway between wound and slack, neither counting nor resting. You touch it and a flake comes away on your fingertip, red-brown, and the rest does not so much as shiver. The spiral has stopped spiralling; even its small meaningless turning is spent.

The hard little tick the next room keeps — you listen for it, and it is not here. Only the faint tin whisper of rust growing, if rust makes a sound, and the memory of a clock that gave up midcount.

To the east the iron is still sound and the mechanism still ticks, running well and caring nothing. To the south the squared cold softens back toward the lapsed charm, a kept room going quietly to dust. To the north the rust wins outright, a future that built the engine and forgot to wind it. To the west the metal crumbles to grit and the long grey silence, the Waste that machines come to. You were going somewhere, by some mechanism. This one ran down before you arrived, and points nowhere.
