Fog

The sea breathes out, and the world goes soft and grey. Fog is natural — no hand could pour it — and barely there at all: a cloud that has lain down in the valley, water you walk straight through and cannot hold. It eats the hills, the lane, the lamp three steps ahead, and turns every near sound strange. Wait long enough and the sun drinks it back up. Speak the names, and let the grey way clear.

North

I fall in threads from grey, low cloud
and drum upon the pane;
I fill the ditch, I feed the field,
and gurgle down the drain.

East

You cannot see me, cannot hold,
yet I can bend the tree;
I slam the door, I fill the sail,
I whistle wild and free.

South

I am too wide to walk around,
my water stings with salt;
I heave and crash from shore to shore
and never come to halt.

West

On cold still mornings I appear,
all silver on the lawn;
I draw white feathers on the glass
and melt away by dawn.
