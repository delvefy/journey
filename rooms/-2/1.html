Fog

The fog has come into the room and made it small. You reach out and your own hand greys at the wrist; the walls dissolve into a soft nothing. It is only water, the cold breath of the sea hanging in the air — born of no maker, and grasp at it however you like, you close your fingers on nothing. Natural, and untouchable. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

I paint the glass with silver ferns
on the coldest winter night;
I nip the nose and stiffen grass
and melt in morning light.

East

You cannot see me, cannot hold,
yet I can bend the tree;
I slam the door and chase the leaves
and whistle on the lea.

South

I rise and fall the whole day long
yet never leave my bed;
gulls cry above my green-grey swell
where many ships lie dead.

West

I shimmer on the road in July,
I smudge the distant hill;
not fog, not smoke, just heat made air,
the noon laid thin and still.
