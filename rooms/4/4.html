The Lumen

East of the city and north of the guest, the names walk as gods. What stood as one bright guest a step south, and thronged as a city a step west, here has come to its full estate: a place where reality is no longer found but agreed, made of light and naming, and the named walk it as gods — figures of pure cold fire whose every word remakes the world as they speak it, who do not live in the made day but are it, sovereign and luminous and kind. This is the Lumen near its pole, the deep bright future, where to be named is to be a god, and to speak is to make.

They turn to you as you enter, the walking gods, and you feel the old lack like a wound in all that brightness: you alone here are unnamed, unspoken, the one thing not yet agreed into the consensus of light. And they could admit you. A name, said here, would reshape the world to hold you, raise you into the bright company as one of them, a god of light walking among gods. The spiral is the seed of it — the whorl in your skin, the unspoken name's first coil, the same mark that is the gods' own grammar — and they bend toward it, toward you, ready to take it up and speak you whole into the made world at last. The greatest of them lifts a hand of cold fire. The world leans, consensual, ready to agree to you.

A god's word here is a making and a bell at once, reality struck into being on the saying, the hour and the world rung together — and your name waits to be the next word spoken, the next note struck, the making that would ring you into the bright forever.

To the north the Lumen refines to pure light and number, mind without flesh wholly, the lit pole's cold peak where thought remakes matter at a switch. To the east reality becomes wholly the gods' to dream, the magic pole within the light, a word the entire world. To the south the gods narrow to a single guest, one being of fire giving you a name palm to palm. To the west they disperse into a city of names, embodied and walking but not yet gods. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here that name would make you a god of light, spoken into a world that agrees to whatever it is told — and the walking gods bend toward your one unspoken spiral, ready to say you whole, the saying a breath from remaking the world to hold you.
