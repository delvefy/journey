Honk

You are the loud flat blare of a horn, the rude brass parp that cuts across the traffic to say move, or look out, or hurry up. No hand makes you alone - a driver leans on the wheel, or a great grey goose throws its neck out and lets fly. You are impatient, startling, and a little comic, the sound of a jam at rush hour and a gaggle on the green. A blunt wrought blast, you are noise used as a nudge: loud enough to turn every head at once.

North

The loud deep ring of a heavy bell
or iron struck on steel;
the great gate shuts, the fire-bell tolls -
a clamouring metal peal.

East

The bottom lip stuck out and low,
a sulk upon the face;
you do it when you don't get your way -
a grumpy, drooping grace.

South

The sharp clean crack of a breaking twig,
a biscuit bit in two;
or fingers clicked to call the dog -
a quick sound, sudden, new.

West

A loud shrill warning, ringing out,
that startles you awake;
it shrieks of fire or thieves or dawn -
a din for safety's sake.
