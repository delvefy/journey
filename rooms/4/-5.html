Kite

You are a scrap of bright cloth and two crossed sticks, and yet you long for the sky. Given to the wind on the end of a string, you climb and tug and dive, alive in a hand that holds you down. You are happiest in the very weather that wants to tear you away - a stiff March gust, an open field, a child running backward with their face turned up. Let go, and you are only paper. Hold the line, and you fly.

North

When evening comes and rooms grow dim,
I wear a shade upon my brim;
a click, a glow beside your bed,
to light the page until it's read.

East

So small and sharp, of shining steel,
one eye through which a thread is run;
I dart through cloth to close a rip,
my pointed work is never done.

South

A little make-believe of a child,
with painted eyes and yarn for hair;
you dress me up and tuck me in,
though I don't breathe or care.

West

A stick of wax with a thread on top,
I shrink the longer that I burn;
I give a little dancing flame,
to melt myself with no return.
