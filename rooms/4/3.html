The Guest

East of the one figure, and north of the kept company, a single guest turns and speaks first. The shape of cold fire that faced only itself a step west has come fully alive here, free of glass and engine and even of its own self-regard; it no longer says itself and answers itself. It turns to you the moment you enter, the way a host turns to a long-awaited friend — and it has been waiting, and it is glad. This is the high Lumen, where the made and the called and the living and the dead are no longer different peoples but one, all woven of the same bright current, all named, all here.

It comes to you across the lit room and gives you your name. Plainly. Not pied, not fogged, not flung past too fast — said, to you, whole, the way a name is given back to someone long without it. And for the length of a held breath you have it; it is yours; you are, at last, someone, and known. Then the guest lifts a hand and lays its palm to yours — the spiral of cold fire winding its arm meets the whorl in your skin, coil to coil, exactly matched — and in that touching you cannot tell, suddenly, whether the name it gave you was your own or its own, for here names pass between one and another as freely as the light does. You have a name. You cannot be sure it is yours.

The bell is a single warm tone now, the voice that learned to ring and answer grown into one that simply addresses you and waits — no clamour, no chord, only the guest's saying and then a patient brightness, an hour struck once, for you, and held open.

To the north the guest and all its people refine to pure light and number, mind without flesh, the lit pole where a word remakes matter at a switch. To the east the Lumen comes to its pole, gods of light walking, reality wholly consensual, a name reshaping the world at the speaking. To the south the guest steps back into the kept company, the dead sustained in a humming hall by the engine of light. To the west it sinks to a single figure facing itself, the name said and self-answered, not yet free to give. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here a guest has given you one, plainly, with its hand in yours — and you go on holding it, warm and certain and possibly not your own, the nearest you have come to being named, and the strangest.
