Whistle

You are a thread of sound pulled tight between two lips, or blown sharp through a little tin. Round your mouth, push the air, and out you come - bright, piercing, a tune with no words. You call the dog, you start the race, you warn the train along the dark. Shepherds shape you, kettles steal you, boys practise you for weeks. You cost nothing and carry far, a small clean note that cuts straight through the noise of the whole loud world.

North

When you are cross or far away,
you open wide and call;
a loud quick word you fling and say,
that echoes off the wall.

East

I lift my shoulders to my ears,
and let them fall again;
'I do not know,' my answer here,
without a word - just plain.

South

Tickle me or tell a joke,
and out of me I burst;
ha-ha, hee-hee, I'm no soft folk,
the funny bone comes first.

West

One eye shut and one eye wide,
a secret quick I send;
a little sign I cannot hide,
between you and a friend.
