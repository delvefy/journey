Mug

Big, chunky, and warm in both hands, the mug is the cup's sturdier cousin. It carries no saucer and stands on no ceremony - just a thick barrel of a thing with a good fat handle, made for cocoa on a cold night or tea by the gallon. Wrap your cold fingers round it and it gives the heat straight back. Among made things it is honest and homely: a deep round hold for a hot drink, and a friend on a grey morning.

North

With prongs held up I spear your food,
I lift the meat in hungry mood;
I twirl the noodles, stab the pea -
the pointed tool, that's surely me.

East

I sit upon the stove and sing,
I whistle loud, a steamy thing;
I boil the water, hot and quick,
for tea I'm just the perfect trick.

South

I am clear and hold your drink,
juice or milk or water pink;
I have no handle, smooth and tall,
and shatter if I take a fall.

West

Deeper than a plate, I'm round,
I hold the soup without a sound;
your cornflakes swim in me with milk -
a curving dish as smooth as silk.
