bookcase

A bookcase is a tall set of shelves built just for books, where they stand shoulder to shoulder in long leaning rows - fat ones and thin ones, bright spines and faded. Run your finger along the tops and pick one out; slot it back when you're done. The best ones reach the ceiling and need a little ladder. A bookcase is a quiet treasure chest, holding a hundred stories all waiting patiently for someone to open them up.

North

A big soft chair with arms each side,
sink right in and put your feet up;
cosy by the fire at night -
read your book and sip your cup.

East

A flat thin screen you tap and swipe,
watch your shows and read your books;
smaller than a telly, no buttons at all -
touch the glass with fingers and looks.

South

A box on the wall with a door that swings,
shelves inside for your plates and cups;
open me up to find the snacks -
Mother Hubbard's was bare, poor pup.

West

A grid of squares for every day,
a page for each month, hung on the wall;
cross off the days till the holidays -
it counts the year, the big and small.
