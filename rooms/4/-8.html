Hoop

You are a great round ring of wood or steel or coloured plastic, a circle that exists to be in motion. The old children bowled you down the lane with a stick, running to keep you upright; the young ones whirl you round and round their hips and count how long you last. You are the basket's rim that the ball drops through, the loop the dog jumps, the shape a circus tiger leaps. Empty in the middle, perfectly round, you are play made into a single rolling line.

North

A little ball of coloured glass,
I roll across the ground;
flick me hard and watch me pass,
and click with a rolling sound.

East

A little clip of wood or wire,
I pinch the washing tight;
upon the line I never tire,
and hold the shirts in flight.

South

Blow me up with a great big breath,
all round and red and light;
I float on string, I bob and bounce,
then pop with a fright!

West

A wooden stick with a soft grey core,
I write and draw all day;
sharpen me when I'm worn and sore,
and rub mistakes away.
