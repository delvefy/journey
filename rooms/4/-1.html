The Throng

East of the worn ring the one becomes the many. The single shape that stood in the circle the dark had dreamed is a throng here — the ground crowded with the always-here, presences risen from root and stone and the spaces between, none of them called and not one that could be sent away. They do not press toward you the way the smoke pressed at the glass. They were here first; you have walked in among them; and they make you room the way old trees make room, without quite seeming to move.

They are wilder than the company to the north — that was a hall, and this is a hillside. Horn and bark and a pale fungus-light, eyes that are two low gleams, the turn of an antler far back in the press. The spiral is everywhere underfoot, worn into the earth by the long going-round of so many feet, coil over coil, and where your own foot falls it settles into the groove already there, fitted to a step you do not remember taking. One presence near you is keeping something — you feel it the way you feel a held breath — your name, carried among them like a coal passed hand to hand; it leans to give it to you, and the throng shifts, and it is three shapes away again.

The heartbeat that rose through the soles in the worn ring is many now, a low drumming in the packed ground, all the always-here keeping one old time, felt in the shins more than the ear — the oldest count multiplied, and never once struck.

To the north the throng orders into a hall and the wild shapes become neighbours, the company called and kept, summoning fitted to craft. To the east they grow solid and surer, the old powers fully here and speaking first, the high wild where the dead are simply people. To the south the shapes sink back toward root and beast and burrow, less person than presence, the horned watcher in the dark. To the west the many narrow again to one, in a ring worn round by patient feet. You were going somewhere, under a name. It passes hand to hand through the throng, near and then far, kept for you by someone you cannot reach across the press.
