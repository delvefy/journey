Anvil

A great block of iron stands on its stump, its face scarred by a thousand blows. The anvil is the most made of things and the most solid — forged to be struck, it shapes the red steel and rings like a bell at each stroke. Wrought and heavily touchable, it keeps the south-east. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

Round on my rod I turn and turn
and bear the wagon's weight;
spoked and rimmed, I roll the road
from gate to far-off gate.

East

Heavy head on a wooden neck,
I swing and fall and pound;
I drive the nail and beat the steel
with a hard and ringing sound.

South

A curl of iron made to catch —
I hang behind the door,
I snare the fish, I hold the rope,
one bend, and nothing more.

West

Behind my glass I guard a flame
and set it on the post;
I drive the darkness from the room
where the night would rule the most.
