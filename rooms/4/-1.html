Fork

Four little tines and a handle, and suddenly you can eat without your hands. The fork is the youngest of the table tools and the cleverest - it spears, it lifts, it pins the meat down for the knife. Cold and bright and exactly shaped, it does one job and does it well. Turn it in the light and it gleams; set it at the left of the plate where it has waited, patiently, at every supper of your life.

North

On two round wheels I trundle slow,
behind a horse or hand I go;
heaped with apples, hay, or wood,
I move the load as no back could.

East

I have a handle and a spout,
I pour the milk or water out;
fat in the belly, round and tall,
fill me up and I serve all.

South

A sturdy cup, both fat and tall,
no dainty saucer me at all;
I hold hot cocoa to the brim -
grip my handle, drink within.

West

I am a bowl upon a stick,
I scoop the soup up nice and quick;
I stir the sugar in your tea,
and feed the baby cheerfully.
