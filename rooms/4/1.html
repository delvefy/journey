Poem

It is language folded with care, words chosen for their sound as much as their sense. A poem need not rhyme and need not be long; it only has to make the ordinary ring. You build it line by line, breaking where the breath wants to break, and somehow a feeling too big for plain talk fits inside it. Read it aloud and it lives; leave it on the page and it waits, a small made thing holding more than its few words should.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

I'm told at bedtime, page by page,
with knights and witches, beast and mage;
a beginning, middle, and an end -
the tale a book or gran can lend.

East

I am a question wrapped in tricks,
a puzzle made of clever bricks;
think me through and say my name -
to guess me right is half the game.

South

I have two wheels and carry loads,
I'm pulled by hand down bumpy roads;
pile me with hay or sand or stone -
I haul the weight you can't alone.

West

Moon and June, and cat and mat,
red and bed, and rat and hat;
when two words end the selfsame way,
that matching sound is me, they say.
