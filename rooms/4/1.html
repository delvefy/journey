The Shining Folk

North of the standing folk the dead catch light. A step down they were smoke and held breath, going dimly about their afternoon; here the same shapes are kindled. Someone has fitted the working to craft — lamps in ranks, a frame of lit glass, the warded coil cut round it glowing like a filament — and the made light does not merely fall on the folk, it fills them. They stand in the room as before, neighbours still, but bright now, edged and steady: the called shape set running in light, summoning and made thing fused into one shining.

To the west a single dark pane held one shape pressed to its inner skin, learning to step through; here the panes have thinned to nothing but lit frames, and the folk pass through them as easily as through a doorway, no glass left to keep anyone back. One of them turns, brighter than the rest, and lays a hand of light against your sleeve — warm, weighted, real as a neighbour's touch — and the name blooms, not backward in fog this time but spoken aloud in a lit voice, close and certain and almost wholly yours. You very nearly have it.

The warding spiral that lay loose on a floor and was cut into a wooden frame is here a coil of pure light, turning in the air, the ward become a circuit and the circuit a summoning — the made thing and the working no longer two. The bell's warm answering tone has braided into the lamp-hum, a chord of voices and kindled glass rising together, swelling when a shape firms bright and sinking when it dims, the parish of the dead and the hum of the made day keeping the one hour between them.

To the north the shining is built into an engine of itself, glass after lit glass, summoning made certain and mechanical, the city of light where the dead are infrastructure. To the east the lit frames thin away entirely and the folk need no craft to be bright, the pole where naming alone makes the shape and the dead outnumber the living. To the south the light goes out of the folk and they are smoke again, present but unlit, neighbours in the dim. To the west the working folds back into a single dark pane, one shape learning the surface, the glass not yet thinned. You were going somewhere once, under a name, and here a shining neighbour has it on the tip of a lit tongue. The coil turns; the chord swells; the hand warms on your sleeve; and the name is almost, almost said.
