The Murmuring Lost

South of the old tongue and east of the gathered lost, the talk goes under. A step north the whole dark spoke at once, every word on its one long breath; a step west a close company leaned in and said your name plainly. Here there are more of them than any company — not a gathering now but a country of the lost, the warm dead bodiless and numberless, lived among rather than met, the living so few you are only the rumour passing through. And the speech has sunk below any single mouth. They do not say words to you. They murmur, all of them, a warm unbroken under-sound, names dissolved back into the breath that carries them, the telling gone below telling.

You wade into the murmur and it closes warm over you. It comes from everywhere and from no mouth, the breath of a whole people pressed close in the dark, and somewhere folded in it is your name — not offered, not spoken, only breathed, one warm syllable among the endless soft others, kept for you no more than the next. You strain after it. It is there, it is yours, and it will not rise into a word; it stays under, in the murmur, carried past on the long breath of the many and gone.

They wind warm around you in a slow coil, the spiral you have known as shell and horn and the ring of the gathered lost, here the turning of a whole breathing crowd, drawing in toward you and out again. And the bell has gone under too — no stroke now, only the rise and fall of all that breath, the hour kept as a tide of warmth instead of a sound. North the murmur gathers back into the one tongue that keeps every word, the dark that speaks. East it widens to the pole where the lost are the whole world and the living the ghost, names breathed everywhere and owned by none. South the warmth thins below even breath, into the first dark before there was anyone to lose. West the country narrows to a close company, the lost gathered turning toward you, your name almost a word again. You were going somewhere, under a name; the many breathe it warm around you and never lift it clear — and the murmur turns, and your name goes by on another's breath.
