Hoe

A flat blade on a long straight pole, swung down to chop weeds and scratch the soil between the rows. The hoe is a made thing, the patient tool of the vegetable bed. It keeps the worked south-east, among tools of iron and wood. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A bit of metal bent right round,
I catch, I hang, I hold, I snare;
the coat, the fish, the heavy load —
my curve will keep it in the air.

East

A blade with a row of jagged teeth,
I cut the plank with a back-and-forth;
the carpenter pushes me through the wood —
I sing as I bite my way henceforth.

South

I spin and bite a round, deep hole,
through wood or wall or metal plate;
press me in and round I whirl —
a boring tool. Now name me straight.

West

A long handle and a row of teeth,
I gather leaves into a heap;
across the lawn in autumn time
the fallen gold I drag and sweep.
