The Known

South of the speaking, and east of the gathered, the wild knows you without a word. A step north the horned one had every word and spoke your name plainly; a step west the lost gathered warm and said it with many throats. Here the words sink away — back into beast and root and burrow, into the dark from before there was speech — and yet you are not less known for it. You are more. The whole wild dark turns its attention on you, the lost folded into it now, beast and shade and root all one knowing, and it knows you the way your own blood knows you: wholly, wordlessly, from before there were words to be wrong in.

It names you without naming. As you come into the warm dark a recognition moves through everything at once — the ground, the unseen beasts, the close presences of the lost — a single deep knowing-of-you that needs no syllable, the way kin know a step in the dark before any lamp, the way a hand knows its own glove. The spiral is in it: you draw it and a hundred unseen things take up the coil, and the whorl in your own skin answers, recognized at last by a dark that has always carried its match. You are known here more completely than anywhere you have been. And still you cannot say the name — for there is no name, only the knowing, and the knowing is theirs to give and not yours to keep, a recognition you can be held in but never hold.

The chorus of hearts has gone below sound, all those bloods and yours beating one wordless time in the dark, the count not said now but felt, kin-deep — the oldest knowing keeping you the way a heart keeps its own beat, without ever once naming what it does.

To the north the knowing rises back into words, the wild that has every one, the horned mouth speaking the name. To the east the wild and the dead come fully sovereign, the Wyld pole, the old powers whose knowing-you is a making, named into being by their regard. To the south the knowing sinks below even instinct, the dark before recognition, the deep that knows no one and never has. To the west the wordless dark warms back into a gathered company, the lost saying your name with throats and hands. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here the whole wild knows you, utterly, without a word — and you stand in the warm dark more known than you have ever been, and no nearer to knowing yourself.
