scrape

To scrape is to drag something hard and rough across a surface, with that horrible screech that sets your teeth on edge - a chair pushed back over a tiled floor, a knife across a plate, your knee along the gritty path. It rubs the very top off things and leaves a raw red mark or a long pale line. Scrape the mud off your boots, scrape the bowl clean. It is rubbing turned harsh, all noise and grit and the top layer coming away.

North

Rake your nails across an itch,
scritch-scratch, ahh, that's the spot;
a cat does it on the scratching post -
a thin red line is what you've got.

East

A soft low sound of quiet talk,
a stream, a crowd, a breeze in the trees;
words too soft to quite make out -
a gentle hum upon the breeze.

South

The soft dry sound of leaves in the wind,
or sweet-wrappers crinkled at the show;
a papery whisper, a shuffle of pages -
the quiet sound as dry things go.

West

Walk all stiff and lopsided and slow,
one sore foot you can hardly use;
bob along and lean on a stick -
the limping walk in heavy shoes.
