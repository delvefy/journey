Cart

Two wheels, a wooden bed, and a pair of handles or a harness - the cart is the plainest of carriers, made to take the load off your back. Pile it with hay, with turnips, with firewood or a sleepy child, and it rolls the weight where your own arms could not. It has no engine and asks no fuel, only a push, a pull, or a patient horse. Among made things it is humble and old: a box that learned to move.

North

I am words in tidy lines,
where rhythm beats and meaning shines;
a poet writes me, verse by verse,
to read aloud, for better or worse.

East

I float upon the lake and sea,
with oars or sails you travel me;
I carry people, side to side,
across the water's open ride.

South

Four little spears all in a row,
I help your dinner come and go;
I jab the sausage, hold it tight,
beside the spoon at supper night.

West

I am round and I go round,
I roll the cart along the ground;
without me bikes and cars would fall -
I turn and turn and never stall.
