The Standing Folk

West of here it took a graven ring and a lit candle to call up one shape, and even then it stood penned and patient inside the wards. Here there is no ring, and no need of one. The room is full of them — standing shapes of smoke and held breath, a dozen, a score, more in the dim corners, none summoned and none penned, going quietly about an afternoon you cannot see. The dead are not called here. They simply live in the room, the way neighbours do, and you have walked in among them.

They take little notice of you, and that is the strange thing. A shape stoops at a hearth that holds no fire; another leans in a doorway as if waiting on the weather; two together incline toward one another in the murmur of a conversation a room long and centuries old. When you pass close, one turns — the way a neighbour turns at a step in the lane — and a word moves through the held air toward you: your name, or the name that is half-yours everywhere, offered now not as a question but as a greeting, as though here you are simply expected, and known, and late.

The double ring that held the working below is here too, worn pale into the floorstones, but no one keeps to it; the shapes drift across it as across any old chalk, the spiral gone from a fence to a footpath, a thing walked over rather than drawn. And the bell has become a parish of bells: after each stroke behind your eyes a dozen softer tones take it up around the room, near and far, the dead keeping time with the living the way one steeple answers another across a town at dusk — an hour rung not at you but with you.

To the north the standing folk are fitted to craft and mechanism, the called shape set running in light, summoning and made thing fused. To the east the air thickens past neighbours into a whole country of the present dead, names traded freely, the pole where the living are the few. To the south the wards loosen out of the house entirely into root and dark, the shade less neighbour than always-here, grown wild. To the west the crowd thins back to one penned shape in a graven ring, the working still careful, still asked. You were going somewhere once, and meant to arrive among people. You have. They have kept your place, and your name, and only wonder, mildly, why you took so long.
