caw

Caw is the rough scratchy shout of a crow - no tune to it, no sweetness, just a hard flat bark thrown down from the top of a bare tree. Caw! Caw! It carries across a grey field on a cold day and makes you look up. Rooks and crows and ravens all do it, arguing and warning and bossing one another about. It is the least pretty sound a bird can make, and somehow the most honest.

North

A quick light fall of snow,
a swirl of flakes, then it is done;
not a storm, just a flutter of white -
a flick of winter, soon begun.

East

The owl's soft call in the dark,
'twit-twoo' from the old oak tree;
a round low sound that floats at night -
who is awake? it must be me.

South

The long high crest where two slopes meet,
walk along it, land falls each side;
a roof has one, a mountain's spine -
the narrow top, both far and wide.

West

A wolf does me at the round full moon,
a long sad cry to the cold night sky;
the wind does me round the chimney pots -
a moaning, drawn-out, lonely cry.
