fig

A fig is a soft, sweet, teardrop-shaped fruit, purple or green on the outside and a deep jammy pink within, packed with hundreds of tiny seeds that crunch ever so slightly. It grows on broad-leaved trees in warm sunny lands, and you can eat it fresh and squishy or dried and chewy. Roll it on your tongue and it is honey-sweet. Older than almost any fruit we grow, the fig has been a treat since the very first gardens.

North

Too long in the summer sun
and your skin turns red and hot and sore;
it peels in a week and itches like mad -
so wear a hat and burn no more.

East

A little orange, sweet and small,
loose baggy skin you peel with ease;
pop the soft segments in your mouth -
a Christmas-stocking fruit to please.

South

A peach with no fuzz, all smooth and shiny,
red and gold with a juicy bite;
a hard stone hides right in the middle -
sweet and dripping, a summer delight.

West

A little oval fruit, green or black,
salty and squishy, with a stone inside;
on top of pizza, in a jar -
squeeze me for oil, far and wide.
