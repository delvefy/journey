wildfire

A wildfire is fire let loose in the wild - a blaze that races through dry forest and grass faster than a person can run, leaping from treetop to treetop in a roar of heat and smoke. One careless spark in a long hot summer is all it takes, and then the whole hillside is alight, the animals fleeing before it. Brave crews fight it from the ground and the air. It is fire at its most frightening, hungry and wild and very hard to stop.

North

When the side of a hill gives way
and tons of mud and rock slide down;
it buries the road, it blocks the path -
the whole slope crashing toward the town.

East

When the river bursts its banks with rain
and water creeps across the land;
streets turn to rivers, homes fill up -
boots and boats where houses stand.

South

A sharp quick suck of breath, surprised,
mouth open wide, no word will come;
the whole crowd does it at the trick -
one fast in-breath, then you're struck dumb.

West

In the desert the wind whips up the sand
into a great brown blinding cloud;
it stings your eyes and fills your mouth -
cover your face, it roars so loud.
