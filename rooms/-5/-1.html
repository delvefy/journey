Coal

Black stone dug from deep seams underground, light in the hand and dusty, yet it holds old sunlight in it: set it alight and it burns red and hot for hours. Coal is wholly natural and wholly solid, the buried fuel of hearth and engine. It keeps the rooted south-west, among stones and minerals. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

White and cold, I fall from grey,
I hush the fields and cap the hill;
I melt to water in your hand —
name the winter flake. Hold still.

East

A hard grey stone in the chalk I lie,
strike me with steel and sparks will fly;
the old folk knapped me to an edge —
the fire-stone. Name me, by and by.

South

Small loose stones all heaped and spread,
I crunch beneath the cartwheel's load;
they rake me flat for path and drive —
the little stones that make the road.

West

Soft mud pressed to a crumbling rock,
I lie in beds both grey and dun;
I flake apart in your two hands —
name the brittle, layered one.
