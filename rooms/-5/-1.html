Flower

For a week or two the plant spends everything it has on this: a burst of colour at the tip of a green stem, petals arranged around a dab of gold, opening to the sun each morning and folding at dusk. A flower is the plant's whole invitation - to the bee, to the breeze, to the passing eye. It asks to be looked at and smelled and, too often, picked. Then the petals brown and fall, and the quiet work of the seed begins.

North

A wide still water, calm and deep,
where mountains in my mirror sleep;
no tide, no salt, just quiet blue,
the fish below, the sky on view.

East

I creep and climb on wall and post,
with curling tendrils gripping most;
from me the heavy grapes hang down,
I twist my way to the top of town.

South

A gentle bump upon the land,
not steep enough for ropes or hand;
I rise up green for sheep to graze,
and small folk roll down all my days.

West

I stand up tall with a sturdy trunk,
my branches spread, my roots are sunk;
birds build their nests where leaves grow green,
the biggest plant you've ever seen.
