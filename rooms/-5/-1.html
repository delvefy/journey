The Edgeless

West of the failing day, and south of the road's end, the Waste has no edge. You have walked off the last of the road and out past the last of the light, and what is left is distance: open ground tilting down into an early dark, ash to a horizon that draws back as you go, so that for all your walking you come no nearer any edge, any wall, any end. The grey daylight is nearly gone now — taken not by a sunset but by sheer remove, thinning the further out you stand — until the ground and the low dark sky are one dim reach with nothing to tell them apart.

It is the loneliest shape the journey has taken: not a room you are in but a distance you are lost in, the Waste opened so wide that walking is the same as standing, and standing the same as being nowhere. You scratch the spiral in the cold ground and the wind off the open dark takes it before the coil closes, the way it took the marker's dust, the way it takes everything out here that tries to be a thing. Only the whorl in your skin stays, the one fixed point in all the receding grey, and you hold to it the way you would hold a hand in a crowd — except there is no crowd, and no hand, and no one out here at all but you and the going.

There is no count but the wind, low and level and endless, the Waste's one voice with nothing to say and no one to say it to, blowing across the edgeless ground toward the horizon that never comes — the same horizon, you realize, that your name keeps: always ahead, always drawing back, walked toward forever and never reached.

To the north the ground firms toward the road's end, the trackless Waste with a last grey light and a way that just gave out. To the east the Waste gathers toward the failing day, more light, a ruin somewhere behind. To the south the edgeless ground sinks wholly into the deep dark night, the older cold, toward the pole. To the west the Waste widens to its limit, lightless and answerless, where even distance gives out. You were going somewhere, once. Here there is only the going, out across an edgeless grey toward a dark that never resolves and a name that never arrives — the two of them one horizon, forever the same step away.
