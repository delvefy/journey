Hill

The land cannot lie flat forever; here it gathers itself into a long green swell and rises, gently, into a hill. It is a mountain that never grew up - rounded, grassy, climbable in an afternoon, with a view at the top that makes the puffing worth it. Sheep graze its slopes. Children roll down it and trudge back up to roll again. In summer it is soft underfoot and dotted with flowers; in snow it becomes the finest sledging in the world.

North

A pretty head on a slender stalk,
I brighten up the garden walk;
with petals soft and a sweet perfume,
I'm picked and placed in a vase to bloom.

East

A carpet green from edge to edge,
I grow on lawn and field and ledge;
the mower trims me, cattle munch,
I tickle toes and hide your lunch.

South

A needle sharp upon a stem,
I keep the nibbling beasts from them;
brush past a rose and you will know -
I'll prick your skin and let blood flow.

West

A hollow in the rock or hill,
so dark and cool and deep and still;
the bats hang here, the echoes call,
explore me, but don't trip and fall.
