Lake

Come over the last rise and there it lies: a wide sheet of still water held in the cupped hand of the hills. A lake keeps to itself. Unlike the restless sea it has no tide and almost no waves, only a slow shiver when the wind crosses it, and on a calm morning it gives the mountains back upside down, perfect and silent. Fish hang in its cold green deep. Stand at the edge and skip a stone, and the whole mirror trembles.

North

You cannot see me, taste, or hold,
yet I fill the world, both warm and cold;
you breathe me in and out all day,
and I lift the kite and bird away.

East

Twice a day I come and go,
the sea I lift, the sea I lower;
the moon's my master, near and far,
I bare the sand, then drown the bar.

South

I open bright with petals gay,
I drink the sun the livelong day;
the bees come buzzing for my sweet,
I'm picked to make a posy neat.

West

A strip of golden sand and sun,
where buckets, spades, and races run;
the waves come up to lick my toes,
and back into the sea each goes.
