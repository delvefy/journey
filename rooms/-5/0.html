The Road's End

West of the marker the road gives out. The two faint ruts you followed thin, and waver, and then simply are not — no fork, no ending-place, no reason, only grass closing over the last of them and, past that, open ground to every horizon with nothing drawn on it at all. This is where the going stops: not where you arrive, but where the way itself runs out, the road forgetting it was a road a few steps short of nowhere in particular. Behind you, small now, the waymark; ahead, the open Waste, flat and grey and lit by the same sourceless even daylight, going on past where looking does any good.

And yet you can still go. That is the strange cruelty of the place. The road has ended but the ground has not, and nothing stops you walking out onto the trackless grey except the knowing that there is no track — that every direction here is the same direction, which is none, and that to go on is only to put the marker further behind. You look for the spiral and find none cut in any stone, for there are no stones; only the whorl in your own fingertip, carried past the end of the last road, the one mark left in a country that has forgotten how to be marked.

There is no bell and no count, only the wind, low and level and endless, the Waste's single voice that says nothing and is addressed to no one — the same wind that crossed the marker, here with nothing left to cross, blowing over open ground toward an edge it will never reach.

To the north the trackless ground meets the cold iron's farthest dead reach, rail and rust run out into the grey, the made things' last mile. To the east the open Waste gathers back to a faint road and a last weathered marker, a way that at least once went. To the south the ground sinks toward the older cold and the deep dark beneath, the Waste tilting into night. To the west the Waste widens to the pole, where even the memory of a road is gone, and nothing answers, and nothing recalls. You were going somewhere, on some road. The road ends here, and the going does not — a journey that has outlived its own way, free to walk forever into a grey that leads nowhere, and lets you.
