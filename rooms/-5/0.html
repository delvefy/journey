Snow

White flakes falling soft and slow, settling deep to hush the whole world. Snow is frozen water spun into six-pointed stars; it is wholly natural, cold and bright, here to touch until it melts to nothing. It keeps the natural west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

The sun has set, but the dark's not full,
the grey-blue hour the lamps come on;
the swifts fly home, the day is done —
that fading time: not night, not dawn.

East

Water that falls from a grey-brimmed cloud,
it patters the roof and fills the drain;
the umbrellas bloom, the gutters run —
the wettest weather. I am?

South

Black and hard, dug from the deep,
a forest pressed to stone, age-old;
heap me on the grate and light —
I glow all night and beat the cold.

West

Water gone hard in the bitter cold,
clear and slippery, smooth to the tread;
I sheet the pond, I hang from the eave —
I melt to a puddle when winter has fled.
