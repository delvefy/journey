Noon

You are the hinge of the day, the one moment the sun stands straight overhead and the shadows hide beneath their owners' feet. The morning has spent itself; the afternoon has not begun. Bells ring you, stomachs know you, the whole town stops to eat. You cast the shortest shadow of all the hours and burn the brightest, then tip the long slow slide toward evening. You cannot be held or kept - you are only the top of the climbing sun.

North

Breakfast time, and off to school,
the sun still climbing high;
I am the half-day, fresh and cool,
before the noon goes by.

East

From the kettle's spout I rise,
a hot white breath of cloud;
I fog the glass, I cloud the skies,
and whistle long and loud.

South

When the sun is gone away,
and every candle's out;
I fill the room and end the day,
and make the child cry out.

West

The sun sinks low and red and round,
the birds all seek the tree;
the day grows still, the supper's found,
this quiet time is me.
