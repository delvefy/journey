Morning

You are the fresh beginning, the cool grey-gold stretch when the world wakes and rubs its eyes. The cock crows, the kettle sings, the dew still beads the grass and the light comes slanting low and clean. Beds are left, porridge eaten, satchels swung onto small shoulders bound for school. Everything seems possible in you, the day still whole and unspent. You belong to early risers and to birds, and you slip away the moment the sun climbs high - handed quietly on to the noon.

North

The coldest season, white with snow,
when bare black branches freeze;
the ponds turn hard, the chill winds blow,
and breath hangs in the breeze.

East

The lit half of the turning world,
from sunup to sundown;
the time of work, of flags unfurled,
of children round the town.

South

When the sun climbs to the top of sky,
and stands there, straight and high;
the shortest shadow, lunch nearby,
the middle hour goes by.

West

The west goes gold, then pink, then grey,
the sun dips out of view;
the burning end of the long day,
before the night comes through.
