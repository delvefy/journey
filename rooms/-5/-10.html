Corn

You are the tall green crop that rises higher than a child, ears of golden kernels wrapped in pale husk. No hand makes you; the farmer sows you and the sun and rain raise you in long rustling rows. You feed the hens, the cattle, and the people - boiled on the cob, popped in a pan, ground into meal. You are the field's tall golden grain.

North

A new white baby of the sheep,
all wobbly on its feet;
it bleats for its mother in the spring -
its little voice is sweet.

East

A small round fruit, deep purple-blue,
with sweet soft flesh inside;
a smooth hard stone sits at its heart -
in jam or pie it's tried.

South

A field of golden waving stalks
that ripples like the sea;
its grain is ground to flour for bread -
the loaf begins with me.

West

Soft and furry, long of ear,
it hops on big back feet;
it lives in burrows underground
and finds the carrots sweet.
