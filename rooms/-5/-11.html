Wheat

You are the tall golden grain that fills the fields of summer, your heavy heads bowing and rippling in the wind like a sea. No hand makes you; the farmer sows you and the sun ripens you from green to gold. Cut, threshed, and ground, you become the flour for every loaf of bread. You feed the world.

North

A tall green crop with golden ears
wrapped in a pale green husk;
boiled on the cob or popped in a pan -
it rustles tall at dusk.

East

A small round fruit, bright shiny red,
that hangs in twos on a stalk;
a hard stone hides inside the sweet -
you spit it as you walk.

South

A little seed you grow in pods,
green and long, or in a tin;
Jack climbed a giant one to the clouds -
plant a few and watch begin.

West

A bushy-tailed and nimble thing
that races up the tree;
it buries nuts and digs them up,
then leaps from branch with glee.
