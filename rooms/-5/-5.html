Owl

You are the night's own question, two round eyes in the dark and a hush where wings should beat. You turn your head almost the whole way round and miss nothing the moonlight shows - a mouse in the grass, a moth at the glass. The day-birds fear you; the farmer blesses you. You ask only a hollow tree and the long quiet hours, and answer the dark with one low call: who, who.

North

Light as nothing, soft to hold,
I drift down from the sky;
a bird has dropped me, brown or gold,
I tickle as I fly.

East

Of twig and moss and mud I'm made,
high in the swaying tree;
a cradle where the eggs are laid,
the birds build me, you see.

South

Curved and sharp at the toe's end,
a hook of horn I grow;
the cat, the hawk, the crab, my friend,
all grip the world just so.

West

By the lamp at night I beat
on dusty wings of grey;
the candle flame I cannot meet,
yet round it still I play.
