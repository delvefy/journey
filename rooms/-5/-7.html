Beak

You are the bird's whole face and tool in one, two horny jaws fixed where the mouth should be, made for whatever that bird must eat. Sharp and hooked on the hawk for tearing; broad and flat on the duck for sieving the mud; long and fine on the wren for tweezing grubs from bark. You crack the seed, you preen the feather, you build the nest twig by twig, you tap the shell from the inside when it is time to be born. No teeth, no lips - just you, the bird's bright clever beak, busy from dawn to dark.

North

A curved sharp hook upon the toe,
of hawk and cat and crab;
it grips, it tears, it won't let go,
the sharpest thing they grab.

East

A hard brown shell with a kernel sweet,
upon the tree I grow;
the squirrel buries me to eat,
when the cold winds blow.

South

A ball of yellow fluff so small,
just hatched from out the shell;
it cheeps and follows the hen's call,
the baby bird as well.

West

The soft warm coat of cat or bear,
that keeps the winter out;
all downy thick, a layer of hair,
you stroke it round about.
