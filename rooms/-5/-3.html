The Worn Dark

South of the lightless and east of the old night, the deadness and the age wear into one. A step north the dark was dead, forgetful of light and knowing; a step east it was old, an age pooled and still. Here the two grind together and become a single grey — a dark so ancient and so dead at once that you cannot tell whether it was emptied long ago or simply worn down by unthinkable time, the oldest dark gone deadest, the deadest dark gone oldest, before the Waste was ever wasted and after everything was forgotten, met in the one worn grey.

Everything here has been worn rather than ended. You draw the spiral and it does not blow or sink or subtract; it wears — the line going faint under your finger as you make it, smoothed by an age and a deadness both, like the coil on the waymark long ago, worn to a grey ghost of itself before it is even closed. The whorl in your skin feels worn too, thinned by the place, though it holds; you have the sense of a mark rubbed at by ages, surviving not because the dark cannot take it but because it has not yet finished.

There is no count. The age took the time and the deadness took the keeping, and between them nothing is left to tell an hour by — only your heart, impossibly new in so worn a place and yet already feeling worn itself, beating in a grey that has outlasted the very idea of beating.

To the north the worn dark firms to the lightless, a deadness that forgot light and knowing. To the east it lifts toward the old night, an age pooled and vast. To the south it wears toward the absolute pole, the oldest deadest floor of the deep. To the west the Waste's pole, where age and deadness are one grey and the corner has worn away even itself. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here age and the Waste have both worn at it, smoothing it the way they smooth the coil — not never-was, but worn-away, rubbed to a grey ghost — and you carry on with the thinned whorl in your skin, a mark the worn dark has not quite finished taking.
