Ivy

A dark green climber that creeps up the old wall and the bare tree, clinging with a thousand little rooting feet and staying green all winter. Ivy is wholly natural and wholly touchable, the patient evergreen. It keeps the rooted south-west, among creepers and growing things. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

Small loose stones all heaped and spread,
they crunch beneath the boot and wheel;
raked flat for the path and drive —
name the little stones. They're real.

East

A long green climber, tendril-tight,
I twist up post and trellis tall;
I bear the grape that makes the wine —
name the trailing plant. Don't fall.

South

Fine soft mud the river drops,
where the slow brown water creeps;
grey and smooth, it fills the bed —
name the settled mud. It seeps.

West

A tall coarse fern on the open hill,
that browns and rusts when autumn's near;
it carpets the moor in feathered fronds —
name the hillside fern. Speak clear.
