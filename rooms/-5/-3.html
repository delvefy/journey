Thorn

You are the rose's warning and the bramble's armour - a small hard spike along the stem, sharp enough to draw a bead of blood from any careless hand. Nothing grew you on purpose; you simply harden out of the branch, pointing every way at once. Reach for the fruit or the flower and you make the reacher pay. Snag a sleeve, prick a thumb, and hold fast: that is your whole short, stubborn life.

North

I am a swell of grassy ground,
too small to be a mountain tall;
you climb up one side, roll down the next,
I'm the gentlest slope of all.

East

I am the arm that leaves the trunk,
and hold the leaves up high;
the squirrels run along my back,
the birds sit where I lie.

South

I am so light I float on air,
and drift down soft and slow;
I dropped here from a bird's own wing,
all fluffy as I go.

West

Green and slippery, I can leap,
I sit upon a pad;
I catch the flies with my long tongue,
and ribbit when I'm glad.
