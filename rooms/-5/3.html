Dawn

You are the day's first breath of light, the moment the black sky goes grey and then pink along its eastern edge. Before you, the world lies asleep and shapeless; with you, the hills draw their outlines, the birds begin, and the lamp is needed no longer. You cannot be held or hurried - you come in your own slow time, every single morning, and hand the day over to the climbing sun.

North

When every lamp is snuffed at night,
I fill the house and hall;
you cannot see your nose in me,
I'm blackest of them all.

East

I make you shiver, shake, and sneeze,
I redden every nose;
I come with winter, ice, and snow,
and nip your fingers and toes.

South

I follow you about all day,
and copy all you do;
I have your shape but not your face,
a flat dark twin of you.

West

I am a soft and steady light,
no flame, no sun, no spark;
a firefly wears me, embers too,
a gentle shine in the dark.
