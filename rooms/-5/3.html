Draft

A thin stream of cold air finding its way in — under the door, through the cracked window — to stir the curtain and chill the back of your neck. A draft is wholly natural and cannot be held, an unwanted breath of the moving air. It keeps the high, dim north-west, among winds and weathers. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A soft warm wind out of the west,
the mildest breath the spring can send;
it stirs the blossom, gentle, slow —
name the tender wind, my friend.

East

A gentle wind, both soft and cool,
that lifts the kite and turns the vane;
kind on the cheek of a summer's day —
name the easy wind again.

South

A wind grown wild, far past a breeze,
that strips the sail and bows the oak;
it roars all night without a storm —
name the great wind. Now it's spoke.

West

A brief light scatter of falling snow,
that swirls a moment, then is gone;
a sudden flutter of white in the air —
name the short snowfall. Move on.
