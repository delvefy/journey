Gale

A wind that has risen past all gentleness — it bends the trees double, tears at the sails, and roars in the chimney all night. A gale is wholly natural and cannot be grasped, only sheltered from. It keeps the high, dim north-west, among winds and weathers. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A thin cold stream of moving air,
that slips beneath the door at night;
it stirs the candle, chills your neck —
name the sneaking breeze. Sit tight.

East

Black sky and thunder, wind and rain,
I burst upon the land in wrath;
I rage awhile, then blow away —
name the fury in my path.

South

The grey soft light when the sun has set,
not yet the dark, no longer day;
the hush before the night comes down —
name the fading light. Now say.

West

A sudden blast of wind and rain,
that strikes the boat then quickly clears;
short and sharp, it slaps the sea —
name the brief storm. Out with fears.
