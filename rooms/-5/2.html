Shadow

You cast it without trying. Step into the sun and it springs from your heels - a flat dark shape that has your form but none of your weight, your colour, or your face. You cannot pick it up or pin it down; it lies across the grass like spilled ink and copies your every move. At noon it shrinks to almost nothing beneath you, and when the light goes out, it goes with it.

North

I am the first pale light of day,
that drives the night and stars away;
the rooster crows when I appear,
and morning knows that I am here.

East

On every blade at break of day,
small beads of water sit and stay;
no rain has fallen from the sky,
yet here I am till noon is high.

South

You cannot see or hold me tight,
I have no colour, shape, or weight;
you breathe me in to stay alive,
without me nothing could survive.

West

I curl up grey from every flame,
and climb into the sky;
I sting your eyes, I smell of wood,
where fires live, there am I.
