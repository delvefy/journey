The Rust Plain

North of the last post and west of the last engine, the made world is all ore now. Everything that was ever iron — the rails, the engines, the posts, the works — has come here, ground down and blown out, to lie as a plain of rust-dust under the grey: a red-grey grit to every horizon, the Chrome returned wholly to the ore it was dug from, indistinguishable now from the Waste it dies into. The made and the never-made meet here and are the same dust. You walk on the powder of a whole machine-age, and it gives under your foot like ash, and the wind lifts it and carries it off level with the ground, going nowhere, the way the wind here carries everything.

There are flecks in it, if you stoop — a curl of stripped thread, a scale of plate, a fragment with the ghost of a cast mark, a spiral worn to a wound — the last legible crumbs of everything made and counted and stamped, all of it crumbled to grit, every record and reckoned name ground to the same red dust and blown together. Your name is in it somewhere, returned to ore with all the rest, unreadable. Only the whorl in your skin keeps its shape, the one spiral the rust did not get, carried out over a plain of all the others.

A hum still threads the grit, the last of the cold field spread thinner here than anywhere, a dying carrier laid faint over the rust-plain — the bell and the clock and the drive-beat worn down to a tone almost gone, a count spent so long ago that the hum no longer keeps it, only trails it, fading out across the ore.

To the north the rust-plain meets the cold ghost-hum's last thread, the Chrome's terminus as pure fading field, the dead pole at the Waste's edge. To the east the dust gathers back into a seized engine on a dead rail, the made still half-shaped. To the south the grit firms to the last post, the final made thing, the count of miles ending. To the west the ore blows wholly into the open Waste, made and never-made one grey, the machine-age forgotten. You were going somewhere, by some made road, by some count of miles. It has all come to this rust-plain, ground to ore and blown level with the ground, your name in the dust with every other — and you cross it carrying the one spiral the rust could not take, out toward a grey that was never built and cannot rust.
