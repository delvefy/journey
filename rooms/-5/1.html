The Last Post

North of the trackless ground, and west of the iron line, stands the last made thing. The row of numbered posts a step east has thinned, and thinned, to this: one final stub of iron leaning in the open grey, the very last of everything that was ever forged or cast or driven, and past it nothing — no post, no rail, no rust, only the Waste, trackless and grey and made of nothing anyone made. You have come to the end of the made world. It ends not with a wall or a gate but with this: a single corroded post, and then ground that never knew a hand.

It is mostly ore now, scaled and hollow, leaning the way the last of anything leans. On it, still, the maker's mark — the spiral, cast proud once, eaten through from the centre until it is more wound than coil — and a number, the last in the count of miles, scaled over past reading: the make of a world that ends at this post and goes no further. You set your thumb to it and the rust comes away like pollen and the figure does not. Whatever was built, and counted, and carried this far is carried no further than here. West of this post the only spiral left in all the world will be the one in your own skin.

Strike the last post and it gives one flat dead note, the bell remade as a tool's complaint and worn nearly past sounding; and then the small tick of cold iron settling lower, the last machine-sound there is, the count of the made world spending its final tick into a grey that keeps no count at all.

To the north the last iron meets the cold hum spread thin, the made world's ghost-field at its farthest. To the east the post gathers back into a line of them, the iron mile counting its distance to nowhere. To the south the iron gives out wholly to trackless ground, the road's end, the open Waste with nothing made. To the west, past the last post, the open Waste pole, where nothing was ever made and nothing ever will be, grey to the end. You were going somewhere, by some count of miles. The miles end here, at the last post, with the last number no one can read — and past it the going carries only the one mark the made world never gave you, the whorl in your own skin, on into a grey that was never built and cannot end.
