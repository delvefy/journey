knot

A knot is what you make when you loop a rope through itself and pull it tight - and then it will not let go. It ties your shoes, holds the boat to the dock, and keeps the parcel shut. Some knots a sailor can undo with one tug; others pull tighter the harder you fight them, until you give up and reach for the scissors. It is rope, holding on to itself, refusing to come undone.

North

Take a deep breath and leap right in,
down into the cold deep water you go;
a sudden steep dive, head-first, splash! -
a fast drop down to the dark below.

East

Two long needles, a ball of wool,
click and loop and pull it through;
row by row a jumper grows -
Granny makes a scarf for you.

South

A thick soft cloth with no weave at all,
made of wool all squashed and pressed;
you cut me into shapes for crafts -
a fuzzy sheet the glue likes best.

West

A circle made in a piece of string,
round it goes, then back it bends;
a roller coaster does me too -
a curl where the line meets its own ends.
