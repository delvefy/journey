Seep

You are a seep - the slow secret way water finds through the smallest gaps. Damp seeps up a cellar wall; rain seeps through a worn-out boot; spilled juice seeps into the carpet before anyone notices. You do not rush or gush; you creep, soaking quietly through cloth and crack and crumbly stone, leaving a dark spreading stain. Silent and patient and hard to stop, you are water at its sneakiest, working its way in where no door was ever left open, one slow inch at a time.

North

Liquid that sloshes and spills over the rim,
it splatters the floor with a mess;
the brimming bucket tips as you walk -
a ___, you must confess.

East

Thick slow goo that creeps and leaks,
all sticky and gloopy and slow;
the mud squelches up between your toes -
an ___, soft and low.

South

Spit that dribbles out of your mouth,
in a long wet shining string;
the dog does it eyeing your dinner -
a ___, a slobbery thing.

West

A thin slow stream of running water,
it dribbles down in a line;
the tap not quite off lets it drip and run -
a ___, fine and fine.
