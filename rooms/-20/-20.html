pier

A pier is a long wooden walkway striding out from the beach on tall legs, right over the waves, so you can stand far out above the sea without getting your feet wet. The grand seaside ones carry arcades and ice cream stalls and a little theatre at the end, all lit up at night. Look down through the gaps in the planks and there is the green water heaving below. It is a road that goes nowhere but out, into the middle of the sea.

North

A safe calm place where the boats all rest,
walled from the waves and the wild rough sea;
they tie up here when the storm blows in -
a sheltered home for the ships, you see.

East

A little curved bay, tucked away small,
cliffs round the sides and a crescent of sand;
smugglers loved this hidden spot -
a tiny safe scoop of the land.

South

Where the land meets sea, the sandy edge,
with shells and pebbles and washed-up weed;
'she sells shells' upon this spot -
paddle and dig, it's all you need.

West

A spinning swirl of water round and round,
it sucks down everything that's near;
the plug-hole does it in your bath -
down the funnel things disappear.
