dunk

To dunk is to dip something right into a liquid and pull it out again - a biscuit into your tea until it goes soft, a chip into the ketchup, a paintbrush into the water jar. Hold it too long and the biscuit collapses into a soggy mush at the bottom of the mug, which is the great danger of dunking. Quick in, quick out, and your treat comes up warm and soaked and delicious. It is the happy art of the dip.

North

When the air is full of soft grey haze
and you can't see far across the moor;
damp and ghostly, all blurred and pale -
a cloud come down to the floor.

East

A sudden quick jet of liquid out,
squeezed from a bottle, fast and thin;
juice from a fruit, blood from a cut -
a short sharp squirt, then drawn back in.

South

Drop the paint and watch it fly -
splat! in spots all over the floor;
mud and ketchup, blobs everywhere -
a messy spray you can't ignore.

West

A light and pleasant windy day,
a gentle wind that ruffles your hair;
kites fly well and washing dries -
fresh and breezy in the air.
