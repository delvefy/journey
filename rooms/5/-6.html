Scissors

You are two blades crossed and pinned at the waist, a pair that does its one job only together. Slip your loops over thumb and finger, open wide, and bite - and the paper parts, the ribbon falls, the long hair drops to the floor. Snip, snip, you walk along a line as neat as any ruler drew. Left alone you sit harmlessly agape; closed in a careless hand you nip. Sharp, simple and faithful, you cut the world into the shapes it is wanted in.

North

A sliver thin with a hole to thread,
I dive through cloth and back;
I mend the sock, I hem the bed,
sharp point and silver track.

East

Knitted snug to fit the foot,
I warm you toe to heel;
into the shoe I'm gently put,
soft wool is how I feel.

South

A tiny spike with a round flat head,
I hold the cloth in place;
I prick your finger till it's red,
then fix the seam with grace.

West

A little person stuffed with thread,
with painted eyes that stare;
the child will tuck me into bed,
and brush my woollen hair.
