The Utterance

North of the unfinished city and east of the lumen, the world becomes a word. The city a step south built itself by naming; the gods a step west walked a reality they spoke; here there is no city and no walking, only the saying — the whole bright cosmos a single endless utterance, an unbroken word the gods are speaking and have always been speaking, every star and street and self a syllable of it, the world not made by speech but simply being speech, ongoing, self-creating, alive in the saying.

You stand inside the utterance, in the one silence in it: the place where your name has not yet been said. Everything around you is spoken, is being spoken, is part of the great sentence; and you are the held breath, the unsaid word, the gap the saying flows around. The gods' utterance includes all things but you, and leans toward including you, your name the next syllable forming in the cosmic breath. The spiral is the shape the word turns on, a coil of pure saying, the same whorl in your skin, here the unspoken syllable that would join you to the sentence of the world. The utterance gathers to say you. The world holds its breath on your name.

There is no count but the saying itself, the cosmos sounding without pause, the bell become the world-word — and your name the one note not yet sounded, the silence the great utterance is about to fill.

To the north the utterance refines to pure light-speech, the lit pole's endless word. To the east the magic pole absolute, the world wholly a word, nothing but the gods' saying. To the south it gathers into a city naming itself street by street. To the west it walks as gods making a spoken reality. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the world is a word still being spoken, and yours is the one syllable unsaid — the cosmos gathered to utter it, the saying a breath from speaking you into the sentence of everything.
