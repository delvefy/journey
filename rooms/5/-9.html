Hook

You are a curved and pointed bit of metal, bent to one purpose: to catch and hold. A smith or a machine shaped you, beak-like, from wire or steel - you are wholly wrought. You hang the coat behind the door, you land the wriggling fish, you snag the rug from the high shelf. Once caught, a thing does not easily slip your barb. Small, sharp, and stubborn, you are grip given a shape, the patient little claw that waits on the wall to hold whatever is hung upon it.

North

A little pincher made of wood
that clips the washing tight;
or a stake to hold the tent
against the wind at night.

East

A string of metal rings linked through,
each one inside the next;
it locks the bike, it tows the truck,
a line of iron, flexed.

South

A long thin thread of bendy metal,
it carries light and sound;
it fences fields with barbs and twists,
and coils up on the ground.

West

A stretchy bag blown full of breath,
all red or blue or green;
it bobs upon a string at parties
and pops with sudden scream.
