The Spoken Hall

East of the bright company and north of the charmed house, the dead and the made are one, and their word remakes the room. A step west the dead stood free but kept by a humming engine; a step south the made things served the dead but stayed mere things. Here the engine is gone and the things are not things: the dwelling itself is the dead, fused with the light they are kept in, a hall of sovereign shades and living architecture grown into one substance — and in it, to name a thing is to make it. Speak, and the walls answer; a word, and the light builds a stair, a window, a door that was not there.

They do not need you to speak; they are sovereign, and they speak first. A shade turns and names the air beside you and a place forms there — a niche, a seat, a welcome — the room reshaping to the saying the way water takes the shape of its cup. And you understand, with a slow vertigo, what your own name would do here: said in this hall, it would build you a place, make you real, raise the room around you like a house around a returning householder. The spiral is the working of it, names traced as coils of light that wind matter into being, the same whorl that is in your skin, here the very tool of making. The dead lift a hand to name you. The room leans, ready to shape itself to whoever you are.

A name spoken here rings, and the ringing is the building — the bell that learned to address and to construct now an architect's word, each name a struck note that raises what it sounds, the hours of the hall not counted but built, time laid up in light like courses of stone.

To the north the spoken hall refines toward the lit pole, the dead-and-made woven into pure light, a city naming itself endlessly into being. To the east naming becomes pure making, the magic pole where a word is the world and the dead reshape reality at will. To the south the dwelling loosens to a charmed house, made things serving the dead but staying things. To the west the hall narrows to the bright company, the dead kept free by a humming engine of light. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here that name would build you a place and make you real — and the sovereign dead raise a hand to speak it, the room already leaning to take your shape, and the saying hangs, unspoken, a breath from raising you.
