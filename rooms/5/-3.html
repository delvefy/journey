The Beast-Gods

North of the naming wood and east of the known, the making sinks below speech. A step north the beast-gods spoke the world into being; a step west the wild knew you wholly and without a word. Here the two come together and go deeper than either: the old powers, sovereign and vast and more beast than anything, who do not speak the world or merely know it but make it by knowing — whose regard is creation, whose attention renders true, below all speech, by pure ancient instinct. To be known by them is to be made.

They turn their knowing on you. It is the wordless recognition of the deep dark, grown sovereign and creative: not just that they know you, kin-deep, but that the knowing reaches into you and begins to make you real, the way the dreaming dark dreamed, only waking now and surer, a rendering by sheer regard. The spiral is in their horns and their slow eyes and the turn of their attention, the same whorl in your skin, here the shape that knowing takes when it becomes making. You feel yourself begin to be, under their gaze — assembled out of their knowing of you, made true by being seen by the oldest eyes there are. They know your name without a sound, and the knowing of it is already raising you into the world.

The world-making pulse of the wood has gone to instinct here, the gods' knowing-beat, a heart that makes by recognizing — each beat a thing seen and so made, the oldest count become the rhythm of a regard that renders all it rests upon, your name beaten real on it without ever being said.

To the north the gods make by speech again, the naming wood, the word the world. To the east their regard becomes the whole of reality, the Wyld pole, all things made by being known. To the south they sink wholly to beast and instinct, making by mere being, below even regard. To the west the knowing no longer makes, only knows, the wild that recognizes you and lets you be. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the oldest eyes know it without a sound and make you real in the knowing — and you begin to be, raised by a regard that needs no word, the world assembling you out of how completely you are seen.
