Broom

A bundle of stiff bristles lashed to a long handle - that is all you are, and yet no kitchen keeps its floor without you. You sweep the day's crumbs and dust into a tidy heap, chase the cobwebs from the corners, and stand in the cupboard between times, patient on your stick. Witches are said to ride you; mostly you just lean by the door, waiting for the next mess to need clearing.

North

I sit upon the stove and sing
a whistle loud and clear;
I boil the water up for tea,
then pour it, never fear.

East

I have four legs but cannot walk,
a seat, a back, and all;
you sit on me to rest or eat,
then tuck me to the wall.

South

Round and hollow, tight and taut,
you tap me, rap me, pound;
with stick or palm you keep the beat,
boom-boom, a marching sound.

West

See-through and smooth, I hold your drink,
the light shines clean right through;
but knock me off the table edge -
I smash in pieces, too.
