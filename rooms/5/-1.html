Hammer

A heavy head on a stout handle, swung to drive the nail and shape the iron. The hammer is a made thing, blunt and strong, ringing with every blow. It keeps the worked south-east, among the tools of the smith and the carpenter. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

I am the bar the wheels turn on,
straight through their hubs, holding them true;
without me the cart could never roll —
the still rod at the centre. Who?

East

Two arms that pinch and grip and hold,
I lift the coal, I turn the meat;
the blacksmith grasps the glowing iron
with me — no fingers feel that heat.

South

A thick steel pin with a threaded end,
a nut winds on to clamp me tight;
I fasten beam to beam as one —
turn the spanner, hold it right.

West

The smith's great iron block and bed,
on which red metal is beaten flat;
I take the hammer's blow all day
and ring like a bell. Now name me, that.
