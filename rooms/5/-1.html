Jug

Fat-bellied and round, with a thumb-worn handle and a little pinched lip for pouring, the jug sits on the table waiting to be useful. It holds more than a cup and pours more neatly than a bottle: milk for the morning, water for the flowers, lemonade for a thirsty afternoon. Tip it, and a clean stream arcs out from the lip and stops dead when you lift it back. Honest, heavy, and plain - the willing servant of every kitchen there has ever been.

North

I bob upon the river wide,
and ferry folk from side to side;
with sail or oar I cross the lake,
a hollow hull, for goodness' sake.

East

A row of narrow teeth have I,
but never bite and never cry;
I tidy tangled hair up neat,
and part it down the middle, sweet.

South

Upon the hob I puff and hiss,
and whistle shrill when water's bliss;
a spout, a lid, a handle round,
I make the tea with steamy sound.

West

I have three or four sharp prongs,
I spear your peas the whole day long;
beside the knife at supper set,
I lift the food you haven't et.
