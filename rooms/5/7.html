Frown

You are the face folding into displeasure - the brows drawn down and together, the mouth pulling low, the small vertical line digging in between the eyes. You are worry made visible, or anger, or hard thinking; the teacher wears you, the storm-cloud sky seems to. Children learn early to read you and to dread it. You are the plain opposite of the smile, a weather that crosses a face and warns of trouble to come. Smooth me away, the kind ones say - you'll catch the wind and stay.

North

A long hard look that will not blink,
fixed straight on someone's face;
it makes them shift and squirm and think,
a gaze held out of place.

East

A quick shut-and-open of the eye,
so fast you scarce can see;
'gone in one,' the people cry,
the briefest thing to be.

South

Two shoulders lifted up and dropped,
to say 'I do not know';
no word is spoken, all talk stopped,
a careless little throw.

West

A great loud word flung from the chest,
when softness will not do;
it carries far past hill and crest,
'look out!' it warns to you.
