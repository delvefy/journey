Whine

You are the high thin drawn-out complaint - 'I waaant it', on and on, when a child does not get its way. No hand makes you; the throat draws you out, nagging and grizzling. The dog does you at the door; the gate-hinge does you; the mosquito sings you by the ear. Long and high and wearing.

North

A low and grumbly drawn-out sound,
half a groan and half a grouse;
'ohhh, not again,' you let it out -
the grumble round the house.

East

You don't get your way, so off you go
to sit alone and frown;
you won't speak, you won't join the game -
a silent mood, head down.

South

Your lower lip stuck right out far
because you can't have your way;
no tears, just a cross and sulky face -
it says what you won't say.

West

The loud harsh ring of iron struck -
a gong, a slammed iron gate;
no soft sweet chime, just a great rough bang -
of metal, loud as fate.
