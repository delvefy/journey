The Kept Hearth

East of the company the dead keep house. The dim shapes that stood and inclined a step west are solid here, settled, at home — this is a household, a long hall with a hearth alight in the middle of it, and the folk about the fire are living and dead together and no one minds which. They do not wait to be seen now, nor incline and hold their peace; they look up as you come in, and one of them speaks first, the way a neighbour speaks first when you have been long away: easy, glad, already mid-sentence, as though you had only stepped out.

The hearth is kept. Where the cold rooms to the west hold only fallen hearths and rings of dead stone, here the fire burns low and steady and has burned, you understand, a very long time, fed by hands living and otherwise, the warm centre of a house that death never emptied. Over the lintel the spiral is carved as a house-mark, a welcome cut by whoever raised the roof, and the dead trace it in the air as they greet you, the old sign for kin. And the one who spoke first says your name — not summoned, not almost, but the way your name is said by someone who has known you all your life and simply uses it, in passing, certain, the start of a question about where you have been. You almost answer. You almost know the face. The name sits a breath away, ordinary as a doorlatch.

There is a bell, but a house-bell, a soft thing by the door rung to call the far folk in to the fire; and under the talk runs the warm murmur of the household, the living and the dead keeping one easy time, the count grown homely, no longer marking the hour so much as filling it.

To the north the household is fitted to craft and light, the dead called and kept by made things, summoning-engines at the hearth. To the east the dead come fully sovereign, the elders and old powers at the board, the high pole where the long-dead are masters of the house and the old names bend the world. To the south the hall opens to the wild, the dead less folk than spirits, root and horn and beast come in to the fire. To the west the household thins back to a dim company, shapes that know you and incline but do not yet speak. You were going somewhere, and someone here knows where, and uses your name to ask it. The hearth is warm, the door is open, and a neighbour you cannot quite place waits, mid-sentence, for you to come in and answer.
