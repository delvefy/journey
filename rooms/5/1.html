Riddle

Here is a thing made entirely of misdirection. A riddle takes the plainest object - a key, a bell, an egg - and describes it sideways, in borrowed clothes, so that the answer hides in plain sight until the moment it doesn't. Then it springs open like a lock. This whole hoard is built of them; you have been solving them to stand here. A good one has exactly one answer and makes you feel clever and foolish at once. What am I? You already know.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

I am a piece of what you say,
made up of letters in a way;
'cat' is one, and 'dog', and 'tree' -
string a lot, and you tell a tale with me.

East

A witch will chant me, low and dim,
to turn a prince to frog or him;
a string of magic words I make,
'abracadabra!' for goodness' sake.

South

A wooden shell that floats and rocks,
with oars to row past quays and docks;
it carries you across the deep,
where only fish and water sleep.

West

I'm made of lines that often rhyme,
arranged with beat and care and time;
I sing of love or spring or sea,
and 'roses are red' begins with me.
