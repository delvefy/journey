The Charmed House

North of the hearth the house gets its servants. The long hall where living and dead kept one fire a step south has filled, here, with made things — a lamp on a stand, a wheel by the wall, a small bright engine at the hearthside — but they are not the cold machinery of the works far to the west. They are charmed. They belong to the household, and the household is the dead's: the made things serve, and the long-dead are the masters they serve, who light the lamp with a word and set the wheel turning with a name.

A neighbour-shade looks up from the fire as you come in, easy as before, and speaks first — and as it says your name the lamp on its stand brightens, the latch on the far door lifts, the little engine at the hearth quickens its tending: the made things of the house answer to your name the way the folk do, charmed to know it. The dead need no engine to call them now; it is the other way round. They are sovereign here, at home, and the apparatus is only their craft, their loom and their lamp, the old summoning turned inside out so that the living make and the dead command. The spiral is cut over the lintel as a house-mark and wound, too, into the wheel and the lamp-stand — kin-sign and maker's-mark at once.

There is a bell, a house-bell by the door, but the dead ring it without touching it, a word enough; and the made chime of the engine answers it, craft and the old voices keeping one time, the count grown homely and charmed both, an hour kept by a household that does not mind which of its members are breathing.

To the north the house and its servants weave into one lit apparatus, the dead and the made things fused, magitech at the hearth, the Lumen climbing. To the east the dead come wholly sovereign and need no made thing at all, the old powers' hall, reality theirs to shape at a name, the high pole. To the south the made things fall away and the hall is only folk, the kept hearth, living and dead at the fire. To the west the household narrows to an engine that must call the dead pane by pane, the summoning a machine again. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the dead use it freely and the very house answers to it — lamp and latch and turning wheel — and still, in all the charmed and homely knowing, you cannot lift it to your own mouth.
