Pin

You are a sliver of steel no longer than a child's fingernail, with a sharp point at one end and a tiny round head at the other. The dressmaker holds the cloth together with you while the cut is fitted; the schoolboy fixes his map to the wall with you; you anchor the badge to the coat and the note to the board. You are forever dropped and trodden into the carpet, found at last with a bare heel. Small, sharp and easily lost, you hold a great many things in their place until the proper fastening is found.

North

Two sharp blades that cross and bite,
with rings for thumb and hand;
they snip the paper clean and right,
the sharpest in the land.

East

On the top of your head I sit,
to keep off rain or sun;
of straw or wool, I'm the right fit,
a cap for everyone.

South

A little clip of wood or wire,
I pinch the washing tight;
upon the line, I never tire,
I grip the shirts in flight.

West

A tiny ball of coloured glass,
I roll across the floor;
flick me and watch the others pass,
and click and roll once more.
