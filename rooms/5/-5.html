Needle

You are a sliver of bright steel, sharp at one end and pierced with a single eye at the other - the smallest, most patient tool in the house. Threaded, you dive in and out of cloth a thousand times, drawing two torn edges back into one. You can vanish into a carpet and lie in wait for a bare foot; you can stitch a wound or hem a sleeve. Tiny and easily lost, yet without you the whole world would go about in rags.

North

A hollow barrel, skin stretched tight,
you beat me with your hands;
I boom and bang and keep the beat,
for marching, dancing bands.

East

A little round disc upon your coat,
sewn on so it will not float;
I push through a slit to fasten tight,
and hold the two sides shut and right.

South

Two sharp blades joined with a pin,
they open wide, then close;
I snip through paper, cloth, and hair,
wherever cutting goes.

West

On a string I climb the windy sky,
a diamond bright and light;
my long tail flaps, I dip and soar,
held fast against my flight.
