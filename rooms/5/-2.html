The Naming Wood

East of the horned word and south of the open door, the wild's speaking becomes a making. A step north the wood came in to the household fire as guests; a step west the wild had every word and said your name plainly. Here the hall is gone wholly to wood, the fire ringed not with folk but with horn and root and the old beast-gods, and their speaking does not stop at saying. It makes. When the great horned one names a thing, the thing is: it lows the word for water and water runs; it speaks a beast and the beast stands up out of the dark, made true by the saying. This is the Wyld near its pole, where the word is the world, and the gods that walk it speak it into being as they go.

They are more beast than folk now, vast and antlered and patient, and the spiral is in all of them — wound in horn, coiled in root, turning in the dark between — the same whorl that is in your skin, here the very grammar of making. The horned god turns its head to you, and you understand what its word would do: name you, and you would be made true, rendered real in the wild's way, grown a body of root and horn and warm dark, named into the world the way the running water was. It draws the breath. The wood leans in, ready to make you. The oldest mouth in the world opens on your name.

The herd-beat is a world-making pulse here, the one heart beneath all hearts beating not to keep time now but to make — each beat a thing rendered true, the count from before counting become the rhythm of creation itself, and your name waiting to be beaten into being on it.

To the north the wood draws back into a hall and the gods become guests at a household fire. To the east the word becomes wholly the world, the Wyld pole, reality the gods' to dream, naming and being one. To the south the gods sink toward beast and root, the wild's making gone below speech to pure instinct. To the west the making cools back to mere speaking, the wild that says the name but does not yet render it. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the wild's word would make you true at the saying — and the horned god opens its mouth on your name, the wood leaning to grow you real, and the word hangs in the warm dark, unspoken, a breath from making you.
