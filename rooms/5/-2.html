Bolt

A thick metal pin with a thread cut round its end, driven through two beams and held fast by a nut wound down hard. The bolt is a made thing, plain and strong, the fastener that holds the great frame together. It keeps the worked south-east, among tools and iron. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A heavy head on a stout wood haft,
I drive the nail with a ringing blow;
I beat the hot iron on the block —
name the smith's tool, swung just so.

East

A spiral groove winds down my shank,
a driver turns me round and round;
I bite the wood and pull it tight —
name the twisting pin I'm found.

South

A long flat blade with a row of teeth,
I bite through plank and bough and beam;
push and pull, the sawdust flies —
name the cutting tool's keen gleam.

West

A bent piece of iron that holds and catches,
I hang the coat, I land the fish;
curved at the end to grip and stay —
name the catching crook you wish.
