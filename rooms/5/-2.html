Kettle

Fill it, set it on the heat, and wait. Inside the kettle the water grows louder and louder - a hiss, a rumble, a roar - until at last it boils and the steam comes shrieking out of the spout in a long bright whistle that fetches you from anywhere in the house. It is a simple iron belly with a lid, a handle, and one job: to turn cold water scalding hot for the tea. Few sounds in the world say 'home' more plainly than its whistle.

North

Fat and round with a pouring lip,
and a handle for a steady grip;
I hold the milk, the cream, the squash,
and fill your glass with a gentle slosh.

East

A deep round pail with a handle to swing,
I carry the water from well or spring;
on the beach I build castles of sand,
fill me up with a spade in your hand.

South

Stiff bristles bound on a handle long,
I sweep the kitchen all day long;
the dust and crumbs I push outside,
and on Halloween a witch will ride.

West

A chunky cup with a handle wide,
I keep your tea or coffee inside;
I warm your hands on a frosty day,
and never spill if held the right way.
