fountain

A fountain throws water up into the air just for the joy of it. In the town square it leaps and sparkles, falling back into its stone bowl in a cool white spray; you toss in a coin and make a wish. On a hot day the children dance through it, shrieking. Built of stone and pipes, yet brimming with living water you can splash and drink, it is the happiest thing water ever does - reaching up, again and again, toward the sky.

North

A deep round hole dug in the ground,
with water far below;
drop the bucket on a rope -
down and down it'll go.

East

From deep in the hill I bubble up,
cold and clear and new;
the very start of every stream,
fresh water trickling through.

South

I am a shell with two flat halves
that shut up nice and tight;
buried in the sandy shore,
I clamp with all my might.

West

A slow old creature, hard of shell,
it plods across the grass;
it tucks its head inside its house
and lets the rabbits pass.
