overflow

Overflow is what water does when there is simply too much. The bath fills past the brim and spills onto the floor; the river swells with rain and climbs its banks; the cup runs over your hand. It is fullness that cannot be held any longer, the moment the vessel gives up and lets go. You cannot catch it - it pours away wherever it likes. Natural and weightless, it is the water saying enough, there is no more room, and brimming over.

North

Plip. The tap is not quite off,
and now and then, just one
small drop falls down into the sink -
plip, plop, it's never done.

East

A thin slow thread of running wet,
too small to call a stream;
down the rock it slips and slides -
the gentlest flow, I deem.

South

A salty drop that fills your eye
and slides down your cheek;
it falls when you are sad or hurt -
no word you have to speak.

West

Not proper rain, just tiny drops,
a fine and misty wet;
too light for brollies, soft and grey -
the smallest rain you'll get.
