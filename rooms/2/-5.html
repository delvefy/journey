Slate

Grey rock that splits into thin flat sheets, nailed in rows on the roof to throw off rain, or framed for a child's chalk. Slate is worked from natural stone into a useful made thing, hard and flat and lasting. It keeps the worked south-east, among roofs and building stone. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A flat baked square of clay I am,
laid in rows on roof or floor;
I shed the rain, I pave the hall —
name the fired slab. Say it, sure.

East

A broad flat blade upon a haft,
I scoop the coal, I clear the snow;
dig in, lift up, and fling aside —
name the digging tool, heave-ho.

South

A grey wet paste of lime and sand,
I'm spread between the bricks in a row;
I set hard and bind them fast —
name the glue of the wall. Now show.

West

A long flat board sawn from a tree,
laid down to walk on or to build;
the deck, the gangway over the gap —
name the sawn wood, smooth and milled.
