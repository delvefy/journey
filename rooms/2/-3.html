Pot

Black-bottomed and deep, the pot sits over the flame and does the slow work. Into its belly go the water, the bones, the carrots and barley, and out of it, after an hour of patient bubbling, comes the soup. It is heavier than the bowl it will fill, made to take real heat and not crack. Lift the lid and the kitchen fogs with steam; this is a made thing built to gather many small things into one warm meal.

North

Round and flat, I take my place
set on the table, every space;
your supper rests upon my face,
then soap and water leave no trace.

East

I have a handle and a blade,
for cutting meat and cheese I'm made;
hold me careful, keep me keen -
the sharpest tool the table's seen.

South

Flat and round with one long arm,
I sit and sizzle, snug and warm;
I fry the egg, I flip the cake -
upon the stove the breakfast make.

West

I have a row of metal teeth,
I bite the log and wood beneath;
I go back-forth in steady strokes,
to cut a plank from sturdy oaks.
