Envelope

You are the paper pocket for the letter, folded and gummed, addressed and stamped and dropped in the box. Made to carry words in secret across the world. Lick my flap, press it down, and what is inside is hidden from all eyes until the right hand tears me open. Push me through the slot, and off I go.

North

The dark wet stuff inside the pen
that flows to make your words;
blue or black, it stains your hand -
the squid hides in its swirls.

East

A fat felt pen with a juicy tip,
that draws in bright thick lines;
it squeaks across the whiteboard wide -
and stains your hands sometimes.

South

A stiff card cover that holds your sheets,
all your work tucked safe inside;
you slot the papers in its fold -
and carry them with pride.

West

A little bent wire that holds your sheets,
a pinch to keep them tight;
you slide it on the stack of pages -
they stay, and don't take flight.
