Clock

Further into the made world, time itself is put in a case. A clock is wrought to its last cog — a face that never frowns, two hands that never wave — and it does only one thing, but does it forever: it counts. Tick by tick it spends the hours you cannot get back, indifferent as a coin. Made things keep the purpose they were made for, and this one's purpose is you. Speak the names before the hand comes round.

North

Whistle me while you go to work,
I have no words to say;
I catch inside your head and stay
and will not go away.

East

I am perfectly round, and I roll,
I carry the cart on my rim;
spin me about on my middle pin
and the mill grinds at my whim.

South

I sit upon a saucer round
and hold your coffee hot;
a handle for your finger's crook,
a lip to pour the lot.

West

Iron skin and iron tongue,
I hang and cannot fall;
a rope, a swing, a single note —
I call across them all.
