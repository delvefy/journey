Clock

Everything here was built to count. A clock is a cage of toothed wheels, wound by a hand, ticking out the one thing no hand can hold. Its face never changes its expression; its two thin hands point and point and never rest. It was made for a single purpose and keeps it without pause. Four riddles are set in the walls like hours on a dial — read the time of each.

North

My many strings run short to long
down a tall and curving wooden side;
plucked by the finger, never struck,
I am the bard's and the angel's pride.

East

A toothed wheel, and yet I do not bite;
I mesh and turn my mate in kind;
in clock and mill we run in trains
to drive the hands and the millstone grind.

South

No tooth of mine, yet teeth I crave —
the right key's teeth, and none else's, fit;
I clasp the chest, I bar the door,
and only the true word opens it.

West

Hollow and round, of metal cast,
I wait in the tower for the rope;
one pull, one swing, one ringing note
that carries far the hour, and hope.
