The Hedge-Witch's Threshold

East again, and the listening air keeps its promise. You come into a room hung with drying herbs and strung bone-charms that turn slowly though there is no wind, and the floor has been swept clear and marked: a spiral laid down in white salt, winding inward to a centre where a single black candle stands unlit. This is a threshold made for working, and the work is close to the surface here, no longer half-believed.

When you came down from the shrine you felt that a name spoken aloud might be answered. Here it is truer still. The quiet has a weight to it, an ear in it, and you understand without being told that if you stepped to the centre of the salt and said the word that ought to be yours — the one worn smooth from the place in your mind where it should sit — the candle would catch, and something would lean close to listen. You do not. You are not ready to spend a name you have not yet found.

The bell answers from inside your skull, but changed: where before it only counted, now it seems to wait between its strokes, as though for a reply.

To the west lies the leaning torch and its soot-charm, the magic still timid. To the east the salt-work gives way to true summoning, wards and circles, the dead within call. To the north the charm meets craft and cools. To the south it sinks to root and instinct, older than words. You were going somewhere, under some name. The spiral of salt holds its centre open, and waits for you to fill it.
