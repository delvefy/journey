crown

A crown is a ring of gold set with bright jewels, made to sit upon a royal head and tell the world at a glance: here is the king, here is the queen. It is heavy, and meant to be - the old story says it sits uneasy, reminding the wearer of all they must carry. It comes out for coronations and processions, glinting under the lights. Even a paper one from a cracker makes you sit up a little straighter.

North

A hard round hat to guard your head,
for bikes and builders, knights of old;
clip the strap beneath your chin -
it keeps you safe when you are bold.

East

A grid of squares for every day,
hung on the wall, a month a page;
cross off the days till your birthday comes -
it counts the year, age by age.

South

A soft cloth cap joined to your coat,
pull me up when the rain comes down;
I cover your head and hide your face -
a cosy peak, and not a crown.

West

One of a pair of slim smooth sticks,
held in one hand to lift your meal;
pinch up the rice and the noodles too -
two little sticks of wood or steel.
