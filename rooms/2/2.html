The Limned Name

The light that was kept below and the name that would not be read have come into the same room and married. A lamp stands at the centre, the everlamp's flameless white, but here it does not merely burn: it is set behind a wheel of dark worked panes, the speaking glass cut now to a made purpose, and the light comes through them changed. It falls on the far wall not as glow but as letters — the ghost of letters — thrown large and slow, the cast word the glass below could only fog.

You stand in the thrown light and it crosses your face, and on the wall the shapes gather toward sense and slide apart, a name spelled out a stroke at a time by a hand of brightness that never finishes. The salt-lines and the bare bright disc are gone; in their place a fitted mechanism turns the wheel one notch, one notch, and each notch is the spiral again, coiled now in the rim and driving the letters round, so that the name climbs and climbs and never tops the wall. It is almost yours. It is cast too large and too slow to be held all at once.

The thin wire-note from the lamp below has found syllables. It no longer only rides above the light; it rises and falls with the turning wheel, a low articulate hum — the bell learning not just to answer but to say, and what it almost says is the thing on the wall.

To the south the glass only held the name in its breath, charm fitted to craft but warmer, rawer, not yet geared. To the west the light was only kept, the spiral only an hour, meaning nothing yet. To the north the cast word sharpens toward the engine that will read it, lamps in their ranks, a made day that prints. To the east the letters deepen past casting toward summoning, where the named thing might answer from the far side of the glass. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here it is written in light, and the light will not hold still long enough to read.
