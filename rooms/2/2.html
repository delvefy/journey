Rhyme

Two words end alike and something clicks shut, like a key turning in its ward — moon and June, hand and band. Rhyme is the chime the made word makes against itself, a craft of the tongue you can hear but never hold. It belongs to the high north-east, where everything is wrought and nothing is solid. Speak the matching name at each wall to pass.

North

When minstrels sing, the part that changes,
line by line, is me;
not the chorus all repeat,
but the heart of the melody.

East

Hum me, whistle me, or play,
I need no words at all;
catch me once and all day long
I echo down your hall.

South

A tall frame strung with many wires,
I sing when fingers stray;
pluck my strings from low to high
and angels learn to play.

West

Words and music joined as one,
I live inside the throat;
the lark spills me at break of day,
each bright and lifting note.
