Box

You are six flat sides and a hollow heart, the plainest answer to the question 'where shall I put this?' Cardboard or wood or tin, you swallow whatever is handed in - toys, tools, letters, the long-forgotten odds of years - and shut your lid on it, patient in the dark. On a birthday you are the best thing in the room, ribboned and shaking with secret. Open me, stack me, store me away; I keep what is given until it is wanted again.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A row of teeth on a long straight pole,
I drag across the ground;
I gather leaves into a pile,
and comb the garden round.

East

Of many leaves, but not a tree,
all bound between two boards;
I hold a tale for you to see,
and open up my words.

South

A round glass pot with a screw-on lid,
where jam and honey wait;
the pickle and the sweets are hid,
till fingers lift the gate.

West

Two long rails with rungs between,
I lean against the wall;
you climb my steps to heights unseen,
so hold on, do not fall.
