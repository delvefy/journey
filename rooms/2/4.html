The Standing Word

East of the kindled panes and north of the rising page, the word lets go of every surface and stands on the air alone. No sheet now, no glass — only the lit letters themselves, upright in ranks like a congregation risen to its feet, each one standing clear and working, a made day with neither lamp nor wire left to carry it. Where below a single name half-rose, stooping off its page, here the ranks have multiplied: line on line of standing words, an engine of them, every one full-grown, turned to face the room, spelling and doing in one breath.

You walk the aisle between them. Each word you pass leans a hair toward you and says itself, and a beat behind, in the same throat, answers — call and reply braided so close they are nearly one sound, a bell that has learned both to ring and to ring back. Among the ranks, taller than the rest and turned full toward you at last, the name stands: no longer printed, no longer half-risen, simply spoken into being and holding its shape, waiting to be answered when called. You almost have it; it faces you and asks its own syllable into the bright air.

At the heart of the ranks the old coil turns slow and luminous, the spiral the words climbed up out of, an empty stair now. North they swell to a host, a city of risen names, light with no lamp behind it. East the word sheds even its standing and walks, a summoned neighbour with no engine left in it. South the words sink back half-risen to their page; west they cool into wire and racing pane. You were going somewhere, under a name. It stands here at full height, its mouth open on its own first letter — and the lamps that are not lamps pulse, and it loses the rest.
