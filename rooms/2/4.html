The Quickened Page

North of the rising page, every line stands up. The one printed name that lifted half off its sheet a step south is here a multitude: rank upon rank of lit text, pages racked floor to ceiling, each line peeled upright off its pulp and lit from within, standing and working. This is the made day climbing — not a wall of panes only showing the word, as a step west it showed, but an engine of charms doing it, every standing line acting on what it says, so that the air is thick with small weathers and workings, each one true to its sentence.

You walk the ranks and read as you go, and what you read happens: a line that says warmth, and the air warms at it; a line that says a door, and a draught crosses; a line with a name in it, and the name turns, on its page, to face the aisle. On one shelf, alone, a single line stands brighter than the rest — your name, set in order at last and risen wholly upright, and where it stands the watermark spiral has come up out of the paper and threads the whole rank, a coil of cold light binding line to line, the same mark that warded the circle and lay pied in the lead, here the wire the working runs along. The line says itself, and acts on the saying, and what it does, slowly, is turn toward you.

The call-and-answer the rising page found in one throat is a whole reading here: the ranks speak themselves aloud, line answering line down the aisles, a hall reading itself with no reader in it, the bell that learned to say grown into a choir of standing words — none of them yet waiting for you to say them back.

To the north the lit charms refine past paper and even light into pure number, a made day at the pole where every line is a living circuit and the working needs no page. To the east the standing lines step off the shelves entirely and become the things they spell, charms grown into beings, the high Lumen where written and called are one people. To the south the ranks lie back down to a single page and one half-risen name. To the west the working cools to mere showing, a wall of panes where the word is displayed and barely acts. You were going somewhere, under that name. It stands lit on its shelf among ten thousand working lines, doing the one thing it says — turning toward you — and the ranks read on around it, and will not pause for you to read it back.
