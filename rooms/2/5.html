Race

Toe on the line, heart already going, you wait for the word. A race is the simplest contest there is: whoever reaches the far mark first has won, and everyone knows it the instant it happens. It can be run on foot or rowed or ridden or driven; it can last ten seconds or all afternoon. What you are really racing is not the others but the clock, and your own legs, and the small voice that says you cannot. Then someone shouts go.

North

A running game where one is 'it',
who chases all the rest a bit;
touch a friend and off you fly,
for now they're 'it' - so say goodbye.

East

Strike your palms with a smacking sound,
again, again, the whole way round;
the way a crowd will praise a song,
a happy noise both loud and long.

South

With dice or ball or board I'm played,
by rules that must all be obeyed;
two sides, a winner, and some fun,
I'm finished only when I'm won.

West

I am not true, though I sound so,
a story made to fool, you know;
tell one, and you'll likely need
another one to hide the deed.
