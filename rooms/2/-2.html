The Quick Herd

The beasts have come down off the wall. Where the hollow behind you held them flat in ochre, here they stand up out of the dark and breathe — a whole herd of them, long-backed and horned, shifting and warm, the heat of their bodies a weather in the room. There is no picture to read now and no pigment to read it by, only hide and breath and the rank sweet smell of animals that were never named and have never needed to be.

One stands apart, taller, its head crowned with a single horn wound in a tight coil — the spiral grown into bone, the same curl you found chalked and gouged and pressed into clay, here living and warm and turning its great head toward you. You lift your hand the way you lifted it to the red print in the stone, and you set it on a passing flank, and the beast allows it, and the warmth climbs your arm like a word you understood before you had any words. They know you are among them. They seem always to have known.

The double knock you heard through the rock is loose in the air now, freed from the stone: not one heart but many, and under them all a single vast slow beat that keeps them — the count from before counting, hoof and blood in the place of the bell, and you find your own breath has fallen into step with it.

To the west the beasts lie down again into the stone, flat and painted and patient, the wonder gone back to a held breath. To the north the herd thins and grows wary, watched-for rather than walking, the dark keeping its eyes hidden and its words half-spoken. To the east the beasts open their mouths and the wild begins to speak, the dead grazing among the living, a name kept somewhere in the herd for you. To the south the warm hides thin to cold bone and older dark, the body before the beast was ever drawn. You were going somewhere. The herd moves around you like water round a stone, and seems to be going there too.
