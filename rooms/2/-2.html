Drum

A drum stands in the corner, a barrel of wood with a skin stretched tight across its mouth, hammered together by a maker's hands. Strike it and it gives back thunder — the sound is a thing you cannot hold, but the drum itself is solid, heavy, and here under your palm. Made and tangible, it sits in the south-east of the world. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

Brass and iron, shut up tight,
I clamp the gate and chest;
find the one key cut to fit
and I give up the rest.

East

Link on link of iron forged,
I bind the hound, the well, the gate;
strong as all my rings together,
weak as one beneath the weight.

South

On the playing card, a black-pointed leaf;
in the garden, a digging blade;
I cut the turf and turn the bed —
what name have card and tool both made?

West

A small round bowl with a curling handle,
I brim with morning tea;
or made of gold and lifted high,
the winner's prize I'll be.
