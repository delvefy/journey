Verse

A line of words steps out in measure, beat falling after beat, and a verse takes shape in the air. It is a made thing, shaped on a maker's tongue, yet you cannot weigh it or close your hand on it — it lives only in the saying and the hearing, and then is gone. Crafted and untouchable, it keeps the north-east, where the spoken word holds sway. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A holy song the people raise
in church on Sunday morn;
to organ-swell and steady tune
the praises high are borne.

East

A merry song from door to door
through the cold and snowy night;
of shepherds, kings, and one bright star,
we sing it by candlelight.

South

Cat and hat, and moon and June —
I chime at the line's far end;
two words that ring with the selfsame sound,
the trick that poems lend.

West

Once upon a time, I start,
and lead you by the hand
through giants, woods, and far-off kings
to a happy-ever land.
