The Rising Word

North of the cast name the light stops only showing and starts to work. The wheel of glass that threw the word slow across the wall below is fitted here under ranks of the flameless lamps, a made day that does not merely print but reads what it prints — the cast letters sharpened to an edge, struck onto sheet after sheet, and then, where the lamps cross brightest, lifting. A page hangs in the bright air and the words printed on it peel slowly up off the paper and stand, lit from within, a charm that has stopped describing a thing and begun to be one.

On the sheet that hangs apart the name is printed, the same name, set at last in clean order — and it is rising. Letter by lit letter it lifts from the pulp and turns in the air to face where a reader would stand, assembling itself upright, the printed charm acting on its own saying. It is half-risen, no more: it stands but stoops, it faces but has not quite found your eyes, and what it spells it spells toward itself, answering its own call before yours. The watermark turns up through it as it goes, the spiral pressed in the paper wound now into the standing word, the coil that lifts it.

The braided hum of lamp and press has come the last of the way into a voice. It says a syllable and, a beat later, something answers it in the same voice from a hand's breadth off — the bell that learned to say now learning to reply to itself, call and answer in one throat.

To the north the rising words multiply into ranks, an engine of lit charms where every line stands up and works, the made day climbing toward the pole. To the east the standing name leaves off being print at all and comes wholly upright, the cast charm grown into a summoned thing stepping off the page as a neighbour. To the south the word lies back down onto the wall, only cast and slow, light married to a name but not yet risen. To the west the letters cool from charm to glow, printed and warm and meaning but not yet doing. You were going somewhere, under a name. It stands here half-up in the lit air, spelling itself, and turns the last degree toward you just as the lamps pulse and lose it.
