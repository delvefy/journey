Dish

You are a shallow round vessel of china or clay, made to hold food at the table - the stew, the pie, the heap of peas. A hand throws you on the wheel and bakes you hard. After the meal you go to the sink in a clattering stack to be washed and dried. Plate's deeper cousin, you are the open bowl the dinner is served in.

North

The flat round top that caps a pot
or shuts a box up tight;
lift it off to peek inside,
then press it back down right.

East

A soft white stick that writes on slate,
it squeaks across the board;
it crumbles into dust and snaps -
the teacher's writing sword.

South

A soft light plug for a bottle's neck,
you push it in to seal;
'POP!' it leaves the wine at last,
and bobs - it floats, it's real.

West

Cloth for the top half of you,
with buttons down the chest;
a collar at the neck, two cuffs -
worn plain or in your best.
