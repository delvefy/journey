Tin

You are a little box of bright metal, pressed and soldered and sealed by a machine's hand. Beans and soup and biscuits keep behind your shining label; a sharp blade bites your rim to let them out. You rattle in the cupboard, dent if you are dropped, and rust at last to brown lace if the rain ever finds you. Made by no root or river - all hammer and press - you are the larder's faithful, patient keeper.

North

A round glass pot with a screw-top crown,
where jam and honey keep;
pickles bob inside my brine
in the larder, dark and deep.

East

I hold a little well of ink
and let it down a nib;
I write your name, I sign the note,
and never tell a fib.

South

I cap the pot, I cap the jar,
I keep the good things in;
lift me off and peek inside,
then press me down again.

West

A coarse and roomy cloth-mouthed bag
for coal, or grain, or bread;
slung up on the farmer's back
and emptied in the shed.
