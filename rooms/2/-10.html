Lid

You are the cap that closes a thing - the saucepan's, the jar's, the box's, the bin's. A maker shaped you to fit one mouth exactly, to lift off and set back, to seal in the heat or the jam or the smell. You rattle when the soup boils under you; you go missing when the pot needs you most. Round or square, screwed or sprung, you do one humble wrought job: to be the top of things, to keep the inside in and the outside out.

North

A round bright can of sealed-up metal,
with beans or peas inside;
a sharp blade bites around its top
and opens it up wide.

East

A flat marked stick from naught to twelve,
to measure how long things lie;
I draw a line dead straight and true
when held against the eye.

South

A shallow bowl for serving food,
set out upon the table;
wash me up when dinner's done
as soon as you are able.

West

A tall thick shoe that guards the leg,
for snow and mud and rain;
pull me on and stamp about -
dry feet are mine to gain.
