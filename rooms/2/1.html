Tune

Here the song sheds its words and keeps only the shape of itself. A tune is wrought — picked out note by note — but lighter even than a song: no story, no message, nothing to touch, just a rise and fall you can whistle on the road. It will lodge in your head uninvited and play there all day long. Hum it and you remember; stop and it is gone. Speak the names, and the walls will hum along.

North

Snap the wishbone, shut your eyes,
and want with all your might;
tell no soul the thing you crave
or it won't come out right.

East

Cat and hat, and moon and June —
my end-sounds chime like a bell;
I knit two lines with matching tails
so the verse rings as well.

South

I keep a face but never smile,
two hands that point, not hold;
I march the minutes one by one
and tell you when you're old.

West

Write me at the top of the page,
call me across the yard;
lose all you have, you keep me still —
your first word and your guard.
