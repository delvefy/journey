The Speaking Glass

Charm and craft change places here, and you cannot tell which is serving which. The room is panelled in dark glass, pane on pane, each one backed not with silver but with something that drinks the light, so the surfaces hang nearly black and very deep. Around every frame runs a fine line of salt pressed into the wood — the hedge-witch's circle, but fitted now to a made thing, a charm given edges and a purpose.

When you lean close, your breath blooms on the nearest pane the way breath does, and in the bloom a shape gathers: letters, you think, or the ghost of letters, a name surfacing from the dark behind the glass. It is almost legible. It is almost yours. You hold your breath to read it and the fog shrinks and takes the name back, and the glass is only glass again, black and patient, waiting for you to breathe.

The held note from below has found a voice at last — not words, but a low answering hum that rises when you breathe onto the glass and sinks when you stop, as though the bell were learning to reply.

To the west the glass only caught and bent the light; here it has learned to hold a name. To the south the salt lies bare on the floor, charm without craft, older and plainer. To the north the working cools into mechanism, the wonder pressed flat into gears and lenses. To the east the glass deepens past speaking toward true summoning, where what surfaces might step through. You were going somewhere, under a name. Breathe, and the dark almost gives it back.
