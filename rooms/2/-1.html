The Tended Dark

The green dims, and something keeps it. The light that pulsed through the moss has sunk almost to nothing here, only a faint phosphor along the wet roots, and the room is more burrow than chamber — low, close, breathing. But it is not empty. You feel that before you see anything: the certain, animal knowledge of being attended, the held stillness of a place where something has just gone quiet to watch you come in.

Across the dark, low down, are two faint lights that are not phosphor and might be eyes, and a shape behind them the dark will not quite give up — tall, antlered, or only a snag of root grown into the suggestion of antlers. From its crown a single horn curls in a tight spiral, ridged like the shell and the fern and all the wound-up things you keep finding, the same coil grown now into bone and branch. It does not move toward you. It does not need to.

The animal call from the room before has come closer and slowed to a heartbeat — not yours; older, vast, keeping the time of something that was counting long before you woke.

To the north the salt-charm and its waiting candle, the working that still uses words; here there are none, only instinct and root. To the west the green grows lighter and tamer, wakeful but kinder. To the south the wood deepens into older wonder, where the watching things do not stay hidden. To the east the presence thickens toward the true wild, where the dead are neighbours and the wood keeps a name for you. You were going somewhere. Something in the dark already knows where, and will not say.
