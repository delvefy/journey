Cup

Here the made world fits your hand. A cup is wrought — turned on a wheel, fired hard, given a single handle and one curved lip — and wholly tangible: it warms with the tea you pour, it chips, it rings when a spoon taps it. It holds just enough and asks to be filled again. Empty, it waits on the shelf; full, it is the first thing your morning reaches for. Speak the four names, and let the walls fill and pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

Upon the wall I show my face,
two hands go round all day;
I tick and tock and never stop
and tell the time away.

East

A little bowl upon a stem,
I stir your cup of tea;
I lift the soup up to your mouth —
no fork can do as me.

South

Flat and round, I bear your meal,
I clatter when I fall;
too shallow ever to hold soup,
I'm stacked beside the wall.

West

Round and flat and made of metal,
two faces, head and tail;
flip me spinning in the air
to make a choice not fail.
