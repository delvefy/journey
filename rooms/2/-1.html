Lock

A lock hangs cold and heavy in your hand, all wrought iron and turned brass — a thing some smith hammered into being. You can weigh it, rattle it, press it to your ear, but it keeps its one secret until the right shape slides home. That is its nature here: made by a hand, solid to the touch, and shut. Speak the name at each wall, and the lock that is the wall gives way.

North

Without a single weary breath
my two thin hands go round;
I never walk, yet pace all day,
and mark the hour with sound.

East

I drink up oil, I bite a wick,
and in the dark I bloom;
one small bright tongue upon my brow
drives shadow from the gloom.

South

A hollow barrel, skin pulled tight,
no mouth, and yet I boom;
beat me with a pair of sticks
and I will fill the room.

West

I wear a face but have no head,
I'm round and flat and bright;
I buy your bread, I pay your way,
and jingle out of sight.
