Pan

Flat-bottomed and shallow, with one long handle to keep your knuckles off the heat, the pan is where breakfast happens. Set it on the flame, drop in a knob of butter, and it sizzles the egg, browns the sausage, flips the pancake high. It is broader and lower than the deep pot, made for quick frying rather than slow boiling. Among the most-used made things in any house, it works hot and hard, then hangs on its hook till the next hungry morning.

North

Deep and round, of metal made,
I boil the stew the cook has laid;
two handles help to lift me hot,
I bubble dinner, big and squat.

East

I have a neck but not a head,
a screw-on cap to seal instead;
I hold the lemonade and pop,
poured glug by glug down to the stop.

South

I am flat with edges low,
I carry cups where they must go;
breakfast comes to bed on me,
I hold the cakes and pot of tea.

West

With a wooden shaft and iron blade,
for chopping wood I'm chiefly made;
one big swing, the log goes crack -
the woodcutter has me at his back.
