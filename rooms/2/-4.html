Tile

A flat baked square, fired hard and laid in rows to make a roof or floor. The tile is a made thing, plain and durable, fitted edge to edge with its fellows against the rain. It keeps the worked, solid south-east, among bricks and built things. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

Step on my shoulder, drive me down,
my flat blade slices through the loam;
I dig the hole, I lift the turf —
the gardener's friend in every home.

East

Out in the field, not on the plate,
my iron prongs pitch up the hay;
I turn the heap, I lift the load —
a farmer's tool of the working day.

South

A flat grey stone split thin and true,
nailed in rows on the roof to keep
the rain outside; or framed in wood,
the schoolchild chalks me, then I'm wiped.

West

Baked from clay in an oven hot,
red and four-square, hard and true;
laid in rows with mortar between,
I build the wall and chimney too.
