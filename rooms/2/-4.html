The Waking Wild

South of the answer the warm one slips away. A step north a warmth turned to you in the black and answered when you called, its step fallen into time with yours; here that warmth thins, the way a held hand loosens in sleep, and the deep takes it back into an older dark — the dark from before any company, that was never once lain in by two. You reach after it, and it is gone. But your reaching is not unmet: for as the warm other leaves, something else comes up to meet your hand, slower, vaster, neither warm nor cold — the root-dark you felt stirring a step west, woken now the whole way, risen solid, turned to you.

It is not the one you lost. It was never a person; it has no breath, no name to give; it is the always-here grown up out of the deep into a presence you could lay a palm against, the wild itself made answerable. Where you reach, it leans; where you draw the spiral on its dark — and there is a surface now, cool and faintly grained as old root — it knows the mark, and takes it, and holds it: the first thing in all the deep to keep the coil instead of letting it close. You have lost the warmth that almost said your name. What you have found does not say it, and does not need to. It simply knows you are here, and has risen, after an age, to stand with you in the dark.

The warm doubled heart of the answering dark has fallen back to one, yours alone again; but under it, strong now where it was sap-slow before, beats the wild's own pulse, the root-dark's ancient heart risen with the rest, vast and unhurried, keeping a time so old your single heart falls into it the way a small step falls into a great one.

To the north the dark warms and the lost other returns, the breath beside you answering, the doubled step. To the east the risen wild grows surer and many, the deep Wyld where the dark speaks and keeps your name, beast and dead and root all turned to you. To the south the presence sinks back below its waking, the seed-dark before there was anything to rise, the deep that has not yet learned to turn. To the west the wild settles to a first faint stirring, the dark only beginning to know you there. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. That warmth has thinned into the older dark; but the wild has risen in its place, and stands with you now, nameless and ancient and unmistakably here.
