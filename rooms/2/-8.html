Jar

You are a round belly of clear glass with a screw-top hat, the keeper of the kitchen's sweet things. Jam glows ruby in you, honey hangs gold, the pickles float in their cloudy brine, the loose change and the buttons rattle when there is nothing else to hold. Your lid is always stuck the first time and yields all at once the second. Held to the light you show off your contents like treasure. Emptied and washed, you are saved 'in case' - for there is always, in the end, something that wants keeping.

North

Six flat sides, a lid on top,
I hold what's tucked inside;
your toys, your tools, the whole lot,
behind my flaps they hide.

East

A wooden stick with a soft grey core,
I write and draw all day;
sharpen me when I'm worn and sore,
and rub mistakes away.

South

A small round can of shining steel,
with beans or soup inside;
the opener bites my lid to peel,
and shows the food I hide.

West

Woven of reed with a handle of cane,
I carry your shopping home;
the eggs, the loaf, the fruit, the grain,
wherever you may roam.
