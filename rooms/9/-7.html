Dress

You are one whole garment from the shoulders to the knees, slipped on over the head and let fall - the frock a girl twirls in, party-bright or everyday-plain. A hand cuts you and sews you in one piece, no trousers, no separate top. You swish when she spins. You hang on a peg, waiting for the day out, the single flowing garment.

North

A strap of leather round the waist,
buckled snug and tight;
it holds your trousers up all day -
punched holes from left to right.

East

A sleeveless top worn next the skin,
soft cotton, snug and white;
no arms, no buttons, slipped straight on -
worn under shirts, from sight.

South

A woolly hand-warmer in the snow,
all four fingers kept as one;
the thumb gets its own little house -
on a string through your coat it's spun.

West

A long warm strip wound round the neck
to keep the cold winds out;
its ends hang down or tuck inside -
of wool, there is no doubt.
