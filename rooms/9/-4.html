Towel

You are a thick woven oblong of cloth, hung on a rail to dry the wet. A maker looped your surface deep so you would drink the water from a body or a dish. You rub the swimmer warm, you wrap the baby, you hang damp over the radiator and steam. Beach towel, bath towel, tea towel, all of you do one job: to take the wet away and give back dry. A humble wrought thing, soft and faithful, always a little damp.

North

A thick warm cover for the bed,
pulled up against the cold;
of woven wool laid over you
when night-time takes a hold.

East

A soft and holey squishy block
that drinks up all the spills;
squeeze me out into the sink,
I mop up what it fills.

South

A small thick carpet on the floor,
laid down before the hearth;
I catch the mud from muddy boots
along the hallway's path.

West

A scented bar that lathers white
and slips around the bath;
it scrubs away the grime and grease
and leaves a sweeter path.
