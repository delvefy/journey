Ding

You are the bright little ring of a small bell - the counter bell, the lift arriving, the timer telling you the cake is done. No hand can hold you; you are a single clear note struck and gone, the politest of all the made sounds. The microwave dings, the shop door dings, the bicycle dings its warning. You are a tiny wrought chime, a one-note message that says, gently and at once: here, now, ready, done.

North

A wobbly, bubbling little laugh
that bursts out through the nose;
it spreads around the quiet class
and grows and grows and grows.

East

The small bright sound two glasses make
when raised up for a toast,
or coins that jingle in a jar -
a tiny ringing boast.

South

The small dry sound a switch will make,
a latch, a pen, a key;
one tidy snap as something catches -
a little click, you see.

West

A loud and sudden slamming sound,
a gunshot or a door;
the firework bursts, the balloon dies,
it echoes round the floor.
