Wand

You are the thin straight stick of magic, a star on the tip or a glow at the end - the wizard's, the fairy's, the conjurer's. Made to point and to command. One flick, one word, and the pumpkin is a coach, the frog a prince. Wave me, and wish - but the magic, they say, was always in the hand.

North

You wear it on your face to hide,
at parties or on stage;
a monster, a hero, a cat, a clown -
pull it on, change your age.

East

Two round lenses in a frame
you wear upon your nose;
they make the blurry letters sharp -
and where you look, they go.

South

A big black pot the witch stirs up,
on the fire it bubbles and steams;
she throws in frogs and bats and worms -
for her wicked spell, it seems.

West

A shiny brass horn you blow and toot,
with three small keys to press;
it blares out loud across the band -
a golden noisy mess.
