Desk

You are a flat-topped table made for work, four square legs and a drawer that slides. Pencils rattle in your tray; a lamp leans over your edge. Children bend above you through the long school morning, and at home the letters and the bills pile up in one corner. A maker sawed and joined and screwed you together - a wrought and solid thing - and yet what happens on you is all thought: sums, words, the day's small plans.

North

A square of glass set in the wall,
I let the daylight through;
shut me, and the cold is barred,
open, the breeze comes too.

East

I hold the door against the night,
a keyhole in my face;
no thief gets by until a key
has turned me in my place.

South

When winter creeps across the bed
I'm spread to keep you warm;
pull me up beneath your chin
and sleep right through the storm.

West

A board set flat against the wall
where books stand in a line;
I hold them up so none will fall -
their resting place is mine.
