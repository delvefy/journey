Ankle

You are the ankle - the narrow hinge between leg and foot, where the two meet and turn. You bend it to point the toes and flex it to climb a hill. Twist it wrong off a kerb and it swells and aches for a week. Slim and strong, you carry the whole weight of a person and still let the foot swivel this way and that. The knot that ties the leg to the ground.

North

On each side of the head I sit,
a little curl of skin;
I cannot say a single word,
but catch each sound within.

East

On glassy wings I dart and hover
above the summer pond;
a long bright body, shining blue -
of zipping flight I'm fond.

South

In the tall grass I leap and spring
on legs that fold up high;
I rub them quick to make a song
beneath the August sky.

West

Small and hard, I'm tucked in the ground,
I wait for rain and sun;
then up I push a thread of green -
a whole new plant's begun.
