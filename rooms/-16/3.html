Spout

You are a spout - the little tube or lip from which water pours out. The teapot's curving spout, the kettle's whistling spout, the gutter's spout that gushes in the rain, the whale's blowhole spout that shoots up like a fountain. Tip me, and out comes a neat bright stream, aimed and arching. You are the gate the water leaves by, the place where a held thing becomes a pouring one.

North

A deep dark shade of blue,
like the sea at night;
sailors wear it on their coats -
it's almost black, not bright.

East

When the tap is turned full on
and water rushes out;
a great big flood comes pouring fast -
a ___ with a shout.

South

White froth of a thousand bubbles,
on the wave and in the bath;
light as air and quick to melt -
it foams along your path.

West

A thick grey mist that hugs the ground
and hides the hills from sight;
you cannot see your hand in me -
a cloud come down to white.
