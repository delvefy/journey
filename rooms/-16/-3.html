Toe

You are a toe - one of the ten little stubs on the ends of your feet, hidden away in socks and shoes. The big one leads, the tiny one trails, and together you grip the ground and help you balance as you walk and run and tiptoe. You wriggle in warm sand and ache in cold water; you stub yourself on the bed leg in the dark. Small and humble, you are the body's furthest outpost.

North

I sit between your eyes and mouth,
two nostrils down below;
I sniff out smells both good and bad,
and run when cold winds blow.

East

A tiny pink curled sea creature,
smaller than your thumb;
it swims in the salty shallow sea -
on a sandwich it can come.

South

One on each side of your head,
I catch each sound and word;
music, voices, barking dogs -
through me they all are heard.

West

The short fat finger set apart,
the strongest one you've got;
hold me up to say 'well done!' -
I help you grip a lot.
