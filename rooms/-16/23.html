muck

Muck is all the dirty stuff you would rather not touch - mud and mess and gunk and worse, smeared on your boots and under your nails. The farmyard is full of it; so is the bottom of the pond and the back of the fridge. Muck out the stable, the farmer says, and means shovel the filth. It is grubbiness itself, the brown sticky business of a world that will not stay clean. Wash your hands when you are done.

North

Wet brown earth all soft and squishy,
after the rain the garden's full;
pigs love to roll, your boots get stuck -
squelch through me, it's wonderful.

East

A clear sweet ring, a tinkling note,
the doorbell sings, the clock marks time;
wind-bells dance and ding in the breeze -
the bell-sound's name, it rhymes with time.

South

I'm green and gooey, cold and thick,
I slip and slither, wet and slow;
a snail will leave me as a trail -
the gungy goo you surely know.

West

On cold clear nights I paint the glass
with feathers white that no bird grew;
I edge the grass and crisp the leaf,
then melt to nothing in morning dew.
