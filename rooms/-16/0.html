Shore

You are the shore, the long edge where the land lies down to meet the sea. Sand and pebbles, shells and weed, the line the waves keep washing clean. Twice a day the tide creeps up to cover you, then slides away and leaves you wet and shining. Gulls walk your rim; children dig and run. You belong to neither water nor dry ground, but to the meeting of the two.

North

I am the sound of water
swung side to side in a pail;
wet and sloppy, back and forth,
I spill over the rail.

East

A great curved bite the sea has taken
out of the bending land;
boats ride safe inside my arms,
on a beach of yellow sand.

South

Five fingers and a flat warm palm,
I clap and wave goodbye;
I hold the spoon, I throw the ball,
and reach up to the sky.

West

Wider than any field or town,
and salty, blue, and deep;
waves roll in and waves roll out,
and great whales in me sleep.
