You are a number, two and two, the legs of a chair, the wheels of a car, the sides of a square, the seasons of the year. A made idea, steady and square. Stand a table on me, and it will not wobble. Count to me, and you have a good firm handful, four-square and sound.

North

Bring the blade down hard and quick,
to cut the wood or carrot through;
the cook does it, the woodsman too -
'thunk!' the pieces flew.

East

A dozen, count them, twelve in all,
the months that make a year;
the hours on the clock's round face -
how many, far and near?

South

One more than seven, one less than ten,
two fours stacked up high;
turn it sideways - there's the sign
for endless, by and by.

West

The bright thin ring of little bells,
of keys and coins that chime;
the sleigh, the ice-cream van's sweet tune -
a tinkling, merry time.
