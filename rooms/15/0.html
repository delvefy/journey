Digger

You are the great yellow machine that bites the earth - a long jointed arm, a toothed steel bucket, scooping out holes for houses and pipes. Made by clever hands to do the work of a hundred spades. Roar and clank and swing, and a mound of dirt rises beside the pit. Every child stops to watch you dig.

North

One more than five, one less than seven,
the dots on a die's high face;
a half of twelve, two threes in a row -
how many in this place?

East

A tall machine on a building site,
with a long arm reaching high;
it lifts the heavy beams of steel -
up against the sky.

South

Flick me up and the light comes on,
flick me down, it's gone;
on the wall by the door I wait -
click, and the dark is done.

West

The big farm machine with great fat wheels
that pulls the plough and cart;
it growls across the muddy field -
the farm's strong beating heart.
