Chop

You are the hard downward cut of the blade - the axe through the log, the knife through the carrot, the karate hand through the air. No gentle nick in you; you come down whole and heavy and split the thing in two. Raise the cleaver, bring it down: thunk, and the job is done.

North

Run your tongue along the treat,
the lolly or the spoon;
the cat does it to wash its fur -
a wet and tasty tune.

East

Cut it thin with a long sharp knife,
the bread, the cake, the ham;
flat pieces fall, one after one -
neat as neat, no sham.

South

One more than three, one less than five,
the legs upon a chair;
the wheels on a car, the sides of a square -
count them, fair and square.

West

The quick small cut of the scissors' blades,
'snip-snip' through paper and thread;
the barber takes a little off -
short clips around your head.
