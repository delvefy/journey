buckle

A buckle is the small metal frame at the end of a belt or strap, with a pin that drops into a hole to hold everything fast. Thread the strap through, pull it snug, push the pin home, and click - you are held. It clasps the belt round your middle, the strap round your shoe, the band round the dog. Shiny and clinking, half jewel and half tool, it is the catch that keeps the strap from ever slipping free.

North

Made from the hide of a cow,
brown and tough, it bends but won't tear;
shoes and belts and jackets too -
a hard-wearing skin to wear.

East

Two rows of little metal teeth,
pull the tab and they lock up tight;
zzzip! to close your coat or bag -
slide it down to open at night.

South

A little round disc upon your shirt,
push me through a slot to fasten you;
sewn on with a cross of thread -
pop me open, pop me through.

West

Two scratchy strips that grip and stick,
press them shut with a riiip and a press;
no laces, no buttons, just a tug -
quick to fasten on a dress.
