Week

You are a measure of seven days, Monday through to Sunday and round again. No one can touch you - you are only the turning of the calendar, the rhythm of school and work and rest. You begin on a fresh page and end with a day for stopping; children count you down to the holidays, and grown-ups count you out in wages. Seven sunrises long, you are a small wheel of time that keeps on turning, week after week.

North

Always coming, never here,
I'm one day past today;
wait all night and I arrive,
then run again away.

East

The coldest season, dark and white,
when snow lies on the ground;
the shortest days, the longest nights,
and frost on all around.

South

The sun goes down behind the hills
and shuts the door on day;
the sky turns red, the shadows long,
then night is on its way.

West

Cast by a wall or leafy bough,
a cool retreat from heat;
step from the sun and into me,
where light and dark first meet.
