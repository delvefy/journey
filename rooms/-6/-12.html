Hedgehog

You meet a small wanderer of the hedge, curled into a ball of spines. A hedgehog is all prickle on the outside and soft beneath - a creature you can lift, if you are gentle, and feel the weight of in your hands. It shuffles through the leaves at dusk, hunting beetles and slugs, then rolls up tight against the cold. Touch it once and you will not forget it: a living thing, made by no hand but its own.

North

With a bushy tail held high
I leap from tree to tree,
and bury nuts in autumn
for the hungry winter me.

East

Jack climbed the tall stalk
that sprang from me one night;
in a green pod I grow,
a seed both plump and white.

South

When a great tree falls,
I am the stub that stays -
a low round stool of wood
left rooted in its place.

West

In black and white stripes
I dig a deep set underground;
I sleep all through the day
and snuffle by night without a sound.
