The Same Grey

North of the last trace and east of the rust plain, the two greys become one. All the way out here, the made world ground to ore and the Waste that was never made meet and stop being different things: the Chrome's dead dust and the Waste's old grey are, you see at last, the same grey, the killed future and the never-born past arrived by their two long roads at one identical nothing. There is no telling which is which. The rust and the never-rusted, the forgotten engine and the thing that was never built, lie here as one flat grey, indistinguishable, the end that disenchantment and emptiness always secretly shared.

It is a strange thing to stand where the two roads meet. Somewhere far behind you a future shed its wonder to be certain, and somewhere else a waste never had any wonder to shed, and here they are the same dust, and neither can be told from the other. You stoop, and the fleck you lift might be a maker's mark worn to a grain or a grain that was never anything; you cannot say, and the grey does not care. Only the whorl in your skin is certainly a made thing, certainly a mark, the one spiral that belongs to neither grey and survives them both.

There is no count. The Chrome's last hum and the Waste's old silence are the same silence here, the dead-machine quiet and the never-quickened quiet one and the same — and your heart beats into it, the one thing that is neither killed nor unborn, keeping a count both greys forgot.

To the north the merger firms toward the Chrome's terminus, the dead-machine pole at the Waste's edge. To the east the grey sorts back into rust, the made world still half-told from the Waste. To the south it thins to the last trace, the made world's final flecks. To the west the one grey widens to the Waste pole, future and past wholly one dust. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the made world's records and the Waste's never-records are the same grey, your name dissolved into both at once — and you carry on with the one mark that belongs to neither, the warm whorl, across a grey where the future and the past have nothing left to tell them apart.
