Smoke

You are what fire breathes out - a grey ghost that climbs from every flame and will not be held. You have no edges and no weight; a hand passes through you and closes on nothing. You sting the eyes, you carry the smell of burning far across the valley, you write slow grey letters on the wind and rub them out again. Where you gather thick, there is heat below. You are the sign of the fire, going up to be lost in the sky.

North

When flames lie down and fires grow old,
I am the light the embers hold;
no flicker and no leaping spark,
a soft warm shine against the dark.

East

I copy you in every way,
I walk beside you through the day;
the sun makes me grow short or tall,
but in the dark I'm gone, that's all.

South

Your nose is how you let me in,
from baking bread or rotting bin;
sweet flowers, fire, the salty sea -
all reach your brain by way of me.

West

I spill from the sun and the open flame,
and chase the shadows away;
you cannot grasp me in your hand,
but by me you see your way.
