Moth

You are the night's grey butterfly, soft and dusty-winged, drawn helplessly to every flame and lamp. By day you fold flat against the bark and disappear; by night you blunder at the window, beating to reach the light that does you no good. Your young are quiet eaters of wool, the small holes in the winter coat. Plain where your cousin is gay, you ask only the dark and the moon to steer by - and break your heart, again and again, on the candle you mistake for it.

North

A small grey thing with a long thin tail,
I scurry round the floor;
I nibble cheese, I leave a trail,
and squeak behind the door.

East

On hushed soft wings I sweep the night,
with great round staring eyes;
I catch the mouse, I take to flight,
'whoo, whoo' my call replies.

South

The soft round foot of dog or cat,
with pads of leather fur;
it leaves a print where it has sat,
and hides its claws for sure.

West

In yellow stripes and black I fly,
with a sting sharp in my tail;
I share the jam, I buzz too nigh,
and make the picnic fail.
