The Dead Weight

South of the pitch and west of the worn dark, the dead substance takes on weight. A step north it held you like cold tar, dense but only holding; a step east the dark had been worn by unthinkable age into a single grey. Here the holding and the wearing settle into one thing — the pitch deepened toward the corner, no longer a medium you move through but a weight that has lain so long it has forgotten it was ever poured. The dead dark compressed, worn, heavy, sinking of its own deadness toward the absolute floor of the Waste.

You are held past moving now, and there is nothing in the weight to move toward. You draw the spiral and the dead weight neither keeps the gesture nor loses it; it only lies on it, the way ages of dead dark lie on everything, pressing the coil thin. Only the whorl in your skin holds out, worn and pressed but unwiped — one small turning the dead weight has leaned on since before there was a word for leaning, and has not yet managed to flatten.

There is no count, only the long lying. Your heart beats once, far down under the mass, and the dead substance takes the sound and holds it; and a great worn while later lets another come — not slowed by cold or age alone now but by the sheer weight of dead dark lying over it, a beat surfacing up through pitch.

To the north the weight lifts toward the pitch, the dead dark merely holding. To the east it thins toward the worn dark, where age and deadness wear to one grey. To the south it sinks toward the floor beneath the floor, the dead weight come finally to rest. To the west the Waste's pole, the corner, dead and worn and weighted through and through, where nothing was ever poured and nothing ever lifts. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the dead weight lies over it with everything else — neither kept nor lost, only pressed flat and held — and still the worn coil in your skin refuses the weight, and still one far beat surfaces up through the pitch.
