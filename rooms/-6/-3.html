Frog

You begin as a black comma in a jelly of eggs, grow a tail, lose it, and climb out onto the bank a small green leaper. You live a double life - half in the cool water, half in the sun-warm mud - and you are built for the leap, all folded legs and sudden spring. Your tongue is faster than your eye can follow. On warm wet nights you fill the dark with a chorus of croaks, a whole pond singing one wet, ribbiting song.

North

A hollow mouth in the side of a hill,
where the daylight cannot go;
cool and dark and dripping stone,
the bear sleeps down below.

East

Sharp and small upon the briar,
I make the picker bleed;
I guard the berry and the rose,
a spike the brambles breed.

South

A whiskered scrap that's quick and shy,
I scurry by the wall;
I steal a crumb, I gnaw a hole,
the smallest beast of all.

West

I never leave the water cold,
with silver scales and fins to row;
I breathe the stream, I never blink,
and dart where currents flow.
