The Gloaming

South of the Waste the grey begins to go out. The flat daylit pole a step north showed its emptiness plainly, answerless under an even light; here that light is leaving, not setting but simply failing, the grey thickening toward a night with no stars promised in it, and the cold coming up out of the ground to meet the dark coming down. The Waste tilts into evening. What was an emptiness you could at least see is becoming one you cannot — unanswered, and now unseen as well, the worst of both the poles it lies between.

You call into it, the old wish to be answered, and the gloaming gives back nothing, and takes even the sight of the nothing as it darkens. Distance gives out with the light: the receding horizon of the edgeless Waste a step east is here swallowed, so there is no far and no near, only the grey going dark around you. You draw the spiral and can barely see it before the dusk takes it; only the whorl in your skin stays sure, felt if not seen, dimming with the day but holding.

The wind that crossed the open Waste is dropping as the dark comes, toward the standstill it will find a step on; there is no count but its falling, and then not even that. Only your heart, beating into a grey that no longer answers and no longer shows, unseen and unheard both, keeping on.

To the north the grey lifts back to the daylit Waste, the pole that at least shows its emptiness. To the east the dark opens to the edgeless Waste, a horizon still receding in a last light. To the south the gloaming sinks wholly into the night-Waste, the lightless dead dark. To the west the pole absolute, dark and answerless, where even the dusk is forgotten. You were going somewhere, under a name. The Waste goes dark around you now as well as silent, your call lost to both the night and the nothing — and you go on by the felt whorl alone, into a grey that will not answer and will no longer even let itself be seen.
