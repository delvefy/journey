Tree

You began as a seed no bigger than a tear, and now you hold up half the sky. Roots grip the dark earth; a trunk no two arms can circle climbs straight up; and your green crown fills with leaves, with birds, with wind. You give shade in summer, apples in autumn, and a place for every child to climb. You stand a hundred years where you were planted, and ask only for rain and light.

North

I am the sandy edge of land,
where waves run up to play;
you build a castle, dig a moat,
and splash here all the day.

East

I am the brightest part of the plant,
with petals soft and gay;
the bees all come to drink my sweet,
I bloom then fade away.

South

I am a hollow in the hill,
all dark and cool and deep;
the bats hang up inside of me,
where bears may go to sleep.

West

I have two wings, a beak, two legs,
I build a nest up high;
I sing at dawn, I lay an egg,
and soar across the sky.
