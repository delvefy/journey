Paw

You are the soft padded foot of the four-legged ones, the cushioned step of cat and dog and bear. Beneath your fur lie tough little pads that feel the ground and grip it, and tucked between them the claws that show only when needed. You pad silent across the floor, you knead the blanket, you reach up to scratch at the closed door. A muddy print of you is left across the clean kitchen tiles. Warm, clumsy and trusting when laid in a human hand, you are the gentle end of the animal.

North

Grey of wing, by night I fly,
and beat against the pane;
the candle's deadly flame I try,
and nibble wool again.

East

A curved sharp hook upon the toe,
of cat and hawk and crab;
I grip, I scratch, I will not go,
the sharpest thing I grab.

South

The soft warm coat of cat or bear,
that keeps the cold outside;
it covers them with downy hair,
and you can stroke its hide.

West

A hard curved spike upon the head,
that bull and goat both grow;
they lower me and charge ahead,
and butt their stubborn foe.
