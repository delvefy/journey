Cave

You are the dark held inside the hill - a mouth in the rock that breathes cool air and never blinks. Step in past the daylight and the world goes quiet and close around you. Water that took a thousand years to fall drips from your ceiling, and grows it back, drop by patient drop, into teeth of stone. You keep what the weather cannot reach: old bones, old paintings, a bat or two folded in the roof. You are a room the earth made without hands.

North

I stand with roots dug in the ground,
and lift my arms up high;
in summer green, in autumn brown,
my leaves come floating by.

East

I rise up green and gently round,
not steep to climb with rope;
you puff to reach my grassy crown,
then roll back down my slope.

South

I sit upon a lily pad,
all green and cool and wet;
I leap to snatch a passing fly,
the best jumper you have met.

West

A small still pool, no waves, no tide,
where ducks and lilies love to ride;
the frogs all sing around my rim,
and dragonflies above me skim.
