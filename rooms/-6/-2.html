The Pitch

South of the gloaming and east of the lightless, the dark stops being a space and becomes a substance. The night-Waste a step each way was at least an emptiness you stood in; here the dead dark has thickened into a medium, a lightless inert pitch that fills the room to its corners and holds you in it like cold tar — not pressing, not drowning, only holding, dense and dead and absolute, the Waste's dark gone solid. This is the dark pole: not living dark, not empty dark, but dead dark, the corpse of darkness, inert through and through.

You move in it the way you would move in deep water, slow and held, and it closes behind you without a trace. There is nothing here to find and nothing to do but be held by the dead substance. You draw the spiral and the pitch takes your hand's motion and gives nothing back, the gesture lost in a medium too dead to keep or lose it; only the whorl in your skin stays, held inert in the pitch, the one thing in it that is not dead. The pitch presses close around that one warm coil as if it were the last warmth in a cold world, which, here, it is.

There is no count. The dead substance holds your heart in it like everything else, slowing it not by cold or age but by sheer inertia, the pitch too dead to let a beat ring — one stroke, swallowed whole, and a long held nothing, and another.

To the north the pitch thins to the gloaming, the Waste going dark but not yet solid. To the east it opens to the lightless, a dead dark you can at least stand in. To the south it deepens toward the corner, the dead substance at its absolute. To the west the Waste pole, dead through and through, the corner. You were going somewhere, under a name. The pitch holds it dead and inert with the rest of you — neither answered nor forgotten, only held, dead, in the dark gone solid — and still the one warm coil refuses, held close, to go dead with it.
