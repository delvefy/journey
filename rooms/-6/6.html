Sunset

You are the day's last extravagance, the sky set ablaze as the sun goes down - banks of cloud lit rose and orange and molten gold, fading slowly to ash and violet and the first cool grey. For a few minutes the whole west is on fire and the world stops to look. Then the colours drain away, the air cools, the birds fall silent, and the first star pricks the dimming blue. You are the beautiful goodbye the daylight makes, the same and never twice the same, and gone before you can be kept.

North

Seven days from end to end,
from Monday round to Sun;
Saturday and Sunday, friend,
are best when work is done.

East

The early half before the noon,
with breakfast and with school;
the dew not dry, the sun still soon,
the air still fresh and cool.

South

The sun sinks low, the lamps are lit,
the supper's on the table;
the day winds down, we rest and sit,
and talk while we are able.

West

The season after winter's cold,
when buds and blossoms show;
the lambs are born, the green unfolds,
and warm soft breezes blow.
