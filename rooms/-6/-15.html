Pinecone

You are a pinecone - the hard brown seed-case of the pine tree, all overlapping woody scales, shaped like a little fat egg. You drop from the high dark branches onto the forest floor, and when the day is dry your scales spring open to let the winged seeds fly. Children gather you for crafts; squirrels nibble you to bits. Sticky with sap, rough to the hand, you are the forest's neat brown parcel.

North

A shiny brown nut from the chestnut tree,
hard and smooth and round;
thread me on a string to swing -
the best one you have found.

East

A crisp green leafy salad plant,
it grows in a loose round head;
crunchy, cool, and pale inside,
in sandwiches it's spread.

South

A feathery green plant of the woods,
with curling leaves like lace;
no flowers at all, just fronds that uncurl
in a shady, damp old place.

West

The slowest beast in all the wood,
I hang from the branch all day;
so slow that moss grows on my fur -
I creep, I never play.
