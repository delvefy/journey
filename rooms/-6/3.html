The Grey Terminus

West of the hush and north of the same grey, the dead machine reaches its terminus. A step east a hum still had a last thread to give up; a step south the killed future and the never-made lay as one flat dust. Here both come to their end at once — the Chrome's terminus laid against the open edge of the Waste, grey and level and lit by no source you can name. The made world does not end in wreck but in this: a cold uniform glare over nothing, the last idea of the made gone out into the grey silence, neither humming now nor wholly dark.

There is a light here, if you can call it that — a dead even grey, the colour of a pane with nothing behind it, the ghost of all the Chrome's lamps averaged down to one flat nothing. You draw the spiral in it and the glare neither holds the line nor lets it go; the mark only sits, unlit and unshadowed, the way everything sits in a light that falls from nowhere. Only the whorl in your skin keeps a true turn — the one spiral with a maker, the last warm mark at the dead edge of the made.

There is no count, only the dead carrier under everything: not a hum now, past even the hush's fading, but the memory of a hum, a flat dead level where a signal used to ride. Your heart beats across it, the one live tick at the terminus of the made hour, keeping a time the dead glare has no way to keep.

To the north the terminus firms toward the dead-machine pole, the Chrome's last cold idea at the Waste's edge. To the east the hush returns, a silence with the hum still fading in it. To the south the same grey, where the killed future and the never-made are one dust. To the west the open Waste takes the terminus wholly, grey silence to the pole, the made forgotten past even its ending. You were going somewhere, under a name, and the made world once carried it in light. Here the light is dead and even and means nothing — your name unlit in the grey glare — and still the warm coil turns, and still one live beat crosses the dead level, unsignalled, going on.
