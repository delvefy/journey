The Last Trace

North of the Waste and east of the last post, the made world dwindles to a scatter. Past the final post there were no more objects, only the open grey; here, strewn faint across that grey, are the very last flecks of everything that was ever made — a grain of rust, a scale of ore, a crumb with the ghost of a cast mark, set in the pure Waste like the last few stars before a sky goes wholly dark. This is the made world's last trace: not a ruin, not a wreck, only a thinning sprinkle of what was once an age of iron, dissolving grain by grain into a forgetting that has no edge.

You stoop and lift a fleck, and on it, almost, the spiral — a maker's mark worn to a single rust-grain, the last legible crumb of all that was stamped and counted and built. The records of the made world have come to this, scattered to grains in the grey, every reckoned name among them dissolved to rust, yours a fleck somewhere in the scatter, its last trace going. Only the whorl in your skin keeps a whole coil, the one intact spiral in a world thinned to its last grains.

There is no count, only the wind moving the last flecks, a grain at a time, out into the pure Waste — the made hour reduced to the faint stir of rust dissolving, and then not even that. Your heart beats on, the one made thing the Waste never made, among the last traces of all the rest.

To the north the flecks gather toward the made world's farthest dead reach, the Chrome's edge firming out of the grey. To the east the scatter draws to a single last post, the final made thing standing. To the south the flecks thin into the pure Waste pole, nothing made, nothing answering. To the west they dissolve wholly, the pure Waste, no trace of the made ever again. You were going somewhere, and the made world held it, once, in all its records — come now to a scatter of rust-grains in the grey, your name among them, the last trace dissolving as you cross it, the whole coil kept only in your skin.
