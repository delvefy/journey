Midnight

You are the deep middle of the night, the stroke of twelve when one day dies and the next is not yet born. The whole house sleeps; the clock speaks alone. You belong to owls and foxes, to bakers at their ovens and children awake with fright. Old tales say the world goes strange at your hour - ghosts walk, wishes turn, the year tips over. You cannot be touched or kept, only counted: twelve slow bells, and then the long climb back toward the light.

North

The sun goes down all red and low,
the lamps come on indoors;
the day is done, the supper's slow,
this quiet hour is yours.

East

Blow the candle, close your eyes,
I fill the room up tight;
no colour lives, no shadow lies,
I am the loss of light.

South

The ember keeps me when the flame is gone,
the firefly wears me green;
a soft warm shine that lingers on,
the gentlest light there's seen.

West

The east turns pink, the cock will crow,
the night begins to thin;
the great gold rim climbs up to show
the brand-new day begin.
