Evening

You are the day folding down toward sleep, the soft brown hour when the sun has gone but the dark is not yet sure of itself. The lamps come on one by one along the street; the cooking smells drift out of open doors; the birds settle, the children are called in. Work is laid aside, and the family draws together over supper and slow talk. You ask nothing, only that the day be let go gently. Neither bright nor black, you are the world's long quiet breath before night.

North

The sky burns red and gold and rose,
the sun slips out of sight;
the day's last blaze before it goes,
and hands the world to night.

East

When the sun is straight overhead,
and shadows hide away;
the bell rings out, the table's spread,
the middle of the day.

South

The clock strikes twelve, the house is still,
the deepest part of dark;
when ghosts are said to roam at will,
and not a dog will bark.

West

The warmest season of the four,
with long bright days of sun;
the children swim, the ices pour,
and school is done and gone.
