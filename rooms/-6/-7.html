Fur

You are the warm coat an animal wears, grown thick against the winter and shed soft in the spring. No tailor cut you; you sprang from the skin of fox and cat and bear, a thousand fine hairs holding the heat close. A hand drawn down you finds you soft, and a cat will lift her back to meet it. You are the wild's own blanket, made by no loom, only by the long cold and the need to be warm.

North

A soft and padded foot it is,
with claws tucked out of sight;
a cat will wash one with her tongue
and prowl on it at night.

East

A bird's hard mouth, two horny halves,
no teeth are set within;
it cracks the seed, it spears the worm,
and preens the feathered skin.

South

I purr beside the fire all day
and wash my whiskered face;
I chase the mouse, I climb the tree,
and land with easy grace.

West

Cut from the sheep's warm winter coat,
I'm spun into a thread;
knitted up to socks and scarves
to keep you warm in bed.
