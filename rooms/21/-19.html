hacksaw

A hacksaw is the saw you reach for when the job is metal, not wood - a thin, fine-toothed blade stretched tight in a sturdy bow of a frame. Where a big wood saw would just skid and screech, the hacksaw bites slowly through a pipe or a bolt or a padlock, back and forth, back and forth, leaving a little pile of bright metal dust. Change the blade when it goes blunt. Patient and tough, it cuts the things nothing else will.

North

Rough scratchy paper, gritty and tough,
rub it on wood to make it smooth;
it wears the bumps and splinters off -
scritch-scratch, it sands the groove.

East

A rubber cup upon a stick,
push it down on the blocked-up drain;
squelch and pull with all your might -
pop! the water flows again.

South

A long-handled tool with a flat blade end,
chop the weeds and break the soil;
drag it through the vegetable patch -
a gardener's friend for the digging toil.

West

A long iron bar with a curved flat end,
shove it under and lever it up;
prise the lid off the heavy crate -
pop! with a heave and a tug.
