All

South and west, where nothing was made and everything can be held, the world keeps the heaviest of its corners. Not a stone, not the earth, but every stone and all the earth at once — the whole tangible weight of the hoard pressed into a single word. You have gathered the world room by room to arrive here. Stand in it. There is nothing left outside these walls; what you are standing in is everything there is.

North

I am the dark that feeds the green,
not all of sand, nor all of clay,
but the rich third the spade turns up
where the world rots itself to stay;
no gardener wants a better bed.

East

I am the metal still asleep,
the wealth a mountain will not spend;
fire must wed me first to air
before I harden to a coin or blade.
Dig, and you hold me; smelt, and I end.
