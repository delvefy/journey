Post

You are an upright of wood or iron, driven deep and standing firm - the gatepost, the fence post, the signpost at the crossroads. A hand sinks you in the ground and there you hold, year after year, while gates swing on you and signs hang from you. Sun and rain weather you grey. You do not move; you only mark the spot and bear the load.

North

A long flat board of sawn-up wood,
straight and smooth and wide;
you walk it on the pirate ship
or lay it as a bridge to stride.

East

You pull it on over your head,
or button up the front;
it covers chest and back and arms -
a collar and two cuffs.

South

A line of posts with rails between
that runs around the field;
it keeps the sheep from wandering off
and marks where land is sealed.

West

Small and round and green or purple,
it grows in heavy bunches;
you eat it fresh or squeeze for wine -
sweet at picnic lunches.
