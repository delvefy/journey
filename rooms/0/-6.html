Coal

You are a lump of black rock that remembers the sun. Pressed for an age out of old forests buried deep, you hold their long-ago summers locked away in your weight. Cold, you are just a dirty stone that blacks the hands. But lay you on the fire and you give it all back at last - a fierce red heat that warms the whole house through the winter, and burns down, in the end, to a little grey ash.

North

I am the white grain from the sea,
I make your dinner tasty;
too much of me will spoil the dish,
so sprinkle, don't be hasty.

East

I have a handle and a blade,
but I am not a knife;
you dig the garden deep with me,
and bury seeds for life.

South

When the fire has burned right out,
I am the grey that stays;
soft and powdery in the grate,
the end of all the blaze.

West

I carry my house upon my back,
and creep along so slow;
I leave a shining silver trail
on every path I go.
