Silence

The room holds its breath. Whatever sound you brought in with you is swallowed at the door, and now there is nothing — no drip, no echo, not your own heartbeat once you still. Silence cannot be touched or made; it is only the absence of every other thing, and it is broken the instant you name it. Speak softly. Each wall keeps one quiet word, and the right one opens.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

The softest way a word can go,
breathed at your ear so none else hears;
in the still library I am kept,
and secrets pass through me for years.

East

Lift up your voice and let it ride
on a tune that climbs and falls;
the choir makes me, the lark makes me,
and I drift down the echoing halls.

South

Speak to the well, or the empty hall,
and a beat behind, your voice comes back;
fainter each time, it fades to none —
your own last word along the track.

West

Free, yet you take me all day long;
the cold air shows me, white and thin;
blow out the candle with one of me,
the doctor says, “now hold it in.”
