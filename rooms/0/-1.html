Stone

Everything here is heavy. The floor is one unbroken slab; the walls are dry-stacked rock that has not shifted in a thousand years and will not shift for you. A coldness comes up through the soles of your feet — the patience of things that do not grow and do not rot. Whatever the next rooms hold, you will earn them one stubborn word at a time.

North

I have teeth, and I never eat,
I have a stem, and grow no flower;
fit me to my waiting slot
and I will hand you power.

East

Round and small, I cross the land,
I bear two faces, carry none;
spent too freely from the hand,
I am quickly gone.

South

I am the white frame under the flesh,
I do not bend and do not bleed;
dig deep enough in any grave
and I am all that's freed.

West

I go down as the tree goes up,
I drink the rain the soil has kept;
unseen, I hold the oak in place
while all its leaves have slept.
