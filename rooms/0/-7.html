Ash

You are what is left when the fire has said everything it had to say - a soft grey powder, weightless, still faintly warm. Once you were wood, a whole bright blaze; now you are the quiet after, fine enough to lift on a breath and scatter. Gardeners feed you back to the soil, where you make things grow; the wind takes the rest. You are the proof that something burned here, and the gentle, final form of nearly everything the flames have touched.

North

A hard black rock dug from the deep,
I burn with a red-hot glow;
I warm the hearth, I drive the train,
the blackest stone you know.

East

Two long legs with rungs in rows,
I lean against the wall;
you climb my steps to reach the roof,
so do not slip and fall.

South

A block of clay baked hard and red,
I'm stacked to build a wall;
with many more I make a house,
strong and square and tall.

West

Eight legs and a clever silken thread,
I spin a web to trap;
I wait so still in the corner's gloom,
for flies to fill my lap.
