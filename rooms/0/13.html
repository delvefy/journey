Doze

You are a light drifting half-sleep, the gentle slide into a nap you didn't quite mean to take - in the armchair after lunch, on the warm train, in front of the fire. No hand makes you; the heavy eyelids do it, the head nodding, the book sliding off the knee. You wake with a little start, not sure you slept at all.

North

A short little sleep in the middle of the day,
ten minutes, no more, no less;
you set the alarm, then bash it again -
'just five more,' you confess.

East

Turn over and over along the ground,
the way a ball will go;
down the grassy hill you tumble,
round and round, just so.

South

When Mum says 'shush, be ___ now,'
she means: make not a peep;
no shouting and no banging drums -
just still enough to sleep.

West

The tiny dancing flecks of light
on the sea, on the snow, on a ring;
a thousand winking little stars -
a bright and twinkly thing.
