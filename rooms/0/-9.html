Tile

You are a flat hard square baked from clay or cut from stone, laid down by the hundred to make a surface smooth and clean. On the roof you overlap in neat grey rows and turn the heaviest rain away; on the floor and wall you shine, scrubbed and cool underfoot, easy to wipe and hard to harm. The mosaic-maker sets your small bright kind into pictures that outlast empires. Plain and square and patient, you ask only to be laid level and grouted true, and you will keep the wet out for a hundred years.

North

A red baked block of hardened clay,
laid row on row up high;
I build the wall, I build the way,
a house that scrapes the sky.

East

A big rough bag of cloth or jute,
tied tight up at the neck;
I hold the spuds, the grain, the loot,
slung heavy on your back.

South

A long flat board of sawn-up wood,
laid down to make a floor;
or across the stream where once I stood,
a bridge from shore to shore.

West

A great hard lump of solid stone,
too heavy to be thrown;
I sit unmoved, all grey and lone,
the mountain's stubborn bone.
