Whisper

A whisper moves along the wall, almost not a sound at all — words shaped without a voice, so faint you must lean in to be sure they were ever there. It is the quietest of made things, a hair away from silence, and you can no more hold it than you can hold a passing thought. Speak the name — louder than a whisper — at each wall to pass.

North

I come to you when eyes are shut
and paint the dark with sights;
a king, a fall, a long-dead face —
I rule your sleeping nights.

East

An old wives' one, a fairy one,
a tall one stretched too far;
spun beside the winter fire,
I say how things once were.

South

Speak, and you have lost me;
shut the door on every sound;
I am the deep and listening peace
where not one noise is found.

West

The sound of relief, the sound of grief,
a breath pushed slow and deep;
lovers make me, the weary too,
and sleepers in their sleep.
