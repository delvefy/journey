The Composing Room

The made world keeps the promise the water-clock made. You climb into a long room lit by the same straight grey daylight, falling now through a row of slotted windows onto a machine of black iron and oak: a press. A great screw rises through its heart, threaded in a slow steel spiral, and beneath it a bed of set type waits face-up, every letter a small reversed thing of lead, inked and patient. The whole room smells of oil and lampblack and damp paper — the warm honest reek of words about to be many.

You lean over the bed and read the type backward, the way the printer must, and the lines resolve into sentences you half-know, a page from a book you feel you were once reading, or once writing. One line near the foot has come loose: the letters have spilled and lie jumbled in the channel, a name pied into nonsense, the very name you keep reaching for, scattered an inch from sense. You could set it right. Your fingers know the weight of a sort without being told. But you do not know which letter goes first, and the line waits, broken, for a compositor who has stepped out.

The thin even click of the clock below has filled out into a working beat: somewhere down the room a press is pulling though no one stands at it, the long rhythmic thump and sigh of the screw biting paper and letting go — the bell grown into a machine that prints the hour instead of telling it.

To the south the press gives way to the climbing water-clock, the made thing still only counting, the number before the word. To the north the presses multiply and quicken past the hand, the word flung faster than reading onto wire and onto light, a made day of nothing but print. To the east the set letters begin to mean what they spell, words that do the thing they say, the loose name half-wanting to stand and answer. To the west the printing goes cold and clerkish, ledgers and notices, the word stamped out and believed by no one. You were going somewhere, and someone was setting it in lead. The pied name lies in the channel, an inch from sense, waiting for the hand that knows the order.
