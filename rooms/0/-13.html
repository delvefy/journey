Stick

You are a thin dry length of fallen wood, broken from the tree and lying in the path. No hand makes you; the wind and the snapping branch give you up. A dog brings you back again and again to be thrown; a child pokes the fire with you, draws in the mud with you, fights a duel with you. Plain, brown, and handy.

North

A line of posts with rails between
that runs around the field;
it keeps the sheep from wandering off -
the boundary, sealed.

East

An open shoe for a summer's day,
just straps across the toes;
your bare feet breathe, no socks at all -
to the beach in them one goes.

South

A long thin rod of wood or steel,
stood up straight and tall;
the flag flies high on top of one -
you climb it, lean, or haul.

West

A round fruit named for its own colour,
sweet and full of juice;
you peel its dimpled skin in bits -
the segments come loose.
