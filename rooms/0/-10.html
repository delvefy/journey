Plank

You are a long flat board of sawn timber, straight-grained and splintery, cut from the trunk of a tree by the saw. Once you were living wood; now you are wrought into a useful length, laid down for a floor, nailed up for a fence, run out over the water for the pirates' grim walk. You bridge the ditch, you shelve the wall, you bear the weight laid on you and complain only with a creak. Solid, honest, and plain, you are the tree made flat and faithful.

North

A flat square slab of baked hard clay,
laid in rows upon the floor,
or stuck upon the bathroom wall -
smooth and cold, and more.

East

A heavy shoe that rises high
to guard the leg and ankle;
it tramps through mud and snow and rain
where lighter shoes would rankle.

South

A stout upright stake of wood
driven in the ground;
it holds the fence, it bears the sign,
it props the gate around.

West

A small round jewel of milky white
grown in an oyster's shell;
no jeweller cut me, the sea did -
a treasure none can tell.
