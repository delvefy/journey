Ash

Ash lies cold and grey across the hearth, all that the fire left behind. It is the soft remains of wood and bone, fine enough to lift on a single breath yet heavy in the hand by the scoopful — the very end of burning, touchable and made by no one. It rests low on the tangible axis, due south of the origin. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

Hard and pale, I prop you up,
two hundred make your frame;
snap me and the limb hangs limp —
the doctor knows my name.

East

Flour and yeast and water kneaded,
left to rise, then baked;
sliced for toast or torn in chunks,
the daily loaf you make.

South

White as snow but never cold,
I sting upon a wound;
I sit in cellars on the board
and make the dull meat good.

West

Smooth and white, I have no door,
yet life is locked inside;
the hen sits warm above my shell
until the chick has tried.
