Shell

On the deep tangible axis, the sea leaves a little house behind. A shell is no one's craft — the soft thing inside grew it grain by grain, then left — and it is solidly tangible: cool and ridged in your palm, it survives the creature by a thousand years. Hold it to your ear and the whole ocean seems to hum inside. Children fill their pockets with them. Speak the names, and let the curled door open.

North

Hard and white, I prop you up,
I'm hidden in the meat;
I'm what the doctor sees in scans,
I'm in your hands and feet.

East

Toothed and flat and bright with steel,
I bite the wood in two;
I screech along, then back, then on,
until I've cut clean through.

South

A million grains too small to count,
I warm your wandering feet;
I make the castles on the shore
the rising tide will eat.

West

Pink and long with not a bone,
I burrow through the earth;
the early bird will gobble me,
the angler knows my worth.
