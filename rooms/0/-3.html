The Warm Dark

The last stain of light goes out behind you, and there is nothing made to find. No flint now, no curl of worked bone, no clay willing to take a mark — the urge to make a thing has not been born this deep, and there is not even cold enough to fear: only a close, blood-warm dark, thick as the inside of a closed hand, and your own body standing in it, the single certain thing left in a world that has put everything else away.

You cannot see your hand, so you feel instead, and the body answers questions the mind has lost. Your thumb finds the pad of a finger and there it is again, the spiral, whorled into your own skin, carried with you the whole way — the one mark that needs no chalk and no clay. And when you reach out into the dark your fingers brush a warmth that is not yours, the give of someone breathing close, someone who ought to have a name, who was perhaps going where you were going — and then the dark shifts and the warmth is gone, and you cannot say if it was ever a hand and not your own heat thrown back at you.

There is no drip, no bell, no tick, no voice. Only the heartbeat the room above named the first clock, louder here because it is all there is — your own blood keeping the oldest time, the count you were already keeping when you woke, the one the made hours and the tolled bells were only ever copying.

To the north the dark gives back a first cold edge, the unstruck flint and the hand that knows it, the made thing about to be born. To the east the warmth you brushed stays and turns toward you, the dark waking into company, shades that breathe and may yet speak. To the west the warmth leaches out and the body is left alone with its own cooling, the dark emptying toward ash and bone. To the south there is older dark still, before even the body, the deep that never needed anyone to lie in it. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Your heart keeps the count of it in the dark, and will not be hurried, and will not stop.
