Key

You wake holding a key, though you cannot say what it opens. The walls are bare stone, and set into each of the four is a door with no handle — only a narrow slot, and beside it a riddle scratched into the rock. The key in your hand fits none of them. Then you understand: here the lock is the riddle, and the key is the word that answers it. Speak the word, and the way will open.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

I give you back your own last word,
I have no mouth, and I'm not heard
until you speak; in cave and hall
I am the answer the cliffs recall.

East

I have a tongue but cannot taste,
a mouth that never eats;
I speak just once, and only when
a hammer-stroke me meets.

South

I lie in the field and build the wall,
I do not grow, I do not fall;
strike me, and I will give you fire,
stack me, and I will stand an age entire.

West

I run but never walk a mile,
I have a mouth but never smile,
I have a bed but never sleep,
a bank, but not a coin to keep.
