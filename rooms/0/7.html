Yawn

You arrive uninvited, in the dull middle of a lesson or the last hour of a long night, and there is almost no stopping you. The jaw stretches wide, the eyes water, a great slow breath is drawn and let go - and the body has quietly asked for more air, or for bed. Strangest of all, you are catching: see one and you make one, and even reading the word can start you. A small honest signal that the day, or the company, has gone on long enough.

North

A rumbly, buzzing, sawing sound,
that comes from those asleep;
it shakes the room, it wakes the dog,
but not the one in slumber deep.

East

Press your lips and make a tune,
no words come out at all;
the bee and the wire both do it too,
a low and droning call.

South

'Be still,' a finger to the lips,
a whispered call for quiet;
when the baby sleeps, we ask for me,
no noise, no fuss, no riot.

West

A wanderer of the midnight sky,
I drag a tail of glowing light;
I blaze across then disappear,
for years until my next flight.
