Brick

You are a small block of clay baked hard in the kiln, red and rough and heavier than you look. Alone you are nothing much - a doorstop, a thing to throw. But laid in your thousands, corner on corner, bound with grey mortar, you become the wall, the chimney, the whole tall house that keeps the weather out. The wolf could not blow your kind down. Plain and patient and square, you are how people first learned to pile up shelter, one steady course at a time.

North

When the fire has burned right down,
I'm the soft grey dust that stays;
weightless, warm, of palest brown,
the end of the fire's days.

East

Woven of reed with a handle on top,
I carry the eggs and bread;
to market and home I never stop,
all heaped up high instead.

South

A flat hard square of baked red clay,
I cover roof and floor;
in rows I keep the rain away,
and shine when scrubbed once more.

West

A small smooth stone the river rolled,
round and grey and worn;
you skip me where the water's cold,
or pocket me at morn.
