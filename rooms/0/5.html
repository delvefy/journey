The Made Day

North of the wire the word lets go of its last body. A step down the letters still had a shape, dots pricked in a ribbon, a thing the hand could lift if not read; here even that is gone. The word has become light and number outright, flung up off the wire into a made day with no lamp and no sky — a bright air thick with running figures and streaming script, the whole word of the world poured out at once into a brightness that needs no page, no pane, no paper, nothing to be written on but the lit air itself.

It is neither cruel nor kind. This is not the dead even glare of the disenchanted works, nor the warm pulse of the lit charms — only the pure made day of the axis, the word shed of its body and not yet given a soul, signal for signal's sake, vast and even and bright. Your name is in the stream. It pours past in light with all the rest, set true and flung fast, a thread of brightness in a torrent of it, and when you reach the made day pours it through your fingers like water through a grate, everywhere in the air at once and nowhere you can close a hand. The spiral runs the stream as a coil of light, a slow turning glyph the figures wind around, the one mark in all the brightness that is not a number.

The sounder's ping has spread into a single bright tone, the made day humming to itself, the bell flung so wide and so fast it no longer rings but holds — one note, continuous, the hour become a carrier the whole day rides, counting everything and striking nothing.

To the north the made day refines to the pole, light and number with no word left in it, pure thought streaming for its own sake, the cold bright peak. To the east the flung light begins to mean and act, the word doing what it says, the bright air answering to a name. To the south the day narrows back to a wire and its tape of dots, the word given a body again, slow enough to lift. To the west the made day goes cold and clerkish, the stream filed and unread, signal with the sense stamped out. You were going somewhere, under a name. It pours through the made day in light, everywhere and at once, flung faster and brighter than ever — and never once slow enough, anywhere in all that brightness, for you to take it up.
