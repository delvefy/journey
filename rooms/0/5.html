The Bright Wire

Above the quickening press the paper falls away. A step down the sheets still stacked and toppled, the word held a moment in lead and ink before it flew; here there is no sheet left to hold it. The presses have shed their paper entirely. What stands in the bright even day is light alone — ranks of flameless filament burning steady, and the word flung onto them as pure number, nothing set in lead now and everything sent, the made day climbing the last hand's-breadth into a room that prints nothing and transmits all of it.

It is neither warm nor cold here, only quick. The word does not do what it says, the way it half-begins to a step east, and it has not gone clerkish and unread, the way it greys a step west; it simply travels, multiplied past counting, light copying light down a line whose end you cannot see. The same broken line you left pied below runs everywhere at once now, struck in light a thousand times a second, an inch from sense and flung the faster for being wrong, sent before it can be set right and sent again behind that, the room paying it endlessly away.

At the room's heart the wire is wound to a bright coil, the spiral paying the word out as light, turning too fast to follow — and along it, sent and resent, runs your name, the name an inch from yours, a thousand of it racing ahead down the line. You reach for one pass of it and it is already gone on, the next already behind. The press's chatter has thinned to a clean fast pulse, a bell that only sends, tolling the hour by flinging it onward and keeping nothing. North the word sheds even the wire, pure number at the pole, everywhere and nowhere and set in nothing. East it begins to mean what it spells, the name half-wanting to stand and answer. South it slows back to the racing presses, lead and oil and paper. West it goes cold and clerkish, the same speed sent for no one. You were going somewhere, under a name. It is light now, and sent, and a thousand times ahead of you down the bright line.
