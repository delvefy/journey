Night

The sun goes down and the world changes hands. Night is not a thing but a turning-away, the slow roll of the earth into its own shadow until the lamps come on and the sky fills with the small cold fires of the stars. You cannot touch it, yet it presses close at the window and pools in the corners of the room. It is the oldest quiet there is - the half of every day that belongs to sleep and to dreaming.

North

I am the word you say for 'quiet',
a finger on the lips to try it;
when babies sleep and stories end,
I am the soft 'shh', your friend.

East

I am a thing that is not true,
a tale told sly to trick or fool you;
your nose may grow, your cheeks turn red,
when one of me slips out instead.

South

I close your eyes when night is deep,
and down a dark soft well you creep;
I bring you dreams till morning light,
I hold you safe and still all night.

West

I sail the sky when day is gone,
I'm round and bright, then thin, then none;
I light the night with silver glow,
and watch the quiet world below.
