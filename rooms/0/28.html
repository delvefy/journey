A hundred and seventy. You are high up the counting ladder now, where the numbers come in big round tens and each step is ten more than the last. One hundred, then sixty more, then ten on top of that. It is taller than anyone you know is old, more than the steps up a steep hill, a number you reach only by counting and counting. Up here the air of numbers is thin, and still it climbs.

North

Ten more than me, the next rung up,
a hundred and eighty all told;
count up by tens and here you land -
the highest score in darts, I'm told!

East

Curl your finger, wave your hand,
'come here, come closer,' with no word;
I call you over with a sign -
a silent summons, never heard.

South

Ten less than me, one rung below,
a hundred and sixty, count by tens;
step back down the ladder once -
the number that just before me ends.

West

When the sun goes down and stars come out,
the long dark hours for owls and sleep;
the moon climbs up, the world goes still -
the time when shadows creep.
