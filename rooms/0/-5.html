Salt

Tip a little into your palm: white, dry, and sharp-cornered, each grain a tiny cube. Salt comes up out of the sea when the water leaves, or out of old mines deep in the hills, and a pinch of it wakes up every dull dish on the table. Too much spoils the soup; none at all, and nothing tastes of anything. For thousands of years it was so precious that men were paid in it. Lick your lip after swimming - that taste is the sea's, and yours.

North

On the beach I stretch for miles,
in buckets shaped to castle-piles;
I slip away through fingers fast,
each tiny grain too small to last.

East

I have a heavy iron head,
I bang the nail until it's dead;
swing me hard and hit it square,
I build the house with bang and blare.

South

A lump of black dug from the ground,
I burn up red with hardly a sound;
I warm the hearth on winter's night,
bad children find me - serves them right!

West

The smallest worker that there is,
I lift a leaf ten times my size;
a tiny soldier, six legs strong,
in single file we troop along.
