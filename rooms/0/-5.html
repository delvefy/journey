Sugar

White grains, sweet on the tongue, spooned into tea and stirred into cake. Sugar comes from cane and beet, refined to a glittering dust; it is wholly solid and wholly there to taste and touch. It keeps the tangible south. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

White grains too, but never sweet,
I season soup and cure the ham;
from the sea and the deep mine alike —
shake me out: the savoury one I am.

East

A long flat board sawn from a tree,
laid down to walk or build a floor;
the pirate makes you tread me out
above the waves — then you're no more.

South

Golden and sticky, sweet and slow,
the bees make me and fill the comb;
spread me on bread, stir me in tea —
from the hive I'm carried home.

West

A small soft fruit on bush or briar,
red, black, or blue, and sweet to eat;
the bird, the bear, the child all pick
me by the handful, juicy-sweet.
