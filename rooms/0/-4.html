Sand

Run it through your fingers and it pours like slow water, a thousand thousand tiny stones too small to count. Sand is what rock becomes after the sea and the years have worn it down - the patient end of every cliff and boulder. It fills your shoes, builds your castles, and slips away from any grip. Tangible and plain, you can hold a fistful and still call it one thing, though it is really the whole beach, ground small.

North

I am the home a snail once wore,
washed up in pieces on the shore;
hold me to your ear, they say,
and hear the ocean far away.

East

I have a handle and a head,
I chop the logs for fire and bread;
the woodman swings me, strong and true,
I split the oak in one or two.

South

I am white and fine as sand,
I sprinkle from the shaker's hand;
the sea is full of me, they tell -
on chips and crisps I taste so well.

West

I dig my tunnels underground,
I push up little hills, all round;
I'm small and blind with velvet fur,
the gardener's woe, a digging blur.
