Salt

A pinch of white grains, sharp on the tongue, that the sea leaves behind when it dries. Salt is dug or dried, not built — and yet it is utterly solid, a thing you measure, scatter, and taste. It keeps the meat from rot and a meal from dullness. Touchable, on the line between wild and wrought, it sits due south. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

When the fire has burned all night
and the last red ember dies,
I am the soft grey powder left
where the warm hearth lies.

East

Baked from clay in a fiery kiln,
red and squared and true;
stack me up with daubs of lime —
a house will rise for you.

South

White as I am, but sweet not sharp,
I melt upon the tongue;
I sit in the bowl by the morning tea
and through the cakes I'm flung.

West

In a little wooden cap I grow
upon the spreading oak;
drop me down and an age from now
I'm a tree for forest folk.
