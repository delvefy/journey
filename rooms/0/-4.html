The First Dark

Older than the warm dark, and emptier. A step to the north your own blood kept you warm and a second warmth pressed close enough to brush; here the warmth simply is not, and was never taken away, for there was never anything here to be warm. The dark does not press. It has no inside, no walls, no floor you could prove with a foot — only a vastness with no edge, the deep that was here before there was a body to come into it: before heat, before the first lying-down, before anyone at all needed somewhere to be.

You stand in it anyway, and the body is the strange thing now — a made thing, almost, in a place from before making, your weight and your warmth a small late intrusion the deep neither refuses nor notices. You find the spiral whorled into the pad of one finger, carried this whole way in the single place no dark can smooth it flat, and it is the only mark in a world that has not yet learned how to be marked. There is no one to reach for. The deep never had a second; it never once lay down two.

Your heartbeat sounds here too loud and too new, the first clock the warm dark named, a thing from some later age that has wandered too far back. The deep keeps no time of its own. Your pulse beats against the silence like a word said aloud in a room built before there was speech.

To the north the deep gathers a first warmth and a body to hold it, the warm dark and its doubled heart. To the east the older dark stirs and wakes, a presence grown straight out of the root-dark, never made and never called, the wild that was always here. To the south the deep goes deeper still, before even the dark had a before, down under being itself. To the west the dark thins and cools toward ash and given-up bone, the body's leavings, the Waste. You were going somewhere, once — though the deep is older than any going. Only your heart insists, beat on beat, that you were.
