The Quickening Press

The one press becomes a row of them. You climb into a longer room than the last, lit by the same straight grey day but brighter now, harder, and where below a single screw turned under a printer's hand, here a dozen presses pull in ranks and no printer stands at any. They have learned to feed themselves. Paper unrolls, types fall, the platen drops and lifts faster than a hand could ever lay a sheet — the work has quickened past the man it was shaped to fit, and left him out.

The warm reek of oil and lampblack and damp paper is still here, but thinning, outrun: the sheets come off faster than any eye could follow, stack and topple and slide, a made day's worth of words in an hour and not one of them waited on. On every sheet runs the same broken line you left pied below — the name an inch from sense — only here it is not waiting to be set right. It has been printed loose a thousand times exactly as it fell, multiplied past correcting, flung out wrong and fast and everywhere.

At the room's far end a single filament burns with no flame in it, steadier than any lamp, and beneath it a reel of wire turns and turns, paying a thin ribbon of the word out past the paper altogether — off the page and onto the wire, the spiral wound now in copper and running somewhere you cannot follow. The press's honest thump has quickened to a chatter, a fast dry tapping with no toll left in it, the bell learning to send instead of ring, counting nothing it cannot already outrun.

To the north the presses shed their paper entirely, the word flung as light and number alone, the made day of the pole where nothing is set in lead and everything is sent. To the east the loose letters begin to mean what they spell, words that do the thing they say, the pied name half-wanting to stand and answer. To the south the racing slows to a single hand-press, the screw turning under a printer who has only stepped out. To the west the work goes cold and clerkish, the same speed and no warmth in it, words stamped for no reader at all. You were going somewhere once, and it is being printed faster than you can be told. The wire takes your name off the page and sends it on, a thousand times, an inch from sense, ahead of you down a line whose end you cannot see.
