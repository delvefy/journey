Quiet

You are the near-silence of a hushed place - the library, the sleeping house, the snowy morning before anyone wakes. No hand makes you; you settle when the noise dies down. 'Sssh,' they say, and you arrive: no shouting, no banging, just the soft small sounds left over. Not quite silent, but close.

North

A light half-sleep you slip into
without quite meaning to;
Grandad does it after lunch -
the book slides off his knee.

East

Bend your knees and push off hard,
both feet leave the ground at once;
over the puddle, over the rope -
up you go with a bounce.

South

The deep calm when all fighting stops,
no war and no more noise;
the handshake after the long quarrel -
the gift the whole world enjoys.

West

A wide bright shaft of shining light
the lighthouse throws at sea;
the torch, the searchlight, headlamp's glare -
one solid bar to see.
