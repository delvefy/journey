hurdle

A hurdle is a little fence built to be jumped. On the running track they stand in a row, low wooden bars on legs, and the racers fly over them one after another without breaking stride. Knock one and it tips - it is made to be cleared, not crashed. People use the word for any small barrier in the way: one more hurdle to get over. Wrought by hand, yet you cannot hold it; it lives in the leaping.

North

Bend your knees and push the ground,
then both your feet leave the floor;
up you go and down you come -
do it once or do it more.

East

Hold the waist of the one in front
and kick your legs out wide;
a wiggly line that snakes about -
a party dance, this ride.

South

Plant your hands upon the horse
and swing your legs up high;
over the top in one big leap -
in the gym you seem to fly.

West

A great big springy leap ahead,
the way a deer will go;
it bounces forward, light and quick,
across the field of snow.
