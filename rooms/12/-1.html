Torch

You are a handheld tube of light, a battery and a bulb behind glass, throwing a beam wherever you point. A hand builds you and clicks you on. Under the bedclothes you light a secret book; in the dark garden you find the lost ball. Your beam grows dim as your battery dies.

North

Bigger than a van, with great fat wheels,
it rumbles down the road;
it hauls the heavy loads of the world -
the road's strong beast of load.

East

A light kept safe inside a glass cage,
swung on a handle or hung on a hook;
the old night-watchman carried one -
a candle's caged-in look.

South

A little round ball stuck on a door,
you grip it, give a turn;
it works the latch and lets you in -
a small round thing to learn.

West

A house of cloth held up by poles,
pegged down against the wind;
you sleep in it out in the field -
dry while the rain has pinned.
