Sleeve

You are the arm of the shirt or coat, the tube of cloth your arm slides down to the cuff. Made by the tailor, long for winter, short for summer, rolled up for work. Tug me up, push me down. You wear your heart on me, they say; and the conjurer keeps his secrets up me, and the runny nose finds me too.

North

False hair you wear upon your head
when your own is thin or gone;
the judge, the clown, the dressing-up box -
you pop this borrowed mop on.

East

The end of the sleeve, turned back round
the wrist where the hand comes out;
you roll it up to wash your hands -
a button holds it, no doubt.

South

A round metal prize on a coloured ribbon,
you win it in a race;
gold, silver, bronze, you hang it proud -
around the winner's place.

West

A short coat with sleeves and a zip,
warm against the chill;
you shrug it on before the door -
on a hook it hangs, until.
