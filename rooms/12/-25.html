kayak

A kayak is a narrow little boat you wear almost like a shoe. You sit low in its belly, legs straight out, the water inches from your hands, and swing the double paddle side to side. Light enough to lift on one shoulder, it slips along the river quick and quiet, turning on a thought. Built of plastic or skin over a frame, it is wholly a made thing you can grip - the snuggest, closest boat there is, half a boat and half a coat.

North

A boat that dives beneath the waves
and travels deep and dark;
it never needs to come up for air -
it hunts below the shark.

East

A small but mighty chugging boat,
it pulls the big ships in;
it tows them safely into port -
so strong, though small and thin.

South

A flat-bladed stick held in both hands
to push the water back;
dip left, dip right, and on you glide -
it keeps the kayak on track.

West

A flat rubber sole, a strap for your toe,
you wear me down to the beach;
flip-flop, flip-flop, I slap as you walk -
the sand is in easy reach.
