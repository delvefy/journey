lap

Lap is the smallest sound the water makes - the soft slap-and-slide of little waves against a hull or a stone, over and over, never quite the same. You sit by the lake at evening and listen: lap, lap, lap, a patient tongue tasting the shore. No one set it going and no one can stop it. Natural and weightless, it is the water talking quietly to the land all night long.

North

Scrub the dishes, scrub your hands,
with water, soap, and care;
to clean a thing by rubbing wet -
one word; you do it everywhere.

East

The sea heaves up its great smooth back
before a wave will break;
it rises, rounds, and lifts the boats -
a slow deep ocean ache.

South

The little brook talks all day long
in bubbling, gurgling words;
it never stops to take a breath -
its silly chatter's heard.

West

Throw the bucket on the fire
and put the flames right out;
to soak a thing and kill the blaze -
that's me, without a doubt.
