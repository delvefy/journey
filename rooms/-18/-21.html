seaweed

You stand where the tide has gone out, and the rocks wear long green hair. Seaweed - slick, salt-smelling, ribboned and torn - is the sea's cast-off, draped to dry and darken on the stones. You touch a frond and it is cool and rubbery, popping its little bladders under your thumb. Natural, and wholly touchable: a plant that never wanted soil, rooted instead to bare rock and swayed its whole life by water.

North

I am a bite the sea has taken
from the curving, sandy land;
boats rest quiet in my shelter,
held between two arms of sand.

East

I am the hillside's gentle tilt,
not flat, not cliff, but in between;
roll a ball and I will take it
downward over grass and green.

South

I hide in every drop of sea
and on the chips upon your plate;
white and sharp upon the tongue,
a pinch can make a dull meal great.

West

When the tide goes out it leaves me
caught among the stones to stay;
crabs and tiny fish swim in me
till the sea comes back this way.
