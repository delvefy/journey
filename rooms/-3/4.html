Gleam

A thin bright shine — sun caught on a far window, a glint on water, a flash from polished steel. The gleam is wholly natural where light finds a surface, and wholly out of hand; you see it and it is gone. It keeps the airy north-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A soft warm light that does not flame,
the ember's red, the firefly's spark;
I shine without a blaze or beam —
a gentle light against the dark.

East

The east goes pale, the night wears thin,
grey turning gold at the edge of the sky;
before the sun has cleared the hills,
the first faint light. What time am I?

South

Seven bands of colour curved
across the sky when showers clear;
red to violet, hill to hill —
chase my end, I'm never near.

West

A short fierce shove of moving air,
I bend the grass and flip the page;
here in a blink and gone as fast —
a sudden blast, a brief wind's rage.
