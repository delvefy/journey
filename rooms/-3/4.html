The Greying

West of the unread light and north of the forgetting hall, the cold day greys out. A step east the made light still hung in even rank, dimming only by the hair, the record racing round bright for no one; a step south it was iron yet, a hall of engines minding its worn round. Here the glow has gone ragged. Whole banks of the cold light stand dark now, the even white broken into islands, and between them the grey has come in — not black, never the clean dark, only the long Waste silence seeping up through the made day where the sending has forgotten itself. What still burns burns fainter, filing its record round and round into stretches that no longer carry it, the bright word loosed into a grey that does not pass it on.

You stand in the failing of it and there is no eye but yours to mind the loss, and yours mends nothing. The light asks nothing of you, sends to no one, keeps for no one; it only greys, evenly, patiently, a made day forgetting at the speed of cold. You think, with no warning, of a rushlight in an iron bracket, the first small flame you can almost recall, the way it guttered and held — and here is its last cold cousin at the far end of everything, the made day come round to the same guttering, dimming in its socket with no hand to trim it. The memory greys even as it comes. You cannot keep the rushlight either.

The coil is a ring of cold light still, shed of all its iron and copper, turning round on round — but guttering now, half its arc gone dark, a spiral that completes itself in silence as often as in light. And the bell has thinned to a signal greying out: a cold even pulse that drops a stroke, then a stretch of strokes, the hour sent into the dimming and arriving nowhere. Your name runs the failing ring still, struck in light and greyed at once, a letter lost each pass, then a word, thinning toward the smear all of it goes to. North the light sheds even its glow for pure cold number at the pole, no one to read, the silence gone fine. East it firms back to the unread light, the record still mostly bright. South it thickens to wire and iron, the forgetting hall turning yet. West the glow greys out the whole way, the sending wholly forgotten, the cold made day gone to the long silence. You were going somewhere, under a name. It is here in the dimming light, fading faster than you can read it — and the ring gutters dark, and comes round, and there is a little less of the name each time.
