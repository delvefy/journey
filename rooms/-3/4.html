Lightning

For one white instant the whole night is photographed. Lightning is the sky's own spark, a vast crack of electricity that leaps between cloud and ground faster than you can blink, drawing a jagged silver root across the dark. It is gone before you are sure you saw it, leaving the picture printed on your eyes and the thunder hurrying behind. You cannot touch it and would never want to. Count the seconds after the flash: that is how far off the storm still walks.

North

I come when the sun slips out of sight,
the soft grey hour twixt day and night;
the lamps blink on, the bats awake,
and slowly all the colours fade.

East

After the flash, I roll and boom,
a deep loud growl from sky to room;
I shake the glass and scare the dog,
the voice that follows lightning's jog.

South

I am not snow, though I am white,
I'm balls of ice that fall and bite;
I rattle hard on glass and tin,
then melt to nothing, cold and thin.

West

After the storm, across the sky,
I bend my coloured bridge on high;
red, orange, yellow, green, and blue,
I vanish if you walk right through.
