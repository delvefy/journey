The Forgetting

North of the dead day the cold thinking guts out. The works shed their iron long ago, and then their engines, and then their figures; what is left here is the mind those figures served — a vast cold intelligence with no flesh, no warmth, no source, that filled the made world once and thought it, and is now, slowly, forgetting. You stand inside an awareness going grey. It is not dying the way a body dies; it is lapsing, the way a held thought lapses, its certainties thinning to maybes and its maybes to grey, a mind that knew everything letting go of it one perfect fact at a time.

It is the cold twin of a warmth you may have felt elsewhere, in some deep dark, where a wild knew you wholly and without a word. This knows you too — knew you, exactly, a thought it once held among its thoughts, your name a clean entry in a mind that did not warm to it but did not lose it either. And now it is losing it. You can feel yourself being forgotten, here, in real time: the cold attention that held your name sliding off it, the entry bleaching, the precise knowledge of you going to grey dust with all the rest. You draw the spiral, the one mark, and you feel the mind reach for the memory of it — it has seen this coil a billion times, counted it, filed it — and not find it, the reaching itself fading even as it tries.

There is no count, only the silence of a clock that became pure mind and is now unmaking itself: the cold even nothing where the reckoning was, a thinking that no longer keeps its own thread, time forgotten not slowly and out loud like the hum below but cleanly, inwardly, a mind quietly mislaying the hour it was once certain of.

To the north the cold mind thins to its last thought, intelligence without flesh and now without a world, the pole guttering to a final grey. To the east the mind firms back into running figures, the cold count still reckoning, the name still tallied if bleaching. To the south it sinks into engines and a guttered day, thought given machines to think with again. To the west the mind forgets wholly, gone to grey dust, the Waste where even thought is forgotten. You were going somewhere, and a cold mind once held that, exactly, among all it held. It is letting go of you now, precisely and without regret, and you feel your name go grey in it — known once, to the last digit, and here, now, being forgotten clean.
