The Never-Was

South of the scoured flat the ground stops being ground. West of the long before the grey pales out of even grey. Here the two nothings come to their floor together: raw cold earth that was never built on and never lain in, sinking into an older dark beneath; and over it a pallor past all colour, the Waste proper, ash without a fire and now without even the ash. The wind that was the whole of the flat above does not reach this low. The grey that lay still a step east has paled away to less than grey. There is nothing here, and there never was, and the never-was is the oldest thing in the room.

You reach, out of the oldest habit, to draw the coil — your one thread back to yourself — and find no ground will take it: no dust, no stone, nothing to draw in or upon. And worse, for a breath, you cannot find the turn of it. The shape that has followed you as chalk and horn and bright light gutters in your hand, almost gone. That small cold fear — of losing even the spiral — is, strangely, the most like yourself you have felt down here. You are still the one who would miss it. That much, at least, the Waste has not taken.

There is no wind, no drip, no bell. Nothing leaches, because nothing was ever warm; the dark has never once heard a sound it had to keep. Only your pulse, smaller than ever, ticking once and once again into a place that will not number it — the last count in a world that kept none, beating only because you carried it in with you.

To the north the sunk ground firms back up into the scoured flat, a ground at least for a wind to comb. To the east the pallor settles back into the grey of the long before, the fine grey lying still, the memory of a floor. To the south the floor gives out into the deep before the deep, before there was a ground or a dark to be in at all. To the west the Waste widens paler still, the void going on past even this, where not even the never-was ever was. You were going somewhere once, and were not alone in the going. Here there is nothing to hold your name and no one to have lost it. You carry your own pulse, and the half-remembered coil, on into the nothing.
