Fern

A green plant of damp and shadow, its leaves uncurling from a tight coil like a bishop's crook. The fern grows where it is wet and dim, bears no flower and no seed you can see, and is wholly natural and wholly there to touch. It keeps the green, solid south-west, among roots and growing things. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A soft green coat on stone and log,
I creep where it is damp and cool;
no flower, no stem that you would notice —
just velvet by the shaded pool.

East

A sharp small spike upon a stem,
I guard the rose, I snag the hand;
pluck the bright bloom if you dare —
I prick whoever won't understand.

South

A tall thin stalk by the river's edge,
I rattle dry when cold winds pass;
cut me hollow, blow me through —
I pipe a note, a marsh-side grass.

West

I climb the wall and wind the post,
I hang in loops, I will not stand;
the grape, the ivy, the runner-bean
all twist along me, hand over hand.
