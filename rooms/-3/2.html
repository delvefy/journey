The Returning Iron

Here the hand-tools are behind you and the engine itself stands: a true machine, taller than a man, braced to the cold floor, built to turn without a hand to turn it and to keep no faith while it turned. South of here the iron still lay out as chisel and wedge, things a hand might lift; here it is welded into one great wheeled work that asked for no hand at all. That was the further trade. And like the smaller one, it did not hold.

A step east the same machinery stands seized but whole, its spring locked orange in mid-coil; here the coil is past locking. The rust has eaten clean through the spring, and the spring has gone to flakes, and the flakes to a brown dust banked at the engine's foot — iron remembering it was only ever ore, borrowed cold from the ground. You set a thumb where the maker's spiral was struck and there is no spiral, only a soft brown crater where the metal gave up holding any shape at all. Machinery gone to ore again, exactly as the east door promised.

South of here an anvil could still be struck for one flat joyless note; east of here the cold iron still ticked, now and then, surrendering an old warmth. Here there is neither. You knock the engine's flank and it answers with the dull give of rotten iron — no ring, no tick, the bell rusted past complaint. Even the small sound of settling has left. There is only the grey light, coming from nowhere, even and shadowless, beginning.

To the north the works multiply and stand whole and running, engines minding engines under a flat dead light, the wonder banished for good and no rust permitted. To the east the same engine stands seized but still itself, the iron stopped and not yet surrendered. To the south the engine breaks back down into hand-tools a body could lift, the trade smaller and the iron not yet wholly lost. To the west the brown dust opens out and forgets it was ever an engine, ore without a memory of the forge, the grey Waste that takes every made thing. You were going somewhere once, by some engine's hour. This engine has run its hour all the way out, and stands in the even light with nothing left to turn.
