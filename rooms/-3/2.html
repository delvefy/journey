The Spent Works

North of the bench the trade got its engine. The cold hand-tools rusting a step south were only the promise; here is the thing they promised — a great iron machine raised to do the work with no hand to mind it, wheel and shaft and cog set running once on nothing but its own grey certainty. It ran. You can see that it ran: the floor is worn in a pale arc beneath one wheel, the bearing burnished where it turned ten thousand times for no one. Then it stopped, and no one came, because there was no one to come; and the stopping is older now than the running ever was.

To the east an engine like it still holds its shape, seized but whole, ticking now and then as it gives up an old warmth. Here the iron is further gone — softening at every edge, shedding in orange leaves, the made thing already half returned to the grey it was smelted out of. The spiral is here as a maker's mark, stamped flat into a cracked plate, and the rust eats it first, the coil filling brown from the centre out. You set a thumb to it and it comes away like pollen, and nothing turns.

East of here a tick still sounds sometimes. Not here. Once, only, the long groan of metal settling lower into itself — the bell worn all the way down to a tool's complaint, and then not even that, the count abandoned by the thing that was built to keep it.

To the north the works multiply and need no fire and no hand, engines tending engines under a dead even light, the wonder banished for good. To the east the iron firms back into the seized forge, whole and still faintly ticking. To the south the engine unmakes to a cold bench of hand-tools, the trade not yet wholly given. To the west the last of the shape blows off as ore-grit and the grey opens out, machinery gone back to the ground, the Waste even the rust has finished with. You were going somewhere, by some engine's hour. The great wheel holds where it stopped, burnished bright by all its turning, and will not turn again to carry you.
