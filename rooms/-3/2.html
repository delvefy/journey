Cloud

A great soft shape drifts past the high window, white at the crown and grey beneath. A cloud is made by no hand — only water lifted and gathered in the cold high air. Reach for it and there is nothing; it is all distance and vapour. Natural and beyond your grasp, it keeps the north-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

When the rain has passed and the sun breaks through
I arch across the sky;
in red and gold and green and blue —
no hand can hold me, though you try.

East

On the coldest night I creep about
and powder the world in white;
I stiffen the grass and bite the toes
and vanish in the light.

South

A thin and milky dimness hangs
and softens the far hill;
not fog, not smoke, just air worn pale,
the noon laid faint and still.

West

I darken the whole sky at once
with thunder, wind and rain;
the ships all run for the harbour wall
when I rage across the main.
