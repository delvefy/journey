Snow

It falls without a sound and changes the whole world while you sleep. Snow is water gone soft and white and slow, drifting down in flakes no two alike, until the road, the roof, and the bare black tree all wear the same clean coat. You can gather it, pack it, build with it - and still it slips between being a thing and being weather, here in a hush, gone in a thaw. You catch one flake on your glove and watch it vanish.

North

I am hard white ice from the sky,
I clatter on the roofs from high;
in summer storms I bounce and bound,
small frozen balls upon the ground.

East

I fall in drops from clouds of grey,
I patter down on roofs all day;
I fill the puddles, feed the flower,
you grab a brolly in my shower.

South

I paint the window panes with white,
a feathery lace I make at night;
I nip your nose and bite your toes,
and melt away when sunlight glows.

West

I form at dawn on grass and web,
small beads of wet before light's spread;
I am not rain, I just appear,
a morning sparkle, cool and clear.
