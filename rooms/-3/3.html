The Forgetting Hall

South of here one great engine had rusted all the way back to ore; a step north the engines multiply again — a hall of them, rank on rank, built to mind each other and to let no rust in. And a step west of the unwatched works, the rust has come anyway. The hall stands whole, almost, and running, almost; but the closed round is forgetting itself. A belt cracks and slaps and snaps. The copper greens. A wheel that drove ten thousand others drives nothing now, and the engines to either side of the gap close over it and limp on, minding a round with a hole worn through it.

The hall was raised to banish two things, wonder and ruin. It has kept the first banished; the second walked back in through no door at all. The cold white light yellows at its edges. Oil films and stiffens to varnish. Here and there an engine has stopped outright and stands seized, orange-blooded, on its slow way to the brown dust banked at the foot of the one below — the whole hall a single machine in the act of forgetting it ever turned, sliding piece by piece toward grit.

The copper wire that once carried the hall's one thought from machine to machine lies green now in its slack coil — the spiral gone to verdigris, the thought wound into it no longer coming back round but breaking off mid-loop at the worn places. The room-sized pulse that did not skip has begun to skip: ten thousand taps thinned to nine, to eight, the clock the size of a room losing its beat as the teeth wear through. And somewhere a counter still adds you up, but its wheels jam and slip; the number it kept for you drops a digit, then another, your tally coming quietly apart with no one ever to have read it whole.

To the north the failing works thin toward wire and then to light, engines shedding their iron to think without a head, the cold made day of the pole, the forgetting gone fine and bright. To the east the round firms back into the unwatched works, the belts whole and the white only yellowing, the hall still minding itself. To the south it narrows to the one great engine, rusted all the way to ore, a single ruin below the many. To the west the closed round cracks the rest of the way open, engine to ore and ore to dust, the grey Waste that all machinery comes to. You were going somewhere once, by some engine's hour. This hall's hour is running down ragged, and the count of you is coming apart in it.
