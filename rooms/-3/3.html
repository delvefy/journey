The Dead Day

North of the spent works and west of the even light, the made day goes out. The engines that minded engines a step east, the dead-even glow that fell from no lamp — here they have run down together, and the light with them. It does not fail like a natural dusk; it gutters, the way a flat electric glow gutters when the last of the drive dies somewhere unseen, dimming in flat steps toward a grey that is neither lit nor dark, only over. The works stand everywhere, and nothing turns. The iron has gone to ore, banked in grey drifts against the dead machines, and the wonder that was banished from here long ago has been followed, at last, by the engine that banished it.

This is the end the cold trade was always coming to. The great wheel that turned ten thousand times for no one, the governor that spun smoothing nothing, the presses that filed the unread name — all of it stopped, all of it crumbling to the same grey the Waste is made of, machinery gone back to the ground it was dug from. On a buckled plate a maker's mark is still half-legible: the spiral, scaled and hollow, and beneath it a name worn first to a polish and then to nothing, the make of a world that no longer runs.

There is no count. The drive-beat that drove a thousand shafts stopped between one tick and the next, and the silence it left is not patient like the Waste's nor waiting like the dark's; it is simply finished — a clock not stopped but spent, an hour that ran out and was never wound again.

To the north the works shed even their iron, the cold thinking that needed no hand guttering out too, the bright pole gone dark, mind without flesh sunk to grey. To the east the engines firm back into turning, the dead even day still cast, the wonder banished but the machine still running. To the south the works narrow to a single great seized wheel, burnished and rusting, the spent engine that at least once ran. To the west the ore blows wholly to grey dust and the made day is out entirely, the Waste where even the machines are forgotten. You were going somewhere, by some engine's hour. The hour ran out here, and the light with it, and nothing is left running to tell you how long ago.
