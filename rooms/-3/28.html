satellite

A satellite is a machine we built and flung up into space, where it loops round and round the Earth without ever falling - a little metal moon of our own making. From up there it bounces down the telly and the phone calls, watches the weather brewing, and tells your map exactly where you stand. On a clear night you can spot one, a steady speck of light sliding among the stars, going about its quiet circling work.

North

A pale silver ray of light at night,
it slips through the curtains while you sleep;
not the sun's gold, but the moon's soft glow -
a thread of light, both still and deep.

East

A great tall wave that curls and falls,
it crashes white upon the shore;
surfers ride me in to land -
I tumble, foam, and crash, and roar.

South

When the moon slides right in front of the sun
and day turns dark at the height of noon;
a black hole rimmed with fire in the sky -
or the earth's shadow swallows the moon.

West

A fire burnt low, no flame at all,
just glowing red and a curl of smoke;
it smokes for hours, almost out -
the slow last burn of the dying oak.
