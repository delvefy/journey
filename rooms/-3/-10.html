Goat

You are the bearded climber of the rocky hill, sure-footed where nothing else will go, horns curving back and a strange square eye. No hand made you - you scramble the crag, you butt the gate, you eat the thistle and the tin can and the washing off the line. You give rich milk and a stubborn will. Clever, greedy, and tough, you are the mountain's own goat, picking your way up where the grass runs thin.

North

A pink fat farmyard beast that wallows
deep in cooling mud;
it grunts, it snorts, its tail's a curl,
and roots up field and wood.

East

A little lawn flower, white and gold,
that opens with the day;
children chain me round their necks
and pull my petals away.

South

A big strong beast you ride or drive,
with mane and hoof and tail;
it gallops fast, it pulls the cart,
and whinnies in the dale.

West

A small round fruit with a purple skin,
sweet and soft to eat;
a hard stone sits inside my heart -
a juicy summer treat.
