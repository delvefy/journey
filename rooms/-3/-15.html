Pumpkin

You are the great round orange globe of autumn, fat on its trailing vine, heaviest fruit of the patch. No hand made you; the soil and the late sun did. Carved hollow with a candle inside, you grin in the dark at Halloween. Or scooped and spiced into a pie - either way, the biggest thing in the garden.

North

A lumpy brown root from the ground,
you dig it from the dirt;
you mash it, chip it, bake it whole -
with salt, it's worth the work.

East

A big round ball of green leaves,
that grows down in the row;
leaf wrapped on leaf, all tight and crisp -
it's chopped for soup, you know.

South

A long green veg, all cool and crisp,
that grows on a trailing vine;
you slice it thin for sandwiches -
in salads it is fine.

West

A shiny veg, red, green, or gold,
that's hollow, with seeds inside;
you slice it into crunchy rings -
in salads, far and wide.
