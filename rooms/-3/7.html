Moonlight

You are the borrowed light of the night, the sun's own glow handed down by the cold white moon. You fall on the sleeping world soft and silver, draining the colours away and leaving only shapes - the pale road, the black tree, the glint on the still water. By you the owl hunts and the lovers walk and the frightened child is half-comforted. You make no warmth and cast no harshness; you are simply enough to see by when the day has put out all its lamps.

North

The far faint shine of the night-time sky,
from a thousand specks of white;
'star bright,' you wish on me up high,
the oldest, palest light.

East

You cannot see me, cannot hold,
yet by the fire you know;
I melt the frost, I beat the cold,
and make the summer glow.

South

The warm gold light the sun lets fall,
across the field and street;
it cheers the world and brightens all,
and dries the rain-wet wheat.

West

When the noon has rung and lunch is done,
the long warm slope of day;
the drowsy hours beneath the sun,
before the light goes grey.
