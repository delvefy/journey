Hen

You are the busy brown matron of the farmyard, scratching all day in the dust for grubs and grain, talking to yourself in soft contented clucks. You are no flier and no beauty, but you give the world its eggs, one warm and perfect each morning, and you sit them faithfully into chicks. Round you the yellow chicks scurry; under your wing they hide from the hawk's shadow. Fussy, plain and endlessly useful, you are the homeliest of birds - and the breakfast table would be a poorer place without you.

North

With a flat broad bill and webbed feet,
I paddle round the pond;
of weed and bug I love to eat,
and quack the whole day long.

East

A white cup on the still pond's face,
a flat green pad below;
the frog sits in my resting place,
where the slow waters flow.

South

Pink and round with a curly tail,
I roll in the mud all day;
'oink, oink,' I snuffle for my meal,
in the sty I love to stay.

West

Red or green and round and sweet,
I grow upon the tree;
crunchy, juicy, good to eat,
so take a bite of me.
