Haze

A thin haze hangs in the room, and the far wall goes soft and blue, the way hills do on a hot afternoon. It is not quite fog and not quite smoke — only the air gone milky, the light worn thin. You cannot gather it or push it aside; reach in and your hand comes back empty. Born of no maker and impossible to hold, it keeps the wild north-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A puff of white in the daytime sky
that wanders, changing form;
I float upon the wind all day
and blacken before a storm.

East

A thick grey breath off the cold sea
that fills the lane at dawn;
I hide the hills, I drown the road,
and by midday I'm gone.

South

Twice a day I haul the sea
high up the slope of sand,
then twice I let it slide away —
the moon's slow pull my hand.

West

I am the grey half-dark that fills
the wood when day grows old;
not night, not noon, a heavy dusk,
a dimness damp and cold.
