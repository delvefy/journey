Frost

You wake to a world gone white and brittle. Frost came in the night - not snow you can gather, not ice you can hold, but a breath the cold left on every blade and pane. You press a fingertip to the glass and the ferny silver vanishes, leaving only water and the warmth of your own skin. It is the most fleeting of the cold things: here at first light, gone by the time the sun clears the wall.

North

I fall in soft and silent flakes,
white blankets are the quilts I make;
I melt to nothing in your hand,
yet hush and whiten all the land.

East

I am a cloud come down to ground,
I smother shapes and muffle sound;
ships sound their horns when I am near -
the grey that veils the harbour pier.

South

I rise and curl and rush ashore,
I never rest, I knock for more;
I lift the surfer up with glee,
a moving hill upon the sea.

West

I am the lightest kind of wind,
too soft to bend a branch or limb;
I turn the kite and cool your face,
a gentle stir from place to place.
