The Cold Forge

North of the shrine the kneeling is over. The stone that lifted to carry a prayer has been squared and set down flat here into a bench, a block, an anvil — the same grey rock, but trued now with edges, made to be worked at rather than worshipped in. Cold iron lies on the bench in rows: a chisel, a wedge, the rim of a wheel not yet round, hand-tools true-cut and joyless, belief traded clean across for the certainty of a thing that does what it is shaped to do and asks nothing back. No one knelt to make these. That was the trade.

But the trade did not hold either. The iron is already going: a brown bloom along the chisel's edge, the wheel-rim flaking at the join, the made cold corroding in the made cold, on its slow way to the grit that lies a step east as a whole seized engine and a step further as no engine at all. The old spiral is here too, struck cleanly into the flat of a blade as a maker's mark — not graven in faith, only stamped to say whose work it was — and the rust is eating it first, the coil filling brown from the centre out.

There is no tick yet; the mechanism that would keep time has not been built. But strike the cold anvil with the wedge and it gives one flat ringing note, hard and brief and meaning nothing — the bell remade as a tool's complaint, a sound with no faith in it, dead the instant it is made.

To the north the cold making hardens past hand-tools into the true engine, iron raised and set running without a soul to mind it, the grey future at the end of the trade. To the east the bench gathers into one great seized machine, the iron whole but stopped. To the south the bench softens back to the altar, the trade not yet made and the kneeling not yet given up. To the west the iron loses even its shape and blows away as grit, the long grey silence, the Waste that every made cold comes to. You were going somewhere, by your own two hands or by some engine. Here the tools to take you lie out cold and rusting, and not one of them was blessed.
