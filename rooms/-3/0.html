The Spent Shrine

The keeping falls away entirely, and what is left was built to last. Where the witch's cottage merely lapsed, this is a made and serious place — a small stone shrine, or the cell of one, with niches cut into the walls and a low altar worn smooth by hands that have stopped coming. It was raised to be answered in; you can feel that in the proportions, in the way the ceiling lifts to carry a voice. But the voice has stopped, and the lift carries nothing now but the cold.

Above the altar a spiral has been cut deep into the stone, the old coil graven by someone who believed it would hold something. Someone else has been at it since, with a chisel or a thumb, scoring across the turns, half effacing it — not in anger, you think, but in the slow doubt that unmakes a thing simply by ceasing to mean it. You set your palm to the cold stone the way you would have once, and wait for the small leaning-close, the ear in the quiet. None comes. And under the disappointment is a colder thought: that perhaps none ever did, and the leaning was always your own hope, bent back at you off the stone.

There is a bell, high in the dark of the vault — you can just make out its dull curve — but no rope hangs from it, and it has not been struck in a long age. The air keeps the shape of its ringing the way a room keeps a scent after the thing is carried out: a silence cut exactly to the size of a sound, and the sound gone from it.

To the east the shrine warms back toward the witch's lapsed cottage, the keeping only stopped, the charm still faintly remembered. To the north the stone squares and hardens into a made cold that never knelt at all, belief traded for iron, the engine that needs no answering. To the south the roof falls in and the shrine sinks to a cold hearth and bare ground, ruin under open dark. To the west there is no shrine and no stone, only the long quiet the keeping fell into, the forgotten distance where nothing answers and nothing recalls that it once did. You were going somewhere, perhaps to pray. The scored spiral holds above the altar, half a coil and half a wound, and does not lean toward you, and does not lean away.
