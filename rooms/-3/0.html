Wave

Three rooms west, the sea lifts a single muscle and shows it to you. A wave is natural — born of nothing but wind and water — and it rides the axis between the touchable and the not: you can dive through it, yet it is only a shape moving, no water travelling at all. It rises, curls, throws white foam up the sand, and is gone, with another already behind it. Speak the names, and let the rolling way open.

North

I come the coldest nights of all
and powder grass with white;
I etch the window into ferns
and vanish with the light.

East

Salt and wide from shore to shore,
I never come to rest;
the rivers empty into me,
the ships ride on my chest.

South

I uncurl my feathered fronds
in cool and shady ground;
no bloom, no berry do I bear,
just green leaves all around.

West

Twice a day I climb the beach
and twice I slink away;
the moon's the one that pulls me up
and lets me down, they say.
