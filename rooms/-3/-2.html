The Long Quiet

This is where the draught is born. Twice the rooms behind you leaned westward and named a wind they did not own — the fallen hearth's cold, the unmade room's slow exhaling — and here at last is the open it comes out of. Not a mouth. Only the long quiet, the ground that was never built on and so kept nothing to fall: pale, level, going on past where the eye gives up, until the dark stands in for a far wall there is no reason to believe in. North of here three courses of stone still held the shape of a faith. Here even the holding is gone.

You stoop and draw the spiral, because your hand still believes in it. The slow steady breath of the place lifts the grit and carries it off level with the ground before the coil is half made, and what your finger leaves is not a mark and not an erasure but a thing that was never quite there — ash without a fire, drift without a ruin to drift from. The Waste does not forget you. It was never given anything of yours to keep.

There is no count but the draught, in and out, far too slow to be a breath, and you can no longer say whether it is the room exhaling or the last of you going out into it on the same long tide.

To the north the ground firms and rises into three cold courses, the roofless shrine that was at least raised to be answered in. To the east the drift banks deeper into the unmade grey, ash that at least consents to settle. To the south the level ground sinks into raw cold earth and the older dark beneath it, the deep before any wind crossed here. To the west the quiet widens and the draught itself thins toward stillness, ash too far out to stir, the Waste with nothing left even to carry. You were going somewhere, once. Out here the going has no road to lose and no one to have lost it.
