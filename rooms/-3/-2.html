Moss

Where the wood is darkest and the stone stays wet, the moss spreads its soft green coat. No hand planted it; it simply came, the way quiet comes to a still room. Press your palm to it and it is cool, damp, and yielding, a living velvet over rock and rotting log. It has no flower to show off, no height to reach for - only patience, and the slow, soundless work of greening every shaded thing it touches.

North

I am a feathered, leafy green,
in shady woods I'm often seen;
I bear no flower, hold no seed,
I curl up tight before I'm freed.

East

Snap - I break beneath your tread,
a tiny branch the wind has shed;
too small for fire, too thin for cane,
the birds will weave me up again.

South

Shorter than a tree I stand,
a leafy lump on garden land;
the gardener trims me neat and round,
I make a hedge along the ground.

West

I am the green that carpets ground,
in parks and lawns I'm all around;
the mower cuts me, cows will graze,
I grow back greener in warm days.
