The Scoured Flat

Here there is only the wind. North of you the ruin still had three courses of wall to break it; east of you the drift had at least gathered into something for the wind to push against. Here nothing breaks it and nothing gathers: the ground is scoured flat, ash so old and so worked over that it is not even ash any more, only the pale ground the wind has been combing since before there was anything to comb.

The cold draught the other rooms felt under their doors is, here, the whole room. It comes out of the further west in one long unbroken exhalation and goes east across you and does not gust, does not turn, has nothing to lift — a wind with nothing left to carry, going nowhere, from nowhere, only going. You lean into it the way you would lean into the idea of a wind, and it passes through where a body should have stopped it, as though a body were the one thing it had forgotten.

You stoop to draw the spiral and the wind takes it before your finger closes the curve, combs the grey flat and even again, not erasing so much as declining to notice. There is no lintel to cut it in, no stone, no bell down in any rubble: nothing was ever built here to fall, and so nothing here remembers having fallen. The only count is the wind, in and out, too slow to be breath, and so steady you cannot find its edges to number it.

To the north the ground gathers itself back into walls and a cold hearth-ring, ruin that was at least raised to be lost. To the east the flat banks up into drift, grey heavy enough to lie still where it falls. To the south the scoured ground sinks into raw cold earth and the older dark beneath it, the deep that was never built on and never lain in. To the west the wind comes from, and there is less even than this — the place the long quiet is born, that was never anything and so has nothing to forget. You were going somewhere once, leaning into a wind much like this one. This wind does not remember you. It goes on through, and takes the going with it.
