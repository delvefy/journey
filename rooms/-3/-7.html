The Dark Before

South of the erasure and west of the cold beneath, you sink past every making. A step north the void wiped the slate clean of even absence; a step east the body's undoing went below the after, into a cold older than warmth. Here both reach their floor: the floor beneath the floor, the dark before the first making — older than cold, older than the body, older than the warmth the cold took, a primordial dark that was here before anything was ever made or unmade or chilled. The body dissolves wholly into it, not erased and not frozen now but returned, given back to the oldest dark, the one that predates all making and waits under everything that was ever made.

It is not the clean nothing of the erasure nor the leaching cold of the after; it is older than either, the dark out of which cold and warmth and the made all later came. You sink into it and there is no surface, no remains, no frost — only the first dark taking you back the way it takes everything, before there was a making to undo. You draw the spiral and the dark before neither keeps nor wipes nor freezes it; it simply predates the gesture, as it predates all gestures. And still the whorl in your skin turns, the one made mark in a dark older than making, the single coil carried down past erasure and cold into the floor beneath the floor.

There is no count, for counting was a made thing and this is before the making. Your heart beats into the dark before, and the dark is older than the beat, older than the first clock, older than time — and still it beats, one warm made stroke in a dark that predates warmth and making both, the last and the first thing, refusing the oldest dark one beat at a time.

To the north the dark before rises to the erasure, the slate at least wiped clean of something. To the east the cold beneath, the body undone in a cold older than warmth. To the south it sinks toward the corner-pole, the dark before the dark before, older than the first making's first floor. To the west the body dissolves wholly to Waste, the undoing past even the oldest dark, the corner. You were going somewhere, with someone, once — before the name, before the cold, before the first making. Here you are given back to the oldest dark — and still the warm coil turns, the one made mark, and still one beat keeps on in the dark before everything, older than the making, refusing the floor beneath the floor.
