The Roofless Shrine

Below the shrine the roof is gone. What stood whole above stands open here — three courses of wall and then the dark itself for a ceiling, the vault that once lifted to carry a voice fallen in and scattered, so the cold comes straight down on bare ground. There is a hearth, or the ring of one, cold stones set in a circle by hands that meant to keep a fire and a faith both; nothing has burned in it so long that the ash is only paler ground. This is the ruin the shrine promised below itself: the made thing half-unmade, sinking back to the open dark it was raised against.

A cold draught crosses the floor and goes out eastward, and you understand it is born further west, out of the long quiet where nothing was ever built to ruin. On a tumbled lintel the old spiral has been cut, but the weather of ages has worn it almost flat, and when you set a finger to trace it the stone gives nothing back — not the lean-close of the answered shrine, not even the doubt that scored the coil above. Only cold, and the small grit of a mark giving up being a mark.

The bell that hung ropeless in the vault above is down somewhere in the rubble, mouth in the dirt; you will not find it, and it would not sound. There is no count here but the draught, in and out, too slow to be breath — and even the silence has lost the shape a sound once cut in it.

To the north the walls rise whole again and take a roof, the spent shrine that was at least built to be answered in. To the east the bare ground gathers into the fallen hearth, ruin that still keeps the cold shape of a fire. To the south the ruin sinks past all keeping into raw cold ground, the body and the older dark beneath it. To the west the draught comes from, and there is only that: ash without ruin, the long quiet that was never built and so has nothing to forget. You were going somewhere, perhaps to pray. The open dark stands in for the fallen roof, and the worn coil on the lintel does not lean toward you, for there is nothing left in the stone to lean.
