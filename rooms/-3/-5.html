Rush

A slim round stalk that crowds the wet margins of the pond, once peeled and dipped in fat to make a poor man's candle, once woven into mats and seats. The rush is wholly natural and wholly touchable, the marsh's quiet crop. It keeps the rooted south-west, among reeds and wet things. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A tall straight grass by the water's edge,
I rattle and hiss when the wind goes through;
cut me for thatch, or blow me to sound —
name the marsh-grass, green and true.

East

A prickly weed with a purple crown,
too sharp to grasp, I guard the wild;
Scotland's flower, all spike and down —
name the thorn-weed, fierce and mild.

South

A wild thorned bush in tangled hedge,
that catches the cloak and pricks the hand;
the rose grows wild upon my stems —
name the thorny shrub of the land.

West

A heavy lump of broken earth,
turned up brown by the plodding plough;
kick me and I crumble to bits —
name the clump of soil. Do it now.
