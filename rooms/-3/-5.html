The Lessening

East of the lees and south of the naught, the Waste begins to take away. A step north there was nothing, and the senses found it; a step east there was a residue, the settled lees of a world that never was. Here even those are too much, and the place subtracts them — takes the nothing, takes the residue, takes, you slowly feel, the very capacity to find anything missing. This is the Waste gone below the void: not absence now but the lessening of absence, a quiet subtraction that does not press or hunger but simply, patiently, takes away.

You feel it work on you. Where the naught left you whole in an empty place, here the emptiness reaches in: a thinning, a diminishment, the sense that parts of you are being quietly subtracted — not painfully, not even noticeably, except as a slow lessening of what there is to notice with. You draw the spiral and it is not blown or sunk or absorbed; it is subtracted, taken from having-been, removed so wholly that even the fact of its removal is removed. Only the whorl in your skin stays, and you feel the lessening reach for that too, patient, certain, the last thing left to take.

The spent count of the lees is lessened further here, your heart's beats taken one by one — not stopped, never the drama of stopping, only fewer, and fewer, the count itself subtracted toward a sum below zero, a heart growing quieter not from cold or age but from sheer arithmetic, the Waste taking back even the beats.

To the north the lessening firms to the naught, a nothing the senses can at least find. To the east it gathers to the lees, the settled residue of the never-was. To the south the lessening sinks toward the absolute pole, below subtraction, the floor. To the west lies the Waste pole, where the taking is complete and there is not even a less left. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here the Waste subtracts even the missing of them, even the wanting of the name, even the you that wanted — and still, below the last subtraction, the whorl in your skin and one quiet beat refuse, for now, to be taken.
