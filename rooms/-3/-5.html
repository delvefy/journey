Berry

You hang in little clusters along the hedge, small and round and bright as beads - red, black, or deep blue-purple. A bird snaps you up whole; a child stains both hands and mouth gathering you by the fistful. Some of you are sweet, some sour, and a few are best left well alone. Sun-warm and full of juice, you are the easiest fruit in the world to pick, and the first gone when summer ends.

North

I am the little nut the oak
lets fall upon the ground;
I wear a tiny wooden cap,
a whole tree sleeping sound.

East

I am the heavy, sticky earth,
all soft and brown and wet;
you squeeze and shape me into pots,
then bake me hard - don't fret.

South

I am a smooth white little box,
no lid, no hinge, no key;
a yellow treasure hides inside,
a chick will set it free.

West

Of twigs and grass and bits of moss,
the birds will weave me round;
high in the branches, snug and warm,
their eggs in me are found.
