Starlight

You are the faintest light there is, the cold silver shine of a thousand thousand stars, each so far that its glow has crossed unthinkable years of dark to reach your eye. On a clear night away from the lamps you pour down soft and grey, enough to walk by, not enough to read. Sailors steered whole lives by you; shepherds named their flocks of you; children wish on you and fall asleep beneath you. You are the oldest light in the world, set out across the whole black sky, patient and pale and almost not there at all.

North

The little flicker of a star,
that winks up in the night;
'twinkle, twinkle,' near and far,
a tiny dancing light.

East

Red and gold, I leap and crack,
I feed on log and coal;
I warm the cold, I light the black,
but burn the careless soul.

South

The pale cold glow the night-sky pours,
borrowed from the sun;
it silvers fields and sleeping doors,
when the day is done.

West

Twelve long months make up my round,
four seasons, turning slow;
from one New Year to the next is found,
the longest count you know.
