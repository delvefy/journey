Reed

A tall hollow stalk that grows in crowds at the water's edge, whispering when the wind moves through. The reed is wholly natural and wholly there to touch; cut and hollowed, it becomes a pipe. It keeps the green south-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

A green plant of the damp and shade,
my leaves uncurl from a tight-wound coil;
no flower I bear, no fruit at all —
I spread my fronds in the woodland soil.

East

Brush my leaves and you'll regret it —
a sting, a rash, an itch for an hour;
I grow in the ditch, I look so soft,
but touch me bare and you'll know my power.

South

A marsh-grass close to the reed, my kin,
round-stemmed and green by the boggy ground;
the old folk wove my stalks to mats
and strewed their floors. Name me, soft and round.

West

Dug in brown blocks from the boggy moor,
old rotted moss pressed dark and deep;
dried in the sun, then burned for warmth —
the highland fire from the soggy heap.
