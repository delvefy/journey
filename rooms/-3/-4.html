The Naught

East of where nothing began, and south of the falling grey, there is not even the grey to fall. The bottomless drift a step east at least had its endless silt to sink through; the raw cold a step north at least had a ground to be cold. Here there is neither. You reach and touch nothing — not a wall, not a floor, not the cool grain of root nor the slump of silt nor the lift of a draught — nothing, in the plain flat sense the word almost never gets to mean: no thing, anywhere your hand goes, in any direction it tries. The senses cast about and come back empty. The naught gives them nothing to be senses of.

It is the Waste taken all the way down — past ash, past drift, past the grey itself, to where even nothingness has nothing to be the absence of. You draw the spiral and there is no medium for it and no event of it; the gesture goes out into the naught and is neither kept nor lost nor witnessed, for there is nothing here to keep, lose, or witness. Only the whorl in your own skin remains, and even it feels thinned, as though the naught were very slowly persuading your hand that it, too, is one of the many things that are not.

There is no count, and worse than no count: there is nothing for a count to sound in. Your heart beats and the naught does not even fail to hear it — hearing is a thing, and there are no things — and the beat goes nowhere, into nothing, kept only inside yourself, a small closed circle of being in an open nothing that neither presses on it nor lets it go.

To the north the naught firms to raw cold ground, the unbegun, where at least nothing has ever begun. To the east the nothing gives onto the falling grey, the bottomless drift, silt to sink through. To the south it deepens toward the pole, where even nothing is too much, the absolute floor of the Waste. To the west it opens wider into the forgotten distance, where even the nothing has been forgotten. You were going somewhere, once. There is nowhere here to go, and nothing to go through, and no you the naught will quite admit to — and still the going persists, the one small thing in all the nothing that refuses, for now, to not be.
