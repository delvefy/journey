Acorn

Pick one up from under the oak: smooth, brown, and snug in its little rough cap, small enough to roll between two fingers. Yet inside this hard bead sleeps a whole tree - trunk, branches, a hundred autumns of leaves - waiting only for soil and rain and time. The squirrels know its worth and bury more than they remember, and from the forgotten ones the forest quietly grows. The biggest things, the oak says, begin as something you could lose down the back of a chair.

North

Too low to be a tree, I sprawl,
a tangle thick and green and small;
with berries, twigs, and leaves I hide
the little birds that nest inside.

East

When the rain has had its way,
the ground turns brown and soft as clay;
it sticks to boots and pigs adore it,
a squelchy mess - you can't ignore it.

South

A little round and juicy fruit,
red or blue, I'm sweet to boot;
I grow in clusters on the bush,
birds gobble me with a hungry push.

West

Nobody plants me, still I grow,
in cracks and paths, in every row;
the gardener tugs me out with a frown,
an unloved plant he wants pulled down.
