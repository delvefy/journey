snowdrift

A snowdrift is where the wind has gathered the snow and piled it deep, heaped up soft and high against a wall or a hedge or the side of the lane. The road may be almost clear, and then a drift as tall as you blocks the way entirely. Step in one and you sink to the waist with a whump. It is snow that would not stay where it fell - blown and banked and drifted into great smooth dunes of cold white.

North

Jump in the puddle, in goes your boot -
up flies the water, all around;
a wet loud burst in the bath or pool,
SPLOSH! with a watery sound.

East

A little ball of ice that falls,
it bounces off the roof - rat-tat!;
hard and round and cold as winter,
down from a storm cloud, just like that.

South

Hot and damp and sticky air,
a heavy day with no fresh breeze;
your shirt sticks to your sweaty back -
close and clammy, no relief, no ease.

West

A blustery day with the wind let loose,
it whips your hair and steals your hat;
kites fly high and the trees all bend -
hold on tight to this and that.
