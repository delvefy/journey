bog

A bog is wet, spongy ground that quakes underfoot and tries to swallow your boots whole. The earth here is more water than soil, soft and brown and squelching, growing strange mosses and the odd hungry plant. Step wrong and you sink to the knee with a great rude squelch. Bogs keep their secrets for a very long time: ancient things dropped in have come up thousands of years later, perfectly kept. Tread carefully - the bog does not give back in a hurry.

North

A slimy black worm in the pond,
it sticks to your leg and sucks your blood;
doctors once used me to cure the sick -
a fat little sucker from the mud.

East

A wet and muddy soggy land,
where reeds grow tall and the water's still;
crocodiles lurk and mosquitoes hum -
your boots get stuck, your wellies fill.

South

A wall built strong across a stream
to hold the water back in a lake;
a beaver makes one out of sticks -
it stops the flow for the river's sake.

West

A ring of water round a castle,
deep and wide to keep foes out;
lower the drawbridge to get across -
no swimming in, without a doubt.
