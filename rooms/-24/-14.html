leech

A leech is a soft black worm that drinks blood. It lives in still ponds and slow streams, and if you wade in, it fastens on your leg with its little sucker mouth and quietly swells. Doctors once used them on purpose, jars full of the wriggling things, to draw out a patient's blood. Slippery, stretchy, and wholly a thing you can touch - though you would rather not - it is the hungriest, most patient little creature in the muddy water.

North

I glue myself to rocks and ships,
a tiny crusty cone;
the tide rolls in, I open up
and feed, then shut like stone.

East

A little grey armoured bug
beneath the rotting log;
poke me and I roll up tight -
a ball in the damp and fog.

South

A soft and squelchy, soggy ground,
all mud and moss and mire;
step in and you sink to your knees -
wet land you can't admire.

West

A tiny black blob with a wiggly tail,
I swim in the springtime pond;
I'll lose my tail and grow four legs -
soon a frog is what I'll be.
