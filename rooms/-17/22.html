foam

Foam is the white lace the sea wears when it breaks - a thousand tiny bubbles crowded into one soft, weightless crowd. It rides the top of every wave and the rim of every washed-up thing, hissing as it sinks back into the water. You can scoop it but never hold it; it pops to nothing in your warm hand. Light as breath, born of air caught in water, it is the sea's own white breath.

North

On cold clear nights I paint the glass
with feathers white that no bird grew;
I edge the grass and crisp the leaf,
then melt to nothing in morning dew.

East

I'm green and gooey, cold and thick,
I slip and slither, wet and slow;
a snail will leave me as a trail -
the gungy goo you surely know.

South

When a sting makes your skin puff up and rise,
or the sea heaves round before it breaks,
or bread grows big and fat in the oven -
that rising-up is the move I make.

West

With soap and water, scrub and rub,
I get the dirt off hands and floor;
the waves do me along the beach -
I rinse things clean and nothing more.
