Sparrow

You are a small plain brown-and-grey bird, the commonest of all, hopping in the gutter and the city square for crumbs. No hand makes you; you nest under the eaves, chirp from the hedge, squabble in a chattering flock over a dropped chip. Cheeky, quick, and never far from people.

North

A tiny speck that bites and leaps,
it hides in the dog's warm hair;
it springs a hundred times its height -
a circus all its share.

East

A small quick reptile, scaled and dry,
that basks upon a wall;
it scurries off on four short legs
and sheds its tail in a fall.

South

A big white bird with an S-curved neck
that glides on the still lake;
graceful and proud, it hisses to guard -
the cygnets in its wake.

West

A soft white bird that coos so gently,
the sign of peace, they say;
it carried the leaf to the ark of old -
it flutters white away.
