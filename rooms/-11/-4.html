Toad

You are a warty, lumpy, hopping creature, a frog's drier and dumpier cousin. No hand makes you; you crouch in the cool damp shade by day and hunt slugs and beetles by night, your tongue flicking out quick. Your skin is dry and bumpy, brown as the earth. You puff up fat when frightened, squat and patient and wart-covered.

North

A great grey fish with rows of teeth
that prowls the deep blue sea;
its fin slices the surface still -
the fiercest fish to be.

East

It hangs to sleep all day, head-down,
then flits about at night;
with leathery wings, no feathers at all -
it squeaks and shuns the light.

South

A tiny speck that bites and leaps,
it hides in the dog's warm hair;
it springs a hundred times its height -
a circus all its share.

West

A little black blob with a wriggly tail
that swims in the springtime pond;
it grows four legs and loses its tail -
a frog it turns beyond.
