glisten

To glisten is to shine with a wet little sparkle - the way dew glistens on a spider's web at dawn, or tears glisten in someone's eyes, or wet pebbles glisten down at the tideline. It is not a steady glow but a glints-and-twinkles kind of shine, the light bouncing off something damp and smooth. Anything wet in the sunshine glistens. It is the brightness of water catching light, scattered into a hundred tiny stars.

North

Polished bright so it catches the light,
a new coin, a clean car, a wet stone too;
it gleams and bounces the sun right back -
all sparkly, bright and new.

East

The wild glad cry that bursts right out
when something's too good to keep within;
hands in the air, up on your toes -
'WHOOP!' you shout with a grin.

South

A light so bright it hurts your eyes,
the sun on snow, a headlight's glare;
you blink and squint and look away -
too bright and blinding to bear.

West

After you run, or on a hot day,
your skin goes wet and salty and slick;
drips on your forehead, damp on your back -
all hot and sticky, quick!
