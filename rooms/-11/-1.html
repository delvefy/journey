Mane

You are the long flowing hair along the neck - the horse's, the lion's, streaming in the wind as they run. No hand makes you; you grow there of your own, thick and proud. The wind combs you; the gallop tosses you. On the lion you are a great gold ruff that frames the roar. You are the crowning hair of the running, roaring beast.

North

A crowd of trees too thick to count,
dark green from edge to edge;
deer slip through it, owls call in it -
the wild wood past the hedge.

East

The hard horn foot of horse or deer
that clatters on the stone;
the blacksmith nails a shoe to it -
it bears the weight alone.

South

A little patch of still cool water,
smaller than a lake;
you swim in one, or paddle one,
where children splash and shake.

West

A long sharp tooth, curved to a point,
in the jaw of wolf or snake;
it sinks in deep, it grips, it bites -
a deadly point to make.
