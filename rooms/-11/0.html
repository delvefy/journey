Forest

You are a thousand trees standing together, a green roof over a shadowed floor. No hand plants you; you grow wild and deep, dark between the trunks even at noon. Birds nest in you, deer hide in you, mushrooms push up from your damp leaf-mould. People lose their way in you and tell tales of you. You are the old wild wood, vast and breathing, older than any road.

North

A tickle on your skin that nags
and begs to be scratched away;
a midge's bite, a scratchy vest -
it will not let you play.

East

The highest heap of rock and snow
that climbs into the sky;
its peak is white, its sides are steep -
too tall for clouds to fly.

South

The long thick hair upon the neck
of horse or lion grand;
it streams behind them as they run -
the proudest hair in the land.

West

A land of sand and burning sun
where almost no rain falls;
the camel crosses, dunes roll on,
and not one green thing grows tall.
