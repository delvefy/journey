Itch

You are the small maddening tickle on the skin that begs to be scratched - a gnat's bite, a woollen jumper, a healing scab. No hand makes you; the skin raises you on its own. Scratch, and you ease for a moment, then return worse than before. You travel, too: watch one person dig at you and soon the whole room is scratching. You are the body's tiny nag.

North

Far bigger than a single flame,
a roaring wall of gold;
the bonfire's leap, the burning barn -
a fierce hot fire, uncontrolled.

East

The dry-mouth need that calls for drink
when you have run all day;
your tongue is parched, you long for water -
it will not go away.

South

Where the trees stand thick as a crowd
and shut the sky from sight;
you walk for miles beneath their boughs
in green and shadowed light.

West

The sharp small bite a nettle gives,
or a bee that's cross with you;
a hot quick prick upon the skin -
red, and smarting too.
