wheelchair

A wheelchair is a chair with two big wheels, made for someone whose legs cannot carry them. You push the rims with your hands, or someone pushes the handles behind, and you roll smoothly where walking is too hard. It has brakes for the slopes and rests for the feet, and it goes wherever a ramp will let it. It is freedom on wheels - a way to move through the world sitting down, going anywhere the path is smooth.

North

When your arm is hurt and must not swing,
a loop of cloth holds it to your chest;
round your neck and under the elbow -
it keeps the sore arm still at rest.

East

A heavy wooden shoe to wear,
I clop and clatter across the floor;
the Dutch will dance in pairs of me,
carved from a block, and nothing more.

South

When you break your arm the doctor wraps it
in something hard and white as chalk;
your friends all sign it with their names
while the bone mends and you cannot walk.

West

The doctor hangs me round her neck,
two tips for ears, a disc that's cold;
I press your chest and hear your heart -
thump-thump, the secret beats I hold.
