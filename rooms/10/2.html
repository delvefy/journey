Hour

You are sixty minutes counted out, the great slice the clock cuts the day into - twenty-four of you, day and night. No hand can hold you, yet people made you: the clock's slow journey of the big hand once around its face. Lessons last you, trains run by you, the bells of the town strike you out across the roofs. You can crawl when you are bored and bolt when you are glad. A wrought measure of time, you are the day's main step, struck and gone.

North

A short sharp electronic note
the microwave gives out;
the truck that's reversing, the watch alarm -
a tone, a tiny shout.

East

A group of twelve, no more, no less,
of eggs or buns or roses;
the baker throws one extra in,
and thirteen he proposes.

South

An hour at school to learn a thing,
with chalk and book and board;
your spelling, sums, and history -
fresh knowledge to be stored.

West

Numbers added in a column
to find what they make whole;
the total on the bottom line -
give it, and pass the goal.
