Sponge

You are the soft full-of-holes squeezable block that drinks up water and gives it back at a squeeze. Once your kind grew in the sea; now mostly a maker foams and cuts you, light and yielding, full of little caves. You wipe the spill, you wash the car, you soak the suds along the bath. Dry, you are hard and shrivelled; wet, you swell up soft and heavy. Thirsty and forgiving, you are the humble wrought drinker of messes, holding far more than your size should allow.

North

Hung in folds across the glass,
I'm drawn at night for sleep;
pulled wide at dawn to let in day -
the window's cloth to keep.

East

A soft fat pad to sit upon,
stuffed plump with foam or down;
it props you on the sofa snug
or props your tired head down.

South

A wide flat cloth spread on the bed,
smooth, white, and tucked in tight;
you lie between a pair of me
in comfort through the night.

West

A thick soft cloth that dries you off
when bath or swim is through;
it drinks the water from your skin
and hangs to dry for you.
