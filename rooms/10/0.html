Bus

You are the big long road-going box that carries many at once, rows of seats behind your wide windows, a driver up front and a bell to ring for your stop. A factory built you of steel and glass; you are wholly wrought. You rumble from stop to stop, doors hissing open and shut, swallowing the morning queue and the schoolchildren and the shopping bags. Red or yellow, single or double-decked, you are the patient public workhorse of the street, never quite empty, never quite on time.

North

An hour at school spent learning things,
with a teacher at the board;
your maths, your spelling, history -
new knowledge to be stored.

East

A boxy vehicle for goods,
with room behind to load;
the postman drives one, so does the plumber,
parked along the road.

South

A coloured cloth upon a pole
that flaps and waves up high;
it stands for country, club, or team
and flutters in the sky.

West

Four wheels, an engine, and a horn,
it carries you to town;
turn the key, it growls awake,
the road goes rolling down.
