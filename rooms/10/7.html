Scratch

You are the rake of nails across an itch, the relief that follows the maddening tickle. No hand-but-one makes you: your own. The dog does you with a back leg, the cat down the sofa-arm. You leave thin red lines and a screech down a blackboard. Quick and rough and satisfying.

North

Put your hands flat and shove it away,
the opposite of pull;
you do it to a heavy door
or a swing when it's not full.

East

Grab it tight and drag it near,
the opposite of push;
the tug-of-war, the cart, the rope -
heave it through the bush.

South

When winter wind slips down your neck,
your teeth go click-click-click;
you tremble, shake, you cannot stop -
a cold and shaky kick.

West

You wake, you yawn, and reach up high,
arms wide, your spine pulled long;
the cat does it, the rubber band -
you pull until it's strong.
