Lock

You are the small steel guardian of the door, a slot and a turning throat that only the right key can persuade. A locksmith cut your pins and springs; you are wholly wrought, made for one job - to hold fast against all but the one who carries your match. You guard the house, the diary, the treasure chest. Click home and nothing passes; turn the wrong key and you stay stubborn. Patient and unbribable, you keep what is kept until the right tooth comes to free it.

North

A coloured cloth that flies on high,
run up a tall straight pole;
it stands for king or club or land
and flaps to play its role.

East

The part you grip to open a door,
or to lift a heavy pail;
a bar or knob to hold and turn,
so your grasp will never fail.

South

I hang in folds across the glass
and draw to hide the night;
pulled back at dawn to greet the day,
I let in morning light.

West

A table where you sit to write,
with drawers to stow your pen;
at school you bend above me,
and tidy up again.
