Clink

You are the small bright ringing of glass on glass or coin on coin - the toast raised and touched, the cutlery in the drawer, the change jingling in a pocket. No hand makes you by itself; it is two hard made things tapping that strike your tiny note. You are the cheerful little sound of celebration and of small money, the ting of the pub, the chink of the piggy bank. A slight wrought chime, you are the merry music of things made of glass and metal, knocking gently together.

North

A big loud swallow, all at once,
of water or of dread;
down it goes in one great glug
before a word is said.

East

The dull heavy sound of a thing let drop,
a sack or a book on the floor;
no ring, no clang, just a soft flat thump -
a sound that says no more.

South

A short flat note a machine gives off,
the till, the alarm, the phone;
a tiny tuneless electric cheep -
a sound the circuits own.

West

One bright clear note of a little bell,
the lift, the shop-door chime;
a single ring, then nothing more -
it tells you supper-time.
