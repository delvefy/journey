Curtain

You are the panel of cloth hung across the window, gathered on a rail to be drawn at dusk and parted at dawn. A maker sewed and hemmed you; you are wholly wrought, sometimes heavy velvet, sometimes thin and bright. You shut out the night and the cold and the prying street; you open on the morning. You rise on the stage to start the play and fall to end it. Soft, full, and faithful, you are the room's own eyelid, drawn closed for sleep and back for day.

North

I clasp the door and hold it fast,
a keyhole for my eye;
the right key turns and I let go,
the wrong one, none gets by.

East

A tall glass pot to hold the flowers,
set upon the sill;
fill me up with water fresh
and roses, daffodil.

South

A soft and holey squashy thing
that soaks up all the wet;
squeeze me dry into the bowl -
the cleanest helper yet.

West

A thick warm woollen bed-cover,
spread over you at night;
pull me to your chin and sleep
until the morning light.
