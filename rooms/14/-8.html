Gown

You are a gown - a long flowing dress that sweeps to the floor, sewn for the grandest of days. A bride's white gown, a ball gown that swishes when you turn, the soft nightgown you slip on for bed. Made by patient hands from yards of cloth, you hang from the shoulders and pool at the feet. Put me on, and the plainest person stands a little taller, a little finer.

North

A little cloth tied round the neck
of a baby at her food;
it catches all the dribbled mush
and keeps her clean and good.

East

A great iron weight on a chain,
dropped from the ship to the ground;
it digs in deep and holds her still,
so she won't drift around.

South

I sit upon the top of you,
to keep off sun or rain;
woolly, straw, or floppy-brimmed -
put me on again.

West

A thin and floaty piece of cloth
that hangs across the face;
the bride wears me upon her head,
all soft and white as lace.
