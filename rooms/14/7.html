Lean

You are the body tipped from straight, weight rested on something else - against the wall, over the gate, into the wind that holds you up. No falling in you, just tilting and trusting. The tower at Pisa has done it for centuries. Lean on me, the song says - and I will not let you down.

North

Bend your knees and sink down low,
all folded, close to the ground;
the cat does it before it pounces -
small, and tight, and round.

East

Pick it up and raise it high,
heave it off the ground;
you lift the box, the weight, the cup -
up, without a sound.

South

Push the air out through your lips,
to cool the soup or the tea;
you do it hard at birthday cakes -
the candles flicker free.

West

You do it when you touch your toes,
the road does round the hill;
a wire once straight is now a curve -
it gives, and holds it still.
