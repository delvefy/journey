Fridge

You are the cold white cupboard that hums in the kitchen corner, keeping the milk sweet and the butter hard. Made by clever hands, you trap the chill inside and breathe out warmth behind. Open my door and the light comes on, the cold air sighs out. Shut it quick, they say, you're letting the cold escape.

North

A tall wood cupboard for your clothes,
a door that swings on a glass;
push through the coats to the very back -
to snow, perhaps, you'll pass.

East

A little box that browns your bread,
two slots to drop it in;
you push it down, it pops back up -
hot toast, all crisp and thin.

South

A little bed with high barred sides
where the baby sleeps;
it rocks, it has a soft warm sheet -
inside, the small one keeps.

West

The long soft seat where the family flops
to watch the screen at night;
deep cushions, room for three or four -
you sink in, snug and tight.
