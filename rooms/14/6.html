Blow

You are the push of air out through the lips - the breath that cools the soup, the wind put on the candles, the big huff at the dandelion clock to scatter its seeds. No machine needed; just your own two lungs. Take a deep breath, purse your mouth, and blow - and the world in front of you scatters.

North

Tip your body to one side,
rest against the wall;
the tower of Pisa does it too -
it tilts, but does not fall.

East

A tiny little drink, just a taste,
you take it slow and small;
too hot to gulp, so bit by bit -
the least swig of all.

South

Sink your teeth into the food,
a chunk torn off to eat;
the dog does it, the apple feels it -
sharp teeth that snap and meet.

West

A short quick blow of breath or air,
the candle's flame put out;
the steam train chuffs, the smoker's pipe -
that breath comes puffing out.
