Walls

East and down, where the made meets the touchable, you come to the corner the world calls by this name. You have spent your whole journey unsaying them — every riddle a wall, every answer a way through. Now they gather here, at the rim, and stand. A wall is only a question that has not been answered yet. You have answered all the others. Lay your hand on this one; it is the last, and it is warm.

North

I am a wall that learned to turn,
the one place in the ring that yields;
hung upon iron, swung on hinge,
I let the road in from the fields.

West

Headed, but witless; I have one leg,
and only ever drive it down.
Iron teeth bit me into being,
iron teeth can draw me from the wood —
yet I hold your roof up, bone by bone.
