shutter

A shutter is a flap of wood hinged beside a window, ready to fold across the glass. Close it and the room goes dark and safe; the storm can howl and the shutter holds. Open it in the morning and it bangs back against the wall to let the daylight pour in. Painted bright on old houses, slatted to let a little air through, it is the window's own wooden eyelid, opening and shutting on the world outside.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

North

A padded bag you climb inside
to keep you warm when camping out;
zip it up right to your chin -
a cosy cocoon, no doubt.

East

Flat and bright, I show you films,
cartoons and games light up my face;
the telly, the tablet, the cinema wall -
a glowing window in its place.

South

A muddy pen where pigs all live,
they wallow and snort in the squelchy ground;
the dirtiest, messiest home of all -
oink, oink, the pigs are found.

West

Pull my cord and down I roll
to cover the window and keep out sun;
I darken the room for sleeping in -
up at dawn when night is done.
