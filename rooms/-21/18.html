drench

To drench is to soak through and through, to leave nothing dry. The cloudburst catches you in the open, and in a minute you are drenched - hair flat, shoes squelching, every thread of you heavy with rain. It is water at its most generous and least careful, holding nothing back. You cannot keep it; it pours off you, runs down, drips away. Natural and ungraspable, the sky emptying itself all over you at once.

North

Hold it under the running tap
to wash the soap away;
a quick clean splash of water -
no soak, no stay.

East

Fill the bowl too full of soup
and tip it as you walk;
it spills and splatters down the side -
a careless, sloshing mess.

South

Not a gush and not a flood,
just the thinnest thread of wet;
down the window after rain
I run, the slowest stream yet.

West

Plip. The tap is not shut tight,
and once in every while
a single drop falls in the sink -
plip, plip, all the while.
