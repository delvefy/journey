driftwood

Driftwood is wood the sea has finished with - a branch torn from some far tree, rolled and bleached and smoothed by months of salt water, then flung up onto the sand. You pick it up: grey, light, bone-dry, worn soft, its bark long gone. Natural and wholly touchable, but homeless now; it belongs to no forest, only to the tideline where the waves leave their wandering, half-rotted gifts.

North

From deep in the hill I bubble out,
cold and clear and fresh;
the very start of every stream,
I rise up through the earth.

East

I build my home beneath the waves,
a stony branching tree;
bright fish dart among my arms
in the warm and shallow sea.

South

A wooden arm out into the sea,
where little boats tie up and stay;
you walk along my narrow planks
above the splashing spray.

West

I am a shell that shuts up tight,
two halves held like a trap;
buried in the sandy shore,
I close upon you with a snap.
