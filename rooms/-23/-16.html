canal

A canal is a river that people dug - long and straight and patient, cut across the land to carry boats where no river ran. The water barely moves. Painted narrowboats drift along it, and at every change of height a lock fills and empties to lift them up or let them down. Ducks paddle the still green water; a towpath runs alongside for walking. It is water put to work, going exactly where we told it to.

North

A wet and muddy soggy land,
where reeds grow tall and the water's still;
crocodiles lurk and mosquitoes hum -
your boots get stuck, your wellies fill.

East

A deep round hole dug down for water,
a bucket on a rope drops low;
wind the handle, up it comes -
cool and fresh from far below.

South

I carry my house upon my back,
a hard domed shell where I can hide;
slow and old, I plod along -
I beat the hare, the story cried.

West

A wall built strong across a stream
to hold the water back in a lake;
a beaver makes one out of sticks -
it stops the flow for the river's sake.
