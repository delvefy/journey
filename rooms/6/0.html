Cog

A wheel rimmed with teeth that bite into the teeth of the next, so that one turning turns them all — the small patient piece that makes the clock and the mill go round. The cog is a made thing of metal and motion. It keeps the worked east, due along the wrought axis. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

The speed at which a tune is played,
fast as a jig or slow as a dirge;
the player holds it steady and true —
name the pace of the song. Let it surge.

East

A bent steel arm set on a shaft,
you grasp and turn it round and round;
it drives the wheel, it works the well —
name the winding handle found.

South

Two long arms that pinch and grip,
I lift the coal, I hold the meat;
the smith takes up the red-hot iron
with me — no fingers feel that heat.

West

The bar the cartwheels turn upon,
straight through their hubs, holding them true;
without me no wheel could roll —
name the still rod. Through and through.
