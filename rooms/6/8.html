Sneeze

You are the body's sudden storm - a tickle in the nose, a gathering, and then the great explosive achoo that nobody can stop. No hand makes you; pepper brings you, dust brings you, the bright sun brings you, and out you burst at a hundred miles an hour. They cover their mouths for you and say bless you after. You scatter a cold from one nose to the next. A wild wrought reflex, half a fright and half a relief, gone in a single blown breath.

North

A short sharp pull of air up the nose,
to catch a cold's wet drip,
or to smell the dinner cooking -
a quick and nosy sip.

East

A bubble of air from a fizzy drink
comes climbing back up loud;
it pops out at the dinner table
and shames you in the crowd.

South

Both eyelids drop and rise again
in a fraction of a beat;
you do it without thinking,
a flutter quick and neat.

West

A long hard look that will not move,
fixed fast upon one spot;
rude at strangers, lost in thought -
a gaze that blinks not.
