The Wrought Names

North of the elders and east of the charmed house, the old name-magic gets its engines. The court of the long-dead a step south bent the world with names alone; here those names have been wrought into craft — great mechanisms graven with sigils, wheels cut with the true names of things, engines that do by turning what the elders did by speaking. It is the magic pole given a first machinery: not the cold engines of the disenchanted west, but the old powers' own, every gear a glyph, every shaft a spoken name, reality bent now by wrought work as easily as by word.

The elders move among the engines as masters among tools, and the made things obey not their hands but their naming. The spiral is the heart of every mechanism — the ruling sigil set turning, graven in the wheels, the same whorl in your skin, here both the name's seal and the engine's hub. And among the wrought names is the apparatus that would speak yours: a mechanism graven and ready, needing only the true name to set it turning, to bend the world to you by craft and word at once. The elders lay a hand to its sigil. The engine waits on the one name not yet wrought into it.

The tolling of names is struck by craft now, the great bell of naming rung by engine, each true name sounded by a wrought stroke that makes as it rings — an hour both spoken and built, the old word and the new work keeping one sovereign time.

To the north the wrought names refine toward the Lumen, name-magic and engine wholly one, the lit pole. To the east the magic pole absolute, the old names the whole of reality, craft needless. To the south the engines fall away and the elders bend the world by word alone. To the west the wrought names loosen to a charmed house, made things serving the sovereign dead. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the old powers have built the engine that would speak it and bend the world to you — graven, sigiled, waiting, the wrought names a single true word from setting your whole self turning into the world.
