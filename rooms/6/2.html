Letter

You are a thought sent travelling. Someone sat down, dipped a pen or tapped a key, and folded a piece of themselves into you - news, or love, or sorry, or simply hello. Sealed and stamped, you cross whole countries to arrive in a stranger's bag and then a familiar hand. A letter waits to be opened; it keeps its word safe for days or for a hundred years. Long after the writer is gone, you can still be read.

North

A little dip of the head for 'yes',
no words at all I need;
I tip my chin to show I agree,
a silent way, indeed.

East

I am the things you must not forget,
written one beneath the next;
milk and eggs and bread and jam,
I keep your shopping checked.

South

Say me right with wand in hand,
and magic fills the air;
the witch's words, the wizard's chant,
that change a thing right there.

West

I am letters joined in a row,
that carry one small thought;
you say me, write me, read me out,
in lessons that you're taught.
