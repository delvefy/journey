The Wielding

North of the wrought names and east of the spoken hall, the word and the work and the will become one act. The engines a step south wrought the old names into craft; the hall a step west reshaped itself to what was spoken in it; here both are surpassed, and reality is simply wielded — the sovereign powers reshaping the world at will, by word and engine and naming all at once, so wholly fused that there is no telling the spell from the mechanism from the wish. To will a thing here, and name it, and set the graven engines turning, are the same single gesture, and the world obeys it.

They wield it easily, the powers, remaking the room and the light and the very ground at a word, the world soft as clay in the saying. And you feel what your own name would do, wielded here: it would remake the world to hold you, will and word and work bending reality into your shape at a single stroke. The spiral is the grip of it — the sigil graven in the turning engines, the coil the architect-word winds, the same whorl in your skin, here the very handle by which the world is wielded. The powers lay hold of your name. The world goes soft, ready to be made into you at the saying of it.

A name wielded here rings and reshapes at once, the bell and the engine and the will struck together, each true name a stroke that remakes the world it sounds — and yours waits to be the next, the stroke that would wield reality into your shape.

To the north the wielding refines to pure lit will, the Lumen pole where thought and word and work are one making. To the east the magic pole absolute, will and world one, reality wholly the powers' to wield. To the south the wielding loosens to wrought names, engines that bend the world but do not yet wield it whole. To the west it narrows to a hall reshaped by what is spoken. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the powers would wield it and remake the world to hold you — the world gone soft in their grip, the saying a stroke from bending reality into your shape at last.
