Chair

You are four legs and a flat lap and a back to lean on - the simplest kindness a room can offer a tired body. You ask nothing and hold whoever comes: the king on his throne and the child at her crayons sit alike on you. You wait at the table, you gather round the fire, you stand empty and patient when everyone has gone to bed. Pull me out, sit down, and the long day finally sets its weight down somewhere.

North

With a handle and a round deep mouth,
I carry water to and fro;
you fill me at the well or tap,
then tip me where you go.

East

With a soft white sheet and a pillow plump,
I'm where you go to rest your head;
you climb in warm when day is done,
and dream until the morning's spread.

South

With a row of bristles, short and tight,
I tidy up your hair;
or dipped in colour, stroke by stroke,
I paint the wall with care.

West

A bundle of bristles on a pole,
across the floor I sweep;
I gather dust into a pile,
the tidy house I keep.
