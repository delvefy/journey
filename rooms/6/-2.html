The World-Dream

South of the antlered court and east of the naming wood, the gods put off the last of the elder and become wholly the wild, and the wild becomes the world. The court a step north still ruled; the wood a step west still spoke. Here there is no ruling and no speaking, only dreaming — vast beast-gods whose every slow breath is the world, in whom naming and being are one act, reality their continuous dream, dreamed and named and lived in the same wild motion. You stand inside the gods' dreaming of the world, and the world is exactly as deep and as alive and as unbidden as a dream.

They dream you as you enter, the way a dream takes up a newcomer without surprise, and you feel yourself become part of the dreaming — named into it, dreamed into it, the two the same. Your name is a word in the gods' long dream of the world, and to be dreamed-and-named here is simply to be, woven into the wild's reality as one of its dreamed things. The spiral is the turn the dream takes, horn and breath and the coil of the world's own dreaming, the same whorl in your skin, here the seed the world-dream dreams you from. A god breathes your name into the dream. The world stirs to hold you, dreamed and named and nearly woven in.

The world-making pulse is the gods' dreaming-breath, the one rhythm under everything, naming and being and dreaming kept on a single slow beat — and your name waits to be breathed onto it, dreamed real on the rhythm of the world.

To the north the dream firms to a court, the gods ruling as antlered elders. To the east the Wyld pole absolute, reality wholly the gods' dream, no telling the dream from the world. To the south the gods sink below even naming to pure beast and dream, the deep Wyld. To the west the dreaming becomes a speaking, the wood whose word makes the world. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the gods would dream it and name it in one breath and weave you into the world's long dream — the world stirring to hold you, a breath from dreaming you real.
