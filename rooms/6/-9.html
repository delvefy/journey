Chain

You are a row of iron rings, each one looped through the last, forged link by link by a hand and a hot fire. You bind, you hold, you lock a gate or hang a swing; pull as they might, you do not break unless your weakest link gives way. Heavy, cold, and strong, you are many small loops made into one long line.

North

A little circle made of gold
to slip upon a hand;
worn at a wedding, round and bright -
the smallest golden band.

East

A little cloth pouch in your coat
where hands and treasures hide;
a coin, a sweet, a marble live
in this warm pouch inside.

South

Thin and long and good to tie,
to fly a kite or wrap a box;
a ball of it lives in the drawer -
you cut a length for parcel knots.

West

A bent bit of metal, sharp at the tip,
to catch and to hang and to hold;
a fish bites one, a coat hangs on one -
it curls like a question, I'm told.
