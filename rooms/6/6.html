Gasp

You are the breath snatched suddenly in, the body's first reply to shock and wonder and fright. The conjuror's trick pulls you from a whole hall at once; the cold plunge tears you out; the unexpected ending of the story catches you sharp. 'Oh!' you say, before any thought is ready, the chest swelling, the eyes going wide. You are surprise made into air, the involuntary 'ah' of a crowd at the firework's bloom. Quick and helpless and honest, you tell the truth a heartbeat before the manners can.

North

A quick shut-and-open of the eye,
so fast you hardly see;
'gone in one,' the people cry,
the swiftest thing to be.

East

A shaking, gulping kind of cry,
when grief has burst its hold;
the tears run down, the breath comes high,
the saddest sound, all told.

South

A feather drawn along the sole,
a wriggle at the side;
it makes you squirm and lose control,
and laugh until you've cried.

West

Two shoulders lifted to the ears,
then let to fall again;
'who knows?' it says, 'don't ask me, dears,'
with no word spoken, plain.
