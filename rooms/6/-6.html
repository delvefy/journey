Sock

You are a soft knitted pocket for the foot, the first thing pulled on in the cold morning and the last warm comfort at night. Wool or cotton, striped or plain, you slip between the bare skin and the hard shoe and make the long day bearable. You go missing in the wash without fail, leaving your partner widowed in the drawer. Pulled up over the knee or slouched at the ankle, darned at the heel by a patient hand, you are small and humble and constantly lost - and sorely missed the moment you are gone.

North

A little disc with holes of two,
sewn upon your coat;
through the slit I'm pushed for you,
to hold it round your throat.

East

Of leather made to fit the foot,
with laces tied up tight;
I take you walking, soft or hard,
through morning, noon, and night.

South

Upon your head I perch up high,
to keep off sun or rain;
of straw or wool, beneath the sky,
I shade you on the lane.

West

Two crossed blades that snip and bite,
with rings for thumb and hand;
I cut the paper, clean and right,
the neatest in the land.
