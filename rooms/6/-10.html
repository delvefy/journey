String

You are a long thin cord of twisted thread, made for tying and binding - the kite's tether, the parcel's knot, the bead's necklace. A hand spins you and winds you into a ball. You tangle if you're left alone; you cut with a quick snip. Thinner than rope, fatter than thread, you tie up the loose things of the world.

North

A line of iron rings linked through,
each looped inside the next;
it locks the gate and holds the dog -
strong steel, with no defect.

East

A little bag where coins are kept,
that snaps or zips up tight;
you carry it to buy your sweets -
fish a penny out, just right.

South

A mesh of knotted string with holes,
to catch the fish or ball;
the goal, the trawl, the butterfly's doom -
it lets the water fall.

West

A long thin thread of bendy metal,
it carries the electric light;
it fences fields, it twists to shapes -
bend it and it stays put tight.
