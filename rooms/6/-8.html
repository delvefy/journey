Ring

You are a small band of metal worn upon the finger - gold or silver, plain or set with a winking stone. A jeweller shaped and polished you from a bar of metal; you are wholly wrought, a perfect made circle with no beginning and no end. You are slipped on at a wedding to mean a love that will not stop, handed down through families, lost down drains and mourned. Small, round, and bright, you are a vow you can wear, a promise made of metal.

North

Set on the head, of felt or straw,
I shade you from the sun;
I'm raised to greet a friend hello
when greetings have begun.

East

A long warm wrap with buttoned front,
for stepping out in cold;
hung on a hook beside the door,
with deep warm pockets to hold.

South

Made of metal loops linked up,
each looped through the one before;
it holds the dog, it locks the gate,
and hauls the anchor from the floor.

West

A little wooden pinching clip
that grips the washing line,
or a stake hammered in the ground
to hold the tent up fine.
