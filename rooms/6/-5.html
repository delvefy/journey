The World-Seed

South of the sovereign wild and east of the quickening, the most-alive world draws down into a seed. A step north the wild's sheer being was the whole sovereign world; a step west being and aliveness quickened up together at the source. Here the two meet at the Wyld pole and sink to their germ: the living wild, wholly sovereign and most alive, drawn down into the single seed it springs from — a world-seed where being and not-yet-being are one, the most alive and the about-to-be folded into a germ that holds the whole sovereign wild coiled and waiting to spring. The deepest source there is, and it is the most alive.

You are in the seed and you are the seed, coiled with the whole living world at the source it pours from, sovereign and unborn at once. Everything that the teeming wild ever was is here, wound tight and warm, not yet sprung but wholly alive in the not-yet. Your name is the germ-word the world-seed holds, coiled in the source, about to be sprung sovereign into a living world. The spiral is the seed's own coil, the tight warm whorl of all the wild folded to its source, the same whorl in your skin — here not a mark on the world but the world's own seed-shape, yours and the wild's at once, coiled to spring.

The world's whole pulse is coiled here to a single seed-beat, the one heart all hearts will spring from drawn down to its germ — and your heart beats with it, wound tight at the source, sovereign and unborn, one warm stroke holding the whole living count coiled and about to spring into the most-alive world.

To the north the world-seed springs to the sovereign wild, the seed risen into the whole living world. To the east the Wyld pole absolute, the living wild wholly sovereign, the world's most alive. To the south it sinks below the seed toward the floor, the wild before even the germ, the source-floor of the deep. To the west the quickening, being and aliveness welling up together. You were going somewhere, with someone, once. Here you are coiled at the living seed of the world, sovereign and unborn, the germ of all the company and the wild and the warmth — and still the warm coil turns, the world's own seed-shape, and still one seed-beat holds the whole living count, about to spring, almost named, almost sprung.
