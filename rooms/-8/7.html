Fear

You are the cold quick grip of being afraid - the heart slamming, the breath caught, the urge to run or freeze. No hand makes you; the dark, the height, the snarl in the bushes calls you up in an instant, older than thought. You sharpen the eyes and speed the blood; you have kept living things alive since the first of them. Sometimes you save a life, sometimes you spoil a night. Ancient and animal, you are the body's oldest warning, screaming danger before the mind has even looked.

North

The nagging, fretful, anxious thought
that something might go wrong;
it chews inside you, round and round,
and keeps you up too long.

East

The cool dim spot beneath a tree
where the sun cannot fall;
step out of the glare and into me -
the coolest place of all.

South

A sudden punch of wind that comes,
snatches your hat, and goes;
it bends the grass and slams the gate,
then off again it blows.

West

The hot red feeling when you've done wrong
and everybody knows;
you want to sink right through the floor
as your hot face glows.
