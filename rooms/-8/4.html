Autumn

You are the turning season, the slow gold goodbye between summer's heat and winter's cold. The leaves catch fire on the trees - red, amber, brown - then loose their hold and come spinning down to crunch underfoot. The harvest is gathered, the apples stored, the swallows gone south overnight. Mornings grow misty, evenings draw in early, the smell of woodsmoke hangs on the cooling air. You are the year growing old gracefully, scattering its riches as it goes, and teaching the world, gently, how to let things fall.

North

Some thirty days make up my span,
and twelve of me a year;
from June to May, the calendar can
name each one, never fear.

East

The east turns pink, the cock will crow,
the dark begins to thin;
the great gold sun comes up to show
the new day's about to begin.

South

A voice so soft, as light as breath,
held close beside the ear;
a secret hushed so none but you
will ever come to hear.

West

A tiny scrap of passing time,
the blink of half a thought;
'just wait a -,' you hear them chime,
so short it can't be caught.
