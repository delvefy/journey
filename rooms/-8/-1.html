Wing

You are the miracle that beats against the air and beats the earth's own pull, the feathered oar that rows the bird across the sky. Spread wide, you catch the wind and hold it; folded, you tuck neat against the side. The sparrow has you, the eagle, the bat, the bee - and with you they go where the legged things only dream of going. You ache after the long migration; you carry the whole weight of the body up into the blue. Without you, the air is just emptiness; with you, it becomes a road.

North

A sheer high wall where the land falls down,
straight to the sea below;
the gulls all nest on its rocky crown,
where no foot dares to go.

East

On two thin legs I hop and peck,
then spread my wings and fly;
I sing the dawn, I build a nest,
a feathered thing on high.

South

A little pool the rain has made,
upon the muddy ground;
the children splash and stamp and wade,
in the small wet round.

West

At the end of dog or cat I grow,
I wag to show delight;
the fish, the cow, the horse also
all swish me left and right.
