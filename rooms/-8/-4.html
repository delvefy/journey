Deer

You are the shy grace of the woodland, all long legs and great dark eyes and ears that turn to catch the faintest snap of twig. The stag carries his branching crown of antlers proudly; the doe hides her dappled fawn in the bracken and stands guard. You step so lightly the leaves barely stir, then bound away in great soaring leaps when the danger comes. Hunted down the centuries, painted on cave walls, you are the gentle wild thing at the forest's edge - here for a heartbeat, and gone like a thought.

North

Sideways on the sand I run,
in a shell both hard and round;
two nipping claws, I fear no one,
where salty waves are found.

East

Red of coat with a long brush tail,
I trot the field at night;
I raid the henhouse without fail,
and slip clean out of sight.

South

A shiny bug in a hard black case,
with six small legs to crawl;
I trundle on at a steady pace,
and curl up in a ball.

West

Like a rabbit, but longer of leg,
I race across the field;
I box in spring, I will not beg,
and to the hound won't yield.
