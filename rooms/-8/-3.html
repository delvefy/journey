Crab

You are the armoured wanderer of the shoreline, a flat round shell on jointed legs that carry you - oddly, comically - always sideways. Two great claws you hold before you, ready to nip the poking finger or crack the shell of supper. You scuttle across the wet sand, you hide in the rock pool, you bury yourself when the gull's shadow falls. When you outgrow your hard coat you shed it whole and grow another. Crusty, quick and crossways, you are the sea's small stubborn knight in his own bone armour.

North

A little pool the rain has made,
upon the muddy lane;
the children stamp, their boots get sprayed,
in the leavings of the rain.

East

With silver scales and a flicking tail,
I swim the river deep;
I breathe the water without fail,
no bed in which to sleep.

South

On slim swift legs through wood I go,
with antlers branching high;
I leap the stream, I fear the bow,
and bound when foes draw nigh.

West

The biggest beast in all the sea,
I spout a fountain high;
I am no fish, though swim I, free,
and sing a long deep sigh.
