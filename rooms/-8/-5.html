Beetle

You are a small hard-shelled crawler, your wings folded under a polished case of black or green or brown. No hand made you - you hatched from an egg in the soil and grew your armour from your own back. You trundle over leaf and stone, you burrow, you click, you fly with a clumsy drone. There are more of your kind than of almost anything alive. Tough little engine of the undergrowth, you go about the world in your own shining shell.

North

A graceful beast of wood and hill,
with antlers on the male;
it leaps the fence on slender legs
and lifts a snow-white tail.

East

A striped and angry buzzing thing
with a sting kept in its tail;
unlike the bee it stings and lives
and dives into your ale.

South

I stand all day and chew the grass
and give the milk you pour;
I have four stomachs, so they say,
and low outside the door.

West

A little buzzing summer pest
that lands upon your food;
it rubs its legs, it walks on walls,
and spoils the picnic mood.
