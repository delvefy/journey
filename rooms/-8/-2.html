Puddle

You are a small flat scrap of water lying where the rain has left it, in the dip of the path, the dent in the road. The sky looks up out of you, perfect and trembling; a careless boot shatters it and the children come running in their wellies just to stamp. You are too shallow to drown in and too wide to step across, the trap for the daydreamer and the joy of the four-year-old. The sun drinks you slowly back up, and by afternoon there is only a damp grey ghost where you shone.

North

A bird beats two of me to fly,
all covered soft with feather;
I lift it up into the sky,
and fold in stormy weather.

East

A small still pool set in the grass,
where frog and lily lie;
no waves, no tide, the clouds just pass
across me from the sky.

South

Sideways on the sand I run,
in a shell both hard and round;
two nipping claws, I fear no one,
where the salty waves are found.

West

When the cold grips the winter pond,
the water turns to glass;
so hard and slick you slide along,
and skate across my mass.
