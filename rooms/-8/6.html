Gust

You are a sudden hard shove of wind, here and gone in a breath - the door slammed, the hat snatched off, the umbrella turned inside out. No hand makes you; the moving air simply gathers itself and pushes, once, hard, then lets go. You scatter the leaves in a spinning rush, you slap the washing on the line, you raise a brief white crest on the pond. Brief and blustering, you are the wind losing its temper for just a moment, then calm again.

North

The cold and shrinking feeling of dread
when something frightens you;
it makes you run, it makes you hide,
your heart goes thudding too.

East

After the snow, before the heat,
the green and growing time;
the lambs, the buds, the longer days -
the year's first gentle climb.

South

A stretch of thirty days or so,
one leaf of the calendar turned;
from June to July, May to June -
by the moon it once was learned.

West

The hot red feeling when you're cross,
that makes you stamp and shout;
it boils up when things aren't fair
and wants to burst right out.
