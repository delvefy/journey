Sunrise

You are the daily miracle no one quite gets used to - the slow burning return of the sun over the rim of the world. First a grey hint, then a blush of pink, then the whole east on fire, and at last the gold disc itself, lifting clear. The birds sing you up; the frost begins to melt; the night's fears thin away to nothing. You promise, every single morning, that the dark was only ever borrowing the sky. Sailors and farmers and tired night-nurses all turn east to meet you, and the day begins again.

North

The warmest of the seasons four,
with the longest days of sun;
the sea, the ice cream, the open door,
and school all said and done.

East

The clock strikes twelve, the dark is deep,
the house is hushed and still;
when all but owls are fast asleep,
and the moon rides the hill.

South

What the ear drinks from the air,
a bark, a bell, a word;
no shape, no weight, it's everywhere,
and felt as soon as heard.

West

The leaves turn gold and brown and fall,
the days grow short and cool;
the harvest's in, the swallows call,
and back the children go to school.
