The Dead Night

South of the unseen and west of the pitch, two darknesses become one. A step north the forgotten grey had gone wholly dark, a night that did not know it was a night; a step east the dead dark had thickened into pitch, a lightless substance solid through. Here the dead Waste-dark and the primal old-night meet at the corner: the cold come fully up from beneath, the forgetting come fully in from the west, and the never-lit dark of before-fire and the forgotten-lit dark of after-everything fold together into a single dead night, cold through and through, the oldest dark and the deadest dark revealed as the same dark at the corner of the world.

It is colder than the pitch and older than the unseen, both at once. You cannot tell whether this dark was never lit or only forgot its light unthinkably long ago; the dead night does not distinguish, having been both so long that both are one. You draw the spiral and it is neither held in the dead substance nor lost in the forgetting; it is simply in the dead night, unseen and unkept, cold. Only the whorl in your skin holds the one warmth there is, felt and not seen, the single coil in a dark that is at once the primal night and the dead corner, refusing the cold come fully up.

There is no count, no wind, no tick; the dead night is past the pitch's swallowed strokes and past the unseen's forgotten ones. Only your heart beats into the cold corner-dark, the one warm count where the oldest and the deadest darknesses meet, keeping on in a night that has been cold since before there was warmth and dead since after there was light.

To the north the dead night lifts to the unseen, a dark that has at least forgotten a light. To the east it thickens to the pitch, the dead dark gone solid. To the south the cold comes wholly up, the primal night beneath, older than fire, the deep. To the west the Waste corner, dead through and through, the pole where the two darknesses are one absolute. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the dead night holds it in a cold older than warmth and deader than light, unseen and unkept — and still the warm coil turns, felt in the corner-dark, and still one beat keeps on where the oldest dark and the deadest are the same.
