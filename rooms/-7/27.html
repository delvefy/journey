taste

Taste is the message your tongue sends up the moment food touches it - sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and the deep savoury one too. Close your eyes and it still knows lemon from honey, knows the difference between a chip and a chocolate. It is how you tell good from bad, ripe from rotten, your favourite from your least. You cannot hold it or keep it; it lives only on the tongue, for as long as the mouthful lasts.

North

A fizzy, prickly, buzzy feeling,
pins and needles in your foot;
a sherbet sweet upon your tongue -
that sparkly shiver, head to foot.

East

A thin light mist on a hot still day,
the far hills go all soft and blurred;
not quite fog, just a shimmer of air -
the view turns vague, the edges slurred.

South

I use my teeth to grip and tear,
a chomp, a nibble, a snap at food;
a dog might do me when it's cross -
one quick clamp, polite or rude.

West

A small sharp bite, a little pinch,
a crab, a puppy, the frosty air;
quick and stinging, then it's gone -
a tiny snap to make you stare.
