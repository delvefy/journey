Island

You are a piece of land that the sea could not quite swallow - a green back rising alone out of all that blue, with water on every side and no road home. To stand on you is to be held apart from the world, ringed by horizon. You keep your own birds, your own weather, sometimes your own small people; the ships pass and do not stop. Lonely or longed-for, you are the same shape on every map: a single word of earth in a long sentence of waves.

North

Sweet and sour, salt and bitter,
my tongue can tell them all;
the sense that loves a sugar-lump,
and hates the medicine small.

East

A strip of sand where land meets sea,
with shells and waves and sun;
you build a castle, dig a moat,
and paddle just for fun.

South

I have two wings and a beak, not teeth,
I lay my eggs up in a tree;
I sing at dawn, I ride the wind,
and feathered, I fly free.

West

A tall steep wall of rock and stone,
that drops straight to the sea;
the gulls all nest upon my face,
don't lean too far off me!
