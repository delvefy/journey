Wool

You are the thick warm fleece that grows on a sheep's back, curl on curl, a whole coat against the hill weather. No hand made you - the sheep grew you through the cold months, and in summer the shears take you off in one heavy, greasy blanket. Washed and combed and spun, you become the thread that knits the jumper, the sock, the blanket on the bed. Soft, crimped, and warm, you are the meadow kept warm so we can be.

North

Hard and curved, I grow from the head
of bull or ram or goat;
I hook, I butt, I have no string,
no music in my throat.

East

The soft warm coat a beast will wear,
on fox and cat and bear;
stroke it down and it lies smooth,
and keeps the wild ones warm out there.

South

I wag my tail and lick your face
and bark to guard the door;
I fetch the stick, I chase the cat,
your friend for evermore.

West

A big white bird with a long curved neck
that hisses, honks, and bites;
it waddles to the village pond
and flies off south at nights.
