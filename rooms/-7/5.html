Summer

You are the warm, wide, generous season, the long bright days when the sun climbs high and lingers and the dark hardly comes at all. School lets out; the gardens blaze; the ice cream melts faster than it can be eaten. You smell of cut grass and salt and sun-cream, of barbecues and far-off thunder. The bees are busy, the corn ripens, the children stay out until the swifts come screaming round the rooftops. You are the height of the year, the season everyone wishes would last - and which, of course, never does.

North

The season after winter's snow,
when buds and blossoms show;
the lambs are born, the green leaves grow,
and warm soft breezes blow.

East

The sun sinks low, the lamps are lit,
the day is nearly through;
the supper's served, we rest and sit,
the quiet time for you.

South

The east turns pink, the dark grows thin,
the cock begins to crow;
the great gold sun comes climbing in,
to set the day aglow.

West

Some thirty days make up my span,
and twelve of me a year;
from June to May, the calendar can
name each one, never fear.
