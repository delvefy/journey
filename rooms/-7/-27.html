brazilnut

The brazil nut is the giant of the nut bowl - a big curved wedge in a shell so hard it laughs at the cracker. It comes from a tall tree deep in the rainforest, packed tight with its brothers inside a pod like a cannonball. Crack it at last and the creamy white nut inside is rich and oily and worth the work. It is always the last one left in the bowl at Christmas, defeating every thumb.

North

A little green nut in a beige cracked shell,
you split me open with a click;
pale green inside and salty too -
a moreish snack you eat so quick.

East

A long brown nut with a wrinkled shell,
a cousin of the walnut tree;
baked in a sticky sweet brown pie -
crunchy, rich, and nutty me.

South

Tall in the field on a leafy stalk,
yellow seeds in rows on a cob;
pop me in a pan with heat -
I burst and jump, a fluffy job.

West

A long green bug with folded arms,
I hold them up as if to pray;
I stay so still upon a leaf,
then snatch a fly and eat my prey.
