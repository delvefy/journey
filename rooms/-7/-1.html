Bird

You are a pocket of song and feather, and the air is your road. On two thin legs you hop the lawn, then open your wings and the ground lets go. You build with grass and spit, lay your pale eggs, and sing the morning up before the sun is sure of it. Some of you cross whole oceans by the stars; all of you carry the sky in your hollow bones. Cat below, hawk above - still you sing.

North

Ringed about by restless blue,
a patch of land alone;
the sea on every side of you,
you stand there on your own.

East

I stand with roots set deep in ground,
and lift green arms up high;
in summer all my leaves are found,
in autumn down they fly.

South

A small still eye of water, clear,
where frog and lily lie;
no waves, no tide, no roaring near,
just clouds that wander by.

West

A bird beats two of me to rise,
all feathered, light and wide;
I lift him up across the skies,
then fold against his side.
