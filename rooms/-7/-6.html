Horn

You are the hard curved weapon a beast grows from its own brow - the bull's, the ram's, the goat's - bone made into a spear by no smith but the blood. You hook the gate, you butt the rival, you toss the careless dog. Smooth and pale and ridged, you are sometimes hollowed to a cup, or sounded to call the hunt across the hills. Grown, not forged; worn, not carried; the wild's own blade, fixed fast to the head that swings it.

North

A yellow-and-black striped buzzing thing
that stings and stings again;
it builds a paper nest in eaves
and hunts through summer's reign.

East

The cushioned foot of dog or fox,
it leaves prints in the snow;
it scratches at the kitchen door
wherever pets may go.

South

I grow upon the sheep's broad back
until the shears come round;
then I am washed and spun and dyed
and into jumpers wound.

West

I chew the cud in the green field
and swish my tail at flies;
I give you milk in foaming pails
and moo with gentle eyes.
