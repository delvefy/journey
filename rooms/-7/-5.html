Wasp

You are summer's sharp temper in black and yellow, a sting on wings that no one welcomes to the picnic. You build a city of grey paper under the eaves, chew wood to a pulp to do it, and patrol the jam and the fallen fruit all the long hot afternoons. Unlike the bee, you keep your weapon and use it again and again, for no good reason a child can see. Quick to anger, brilliant to look at, you are the one guest at the table everyone wishes would leave - and the one most likely to stay.

North

With a coat of red and a long brush tail,
I trot the field by night;
I raid the henhouse without fail,
the sly one, out of sight.

East

Grey of wing, I fly by dark,
and beat against the pane;
the candle flame, my deadly mark,
I nibble wool again.

South

A hard curved spike upon the head,
the bull and goat both grow;
they lower me and charge ahead,
to butt a stubborn foe.

West

A shiny bug in a hard black case,
with six small legs to crawl;
I trundle on at a steady pace,
and roll up like a ball.
