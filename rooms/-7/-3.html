The Old Frost

South of the dead night and west of the dead weight, a cold older than fire comes wholly up. A step north the dead Waste-dark and the primal night had folded into one; a step east the dead substance lay as a weight come to rest. Here the cold rises the rest of the way to meet that weight: a cold from before fire, older than any warmth there was to lose, risen up through the floor to freeze the dead dark where it lies. The Waste's corner and the primal deep are one frozen mass — dead and worn and weighted through and through, and now cold past cold, an old frost where nothing was ever poured and nothing ever lifts and nothing, ever, will thaw.

It is the oldest cold and the deadest weight at once, fused into one immovable dark. You cannot tell whether the cold froze the dead substance or the dead substance always was this cold; the old frost does not distinguish, having been both since before there was fire to be the absence of. You draw the spiral and the frozen dark neither takes it nor holds it; the gesture freezes mid-made, locked in a cold from before making. Only the whorl in your skin keeps a turning warmth, the one unfrozen coil, the single thing the old frost has not yet stilled at the corner of the world.

There is no count; the cold from before fire is older than the first clock, and the dead weight too still to strike one. Your heart beats once into the old frost, and the cold takes the warmth and locks it, and a frozen age later beats again — the one warm stroke in a cold older than warmth and a weight deader than rest, refusing, slow beat by slow beat, to freeze at the corner.

To the north the old frost lifts to the dead night, the dark not yet wholly frozen. To the east the dead weight, the heavy dark come to rest but not yet cold. To the south the cold deepens toward the primal pole, older than fire, the deep before the first warmth. To the west the Waste corner absolute, dead and frozen and weighted through, the pole where nothing was ever poured. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the old frost holds it in a cold from before fire, frozen and weighted and still — and still the warm coil turns, the one unfrozen mark, and still one beat keeps on in the cold older than fire at the corner of everything.
