Fish

You are a sliver of silver in the cold green water, all fin and scale and flicking tail. You were born to the river and you will never leave it; the air would drown you in a minute. You breathe the water itself, drawing its breath through red gills, and hang in the current as easily as a thought. The heron stabs for you, the angler waits for you, the otter chases you through the weed - and still you slip away, a quick bright shadow under the surface of the world.

North

A small still pool set in the green,
where frog and lily rest;
no wave, no tide, the calmest seen,
the heron likes me best.

East

Webbed of foot and green of skin,
I leap from log to log;
with a sticky tongue I draw flies in,
and croak across the bog.

South

With a coat of red and a long brush tail,
I trot the field at night;
I raid the henhouse without fail,
the sly one, out of sight.

West

I scuttle sideways on the sand,
in a shell both hard and round;
two nipping claws I bear in hand,
where the salty waves are found.
