The Traceless

North of the forgotten and west of the last trace, the made world dissolves wholly. A step east the grey still held a faint scatter of it — rust-grains, a scale of ore, a crumb with the ghost of a cast mark, the last flecks of an age of iron; a step south even the forgetting had forgotten itself. Here the two complete each other: the last trace dissolves into the forgotten, and the forgotten takes even that. No grain, no fleck, no rust remains; the made world's farthest dead reach, the Chrome's far edge, is not merely worn but gone, and the grey no longer holds even the memory of having once, at its limit, met the made.

You stoop, out of an old habit you can barely account for, to lift a fleck — and there is none. The last rust-grain dissolved before you came, and the place has already forgotten it was ever here. The records of an entire iron age, scattered to grains a step east, are here scattered past grains, past trace, into a forgetting with no edge. You draw the spiral and the grey neither keeps it nor reads it nor recalls that marks were made; only the whorl in your skin keeps a whole coil, the one intact spiral where the made world has left, at last, no trace of itself at all.

There is no count, and no wind to stir the flecks, for there are no flecks; the made hour, reduced a step east to the faint stir of rust dissolving, is here reduced past stirring, gone still and gone forgotten. Only your heart beats on, the one made thing the Waste never made and cannot dissolve, keeping time in a grey that has forgotten the made world ever kept any.

To the north the traceless meets the Chrome's farthest dead reach, the dead machine's last cold grey firming out of the forgetting. To the east the last trace returns, a faint scatter of the made still dissolving. To the south it sinks into the forgotten, the grey that has forgotten even itself. To the west the pure Waste pole, no made thing ever again, no trace and no memory of trace, grey to the end of the world. You were going somewhere, and the made world held your name once, in all its records — here dissolved past the last grain, traceless, unrecalled — and still the whole coil turns in your skin, the one mark left, and still one beat keeps the time the made world has, everywhere else, forgotten.
