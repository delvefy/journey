windpipe

The windpipe is the ringed tube that runs down your throat and lets your breath go in and out. Press gently below your chin and you can feel its little ridges, stiff as a vacuum hose, holding itself open so the air is never squeezed shut. Down it goes the breath, down it goes the cough; only food must keep away. Soft and ringed and wholly of the body, it is the busy road your every breath travels, day and night.

North

A thin blue river under your skin,
it carries blood along;
back to the heart it flows and flows,
a tube both thin and strong.

East

A bean-shaped organ, two of me,
deep in your back I hide;
I clean your blood and make the wee -
a filter, snug inside.

South

A little cone-shaped shell that clings
so tightly to the stone;
no wave can prise me off the rock -
I hold on all alone.

West

A small brown bird with a bright red chest,
it hops across the snow;
it sings in winter gardens cold -
on Christmas cards I show.
