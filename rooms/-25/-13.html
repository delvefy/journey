limpet

A limpet is a small creature in a little cone of shell, clamped so tightly to a seaside rock that you could never prise it off with your fingers. When the tide is in it creeps about, grazing the green off the stone; when the tide goes out it clamps down hard and waits, sealing the sea inside. Try to pull one free and it grips all the harder. Stubborn and silent, the limpet holds on through every wave that breaks.

North

A tube down your throat that carries the air,
from your mouth right down to your chest;
breathe in, breathe out, the air goes through -
the pipe that lets your lungs do their best.

East

A tiny hard shell glued to a rock,
or stuck to the hull of a ship;
I never let go, I cling so tight -
through every wave and dip.

South

A little black blob with a wiggly tail,
I swim in the pond all spring;
one day I'll grow four legs and hop -
a baby frog, that's the thing.

West

A wobbly blob of see-through goo,
I drift through the sea with trailing strings;
no bones, no brain, just a floating bell -
don't touch me, for my tail still stings.
