pinch

A pinch is what you do with finger and thumb: a tiny sharp squeeze. A pinch of salt over the soup, a pinch on the arm to wake you from a dream, the cold that pinches your nose in winter. It is the smallest grip there is, just two fingers closing on a little bit of something. You cannot keep it - the moment they open, it is gone. Made by the hand, yet weightless, it lives in the quick sharp press and then lets go.

North

Wrap your hand around the orange,
press hard with all your might;
the juice comes dripping out the side -
you hug it far too tight.

East

Wash the woolly jumper hot
and watch it get too small;
it used to fit your daddy once -
now it won't fit you at all.

South

Step in the mud, step on a grape,
and feel it give and squash;
all soft and wet beneath your shoe -
a soft and squelchy splosh.

West

The little bird hops up to rest
upon the highest twig;
it sits and grips with tiny feet -
no nest, no flight, no jig.
