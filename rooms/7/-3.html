The Green Eye

South of the green dream and east of the wild's eye, the seeing goes older than its seer. A step north the world dreamed itself, sourceless and green; a step west the beast-gods' slow regard held all things in being by looking at them. Here the regard sinks below even the gods who held it: the Wyld pole absolute, where the world is seen into being by a sight with no seer behind it — the green's own ancient open eye, older than gods, older than the word, a primordial unblinking regard that the world simply has, and by having, is. To be is to be seen here; and the seeing is no one's, only the old green looking at itself, and so keeping itself real.

The eye is on you, as it was a step west, but there is no face to it now, no god behind the looking — only the green regard itself, sourceless and vast and old past gods, resting on you and so making you real. You feel held in a sight that nobody is having, kept in being by the world's own ancient self-seeing. Your name is the place the old green's looking rests, where you are most wholly here. The spiral is the eye's own coil, the slow green whorl of a sourceless sight, the same whorl in your skin, the mark the old regard knows you by — and you are, because the green, with no eye to do it, sees you.

The seeing is the world's pulse, a green regard that beats, each slow look an age kept in being — and your heart beats under the old eye, held in a sourceless sight, real for as long as the green keeps looking, the count the world's own ancient unblinking attention keeps.

To the north the old eye dreams again, the green dream, the world seen and dreamed at once. To the east the Wyld pole absolute, the old green's sight the whole world, all made by being seen. To the south it sinks below seeing toward the seed, the green before the eye, the world before sight. To the west the regard gathers behind the beast-gods, the wild's eye given its seers. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the old green sees it with no eye to do the seeing and holds you real in the looking — and still the warm coil turns, the mark the sourceless sight knows you by, and still one beat keeps on, seen and kept, in the gaze of the world before the gods.
