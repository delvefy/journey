Cough

You are the sharp bark the body gives to clear the throat - a tickle, a catch, and out you come in a rough gust of air. No hand makes you, yet the body does you on purpose and against its will both: to shift a crumb, to clear a chest, to cover an awkward silence in a quiet room. A whole sick winter rattles with you. You are the throat's rough knock, a small wrought-up commotion of breath, sudden and loud and gone.

North

A rude loud bubble of swallowed air
that climbs back up your throat;
it pops out at the dinner table -
pardon me, you quote.

East

A long low sound of weary pain
or grumbling at a chore;
you make me when you're told to tidy
your messy bedroom floor.

South

A shaking, gulping, weeping cry,
the catch of breath in grief;
it shudders through a crying child
and brings them no relief.

West

A quick shut-open of the eyes,
so fast you hardly know;
it wets and clears and rests them both -
there, did you miss it? No.
