The Wild Word

South of the spoken world and east of the antlered court, the saying goes wild. A step north the world was held in being by the old names spoken on every side, gods walking as neighbours; a step west those gods put on horn and ruled a wood-court by name and instinct both. Here the court falls away and only the wild is left sovereign — the magic pole tipped wholly toward the beast, where reality is no longer spoken into being but grown into it, roared and rooted and bled. The word has become wild: every creak of root, every blood-beat, every green uncurling a saying, the world one living thing shaping itself by instinct as surely as the elders shaped it by name.

There is no hall here and no high board, only the quick wood, dense and breathing, the beast-gods less rulers now than the wildness itself made sovereign. Your name is in it doubly — not a title to be tolled but a wild-true-name, the kind the deep dark knows, the kind a beast knows its own by scent and blood. The spiral is horn and root and the whorl of a curling fern at once, graven nowhere and growing everywhere, the same coil in your skin, here the wild's own seal. The living wood leans toward you, knowing you by instinct, and you feel how near it is to simply being you the way it is everything else — shaping you into itself, wild and unspoken and true.

The tolling of names is gone; the herd-beat is the whole of the count now, a bell struck in the blood, the world's wild pulse keeping the only time — and your own heart beats with it, indistinguishable, one wild stroke among the many, the count gone feral, your name a thing the blood is about to know without a word for it.

To the north the wild word firms back to the spoken world, the saying lifting out of root and horn into walking gods. To the east the wild pole absolute, reality wholly the beast-gods' to shape, the deep Wyld entire. To the south the magic sinks toward the older wild, less god now than green and beast, the world before the word. To the west the antlered court gathers again, the wild given a high board and a crown. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the living wild knows it by blood and is about to grow it true into you — and still the warm coil turns in your skin, root and horn and fern at once, and still one wild beat keeps the time the world is about to name you in, without a word.
