Rope

You are many slender threads that found their strength by holding to one another - twisted tight, you can haul a ship, hang a bridge, or hoist a sail. Alone, a single strand would snap; braided into you, it lifts a ton. You climb, you tow, you bind, you rescue; tie you in a knot and you grip and will not let go. Children skip you, sailors trust you, mountains are climbed on you - old as the first hand that twisted grass together.

North

With wings of steel I climb the air,
above the clouds I soar;
I carry hundreds, sea to sea,
and land them safe on shore.

East

I show you yourself, clear and bright,
but left is turned to right;
whatever face you make at me,
I make it back on sight.

South

On four flat legs I stand quite still,
my top is broad and flat;
we eat our dinner spread on me,
and play our games on that.

West

With many teeth but not a bite,
through tangled hair I run;
I smooth each knot and part the strands,
till all the snarls are done.
