Table

You are a flat field on four straight legs, the steady centre of the room. On you the meal is laid, the homework spread, the cards dealt out, the letter written; round you the whole family draws up its chairs and the day is shared. You ask only to be wiped clean and not to wobble. Kings have signed peace across your kind, and children have hidden beneath you in storms. Solid and plain, you hold up whatever the house sets down.

North

A long thick cord of twisted strand,
you haul and tie and climb;
I bind the load, I tow the boat,
or skip in summertime.

East

A flat plank fixed against the wall,
in a row up off the floor;
I hold your books and jars and all,
and tidy them, and more.

South

With pillow soft and blanket deep,
I'm where you lay your head;
all through the night you lie asleep,
on me, so warm and spread.

West

A round tin pail with a handle to swing,
I carry the water up;
from the well, from the rain, most anything,
I'm bigger than a cup.
