Thread

You are a single strand spun fine and wound on a wooden reel, the thin line that holds the made world's cloth together. Through the needle's eye you go, in and out, in and out, drawing the torn edges shut, hemming the trouser, sewing the button back on. Pull me too hard and I snap; knot me at the end and I will hold for years. Of cotton or silk or strong waxed linen, you are almost nothing in yourself - and yet without you every garment in the world would simply fall to pieces.

North

A floppy head of grey wet string,
upon a wooden pole;
I swab the floor, I swab and swing,
and leave it clean and whole.

East

A woven sheet of cotton or wool,
to wipe, to dry, to wear;
I make the shirt, the sheet so cool,
soft fabric everywhere.

South

Of leather made to fit the foot,
with laces tied up tight;
I carry you on every route,
through morning, noon, and night.

West

A little disc with holes of two,
sewn fast upon the coat;
through the slit I'm pushed for you,
done up around the throat.
