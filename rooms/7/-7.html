Glove

You are a sheath for the hand, five fingers stitched and shaped, made by a tailor or a machine to fit the working or the freezing hand. Wool against the cold, leather for the rider, rubber at the sink, white silk for the dance. You come in pairs and lose your partner; you are pulled on finger by finger and peeled off inside out. No animal grew you - you were cut and sewn to the shape of a hand, the warm wrought skin we wear over our own.

North

I'm strapped or laced upon the foot,
with a tough sole underneath;
two of me will take you far
across the road and heath.

East

A long warm strip of knitted wool
wound round and round the throat;
I keep the winter wind away
above your buttoned coat.

South

A long warm garment for outdoors,
with buttons up the front;
I keep the rain and cold away
when winter bears the brunt.

West

Worn upon the head am I,
of felt or straw or wool;
I keep the sun and rain away
and tip to greet you, full.
