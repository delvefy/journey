Coat

You are the big warm garment for the cold outdoors, buttoned or zipped from neck to knee, with deep pockets and a turned-up collar. A tailor cut and sewed you; you are wholly wrought, lined against the wind, hooded against the rain. You hang on the hook by the door waiting for the weather, you are shrugged on in a hurry and shrugged off by the fire. Heavy and faithful, you are the layer between a person and the winter, worn so the warmth stays in.

North

Pulled on the hand against the cold,
a sheath for every finger;
leather, wool, or rubber-skinned -
in pairs they tend to linger.

East

A cloth cap fixed behind the neck,
part of a coat or cloak;
yank me up against the rain -
I shelter ordinary folk.

South

A little pouch sewn in your clothes
to keep your treasures in;
coins and string and conkers go
down deep, all snug within.

West

A little circle on the hand,
of silver or of gold;
no end, no start, a perfect round -
a promise to have and hold.
