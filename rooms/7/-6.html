Shoe

You are a shaped and stitched thing for the foot, leather or canvas, laced and soled. A maker cut you, sewed you, beat the heel flat - you did not grow, you were built. All day you carry someone down the street, over the wet grass, up the stairs, and at night you wait by the door, mouth open, still holding the shape of the foot that left you. Come morning they step in again, and off you walk.

North

Through the needle's eye I go,
a long and slender line;
I bind the torn, I hem, I sew,
and pull the patches fine.

East

I thread through little eyelet holes
and tie up in a bow;
I keep your shoe upon your foot
wherever you may go.

South

I'm made to fit upon a hand,
five fingers, soft and warm;
I keep the winter off your skin
and shield you from the storm.

West

I'm pulled on first, before the shoe,
and hug your foot all day;
a woollen one in winter time
to keep the cold away.
