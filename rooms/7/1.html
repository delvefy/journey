The Spoken Engine

North of the spoken world and east of the wrought names, the saying gets its first engine — and the engine has almost nothing to do. A step south reality was held in being by the old names alone, spoken on every side; a step west those names had been wrought into great sigil-cut machines that bent the world by turning. Here, this far into the magic, the two meet at their easiest: a wrought thing stands among the speaking, graven and slowly turning, and you can see it scarcely needs to turn at all. The saying has nearly done the work already; the engine only joins it, word and work one, the word still sovereign and the craft its glad echo.

It is a strange, light marvel — a machine in a world that hardly requires machines, a first easy craft laid over an all-sufficient word. The gears are graven with true names, and the names are spoken anyway, all around, so the engine turns the way a hand keeps time to a song it need not play. The spiral is its hub and also the figure the saying draws in the air, the wheel's heart and the word's seal at once, the same whorl in your skin. And here is an engine graven for your name — not waiting on it, as the wrought names waited, but ready to turn gladly the instant the spoken world, already half-saying you, finishes the word.

The tolling of names and the turning of the wheel keep one time, the bell rung and the engine struck on the same stroke, an hour both spoken and built and needing neither to be the other — and your name the next beat, half-said in the air and half-cut in the gear, a breath from being spoken and wrought into the world at once.

To the north the spoken engine refines toward the Lumen, word and work and light wholly one, the lit pole climbing. To the east the magic goes pole-absolute, the names the whole of reality and the engine fallen still, craft needless. To the south the engine falls away and the world is held by saying alone, the spoken world. To the west the craft grows necessary again, the wrought names doing by turning what the word leaves undone. You were going somewhere, under a name. Here the saying and the engine are both gathered to give it — spoken and wrought in one stroke — and still the warm coil turns at the hub, the word's seal and the wheel's heart, and still one live beat keeps the time that word and work are about to name you in.
