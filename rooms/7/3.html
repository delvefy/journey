Kiss

You are a small soft press of the lips, and you carry more than any speech. The mother gives you to the scraped knee, the lover to the waiting mouth, the grandmother to the top of the sleepy head. Goodnight, goodbye, well done, I'm yours - you say them all without a sound. Princes wake to you in stories; the shy go red at the thought of you. Quick as a moth and gone in a breath, you leave a warmth behind that lasts the whole long day.

North

Rap your knuckles, tap the door,
'is anybody there?';
that sound upon the wood once more
announces who is where.

East

A long slow breath let softly out,
when you are tired or sad;
'oh dear,' it says without a shout,
half weary and half glad.

South

In a column, neat and straight,
the things you must not miss;
you tick them off and cross their weight,
from milk to bread to this.

West

My head dips down and lifts again,
to answer 'yes' with ease;
no spoken word, no need of pen,
a silent yes to please.
