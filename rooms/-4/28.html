smoulder

To smoulder is to burn very slowly, with no leaping flame - just a sullen red glow and a thin curl of smoke creeping up. A fire smoulders for hours after the flames have died, deep in the grey ash; a dropped match can smoulder unseen in dry leaves until it suddenly catches. It is fire holding its breath, quiet and patient and not quite out. Poke it and it might flare back to life, or might just sulk and smoke a little more.

North

A great big fire piled up outside,
with wood and leaves all heaped up high;
on Bonfire Night it blazes bright -
sparks and crackle reach the sky.

East

A metal moon we built and flung
way up to circle round the Earth;
it bounces telly and phone calls down -
a speck of light of our own worth.

South

A small red glowing lump of coal,
the last hot bit when the fire dies down;
it pulses orange in the grey ash -
blow on it gently, watch it crown.

West

The round and round path through the sky
a planet or a moon will trace;
it circles forever, never stops -
a loop drawn out in empty space.
