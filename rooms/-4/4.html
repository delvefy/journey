Rainbow

After the rain, when the sun breaks through, you arch across the sky in seven bands of colour - red running all the way down to violet, painted on the falling drops. You are not a thing at all, only light bent through water and thrown back to the eye. Walk toward you and you keep your distance; there is no foot of you to stand beneath, no end to dig for gold. You shine a while, then fade as the drops dry.

North

I rise in clouds from a boiling pot,
all hot and white and damp;
I cloud the mirror, curl and swirl,
from kettle, bath, and tap.

East

I am the bright and jagged flash
that splits the stormy sky;
I strike the ground in a blink of white,
then thunder makes reply.

South

I bite your nose and nip your toes,
and creep beneath the door;
I come with winter and with frost,
and make you shiver more.

West

I creep in when the light is gone,
and swallow every sight;
switch on a lamp and I retreat,
for I can't bear the light.
