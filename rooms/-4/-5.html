The Bedrock

South of the first silence and east of the lessening, the deep past comes to its bedrock. The source a step north was the silence everything rose from; the Waste a step east took away until not even a less was left; here is what lies under both, the absolute floor — below even the first silence, before even the before, where there is no source to spring from and nothing to take away, only the bottom of everything, the bedrock on which the whole long world will one day, impossibly, be built.

Nothing is here, not even the readiness the first silence held. And yet you are, which at this depth is a kind of miracle: a warmth at the bedrock of the deep past, the one thing that is in a place from before is-ness, carrying the spiral whorled in your skin like a seed brought down to the floor of the world from a future that has not happened. You set it to the bedrock and it holds, faint, the only mark on the foundation of everything, the first and the last and the only, kept where there is nothing else to keep it but the fact that you have not let it go.

Your heart beats, once, at the bedrock — and it is the only thing in all the deep past that is, the seed of the first note not yet risen, the whole of the count and the whole of the world held in one warm stroke at the bottom of everything. While it beats, there is something. That is the whole of what the bedrock holds.

To the north the bedrock rises to the first silence, the source everything springs from. To the east the floor gives onto the lessening, the Waste's last subtraction. To the south it sinks below even bedrock, toward the corner, the pole. To the west the Waste corner absolute, where even the floor is gone. You were going somewhere, with someone, once — from before the source, from below the floor. Here at the bedrock of the deep past, your one warm beat is all that is, and the going wound in it the only future there has ever been.
