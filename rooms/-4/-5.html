Nest

You are a cup woven out of almost nothing - dry grass, a feather, a stolen thread, a hundred twigs carried one at a time in a small beak. No nails, no glue, only patience and the turning of a warm body to shape your hollow. You hold the most fragile things there are: pale eggs, then blind and gaping chicks. High in the fork of a tree you rock in the wind and never spill. When summer ends, you are left empty.

North

No one plants me, still I grow,
between the stones, in every row;
the gardener pulls me out with sighs,
a plant that's wild and most despise.

East

Small and round and full of juice,
I ripen red or blue or black;
the birds and children pick me off,
sweet from the hedgerow at the back.

South

I pop up overnight in damp,
a little umbrella on a stalk;
some kinds are good to fry and eat,
and some will kill - so mind your walk.

West

A bird of night with great round eyes,
I turn my head right round;
I hoot a 'who' from the dark tree,
and hunt the mice without a sound.
