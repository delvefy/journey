Pear

You are the green-gold fruit shaped like a teardrop, fat and round at the bottom, narrow at the stalk. No hand made you - you swelled all summer on the bough, drinking sun and rain, until you hung heavy and ready. Bite you ripe and you are honey-soft and dripping; leave you a day too long and you go brown and grainy at the core. Sweet, lopsided, and patient, you are the orchard's gentle weight, waiting to fall.

North

Round and red, or sometimes green,
I grow up in the sun;
crunch me for your lunchbox treat -
for teachers I'm the one.

East

Pink and plump, I love the mud,
I grunt and snort and snuffle;
my curly tail, my flat wet snout,
I'm in a muddy scuffle.

South

A small round fruit of purple skin,
so juicy, sweet, and round;
a hard stone hides inside my heart,
and ripe, I drop to ground.

West

A baby sheep on wobbly legs
that skips across the field;
all soft white wool and bleating cries,
to its mother's call I yield.
