Year

You are the great wheel of time the whole world turns by, twelve months from one New Year to the next, the four seasons rolling round in order - spring, summer, autumn, winter - and back to the start. Birthdays count you; rings inside the tree record you; the child measures growth in you. You are long enough to hold a whole story of a life - the planting and the harvest, the cold and the thaw - and short enough that, looking back, the old ones wonder where you went. Round and round, never once stopping, you carry everything along.

North

The tick of a clock, the briefest count,
and sixty make a minute go;
'just a -,' you say, a small amount,
the quickest tick you know.

East

The far faint shine of the night-time sky,
from a thousand specks of white;
'star bright' you wish on it up high,
the palest, oldest light.

South

When noon has rung and lunch is done,
the long warm slope of day;
the drowsy hours beneath the sun,
before the light goes grey.

West

Sixty seconds make me whole,
and sixty of me an hour;
a small short while, a tick, a toll,
the clock's quick counting power.
