Afternoon

You are the long slow slope of the day after the noon bell, when lunch sits heavy and the sun begins its lean toward the west. The light goes golden and a little tired; the classroom clock crawls; the cat finds the warmest square of carpet and surrenders. Work still must be done, but the morning's sharp edge is gone, softened to a drowsy patience. Tea waits at your end, and the lengthening shadows. You are the gentlest stretch of all the hours, the day grown comfortable in its own skin.

North

Twelve whole months make up my span,
four seasons, turning slow;
from one New Year another ran,
the longest count you know.

East

The cold pale shine the night-sky pours,
when the sun is out of sight;
it silvers fields and sleeping doors,
the soft grey glow of night.

South

The bright lit half of turning time,
from dawn until the dark;
the hours of work, of school, of climb,
the children in the park.

West

The coldest season, white and bare,
when ponds turn hard as stone;
the snow comes down, the branches stare,
and the wind cuts to the bone.
