Grief

You are the deep heavy sorrow that comes when someone you love is gone - a death, a goodbye that lasts forever. No hand makes you; the loving heart, robbed, fills up with you. You sit like a stone in the chest and spill over in tears for days and weeks. Slowly, slowly, you soften, but you never quite leave.

North

A hug when you are hurt or sad,
a soft warm blanket, a cup of tea;
the gentle easing of your tears -
'there, there,' it says to thee.

East

A loud rude jumble of sound,
all clatter, bang, and din;
the building site, the crashing pots -
shut the door, keep it in.

South

The heavy downcast teary feeling
when something good is lost;
it slows your step, it bows your head -
a grey and gloomy frost.

West

The tender sorrow that you feel
for someone hurt or poor;
'oh, the poor thing,' your soft heart says -
you'd help them, to be sure.
