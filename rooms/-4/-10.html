Plum

You are the round purple fruit of late summer, smooth-skinned and dusty-bloomed, sweet and dribbling when you are ripe. No hand made you - you swelled on the orchard tree, drinking the sun, until you hung heavy and dark and ready to drop. Bite past your taut skin and your gold flesh runs with juice around a single hard stone. Picked for jam, for pies, for eating warm from the branch, you are a small dark globe of summer's sweetness, the orchard's purple treasure.

North

A green-gold fruit, fat at the foot,
it dangles from the bough;
it ripens soft and sweet to bite -
go pick one for me now.

East

A bearded beast that climbs the crag
and chews up everything;
horned and stubborn, square of eye,
it bleats in early spring.

South

A small round fruit, bright shining red,
two on a single stem;
a hard stone in its juicy heart -
a sweet and tiny gem.

West

A tall green stalk with golden cobs
that ripens in the field;
boil it, butter it, or pop it -
a sweet and yellow yield.
