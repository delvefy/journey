Grass

You walk on it without a thought - the green skin of the world, growing back as fast as it is cut. Grass asks for nothing but rain and light, and covers every field and lawn and verge in the same patient green. Lie down in it and it tickles your neck; pull a blade and whistle through it. It is the plainest, most underfoot of all growing things, and the most everywhere.

North

I climb the wall and will not fall,
I cling with tiny hands to all;
up the trellis, round the tree,
sweet grapes may hang in bunches from me.

East

A soft green velvet, damp and low,
on stones and logs and bark I grow;
no flower, no stem, I creep and spread,
a cushion for the forest's bed.

South

I reach out sideways from the trunk,
with leaves and twigs and nests I'm hung;
a bird will perch and sing on me,
an arm held out by every tree.

West

I am a little mountain, soft and green,
the gentlest rise that fields have seen;
you climb my side to see the view,
then roll back down with a whoop or two.
