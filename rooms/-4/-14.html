Tomato

You are the round red fruit we treat as veg, plump and shiny on the vine, full of seeds and warm sweet juice. No hand made you; the sun and the greenhouse did. Sliced in a sandwich, squashed into sauce, squeezed into soup. Bite one warm from the plant, and the juice runs everywhere - summer in a single red globe.

North

A tiny green ball in a pod,
you pop them out in a row;
so small the princess felt just one
beneath her bed, you know.

East

A lumpy brown root from the ground,
you dig it from the dirt;
you mash it, chip it, bake it whole -
with salt, it's worth the work.

South

A shiny veg, red, green, or gold,
that's hollow, with seeds inside;
you slice it into crunchy rings -
in salads, far and wide.

West

A round white veg in papery skin,
in layers round and round;
you peel it and it makes you cry -
the sharpest smell around.
