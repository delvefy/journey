The Iron Gone to Grit

West of the cold forge the iron lets go. A step east the tools lay out in cold rows, rusting but still tools — a chisel, a wedge, the rim of a wheel; here the rust has finished its work, and the made cold has blown apart into grit, a grey drift of it across grey ground, the shape of a thing gone but for a hint. North of the unhaunted flat, this is the most a made thing comes to out here: ground once squared, a block set down, iron raised — and then handed straight to the grey silence, not by a world too busy to believe but by no one at all, for no one stayed to mind it.

You crouch and sift the drift. Once it was an edge. You can still, almost, feel where the edge meant to be — the grit a little ordered, the memory of a true line held a moment in the grey — and then it sifts through your fingers and is only grit, on its slow way west to where not even the memory of a shape remains. Nothing was blessed here; nothing is mourned. The trade that squared this ground was made, and kept, and came, like all the made cold, to dust.

The maker's mark struck into a blade a step east — the old coil, stamped to say whose work it was — is here a brown swirl on one last flake of iron, filling in from the centre as the rust eats the rest: a spiral dissolving rather than drawn. And there is no ringing note now. Strike the grit and it gives nothing. Only the wind moving the drift with a thin dry hiss, a bell worn down past sound into the sift of grains, keeping no hour at all.

To the north the made cold hardens up again into raised iron, an engine set running grey and soulless, the cold making climbing toward its grey future. To the east the grit gathers back into cold iron tools on a bench, the forge where things are at least still shaped. To the south the squared ground softens back to plain open country, the unhaunted flat, no tool and no trade in it. To the west the drift blows on into the Waste, the grit losing even the memory of an edge, the long grey silence no made thing was ever come to. You were going somewhere once, by your own two hands or by some engine. The tools to take you have gone to grit, and not one of them carried a name.
