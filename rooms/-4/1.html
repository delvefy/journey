Gloom

You stand where the light gives up without quite going out. Gloom is the grey hour under heavy cloud, the dimness inside a winter wood, the dusk that no lamp was lit against and no hand can sweep away. It is wholly natural and wholly untouchable — a mood the sky puts on. It keeps the cold north-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

❦

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

I gather black and growl aloud,
I hurl the rain and crack the cloud;
the ships all flee, the shutters slam —
what wind-and-thunder fury am I?

East

A thin grey veil on a summer day,
I blur the hills and dull the sun;
no rain, no fog — I drift and stay
till the cool of evening's come.

South

Grey threads let down from a low cloud's hem,
I patter on roof and run down the glass;
the puddles brim, the gardens drink —
I fall a while, then pass.

West

The sun has slipped, but the dark's not come;
I am the grey between the two;
the lamps are lit, the swifts fly home —
the day's last hour, gone blue.
