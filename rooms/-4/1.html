Breeze

It is the gentlest the wind ever gets - a soft moving coolness that lifts the hair from your neck on a hot afternoon. The breeze asks nothing and bends nothing; it only stirs, turning the leaves over to show their pale sides, nudging the washing on the line, carrying the smell of cut grass from three gardens over. You cannot see it or hold it, only feel it pass and be glad. Of all the weathers, it is the kindest.

North

At morning on the spider's thread
I hang in drops, both clear and spread;
the sun comes up and I am gone -
small jewels of water on the lawn.

East

I creep across the grass at night
and leave it crisp and silver-white;
I draw cold ferns upon the glass,
and fade away when warm hours pass.

South

I am the sea's slow breath each day,
I pull the water out, then sway
it back again across the shore -
in, then out, forevermore.

West

I have no colour, shape, or smell,
you cannot see me, hear, or tell;
but breathe me in and breathe me out -
I fill the world all round about.
