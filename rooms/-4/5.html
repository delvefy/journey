Steam

You are water that has learned to fly. Heat the kettle past its patience and you burst from the spout in a hot white rush, climbing, curling, gone. You fog the cold window and write a child's finger-letters there; you lift the lid of the pot and rattle it; you hide the bathroom mirror behind a breath. For all your billowing you weigh almost nothing, and the moment the air cools you forget how to be a cloud and fall again as ordinary drops.

North

The bright lit time the sun is up,
when all the world's awake;
from when you rise to when you sleep,
the hours that you take.

East

The sun slips down behind the hill,
the sky goes grey and dim;
the bats come out, the birds go still,
the day fades to its rim.

South

After the rain, when sun breaks through,
I bend a bridge of light;
red and orange, green and blue,
I vanish if you near my height.

West

The morning's climbed as high as it can,
the sun sits straight overhead;
the shortest shadow of the day,
when lunch is being spread.
