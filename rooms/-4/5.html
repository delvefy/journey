The Pall

North of the chrome and east of the last thought, the dead pole wears past even its deadness. The final thought a step east guttered reaching; the Chrome a step south stood as a dead grey; here even the grey of it has shed its last feature, every neon and signal and flicker gone, down to a pure even pall — a sourceless grey light, featureless and total, the colour of nothing laid over everything, the Chrome worn so far past machine and mind and signal that it is only this, a shroud of grey with not one thing left in it to be dead.

It lies over the room like a pall over a long-cold bier, and there is nothing under it, nothing to mourn, nothing that was ever even there — except, you find, the whorl in your skin, which the pall cannot cover. You hold your hand up into the grey light and the spiral on your fingertip throws no shadow and catches no glint, but it is warm, and it is yours, the one thing the pure grey pall settles over and cannot make grey. Everything else here is shrouded. That alone stays itself.

There is no count, no signal, no flicker; the pall sheds even the dead pole's last tick. Only your heart beats under the grey, the one warm thing the shroud lies over and does not still — a count in a place that has shed counting, kept by nothing but its own refusal to go grey.

To the north the pall reaches its absolute, pure grey nothing, the Chrome's over-pole. To the east the grey stirs to the last thought, a cold mind still guttering. To the south it firms to the Chrome, the dead pole still legible as a future. To the west the pall dissolves into the Waste, grey into grey. You were going somewhere, under a name. The dead pole shed even the signal that might have carried it; the name is under the pall now, shrouded with everything else — and only the warm whorl lies uncovered, the one thing the grey could not make grey.
