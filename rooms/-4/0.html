The Waymark

West of the shrine nothing was built to last, because nothing here was built at all — only used, and then forgotten. A road runs through, or the ghost of one: two faint ruts in pale grassed-over ground, coming from a place you cannot see and going to a place you cannot see, and you upon it with no way to tell which way is on. At the verge stands a single upright stone, set there by some hand long gone — a waymark, a milestone, a marker for the lost — and that is the whole of the made world here: one stone, and a track too faint to keep, in a quiet that has forgotten it was ever a road.

Cut into the weathered face of the stone is a spiral, and beneath it letters — a name, a distance, a direction — but wind and years have worn them to soft hollows, and run your thumb along them as you will, the stone tells you nothing. The coil itself is nearly smoothed away, its last turn faint as a thumbprint in old soap. It does not lean toward you. There is no leaning left in it; it has forgotten how. Whatever it once pointed at, it points nowhere now.

The air does not even keep the shape of a ringing, the way the shrine's vault still did; it has stood empty far too long for that. There is only the wind, low and level across the open ground — the long quiet that is the one voice the Waste has ever had, saying nothing, addressed to no one.

To the north the road hardens to iron and the marker to a made cold milepost, counting the distance without meaning it, the engine that needs no answering. To the east the stone gathers back into the spent shrine, a place at least raised to be answered in. To the south the track sinks into bare cold ground and open dark, ruin and the older cold beneath it. To the west the ruts give out entirely and this is the last made thing, the open Waste beyond it where even the going stops. You were going somewhere, surely. You stand at a waymark that no longer marks a way, and that is the one thing it cannot tell you back.
