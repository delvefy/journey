Tide

Twice a day the sea breathes in and out, and that long pull is the tide. No hand winds it; the moon does, dragging the whole grey water up the beach and back again. Leave your sandcastle at noon and find it gone by dusk. The tide is patient and exact, the slowest clock in the world, marking the hours not in numbers but in wet sand and stranded weed. You cannot hurry it and you cannot stop it; you can only learn its times.

North

I am a soft and gentle wind,
I barely stir the leaves I find;
I cool you on a summer day,
and softly blow the clouds away.

East

I roll across the open sea,
then break in foam and reach for thee;
I rise, I curl, I crash, I hiss,
upon the sand I end with a kiss.

South

I climb the wall with twisting grip,
I cling to trellis, post, and lip;
ripe grapes may hang from me in time -
a long green rope, I twist and climb.

West

I am a sheet of water, still,
held in a hollow by the hill;
no salt, no tide, I do not flow -
the ducks and swans glide to and fro.
