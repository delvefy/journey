Rain

Grey threads fall slantwise past the glass and drum on the sill. Rain is made by no hand — water drawn up to the clouds and let down again on field and roof. It fills the rivers, feeds the seed, and soaks the unlucky traveller. Natural, on the line between the felt and the unfelt, it keeps due west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A grey half-dark that fills the wood
as the daylight wears away;
not night, not noon, a heavy dusk,
a dim and dreary day.

East

Twice the moon hauls up the sea
to climb the sloping strand,
and twice it draws the water back
and bares the rippled sand.

South

A grey stone hiding fire within —
strike me with iron bright
and out leap sparks to catch the straw
and make a flame for night.

West

I fall in soft white silent flakes
and blanket all the town;
the children shout and roll me up
to a man with a coal-eyed frown.
