Weed

No one planted you, and no one wants you - that is what makes you a weed. You are only a plant in the wrong place: pushing up between the paving stones, crowding the carrots, sprawling where the lawn should lie smooth. Pull you out and your roots cling to the soil; tear off the top and you sprout again by morning. Stubborn, green, and unasked for, you grow wherever a seed can fall.

North

I grow out sideways from the trunk,
and wear the leaves in green;
the birds will perch along my length,
the longest arm you've seen.

East

I am the seed the oak lets fall,
in a little wooden cap;
the squirrel hunts me, hides me well,
then loses me, poor chap.

South

High in the tree the birds will build
a cosy cup of twigs;
they lay their eggs and keep them warm,
and feed the cheeping chicks.

West

Light as light can be, I fall
and drift down from a wing;
soft and curved, I help birds fly,
a small and tickling thing.
