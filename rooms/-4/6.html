Day

You are the lit half of the turning world, the stretch from the sun's first edge to its last. In you the work is done, the markets open, the children run shouting in the yard. You hold the morning and the noon and the long gold afternoon, and you end when the light gives out and your dark twin takes the sky. Seven of you make a week; you come whether wanted or not, and no one has ever found a way to hold you back.

North

When the noon bell's come and gone,
and lunch is cleared away;
the sleepy stretch the sun shines on,
the long warm part of day.

East

The warm gold gift the sun lets fall,
that lies across the floor;
it brightens garden, field and wall,
and warms you to the core.

South

From the boiling kettle's spout,
a hot white cloud I rise;
I fog the window inside out,
then vanish to the skies.

West

The early half before the noon,
with porridge and with school;
the dew still wet, the sun still soon,
and all the air still cool.
