The Faithless Engine

West of the returning iron and north of the iron gone to grit, the engine runs anyway. South of here the made cold had blown apart to a grey drift, nothing left standing; east of here a single machine stood whole and rusted, stopped but still a machine. Here is the worst of both: the iron has hardened back up into a raised engine, braced and turning under the flat dead light — and it has rusted clean through in the same breath, so that what stands and turns is no longer iron but ore, a grey wheel of packed dust that has forgotten it was ever an engine and turns on regardless, soulless, climbing nowhere toward the grey future it was promised.

You watch it work. There is no hand on it and never was; it was built to turn without faith and it keeps that bargain past the end of itself, the brown dust sheeting off it as it goes and the wheel filling the loss from its own crumbling, ore feeding ore. It mills nothing. It carries nothing east or west. It has opened out from being a made thing into the grey Waste's own habit of motion, a machine that takes every made thing — itself included — and grinds it back to the dust it borrowed, with no memory of a forge and none of a maker. You set a thumb where a mark would be stamped and find only the soft give of rust, a crater where the meaning was.

The only coil now is the turning itself, the wheel winding round on round, a spiral that marks no one's work and keeps no faith, only goes. And it does keep a count — a dry grey grinding, tooth past tooth, an hour told over and over to no one — the bell become at last a machine that numbers the time and reaches nothing with it. North the engines multiply and stand whole and running, minding each other under the dead light, no rust permitted, the Chrome proper. East the dust gathers back to one stopped machine, the iron not yet forgotten. South the engine breaks down to raised tools and then to drift, the made cold blown apart. West the wheel's last turn blows out into the Waste, ore that forgets it ever turned. You were going somewhere once, by some engine's hour. This one grinds the hour out grey and gives you nothing to go by, and not a tooth of it knows your name.
