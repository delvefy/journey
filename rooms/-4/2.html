The Last Engine

North of the mileposts the line found an engine, and drove it out here to die. The numbered iron a step south rises here into a real machine — a great cold engine set on rails that run out across the open Waste from nowhere to nowhere, hauled to the very edge of the made world and left. It was the disenchanted age's farthest reach: not a city, not a works, only the last engine on the last rail, driven out past where anyone could have wanted it, and stopped, and never recovered. The rails go on past it a little way, thinning, and then are only two lines of rust in the grey, and then are gone.

It has gone to ore the way all iron goes here. The boiler is scaled through, the great wheels seized to the rail and welded there by rust, the whole machine slumping by slow degrees back into the ground it was dug from. On its flank a maker's plate: the spiral cast proud once, eaten hollow now, and beneath it a name and a number — the make, the road, the year — scaled over past reading. You thumb it and the rust comes away like pollen and the letters do not; whoever built this and drove it out and walked away is gone with all the rest.

There is a hum, still — faint, in the dead wires that sag from leaning poles along the rail, a cold even note running down the line out of the dark to the north, the last of the drive that once moved everything here, dying so slowly you cannot be sure it is not your own ears. The bell remade as a machine's hum, and the hum running out: a clock not stopped but spent.

To the north the rail and the wires gather into a cold even hum spread thin across the Waste, the works of the disenchanted age at their farthest, the pole reaching its limit. To the east the engine firms back toward a recognizable works, the great seized wheel, the rust not yet wholly ore. To the south the rail dwindles to a line of numbered posts counting miles to nowhere. To the west the rails give out entirely, the engine gone wholly to ore-grit, the open Waste with nothing made left standing. You were going somewhere, by some line, by some engine's hour. The last engine sits seized on the last rail, hauled out past the end of the going, and the wires hum it a note that is running down to nothing.
