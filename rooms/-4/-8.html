Apple

You are the homeliest and best-loved of fruits, round and red or green or gold, hanging heavy on the orchard bough until you drop. Crisp and white inside, sweet and a little sharp, you crunch beautifully under the first bite. You are baked in pies, pressed into cider, polished for the teacher, given to the doctor's keeping-away. You fell, they say, on a thinker's head and taught him about the stars; you tempted the very first people in the very first garden. Plain, perfect, and full of small brown pips, you are fruit itself.

North

A hard brown shell with a kernel sweet,
that hangs upon the tree;
the squirrel hides me down to eat,
when winter comes for me.

East

I scratch the yard and peck the seed,
and cluck the morning long;
I lay an egg for those in need,
and mother all my throng.

South

A green-gold fruit both sweet and round,
but fat below, thin at the top;
upon the orchard bough I'm found,
and juicy when I drop.

West

A ball of yellow fluff so small,
just hatched from out the shell;
it cheeps and answers the hen's call,
the baby bird as well.
