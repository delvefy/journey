Nut

You are a kernel of goodness shut in a shell hard enough to keep the winter out. Hazel, walnut, chestnut - whatever your name, you hang on the bough through the autumn, then fall to the leaf-litter to be gathered and stored. The squirrel buries you and forgets, and so you become a tree; the child cracks you with a stone for the sweet white meat inside. Tight-locked and patient, you carry a whole forest folded small, waiting only for the right damp spring to begin.

North

Up overnight on the woodland ground,
a little cap on a stem;
no flower or leaf on me is found,
I sprout, a quiet gem.

East

Webbed of foot and broad of bill,
I paddle round the pond;
I bob for weed, I quack my fill,
of water I am fond.

South

Red or green and round and sweet,
I hang upon the tree;
crisp and juicy, good to eat,
a crunch in every bite of me.

West

A hard sharp horny mouth I am,
the bird's own pointed tool;
I peck the seed, I crack the gram,
in farmyard, field, and pool.
