The Last of the Made

West of the dead day, and north of the last engine, even the machines are gone — and only their hum is left. There are no works here to see, no wheel, no rail, no plate of ore; the disenchanted age has spread itself this far and worn this thin, until nothing of it remains but a field, a cold even hum that fills the grey air from no source you can find, the last of everything ever made, dissolving. It is the pole at the very limit of its reach: not a Chrome of glass and engine now, only the ghost of one, a tone laid faint over the open Waste, carrying nothing, fading.

Once this carried the whole made world. You can feel it in the hum — the way a dead wire still warms a hair, the way a struck-dumb hall still owes its echo — that this thin tone was once thick with every record and signal and reckoned name the cold age made, your own among them, counted and filed and sent. Now it carries none of it. The signal has gone to grey, to a flat even nothing, neither message nor silence but the carrier left behind when the message is forgotten. You draw the spiral in the dust the field has sifted out of the air, and the hum thins for an instant where your hand moves, as though even now it were trying, and failing, to make a mark.

The hum is the last count. Not a tick, not a tone struck and let go, but a single flat note held past all meaning — the bell and the clock and the cold reckoning worn down to one fading carrier, an hour spread so thin across the Waste that it no longer keeps time so much as forgets it slowly, out loud.

To the north the field refines to the disenchanted pole, pure dead signal with no machine and no world, the Chrome's empty peak. To the east the hum gathers back into ruined engines and a guttered made day, machines at least still standing as wreck. To the south the field firms to a single seized engine on a dead rail, wires still humming. To the west the hum fades wholly out, the made forgotten entirely, the open Waste where nothing was ever built to be forgotten. You were going somewhere, and it was carried, once, in all of this — counted and sent and kept in the great cold field. The field is still here. It carries nothing now, your name least of all, only the long flat hum of everything made, fading out across the grey.
