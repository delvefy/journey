The Grey Mill

West of the forgetting hall and north of the faithless engine, the engines are past counting. A step east a single hall had begun to crack and limp, minding a round worn through; a step south one wheel ground itself to ore and turned for no one. Here both go to scale at once. The works open out beyond walls — a grey city of machines, rank behind rank past seeing, raised tall under the same flat dead light, every one of them turning. And every one of them is dust. Not iron gone to ore here and there, but the whole multitude rusted clean through and holding its shape on habit alone, a mill of numberless engines that grinds and grinds and has forgotten, to the last wheel, that it was ever made to grind anything but itself.

You walk into the grey of it and it does not slow for you, or speed, or know. The machines mill nothing. They take in nothing and send out nothing; each sheets brown dust off itself as it turns and packs the loss back from its own crumbling, ore feeding ore, the city eating and remaking its own grit forever. There was a meaning stamped on all this once — a maker, a purpose, a hand — and you set a thumb where it would have been struck and find only the soft endless give of rust, a whole city's worth of craters where the reasons were. It runs faithless and tireless. It has kept no faith because it has forgotten there was ever one to keep.

The only coil is the turning, ten thousandfold: wheels within wheels grinding round on round, a spiral multiplied past meaning, marking no one's work and winding back to nothing. And the count is a dry grey thunder, tooth past tooth past tooth across the whole city, an hour milled out over and over and reaching no one — the bell become an engine the size of a country, numbering time into dust. North the mill refines past iron toward wire and cold light, the works thinning fine and bright, the Chrome pole where machines think without a head. East it firms back to the forgetting hall, the round still mostly whole, the rust just walking in. South it narrows to the one faithless engine, a single wheel turning for no one. West the whole city cracks open at last, engine to ore and ore to dust, the grey Waste that all machinery comes to. You were going somewhere once, by some engine's hour. This city grinds every hour to the same grey grit, and not one of its numberless wheels has ever turned up your name.
