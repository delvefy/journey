Cold

It is not really a thing at all - only the going-away of heat, the empty place warmth leaves behind. Yet nothing feels more real on a January morning, when cold reaches through your coat and pinches your ears and turns your breath to smoke. It cracks the puddles into glass and silvers every blade. You cannot hold it or hand it to a friend; you can only shiver, stamp your feet, and hurry in to where the fire is.

North

When sun and rain shake hands at last,
I arch the sky with colours vast;
from red to violet, seven wide,
a pot of gold they say I hide.

East

From thunder-clouds I clatter down,
hard little stones of ice, all round;
I bounce off roofs and sting your head,
a frozen rain that hurts instead.

South

On the morning grass I lie,
small wet beads from a clear night sky;
no cloud dropped me, no rain at all,
I gather while the dark stars fall.

West

I am the first thin light of day,
when night at last is drawn away;
the cockerel crows, the sky turns rose,
and out of dark the morning grows.
