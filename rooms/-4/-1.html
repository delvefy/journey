The Failing Day

West of the marker the day begins to fail. The faint road and its one weathered stone lie behind you now; ahead there is only open ground, pale and level and going grey, and over it a light that is leaving — not a sun setting, for there is no sun to find, only the even daylight thinning toward an early dark, the way a cold room dims when the last coal dies though no hand touched the lamp. Nothing was built here to lose. Nothing burned here to leave a cinder. There is only the ground, and the going-out of the light upon it.

As the light fails a cold comes up out of the ground rather than down from any sky — the deep cold the waymark's road was sinking toward, the older dark beginning to take back the open day. You stoop and scratch the spiral into the dust, and the low steady wind off the Waste lifts it grain by grain and carries it away before the coil can close: ash without a fire, drift with no ruin to drift from. The whorl in your fingertip is the only spiral that will hold here, and even that, you feel, is dimming with the light.

There is no bell, and no count but the wind — and as the day goes, the wind drops too, until there is a silence so complete it keeps the shape of a sound long gone, the same shape the roofless shrine's vault still held. Here even that is wearing smooth.

To the north the ground firms back to a faint road and a weathered marker, the last made thing, a way that no longer goes. To the east the open ground gathers into the roofless shrine, three cold courses raised at least to be answered in. To the south the failing day goes wholly dark, the ground sinking into the older cold and the deep before any ground. To the west the Waste widens past the last of the light, ash to a horizon that never comes, nothing to answer and nothing to recall. You were going somewhere, on some road. The road is behind you; ahead the light fails on open ground, and there is no marker left to say which dark to walk into.
