The Never-Built

South of the unhaunted country the day gives out. A step up there was at least a grey ordinary day laid over it — a fence, a cart-rut, the long flat distance; here the light thins out of even that, the day not so much ending as forgetting to go on, and the flat ground goes down into an older dark that has no edge to it. Nothing is ruined, because nothing was ever raised — not a wall, not the cold ring of a hearth such as gathers a step east. Only bare cold ground, and the long quiet it was always going to sink into.

This is where the draught is born that you felt cross the broken shrine. It lifts off this ground of its own accord and goes east, toward the one fallen thing left standing to be cold in; here it is only the country breathing out, too slow to be breath, over a place that was never built and so has kept nothing and has nothing to forget. You look for the coil, out of habit. It is here, come down as far as a thing can come — not graven, not scratched out, not even a happen-shape in a fence-rail now, only the turn a little ash makes when the draught lifts it and lets it settle. A curl that holds nothing, read by no one. Never asked to mean, it has nothing to stop meaning, and the ground forgets it as it forgets all things.

There is no bell, and below the unhaunted not even a far gate knocking. Only the draught, in and out across the flat, keeping no hour — the one motion in a country that has nowhere to be and nothing it could be late for.

To the north the country firms back into an ordinary grey day, fence and rut and the long flat distance. To the east the bare ground gathers into ruin, walls that were at least once raised, a roof that at least once fell. To the south the day gives out the rest of the way into older cold dark, the deep from before any country, before there was a ground to forget upon. To the west the quiet only lengthens, the forgotten Waste going on past where even a draught will stir, where names died so long ago that nothing recalls they were ever names. You were going somewhere once, under a name. The flat takes it without noticing, the way it takes the ash, and carries neither anywhere.
