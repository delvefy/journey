Vine

A long climbing stem that cannot stand alone — give it a wall or a tree and it winds upward, hanging its leaves and fruit along the way. The vine is wholly natural and wholly there to touch. It keeps the green south-west. Speak the name at each wall to pass.

North

A small round stone the stream rolled smooth,
just right to skip across the bay;
I rattle loose in a child's coat
and click in a heap on the shingle-grey.

East

Coiled like a crook, then opened wide,
my feathery fronds in the shady wood;
I bear no bloom and no bright seed —
just green, where it is damp and good.

South

Cut from the bog in heavy sods,
brown and crumbling, ages old;
stack me, dry me, set me alight —
I smoulder slow and keep out cold.

West

Dark green leaves that climb the wall,
I cling and creep up brick and tree;
I stay all winter, never bare —
round the old ruin, that is me.
