The Old Night

South of the stilled Waste and west of where nothing began, the dark gets old. Not empty — older than empty. A step north the wind had stopped and left a silence; a step east the ground had never begun; here you stand in something older than either, the dark from before there was a Waste to be laid waste, before there was a nothing to settle, the first night out of which all the later ruin and all the later making were still, unimaginably, to come. It does not feel like absence. It feels like age — a weight of years so vast that absence and presence are both too young to have happened yet.

Everything you carry is too recent here. The spiral in your skin, the warmth of your hand, the very habit of reaching — all of it belongs to some late, hurried world that will not exist for ages out of this dark, and you feel almost an intruder from the future, a thing wandered too far back down the long slope of the past. You draw the coil and it is the newest mark this dark has ever held, raw and young against an age that has seen nothing at all and waited through every part of it. The old night takes the gesture the way deep time takes a single day: without weight, without record, a flicker too brief to have been.

There is no count, only the age itself, which is the opposite of a count — not time measured but time pooled, gone so still and so deep that your heartbeat sounds in it impossibly quick, a small frantic new thing in a dark that has not moved since before movement, the youngest sound ever made here, and the only one.

To the north the old night lifts toward the stilled Waste, the windless dark, a silence that at least once had a wind to lose. To the east the dark firms to raw cold ground, the unbegun, where nothing has yet begun. To the south the night deepens toward the pole, older than itself, the deepest past, before there was a dark to be old. To the west it wears toward the Waste's far edge, the oldest dark forgotten, even its age worn down to grey. You were going somewhere, once — the youngest, most recent fact in all this age, a thing from a world still ages ahead. The old night does not refuse it. It is simply too old to have heard of it yet.
