Branch

Halfway between the great trunk and the smallest twig, the branch reaches out into the open air. It is the tree's arm: thick at the shoulder where it leaves the trunk, thinning as it goes, dividing and dividing until it frays into twigs and leaves. Birds line up along it. Children hang from it and dream of treehouses. In a storm it bends and groans but mostly holds, and in autumn it lets its bright load go, one leaf at a time.

North

I am the green that cloaks the ground,
in field and lawn and park I'm found;
mowers cut me, cows me chew,
a blade of me can whistle too.

East

I'm thicker than a weed, but low,
no single trunk on which to grow;
a rounded clump of leaf and stem,
a hedge of me can fence you in.

South

I spring up where I'm not wanted,
through the gravel, bold, undaunted;
the gardener hates me, pulls and grumbles,
I'm the wild green plant he tumbles.

West

I am a tiny wooden spear,
I guard the rose I love so dear;
I prick your finger, draw a tear,
a sharp surprise when you come near.
