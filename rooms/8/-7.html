Scarf

You are a long warm strip of cloth for the neck, knitted or woven, wound round and round against the winter wind. A maker spun and worked you; you are wholly wrought, fringed at the ends and soft against the throat. You are looped twice over and tucked into the coat, trailed behind on a run, lost on the bus and pined for. Striped, plain, or a team's bright colours, you are warmth you wrap around yourself, the cosy wound length that keeps the chill from creeping down your collar.

North

A cord that threads the eyelet holes
and ties your shoe up neat;
or a fine and netted cloth
for collars, fine and sweet.

East

A one-piece garment, top to skirt,
that girls and women wear;
it twirls out wide when you spin round
at parties, fine and fair.

South

A soft cloth cover for the head,
sewn on a coat behind;
pull me up when rain comes down
to keep the wet off, kind.

West

A snug warm wrap for the whole hand,
a finger-sheath for each;
it keeps off cold, it grips the bat,
and lies in pairs in reach.
