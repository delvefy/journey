Number

You are how the world is counted - one, two, three, and on without end. Before you, there was only 'some' and 'many'; with you came the exact, the measured, the fair share. You ride on every clock and coin and calendar, on the score and the price and the page. The child learns you on small fingers; the scientist writes the stars in you. You have no colour, no weight, no place you live - you are pure idea, and yet nothing large was ever built or bought or proven without you keeping the tally true.

North

A long slow breath let out in full,
when you are tired or sad;
'oh well,' it says, a weary pull,
half sorry and half glad.

East

Add the figures in a row,
and write down what they make;
two and two make four, you know,
the total that you take.

South

A string of questions, asked to see
what you have learnt and stored;
get them right and happily
you win a high reward.

West

In a neat straight column down,
the things to buy or do;
tick them off all over town,
till none are left for you.
