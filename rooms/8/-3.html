Pillow

You are the soft white cloud at the head of the bed, a sack of feathers or foam made for one purpose - to cradle the tired head. Plumped up by day, dented all night by the weight of dreams, you take the shape of whoever sleeps on you and lose it again each morning. You are punched in temper, hugged in loneliness, soaked with the secret tears no one sees. Pillow-fights are fought with you; forts are built of you. Wherever the head finally lies down, you are the last kind thing it meets.

North

A flat plank fixed against the wall,
up off the floor, in rows;
I hold your books and jars and all,
in a tidy line, it shows.

East

A thick warm cloth upon the bed,
I tuck you in at night;
I keep the winter cold ahead,
and hold you snug and tight.

South

A slippery bar of suds and smell,
I scrub you in the bath;
I bubble up and clean you well,
then slide out on my path.

West

With pillow soft and blanket deep,
I'm where you lay your head;
all night long I hold your sleep,
warm and snug and spread.
