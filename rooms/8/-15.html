Whistle

You are the whistle - a little tube of metal or plastic that turns your breath into a sharp bright shriek. Blow hard, and out comes a sound that cuts across a whole field: the referee stops the game, the train pulls away, the dog comes running home. A pea rattles inside the loud ones. Small enough to hang on a string round your neck, and louder than any single voice.

North

Six strings stretched across a hole,
you strum them with your hand;
twang out a tune both loud and sweet -
the star of any band.

East

Blow through a little soapy ring
and out I float, so round;
shiny, see-through, light as air -
I pop without a sound.

South

A baby grips me in one fist
and shakes me, shaka-shak;
I'm full of little beads that clack -
a toy that rattles back.

West

Little cubes with spots of black,
from one to six they show;
shake and throw them, count the dots -
then move your piece, and go.
