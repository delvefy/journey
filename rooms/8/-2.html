Shelf

You are a single plank fixed level to the wall, asking only to be allowed to hold things up. Books lean along you in their bright rows; jars of jam stand in the pantry on you; the small treasures of a life - the photo, the shell, the trophy - are set out on you to be seen. You take the clutter off the floor and give each thing a place. You bow a little in the middle under a heavy load, and never once complain. Plain and flat and faithful, you are order, nailed to the wall.

North

I show you back your very face,
the moment you draw near;
yet keep no print, no smudge, no trace,
once you have stepped from here.

East

A table built for school or work,
with a drawer to hold the pen;
you sit and write, you do not shirk
your lessons, now and then.

South

A soft white sack of feather down,
beneath your sleeping head;
I cushion you, the softest crown,
the comfort of the bed.

West

On four straight legs I hold up flat
the dinner and the game;
we draw our chairs and have a chat,
and eat, and chat again.
