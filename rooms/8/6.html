Hiccup

You are the small absurd jolt of the chest, a catch and a squeak that comes again and again whether you will it or not. No hand makes you; you gulp your food too fast, or laugh, or simply start for no reason, and then you cannot stop. People hold their breath for you, drink water upside down, beg to be frightened out of you. A silly wrought interruption, you hop along inside until, as suddenly as you came, you are gone.

North

A long low sound of weariness,
half a moan, half a sigh;
out it slips when chores are set
or a dull speech drags by.

East

Your cheeks go hot and turn bright red
when you are shy or shamed;
it spreads across your face uncalled,
and you can't be blamed.

South

A nip of skin twixt thumb and finger,
a sharp, quick little tweak;
for luck, for spite, to wake you up,
it leaves a small red streak.

West

A gulping, shuddering kind of cry
that shakes you when you weep;
it heaves the chest and steals the breath,
a grief too deep for sleep.
