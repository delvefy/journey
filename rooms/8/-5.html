Cloth

You are woven stuff, warp crossed over weft on the loom, a flat soft sheet made wholly by the hand and the machine. You are cut to shirts and curtains, torn to bandages, spread on tables, wrung out at the sink. No animal grew you whole - you were spun from wool or cotton and woven thread by thread. Plain or patterned, you dry the dish and dress the man; the loom's quiet, patient, everyday work.

four ways out · speak the name to pass

❦

North

A slippery bar that makes a foam
and smells of flowers or pine;
I wash the dirt from hands and face
and leave them clean and fine.

East

A thick warm mat upon the floor
where cats curl up to doze;
I cover up the cold bare boards
and warm your wandering toes.

South

I criss-cross up the front of a shoe
through little holes in pairs;
pull me tight and tie a bow,
then off you go upstairs.

West

A long thin strand wound on a reel,
I follow where the needle leads;
I sew the button, mend the tear,
and stitch up all your needs.
