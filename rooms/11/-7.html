Tie

You are a long thin strip of cloth, knotted at the throat and hung down the front of a smart shirt - striped, spotted, or plain. A hand loops and tucks you into a neat fat knot. Dad wears you to work; the schoolchild wears you with the uniform; you are loosened with a sigh at the end of the day.

North

A thick warm cover for the bed,
stitched up in patchwork squares;
stuffed soft, it keeps the cold of night
from the sleeper, unawares.

East

A soft warm cloth thrown over the shoulders,
no sleeves, no shape, just square;
Granny wraps it round her arms -
it keeps off the cold night air.

South

A long loose cape with no sleeves at all,
it hangs from the shoulders down;
a wizard wears one, swept and dark,
clasped at the throat, no gown.

West

A sleeveless top worn next the skin,
soft, snug, and warm, and white;
no arms, no buttons, slipped straight on -
worn under shirts, from sight.
