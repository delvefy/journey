Van

You are a boxy covered wagon with an engine - bigger than a car, smaller than a lorry, with no seats in the back, only room for boxes. A hand built you to carry things: parcels, furniture, a workman's tools. Doors swing wide at your rear; you rumble down the street and stop at every door. Plain and useful, you are the carrier, the moving room on wheels.

North

At school they hand you out a sheet
of questions, row on row;
you sit in silence, chew your pen,
to prove how much you know.

East

Bigger than a van, with mighty wheels,
it growls along the road;
it hauls the heavy timber, stone, and steel -
a giant carrying its load.

South

A house of cloth held up by poles,
pegged tight against the ground;
you sleep in it when camping out
where no real roof is found.

West

A long bright box on rumbling wheels
that carries crowds to town;
it halts at every painted stop
to let folk on and down.
