Tent

You are a little house of canvas, raised on poles and pegged down tight against the wind. A hand pitches you in a field or a wood, and inside you it is dry while the rain drums on your roof. You fold away to nothing in a bag, then spring up again at the next camp. You are the traveller's roof, a home carried on the back.

North

No seats behind, just empty space
for boxes, crates, and chairs;
a hand drives it from door to door
delivering its wares.

East

A tube you hold inside your fist
that throws a beam of light;
click it on beneath the sheets
to read a book at night.

South

The part you grab to lift or turn -
on a cup, a door, a pail;
a hand was made to fit it round,
to grip and never fail.

West

A bright cloth fixed upon a pole
that ripples in the breeze;
each land has one of its own colours -
it waves for all to see.
