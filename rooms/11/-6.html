Quilt

You are a thick warm bed cover, two cloths sewn together with soft stuffing between, stitched all over in squares. A hand pieces you from patches - scraps of old dresses and shirts, each square a little memory. You lie heavy and snug on the bed, keeping the winter out. Grandmothers make you by the fireside.

North

A little flat thing by the door
to wipe your dirty feet;
it lies before the step all day -
'wipe!' it says, 'be neat.'

East

Two rows of little metal teeth
that lock together tight;
you pull the tab and 'zzzip!' it goes -
to do your coat up right.

South

A long thin strip you knot at the throat,
worn down a smart shirt's front;
striped or spotted, pulled up tight -
a grown-up's collar stunt.

West

A thin bright strip of silky cloth
to tie a present neat,
or bind a braid of long brushed hair -
a bow both soft and sweet.
