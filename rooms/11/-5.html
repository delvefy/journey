Mat

You are a little flat piece of floor-covering by the door - rough and bristly to scrape the mud off muddy shoes. A hand weaves you from coir or rubber and lays you on the step. 'WIPE YOUR FEET,' you seem to say. You lie still and let the whole household tramp across you. Small, flat, and patient.

North

A soft fat pad you sit upon
to soften a hard chair;
stuffed full of feathers, plumped up round -
the comfiest thing there.

East

A box that slides in and out of a chest,
you pull it by its knob;
your socks and pants are folded there -
to shut it is your job.

South

A thick warm cover for the bed,
stitched up in patchwork squares;
stuffed soft, it keeps the cold of night
from the sleeper, unawares.

West

A wide flat cloth, smooth and cool,
spread out across the bed;
tucked tight beneath the mattress -
white, where you lay your head.
