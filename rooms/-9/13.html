Bronze

You are the warm brown-gold of the old metal and the third-place medal - the statue gone green with age, the bell, the suntanned arm, the autumn beech. No hand made you alone; copper and tin, melted together, did. Not as bright as gold, but older, and used for the first tools and the first great bells.

North

A reddish-brown shiny metal,
the colour of an old penny;
of pipes and pots and a fox's coat -
a warm bright glow, if any.

East

The fine wet mist the waves throw up,
that flies off the crashing sea;
the hose, the fountain, the splashing oar -
small drops blown wild and free.

South

The colour of the sun and rings,
of treasure, crowns, and corn;
the shiniest, most precious shade -
a yellow richly born.

West

An empty space with nothing on it,
the page with not a mark;
your mind when the answer won't come back -
all white, and blank, and stark.
