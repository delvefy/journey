Fly

You are the little buzzing nuisance of the summer, two gauzy wings and a thousand eyes, forever landing where you're least wanted. No hand made you - you hatched as a maggot in something rotten and grew wings to find the next. You walk on the ceiling, rub your legs together, taste with your feet, and dodge every swat. You are quick, grubby, and impossible to catch. Small, busy, and unloved, you are the warm day's ceaseless, buzzing, restless little speck of life.

North

Like a rabbit, but wild and long of leg,
it races on the down;
it boxes in the fields in spring
and never lives in town.

East

A crawling bug in a shiny shell,
six legs and feelers two;
it lifts its case and buzzes off
on wings of see-through hue.

South

Long and legless, scaled and smooth,
I slide across the grass;
I flick my forky tongue to taste
the air through which I pass.

West

A slimy, shell-less, crawling thing
that creeps across the ground;
it leaves a silver trail of slime
and eats the garden down.
