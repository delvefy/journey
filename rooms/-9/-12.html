Crocodile

You are the long armoured lurker of the river, all teeth and tail, floating like a log until the moment to strike. No hand made you; the ancient swamp did. You snap your great jaws shut on what comes to drink. Smile, and the rows of teeth show. Old as the dinosaurs, patient as stone - never trust a still crocodile.

North

A slow sea-swimmer in a green shell,
with leathery flippers wide;
it hauls up the beach to lay its eggs -
then back to the rolling tide.

East

The great gold cat with shaggy mane,
the roarer, king of all;
it hunts the plains and naps in sun -
the biggest, proudest call.

South

A beast that bounds on great hind legs,
with a pouch where its baby hides;
it boxes, hops, and thumps its tail -
in hot far lands it strides.

West

A big fat sea-beast, brown and slow,
with two long tusks of white;
it flops on ice and barks out loud -
with whiskers, quite a sight.
