Ice

You are water gone hard and still, the cold's own glass. No hand pours you - the frost lays you across the pond overnight, hangs you in spears from the gutter, locks the puddle so it rings underfoot. You are slippery and clear and bitter to hold, and the sun is your undoing: a morning of warmth and you are water again, running away. Touchable, breakable, born of no maker but the long night's cold.

North

At the end of dog or cat or fox
I wag, I swish, I curl;
I show you when a beast is glad,
and round behind I twirl.

East

A little pool left by the rain
that gathers in the street;
a child will stamp me with a splash
and soak two woollen feet.

South

The biggest beast in all the sea,
I blow a spout of spray;
I am no fish, I breathe the air,
and sing the miles away.

West

A ribbon of water down the hill,
I chatter over stone;
too small to be a river yet,
I run and run alone.
