tail

You are a tail, the body's last word, hung at the back where the spine runs out. The dog wags you for joy, the cat lashes you in temper, the fish sculls with you through dark water, the horse flicks you at the summer flies. You balance the squirrel along the branch and steer the swallow through the air. No one plans you; you grow where the animal grows, trailing behind to tell its mood to all who can read you.

North

A low green dip between the hills,
where a small river goes;
the cattle graze, the mill-wheel mills,
and down the warm wind blows.

East

A fan of feathers at my side,
I beat them and I rise;
spread me wide and out I glide
across the open skies.

South

Water grown too cold to flow,
I'm hard and clear and slick;
I cap the pond where children go
to slide and skate and kick.

West

A hard horn foot the horses wear,
that clops upon the stone;
the blacksmith nails a shoe up there,
on a leg of skin and bone.
