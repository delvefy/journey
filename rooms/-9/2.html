Spark

You are the tiniest scrap of fire, struck from flint or leaping off a burning log, here and gone in a breath. No hand shapes you - the stone strikes the steel, or the wood spits you upward, and you wink out before you fall. Yet from you the whole blaze can begin: catch you in dry grass and you become flame. You are the smallest beginning of light, untouchable, fleeting, the first bright thought of fire.

North

I leave your throat on a breath of air
and carry what you mean;
loud in a shout or soft in song,
I'm heard but never seen.

East

A thin straight line of light I am,
shot from the sun or lamp;
I pierce the cloud, I cross the room,
and warm the morning's damp.

South

The first of all the senses five,
I work by light alone;
shut your eyes and I am gone,
open them, I'm shown.

West

I dance upon the candle's wick
in orange, gold, and red;
I throw a light across the room
and die when I'm not fed.
