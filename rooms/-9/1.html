sight

You are sight, the swiftest of the senses, the one that drinks the world at the speed of light. You ask nothing but open eyes and a little sun. By you come the colour of an apple, the face of a friend across the room, the first star, the last page. You leap a mile in a heartbeat where touch must wait for the hand. Yet shut the lids, and the bright world you gathered simply is not there.

North

A tiny child of flint and steel,
I leap up bright and gone;
one bite of me the dry leaves feel,
and a whole great fire is born.

East

The sense that lives in fingertip,
in skin from head to toe;
by me you feel the cup, the whip,
the smooth, the sharp, the snow.

South

Down where two hillsides lean and meet,
a green and sheltered ground;
a stream winds through, the soil is sweet,
and farms lie all around.

West

You cannot hold me in your hand,
I parch your throat and lip;
I drive you to the spring to stand
and quench me with a sip.
