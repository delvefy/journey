Hare

You are the long-eared runner of the open field, bigger and wilder than any rabbit, and you never go to ground. No hand made you - you were born above the earth in a scrape of grass, eyes open and ready to run. In spring you box and leap like a mad thing; all year you outrun the fox and the hound across the bare brown down. Swift, lean, and solitary, you are the meadow's wild speed, all ears and racing legs.

North

The greatest beast in all the deep,
a giant of the sea;
it spouts a fountain when it breathes
and sings so mournfully.

East

A shy swift creature of the wood,
with antlers like a tree;
it bounds away on slender legs
the moment that it's free.

South

A buzzing pest with goggle eyes
that settles on your jam;
it walks the ceiling upside down
and dodges every slam.

West

I hang upside down in caves all day
and flit at dusk for flies;
I am no bird, I have no feathers,
yet I fill the evening skies.
